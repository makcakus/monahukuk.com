## Banking and Financial Regulation

### BDDK Revoked Bank Mellat's Istanbul Operating License

**BDDK Decision No. 11572** (18/09/2026) revoked the operating license of **Bank Mellat Merkezi Tahran Istanbul Turkey Head Office**, an Iranian state-owned bank. The decision was published in the Official Gazette on 19 September 2026. Bank Mellat had operated in Turkey since **16 April 1982**, following a Council of Ministers authorization dated **5 February 1981** — making it one of Turkey's longest-standing foreign bank branches.

The legal basis cited was **Article 71(1)(b) of Banking Law No. 5411**, which authorizes BDDK to revoke an operating license where continued operation is found to threaten depositors' rights or the confidence and stability of the financial system.

**Background:** Bank Mellat served as a key conduit for Turkey-Iran trade financing and cross-border transfers, particularly for companies engaged in bilateral commerce.

**What it means in practice:** Revocation triggers asset-protection procedures and potentially a transfer to the Deposit Insurance Fund (TMSF). Depositors and creditors should monitor BDDK's official communications. Companies relying on Bank Mellat for SWIFT transfers need contingency arrangements urgently. Expect increased demand for legal advice on Article 71 processes — bank administration, transfer, or winding-up — in the coming months.

### BDDK Grants Temporary Capital Relief for Listed Bank Share Buybacks

BDDK ruled that shares repurchased by banks listed on Borsa Istanbul **after 16 September 2026** will not be treated as core capital deductions through **31 December 2026**, under the Capital Adequacy Measurement Regulation.

The measure is a direct response to liquidity pressures created by recent fund defaults in the market. It is a time-limited exception: banks on Borsa Istanbul may execute buyback programmes with reduced capital constraints until year-end. Banking and capital markets counsel should flag the temporary nature of this relief and ensure clients' buyback programmes account for its expiry.

## Constitutional Court Decisions

### AYM Rejected Challenge to Judicial Review Ban on Military Minor Penalties

**AYM Decision E: 2025/212, K: 2026/127** (4/6/2026, Official Gazette: 17/09/2026):

Ankara Administrative Court No. 23 challenged provisions in **Article 43 of Law No. 6413** (Turkish Armed Forces Discipline Law, as amended by **Law No. 7517**) that close the judicial avenue against warning (uyarma) and reprimand (kınama) sanctions imposed on military officers. AYM ruled the provision does not violate Articles **13, 36, or 129 of the Constitution** and rejected the objection.

**Court's reasoning:** Warning and reprimand are minor penalties, and even where direct review is unavailable, courts can examine their lawfulness when hearing challenges to the more severe downstream consequence of discharge (ilişik kesme).

**Six justices dissented** (Kadir Özkaya, Engin Yıldırım, Rıdvan Güleç, Yusuf Şevki Hakyemez, Selahaddin Menteş, Kenan Yaşar), arguing that warning and reprimand sanctions directly affect officers' personnel records, promotions, and reassignments, making the bar on judicial review a disproportionate restriction.

**Who is affected:** Defence lawyers handling military discipline cases should continue the strategy of challenging the downstream administrative act — typically a discharge — rather than the underlying penalty directly. The ruling confirms this indirect route remains the primary avenue.

### AYM Declined to Rule on Repeat Tobacco Fine After Favourable Amendment

**AYM Decision E: 2026/72, K: 2026/143** (25/6/2026, Official Gazette: 17/09/2026):

The Council of State's Thirteenth Chamber challenged the provision in **Law No. 4733** (Tobacco, Tobacco Products and Alcohol Market) that doubled administrative fines for repeat violations. AYM found that **Law No. 7584, Article 21** (11/6/2026) had already amended the rule in a manner favourable to defendants — adding a one-year limitation period for tekerrür — and that the amended rule was the one applicable in the pending case. It concluded there was **no reason to issue a ruling** on the original provision.

**Practical significance:** In administrative-penalty disputes with a criminal character, a subsequent favourable amendment can terminate AYM's review of the original rule. Lawyers defending tobacco or alcohol sector clients should separately analyse pre- and post-11 June 2026 conduct, as the tekerrür conditions now differ materially.

### AYM Found Automatic Maximum Auction Fine Violated Access to Courts

**AYM Individual Application No: 2022/49505** (Gazmer Petrol A.Ş., 20/5/2026, Official Gazette: 17/09/2026):

Gazmer Petrol filed for cancellation of a 28 September 2021 execution auction of mortgaged property. The court dismissed the claim and automatically imposed the maximum penalty under **Article 134(5) of the Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law (İİK)** — **560,000 TL** (10% of the auction price) — without explaining why it chose the maximum rather than a lower amount.

AYM found this violated the **right of access to courts**: the 2021 legislative reform that granted courts discretion over the penalty amount required reasoned, proportionate decision-making, not automatic maximum-rate application. The case was remanded for fresh proceedings.

**Who is affected:** Lawyers bringing or defending auction-cancellation proceedings should note that an unreasoned maximum fine constitutes a ground of appeal. Clients who suffered similar fines should assess whether an individual application to AYM remains open.

### AYM Found Rights Violations on Freedom of Expression and Fair Trial

Among the six AYM decisions published on 17 September 2026, three identified constitutional violations:

- **Application No: 2016/1845** — Publications seized on "terrorist propaganda" grounds were not properly notified to the parties, leading to a finding of **freedom of expression violation**. The seizure was executed but the notification obligation was not fulfilled.
- **Application No: 2021/9171** — In the case of Sgt. Serdar Uzel, exculpatory witness statements were not addressed in the reasoned judgment, constituting a violation of the **right to a reasoned court decision** under Article 36 of the Constitution.
- **Application No: 2022/56875** — The court reviewed the failure to suspend a sentence or defer announcement of judgment despite a discretionary reduction being applied.

### Presidential Decision Altered Forest Boundaries in 27 Provinces

**Presidential Decision No. 11787** (17/09/2026) removed certain areas in **27 provinces** from the legally defined forest zone.

Under **Forestry Law No. 6831**, such delineation changes alter the legal status of the affected parcels, their zoning eligibility, and their potential for construction permits. Parcels removed from the forest zone will require separate procedures to enter urban or agricultural planning. Real estate lawyers advising clients in the 27 affected provinces should verify which cadastral parcels are involved through title registry and forestry administration records. The decision date will constitute a critical reference point in any **title cancellation and registration** proceedings that may follow.
