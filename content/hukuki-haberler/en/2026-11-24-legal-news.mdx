The week's most prominent development in Turkish law was the government's suspension of enforcement proceedings related to HGS electronic toll collections and the opening of a criminal investigation. The Official Gazette carried a presidential decision granting residence permits to qualifying foreign investors and an amendment tightening pilot licence rules. The Court of Cassation issued binding rulings on tenancy and mediation, while the Constitutional Court unanimously struck down the sole legal basis for state-supervised prostitution regulation under the Public Hygiene Law.

## Judiciary and Bar

### HGS Highway Enforcement Suspended; Istanbul Prosecutor Launches Investigation

Justice Minister Akın Gürlek announced that the Istanbul Chief Prosecutor's Office had opened an investigation following complaints that **enforcement proceedings against HGS electronic toll debtors** had been used as a vehicle for unlawful enrichment. On the same day, Transport and Infrastructure Minister Abdulkadir Uraloğlu declared that all legal and enforcement actions conducted by private motorway companies had been **immediately suspended**, with no new enforcement actions to be initiated until new legislation is in place.

HGS enforcement has long been a source of public complaint. Attorney fees routinely reached multiples of the original debt, and the procedural options available to debtors were limited. The ministerial announcement puts both criminal and administrative liability squarely on the table and signals that a statutory framework setting clear boundaries for institutional debt-collection practice is forthcoming.

### Fund Investigation: TRY 44.58 Billion Recovered, 91 Companies Under Receivership

Justice Minister Gürlek disclosed that the Istanbul Prosecutor's **fund investigation** currently involves **632 suspects**, that **91 companies** have been placed under court-appointed administrators, and that **TRY 44.58 billion** has been recovered from 85 individuals through voluntary restitution and asset tracing.

Receivership is authorised under Article 71 of **Banking Law No. 5411** and the relevant provisions of **Turkish Commercial Code No. 6102**. The measure strips existing management of authority and transfers it to a court-appointed administrator, directly affecting both shareholder rights and the recovery of public claims. The scale of voluntary restitution suggests that the prosecutor's office has adopted a negotiated settlement approach alongside conventional enforcement.

### Istanbul Bar Association Resolves Mandatory Defence Counsel Payment Backlog

The Istanbul Bar Association announced that a technical problem affecting payment of fees to mandatory defence counsel assigned to hear **detention review proceedings** in Magistrate Criminal Courts has been resolved.

Mandatory counsel is appointed under Article 150 of **Code of Criminal Procedure No. 5271**. Payment irregularities can undermine the right to effective representation in pre-trial detention, one of the most time-sensitive stages of criminal proceedings. The resolution removes a structural friction point between the bar and the courts, ensuring that assigned lawyers receive timely remuneration.

### BDDK Transfers Adil Katılım Bank Management to TMSF

The **Banking Regulation and Supervision Agency (BDDK)** published a Board Decision in the Official Gazette transferring — excluding dividend rights — the ownership rights, management and supervision of Adil Katılım Bankası A.Ş. to the **Savings Deposit Insurance Fund (TMSF)**.

Transfers under Article 71 of **Banking Law No. 5411** are triggered when a bank's financial soundness deteriorates or when it appears unable to meet its obligations. Deposit holders within TMSF insurance limits remain protected throughout; the effect on existing shareholders depends on the final outcome of the resolution process.

### Cyber Security Authority Opens Two Regulatory Drafts for Public Comment

The **Cyber Security Authority**, established in 2024, has opened its **Cyber Resilience Regulation** and **Inspection Regulation** drafts for public consultation. Both instruments operationalise the Authority's primary legal mandate, setting out the cybersecurity obligations and audit procedures applicable to critical infrastructure operators and public bodies.

The drafts are consequential for defining covered entities, minimum technical standards and enforcement sanctions — areas that have remained uncertain since the Authority's founding. Technology companies, legal advisers and regulated operators should engage with the consultation process to shape the final text and prepare compliant frameworks in advance of enactment.

### Competition Board Opens Investigation into Sony PlayStation Store Pricing

The **Competition Board** opened an investigation to determine whether Sony's **digital game pricing and commission practices on the PlayStation Store** restrict competition, in a proceeding brought under Article 43 of **Competition Law No. 4054**.

This investigation reflects a broader global wave of competition scrutiny directed at digital marketplaces and mirrors regulatory actions in the European Union and other jurisdictions. For app developers and digital content distributors operating in Türkiye, the proceedings are worth monitoring closely: a finding against Sony could reshape platform pricing and access terms under Turkish law.

## Official Gazette and Parliament

### Municipal Elections to Use Combined Ballot Paper by Alphabetical Order

The Ministry of Environment, Urbanisation and Climate Change published a regulation amending the procedure for elections held within municipal councils when the position of mayor or deputy mayor falls vacant. Under the new rules, elections are conducted by **secret ballot using a combined ballot paper** listing candidates in alphabetical order. Council members mark the box next to their chosen candidate's name; a member who is a candidate may vote but may not serve on the presiding committee.

Operating under **Municipal Law No. 5393**, the regulation responds to transparency concerns about internal council elections by reducing discretion in how candidates are presented and standardising the voting procedure. Its practical significance is heightened by the number of municipalities currently operating under appointed administrators.

### Pharmaceutical Promotion Notification Periods Cut; Factory Visits Now Official Activity

An amendment to the **Regulation on Promotional Activities for Medicinal Products for Human Use** shortened the advance-notification period for domestic scientific meetings from **15 to 5 working days** and for international meetings from **30 to 10 working days**. Promotion of authorised products not on the Turkish market is now prohibited. **Manufacturing facility visits** are formally classified as a promotional activity. The obligation to report attendees and expenses within **30 days** of a meeting and to retain documentation for **5 years** continues unchanged.

Under the oversight framework administered by the **Medicines and Medical Devices Agency (TİTCK)**, notification delays can attract administrative sanctions. The shortened timescales make compliance more demanding while simultaneously reducing lead times. Pharmaceutical companies and healthcare law practitioners should update their internal compliance calendars without delay.

### Pilot Licence Criminal Conviction Threshold Raised from One to Two Years

The **Directorate General of Civil Aviation (SHGM)** amended the **Pilot Licensing Regulation (SHY-1)** to raise the threshold custodial sentence for intentional offences that bars an individual from holding a pilot licence from **one year to two years**.

Aviation licence criteria for criminal record are assessed within the framework of international civil aviation standards set out in **ICAO Annex 1**. The higher threshold moderately broadens the pool of eligible applicants. Whether this remains consistent with aviation safety principles is a question that will require careful evaluation in individual cases; existing licence holders and future applicants will need their circumstances assessed under the new threshold.

### Foreign Investors Can Obtain Three-Year Residence Permit for USD 150,000

A **Presidential Decision on Residence Permits for Foreign Investors** published in the Official Gazette extends residence permit eligibility to foreign nationals without a work permit on the following terms:

- **At least USD 150,000** qualifying investment → **3-year** residence permit
- **At least USD 200,000** qualifying investment → **5-year** residence permit

The investor's spouse and minor children are covered by the same entitlement. Issued under **Law No. 6458 on Foreigners and International Protection**, this decision represents the latest addition to Türkiye's investment-based residency policy. Compliance with the qualifying conditions will be subject to periodic review.

### Parliamentary Committee Approves GSS Premium Amnesty for Pre-2017 Debt

The Grand National Assembly Committee on Industry, Trade, Energy, Natural Resources, Information and Technology approved a legislative proposal to waive **overdue General Health Insurance (GSS) premiums** accrued before **1 January 2017**, together with associated late-payment penalties and interest.

Under **Social Insurance and General Health Insurance Law No. 5510**, this measure would relieve a significant segment of the population that has remained outside the formal health insurance system due to accumulated debt. If the full Assembly passes the bill, enforcement of these debts would cease and affected individuals would regain access to SGK health services.

## Court Rulings

### Constitutional Court Unanimously Strikes Down Brothel Regulation under Public Hygiene Law

The **Constitutional Court** unanimously held unconstitutional and annulled the provision of the **1930 Public Hygiene Law** that constituted the sole statutory basis for the regulated exercise of prostitution under state oversight.

The invalidated provision had for decades supplied the administrative and medical supervision framework applied to licensed premises. With the provision removed, the resulting legislative gap — including the status of authorised establishments and existing administrative acts grounded in that provision — will require resolution by the legislature. All administrative acts and contracts that rested on the annulled provision may need to be revisited.

### Court of Cassation: School Manager Who Installed Security Cameras Acquitted

**Court of Cassation, 12th Criminal Chamber** reviewed an appeal in a case where a school administrator was charged with violating **privacy** under Article 134 of the Turkish Criminal Code for installing security cameras in the teachers' room and classrooms. The Chamber affirmed the acquittal, correcting minor elements of the lower court's reasoning.

The decision creates significant precedent at the intersection of institutional security measures and teachers' privacy rights. The Chamber's endorsement of the acquittal indicates that the purpose of the installation and the nature of the monitored space are determinative factors; each factual scenario nonetheless requires individual analysis.

### Mandatory Mediation Is a Procedural Prerequisite in Tenancy Access Cases

**Court of Cassation, 3rd Civil Chamber** (Case 2026/4277, Decision 2026/3806, 15 June 2026) held that in actions seeking a court order requiring a landlord or tenant to allow inspection of the leased premises, **prior application to a mandatory mediator constitutes a procedural prerequisite (dava şartı)**.

Under **Code of Mediation in Civil Disputes No. 6325** and **Labour Courts Law No. 7036**, mandatory mediation applies to tenancy disputes. Failure to attend mediation before filing results in the case being dismissed for lack of a procedural prerequisite — not on the merits. Practitioners advising on end-of-tenancy matters, including access for prospective new tenants, must build mandatory mediation into their litigation timeline.

### Mandatory Defence Counsel Who Submits Power of Attorney for Fees Only Commits Disciplinary Offence

**Union of Turkish Bar Associations Disciplinary Board** (Case 2025/610, Decision 2025/654, 05 September 2025) found that a lawyer appointed as mandatory defence counsel who subsequently files a separate **power of attorney** in the same case — after the prosecutor's opinion requests acquittal — solely to earn attorney fees commits a disciplinary offence contrary to **the duty of integrity and professional dignity** under Article 34 of the **Lawyers' Act**.

The decision makes clear that instrumentalising mandatory counsel appointments for revenue generation will expose lawyers to bar disciplinary proceedings. Counsel assigned in Magistrate Criminal Court proceedings who seek independent retainers in the same files must manage this boundary with care.

### Party Absent from Mediation Opening Session Recovers Only Half Attorney Fee

**Court of Cassation, 3rd Civil Chamber** (Case 2025/5307, Decision 2026/2209, 15 April 2026) held that where a party fails without excuse to attend the **mandatory mediation opening session**, the successful defendant is entitled only to **half the standard attorney fee**; and that for moral damages claims, following a **Constitutional Court annulment** of the relevant provision, no attorney fee at all may be awarded in favour of the defendant.

Under **Labour Courts Law No. 7036**, this rule converts non-attendance at mediation into a quantifiable litigation cost. The halved fee operates as a deterrent against tactical non-attendance designed to extend proceedings. Practitioners should advise clients that non-attendance at the mediation opening session carries concrete financial consequences even if the eventual judgment is favourable.

### Where Case Dismissed for Wrong Defendant, Only Single Flat-Rate Fee Awarded

**Court of Cassation, 10th Civil Chamber** (Case 2025/7260, Decision 2026/4604, 14 April 2026) ruled that where a claim is dismissed for **lack of standing on the defendant's side** (*husumet yokluğu*), the successful defendant is entitled to only a **single flat-rate dismissal attorney fee** under Article 7(2) of the **Attorney Fee Schedule (AAÜT)**; separate fees for each head of claim — material and moral damages — may not be awarded.

The decision resolves a recurring disagreement about whether the attorney fee for a standing-based dismissal is calculated per claim or per action. It is particularly significant for multi-head claims where the wrong defendant has been named: plaintiffs should anticipate a single combined fee exposure, not stacked per-claim fees, when a standing defect is identified and the case is dismissed.
