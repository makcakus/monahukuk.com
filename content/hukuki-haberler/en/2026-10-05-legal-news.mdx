The week's landmark development in Turkish law was the **Constitutional Court (AYM)** invalidating the word "permanently" in **Article 175 of the Turkish Civil Code (TMK)**, the provision governing spousal maintenance awarded on grounds of financial hardship after divorce. The ruling ends lifetime alimony as an automatic outcome and gives **parliament nine months** to enact criteria-based legislation. On the regulatory side, the mandatory rollout of the **real-estate secure-payment system** was postponed to **1 December 2026**, the list of authorised banks for notary security deposits was narrowed to six institutions, and **SGK** updated its pharmaceutical reimbursement regulation.

## Family Law

### Turkey's Constitutional Court Strikes Down Lifetime Alimony Clause

The **Constitutional Court (AYM)** issued Decision **E.2025/156, K.2026/131** on **4 June 2026**, voiding the phrase **"permanently"** (*süresiz olarak*) in the first paragraph of **Article 175 of the Turkish Civil Code** (Law No. 4721). The decision was published in the **Official Gazette on 30 September 2026** (Issue No. **33386**) and adopted by a **majority vote** (dissenting: Engin YILDIRIM, Selahaddin MENTEŞ, Kenan YAŞAR).

**Background:** Under the existing rule, a financially vulnerable divorced spouse could obtain maintenance for an indefinite term regardless of the obligor's circumstances. The Court of Cassation had treated the clause as mandatory, leaving judges no discretion to set a time limit. An earlier AYM decision from 2012 (E.2011/136, K.2012/72) had upheld the same provision.

**New rule:** The phrase is struck down. Parliament must enact replacement legislation within nine months — expected to introduce objective criteria such as duration of marriage, age, health and each party's economic capacity. If parliament fails to act, judges will need to fill the gap through general principles.

**Reasoning:** Imposing an open-ended payment obligation on the debtor spouse, irrespective of fault, disproportionately restricts the obligor's right to protect their material and moral existence (Constitution Art. 17). The Court found that the State's duty to strike a fair balance between competing constitutional rights (Constitution Arts. 2 and 5) had not been met by the existing rule.

**Effective date:** Nine months after 30 September 2026 — placing the deadline around **late June / July 2027**.

**Who is affected:** All ongoing and newly filed divorce proceedings in which spousal maintenance under Art. 175 is at issue. Family law practitioners should advise clients on the transitional implications, particularly regarding the parliamentary window and possible interim judicial interpretations before new legislation takes effect.

## Administrative and Labour Law

### AYM Unanimously Voids Budget Restriction on Fire Service Overtime

The AYM issued Decision **E.2026/57, K.2026/198** on **8 September 2026** — published **30 September 2026** (Issue No. **33386**) — **unanimously** voiding the phrase "excluding staff engaged in support services" in the **K Schedule** of the 2024 Central Government Budget Law as applied to municipal fire-service support personnel.

**Reasoning:** An appropriation act may not contain provisions unrelated to the budget (Constitution Art. 161). The excluded phrase effectively amended Article 52 of **Municipal Law No. 5393**, which governs flat-rate overtime pay for municipal fire-service staff — a change that can only be made by ordinary legislation, not a budget rider.

**Practical effect:** Municipal fire-service support personnel are entitled to the flat-rate overtime pay previously withheld. Municipal finance and HR departments should reassess affected payroll items in light of this decision.

### AYM Finds Right to Reasoned Judgment Violated in Employment Cases

Two individual application decisions published on **29 September 2026** (Issue No. **33385**) confirm a recurring pattern in AYM case law:

- **AYM BB 2022/90210 (Gülşah Kale)** — the applicant had satisfied the six-month tenure threshold for reinstatement but the court failed to address her core arguments on the merits, violating the right to a reasoned judgment.
- **AYM BB 2022/44330 (Refik Bayhan)** — an application to open a private educational institution was rejected without addressing a submission material to the outcome, again in breach of the right to a reasoned judgment.

Both decisions reinforce the obligation on courts at all levels to address submissions capable of affecting the outcome, not merely recite procedural conclusions.

### Dismissal for Local TV Interview Violates Freedom of Expression

AYM Decision **BB 2022/81264 (Mehmet Güven)**, dated **21 April 2026** and published **2 October 2026** (Issue No. **33388**), held that dismissing an employee for statements made to a local television channel constituted a **violation of freedom of expression**.

The core finding: an employer who treats a public-interest statement as sufficient grounds for termination without adequately weighing the employee's constitutional right to free expression infringes Art. 26 of the Constitution. Labour practitioners should factor this decision into proportionality assessments when advising on disciplinary measures triggered by employees' extra-workplace communications.

## Real Estate and Notarial Law

### Real Estate Secure Payment System Delayed to 1 December 2026

An amendment to the **Regulation on Real Estate Trade** (*Taşınmaz Ticareti Hakkında Yönetmelik*), published **1 October 2026** (Issue No. **33387**), postponed the mandatory introduction of the secure-payment system for title-transfer transactions from **1 July 2026** to **1 December 2026**.

**Background:** The regime was set to require all parties in real-estate transactions involving title-deed transfer to use a government-approved secure-payment channel from 1 July 2026.

**New rule:** The deadline shifts five months to 1 December 2026. The amendment also provides for the Ministry of Commerce to authorise a single payment integrator, with a tri-party protocol to be signed between the Ministry of Commerce, the Ministry of Environment and Urbanisation, and the integrator before the system goes live.

**Who is affected:** Individual and corporate buyers and sellers in title-transfer transactions, and real-estate professionals. Continued preparation for December 2026 compliance is advised.

### Notary Security Deposits Restricted to Six Authorised Banks

Two amendments to the **Notary Law Regulation** were published on **2 October 2026** (Issue No. **33388**):

**Amendment 1 (Art. 76):** The list of banks in which notaries may deposit their security deposits is narrowed to six public and participation banks: T.C. Ziraat Bankası A.Ş., Türkiye Halk Bankası A.Ş., Türkiye Vakıflar Bankası T.A.O., Ziraat Katılım Bankası A.Ş., Vakıf Katılım Bankası A.Ş., and Türkiye Emlak Katılım Bankası A.Ş.

**Amendment 2 (Art. 92):** The **family registration certificate** (*nüfus aile kayıt tablosu*) is now mandatory documentation in guardianship and wardship proceedings before a notary; court-order and identity-document requirements are also clarified.

**Who is affected:** Notaries holding security deposits with institutions no longer on the authorised list should arrange timely transfers. Clients conducting guardianship or probate transactions before a notary need to obtain the family registration certificate in advance.

### NXT Electronic Money Institution Granted Operating Licence by CBRT

The **Central Bank of the Republic of Turkey (CBRT)** granted an operating licence to **NXT Elektronik Para ve Ödeme Hizmetleri A.Ş.** under **Law No. 6493** on Payment and Securities Settlement Systems, Payment Services and Electronic Money Institutions. The decision was published on **3 October 2026** (Issue No. **33389**).

The licence grant, arriving shortly after the revocation of Papel's licence in the previous week, illustrates the simultaneous expansion and consolidation underway in Turkey's electronic money sector. Compliance officers at payment-services entities should keep CBRT supervisory requirements under regular review.
