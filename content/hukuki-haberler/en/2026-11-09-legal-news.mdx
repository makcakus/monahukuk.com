Three developments defined Turkish law this week. The **Turkish Bar Association's minimum attorney fee schedule** for 2026-2027 entered into force after publication in the Official Gazette, setting new floor rates across court types. The **Constitutional Court** struck down the 2025 amendment that required courts to apply fair-market value instead of sale price in pre-emption disputes. And the **Competition Board** opened a formal investigation into Google's AI Overviews and AI Mode while simultaneously ordering interim measures — the first such action in Turkey directed at AI-powered search.

## Legal Community Update

### Fund Investigation: 433 Suspects, Systemic Market Fraud at Bureaucratic Level

Justice Minister **Akın Gürlek** announced that investigators have identified a **systematic and hierarchical criminal organisation** that penetrated high-level oversight mechanisms in Turkey's financial system and upper tiers of the state bureaucracy. Of **433 suspects**, **112 have been detained**. The minister stated the case goes well beyond ordinary stock manipulation: the network allegedly sought to capture the market itself.

Under Turkey's **Capital Markets Law (CMK)**, market manipulation charges can carry significant prison terms, but the layered nature of this investigation — combining organised crime statutes with capital market offences — makes it procedurally complex. With a suspect count this large and institutional dimensions this broad, the case will likely remain in pre-trial proceedings for years.

### AI to Measure Judges' Accuracy: 13th Judicial Package on the Way

Minister Gürlek disclosed that an **AI unit** established within the Ministry's IT Directorate will measure judges' and prosecutors' **decision accuracy rates** and case clearance times. Those metrics will feed directly into appointment and promotion decisions. The minister also confirmed that the **13th Judicial Package** — covering fraud-related offences and social media regulation — is expected to reach parliament following the budget review.

Applying algorithmic metrics to judicial performance raises well-documented concerns about institutional independence. What counts as an "accurate" decision, how the model handles appellate reversals, and whether the methodology will be made public all remain open questions. Bar associations are expected to scrutinise the package closely once it is tabled.

### Competition Board Opens Investigation and Orders Interim Measures Against Google AI

The **Competition Board (Rekabet Kurulu)** decided to open a formal investigation into **Google's AI Overviews** and **AI Mode** features and to impose **interim measures** simultaneously. The board will examine whether these AI search functionalities allow Google to consolidate its dominant position at the expense of competing content producers and publishers.

Proceedings are brought under the **Act on the Protection of Competition (Law No. 4054)**. This is the first competition enforcement action in Turkey focused on AI-integrated search and runs parallel to EU Digital Markets Act scrutiny of similar features. For media companies and digital publishers operating in Turkey, the interim measures may alter how AI-generated summaries are displayed while the investigation continues.

### İzmir Public Prosecutor and Metropolitan Municipality Sign Probation Protocol

The **İzmir Chief Public Prosecutor's Office** and the **İzmir Metropolitan Municipality** signed a cooperation protocol to strengthen probation services, support the reintegration of former offenders into social and economic life, and reduce reoffending risk.

**Probation (denetimli serbestlik)** in Turkish law is an execution method that allows offenders to serve part of their sentence in the community under supervision. The more than 400,000 individuals under probation in Turkey represent a significant population where municipal-level service partnerships can have measurable impact on recidivism outcomes.

## Official Gazette and Parliament

### New Minimum Attorney Fee Schedule in Force: 62,000 TL Floor for Civil Courts

The new **Minimum Attorney Fee Schedule (AAÜT)** for 2026-2027, prepared by the Turkish Bar Association, was published in the Official Gazette and entered into force. The key rates: **62,000 TL** for civil courts of general jurisdiction (asliye mahkemesi), **40,000 TL** for civil courts of peace (sulh hukuk mahkemesi), and **86,000 TL** for serious criminal courts (ağır ceza mahkemesi).

The **AAÜT** sets the minimum amount a losing party may be ordered to pay toward the winning side's attorney fees — it is a floor on court-awarded legal costs, separate from the contractual fee between attorney and client. Courts apply this schedule when calculating litigation costs in judgments; parties and practitioners should update their cost projections immediately.

### EIA Regulation Amended: Nuclear Plants and Inter-Basin Water Transfers Now Covered

A revision to the **Environmental Impact Assessment (EIA) Regulation** published in the 7 November 2026 Official Gazette (Issue 33423) adds two categories to the mandatory EIA requirement: **nuclear power plants** and **water transfer projects between river basins**. For thermal power stations, the EIA threshold is set at **300 MWt** capacity, with a screening list entry at **20 MWt**.

**Before:** These project types operated in an ambiguous or partially exempt zone under the existing regulation. **After:** Mandatory EIA applies. This change is immediately relevant for challenge proceedings: opponents of nuclear or water infrastructure projects now have a clear procedural hook for annulment actions if the EIA requirement is not met or is inadequately performed.

### KGK Publishes New Financial Statements Guide: TFRS 18 Mandatory from January 2027

The **Public Oversight Authority (KGK)** published its updated **Financial Statements Model and User Guide** in the Official Gazette on 4 November 2026. The revision extends the exemption period for **sustainability audit teams** by one year, and makes the new guide — aligned with **IFRS 18 (Presentation and Disclosure in Financial Statements)** — mandatory for financial periods beginning **1 January 2027**.

**IFRS 18** replaces IAS 1 and significantly restructures income statement categories and operating performance disclosures. Companies with Turkish statutory reporting obligations need to begin transition planning now; legal advisers acting in M&A, shareholder disputes, or financial covenant compliance matters should factor the reclassification effects into their analyses.

## Constitutional Court and Court Decisions

### Constitutional Court Strikes Down Fair-Market-Value Rule in Pre-Emption Rights

The **Constitutional Court (AYM)** annulled the provisions inserted into **Turkish Civil Code (TMK) Article 734** in 2025 that required courts to apply the **fair-market value** rather than the agreed sale price when calculating the amount the pre-emption (şufa) right holder must pay to exercise their right.

**Before the amendment:** A co-owner exercising pre-emption rights paid the same price the third-party buyer agreed to pay. **The 2025 change:** Courts were to determine market value independently, raising the effective cost of exercising pre-emption. **The ruling:** The AYM found the fair-market-value rule incompatible with property rights guarantees and the principle of legal certainty. **In practice:** Once the annulment takes effect, courts return to the sale-price standard; ongoing pre-emption cases are directly affected.

### AYM: Sharing Work Messages on Company Phone Does Not Create Full Privacy Expectation

The **Constitutional Court** rejected the individual application of **Alper Uluca**, whose employment was terminated after messages he posted in a **WhatsApp group** — shared with colleagues and accessed through a company-issued phone — were forwarded by another employee to management.

**Legal context:** The boundary between an employer's right to monitor company devices and an employee's right to private correspondence under **Labour Law** and personal data legislation is contested. **The ruling:** The AYM found no violation of private life or freedom of communication, reasoning that professional communication over employer-provided equipment carries a reduced expectation of privacy when conducted in a work-related context. **In practice:** A precedent that strengthens employers' hand in dismissals based on digital communication; employees should treat company-device messaging accordingly.

### AYM: One-Month Suspension for Anti-Vaccine Comments Violated Free Expression

The **Constitutional Court** ruled that the **Istanbul Medical Chamber's** decision to impose a one-month **temporary suspension from practice** on **Dr. Mutia Canan Karatay** for her public anti-vaccine statements violated the constitutional right to freedom of expression.

**Context:** Professional disciplinary bodies may impose sanctions for misconduct, but **Article 26 of the Constitution** protects expression even where it conflicts with professional consensus. The AYM applied a proportionality test and found the suspension disproportionate to the aim pursued. **In practice:** Lawyers handling disciplinary proceedings against professionals who make controversial public statements can cite this ruling to challenge the proportionality of sanctions.

### Court of Cassation HGK: Wrong Date on Notice Prevents Mandatory Deadline from Running

**Court of Cassation Grand Civil Chamber** (File 2025/285, Decision 2026/387, 18 June 2026): If the date of an interim order is stated incorrectly in the official notice, the **mandatory deadline** does not begin to run until a correctly dated notice is served.

**Before:** Some trial courts treated the error as a formal defect and counted the deadline from the actual date of service regardless. **Now:** The Grand Chamber holds that a defective notice cannot trigger a mandatory procedural period to the detriment of the party served. **In practice:** Counsel monitoring appeal and cassation deadlines should scrutinise notice dates; a discrepancy is now a viable ground to challenge a missed-deadline ruling.

### Court of Cassation 5th Civil Chamber: Blocked Expropriation Amount Requires Investigation of Compensation Increase Claim

**Court of Cassation 5th Civil Chamber** (File 2026/7949, Decision 2026/11519, 18 June 2026): Where it cannot be proved that a blocked expropriation payment was actually received by the landowner, the court must investigate whether a **compensation increase (tezyid-i bedel) claim** has been or should be filed.

Turkish expropriation law gives landowners two distinct remedies: receipt of payment and the right to seek a higher valuation. This decision links the two: failure to establish actual payment reopens the door to a valuation challenge. **In practice:** Counsel representing landowners in expropriation matters should check payment documentation carefully before closing off this avenue.

### Court of Cassation CGK: Prosecutor Requesting "Compliance with Reversal" Is Not a Closing Argument

**Court of Cassation Criminal General Assembly** (File 2026/209, Decision 2026/463, 9 September 2026): A public prosecutor's statement requesting that "it be decided to comply with the reversal order" does not constitute a **closing argument on the merits** (esas hakkında mütalaa).

Under **Article 216 of the Code of Criminal Procedure (CMK)**, the prosecution must deliver a substantive closing argument; failure to do so is a reversible error. The Criminal General Assembly makes clear that the procedural act of compliance does not satisfy the substantive obligation. **In practice:** Defence counsel in retrial proceedings should verify that a proper merits-based argument was given, not merely a compliance request.

### Court of Cassation HGK: Divorce Judgment Can Become Final While Fault Is Still on Appeal

**Court of Cassation Grand Civil Chamber** (File 2025/675, Decision 2026/410, 24 June 2026): An appeal limited to fault attribution does not prevent the divorce decree itself from becoming final. The chamber also held that if a spouse dies while the case is pending, the marriage ends by death and the divorce action becomes moot.

**The practical problem this resolves:** Litigants sometimes used fault appeals to delay the finality of the divorce, leaving financial and custody arrangements in limbo. **The ruling:** Divorce finality and fault determination now operate on separate tracks. **In practice:** Family law practitioners can structure cases so that dissolution is finalised without waiting for fault proceedings to conclude, reducing uncertainty for clients on interim financial matters.
