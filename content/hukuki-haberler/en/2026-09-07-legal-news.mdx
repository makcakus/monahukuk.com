Turkey's 2026-27 judicial year opened this week with contrasting messages from the Ministry of Justice and the Turkish Bar Association — a recurring divide between institutional optimism on court efficiency and bar-level concerns about judicial independence. The week's Official Gazette issues brought the Medium-Term Programme into force, expanded the controlled-substances list, and introduced new monthly reporting duties for digital platforms.

---

## Legal Profession News

### Judicial Year Opens with Reasonable Time and Access as Top Priorities

The **2026-27 judicial year** was inaugurated at the Yargıtay İsmail Rüştü Cirit Conference Hall in Ankara. Justice Minister **Akın Gürlek** set out three priorities: strengthening the right to seek justice and access to courts, completing proceedings within a reasonable time, and increasing public trust in the judiciary. Yargıtay (Court of Cassation) President **Kerkez** framed the objective as ensuring citizens reach their legal rights "faster and completely."

Turkey faces frequent condemnation before the **European Court of Human Rights** for violations of **Article 6 ECHR** on reasonable trial duration — one of the largest categories of ECtHR compensation liability for the country. The fact that this issue recurs as the centrepiece of the judicial year opening underlines how persistent the problem has become, while indicating that there is continuing institutional will to address it.

---

### Bar President: Detention Has Become Punishment, Defence Rights Constrained

Turkish Bar Association (TBB) President **Erinç Sağkan** used the judicial year ceremony to deliver pointed criticism of judicial independence and freedom of expression. Sağkan stated that pressure exists on "permitted and non-permitted thought," that pre-trial detention has effectively been converted into a punishment mechanism, and that a system has been created in which even release orders meet with resistance.

These assessments mirror the concerns of the Council of Europe and international bar associations regarding the length and conditions of pre-trial detention in Turkey. For criminal defence lawyers, this broader context has practical significance: it bears on how courts handle release motions, the timeliness of procedural decisions, and restrictions on lawyer-client communication.

---

### Justice Minister Announces Specialised Courts Under Medium-Term Programme

Justice Minister **Gürlek** outlined judicial reforms to be pursued under the **2027-2029 Medium-Term Programme**: strengthening alternative dispute resolution, advancing digital justice tools, and establishing **specialised courts**.

**Specialised courts** are designed to develop expertise and consistency in defined legal fields. Turkey's earlier moves in this direction included subject-matter jurisdiction assignments to Ankara administrative courts. The new programme may extend specialisation to commercial, intellectual property and finance cases. The expected expansion of mandatory mediation is a development that commercial and labour practitioners should incorporate into pre-litigation planning.

---

### Greece Asked to Extradite Leaders of Bayğaralar Criminal Organisation

Justice Minister **Gürlek** met his Greek counterpart **Floridis** and formally requested the extradition of **Ramazan Bayğara**, the alleged leader of the "Bayğaralar" criminal organisation, together with **Nedim Sorguç** and **Ali Yeşildağ**.

Extradition requests between Turkey and Greece are processed under the **1957 European Convention on Extradition** and bilateral agreements. The dual criminality requirement, the political offence exception and the scope of evidentiary disclosure are the key legal thresholds in any such request. As cross-border organised crime prosecutions multiply, expertise in extradition law is becoming an increasingly valuable specialism for criminal practitioners.

### Istanbul Bar Opens Education Centre: Academy, Library and Social Space Together

The Istanbul Bar Association's Education, Research and Practice Centre — combining **BaroAkademi**, **BaroKütüphane** and **BaroLokal** — was formally inaugurated. TBB President Sağkan described the building as "more than stone and steel — it has a soul," while Istanbul Bar President **İbrahim Ö. Kaboğlu** stated that the centre will serve lawyers from across Turkey, not only Istanbul.

The centre brings together continuing professional development, academic research, trainee supervision and professional networking under a single roof. It represents a concrete development for trainee lawyers and experienced practitioners seeking structured CPD opportunities.

### TBB Launches DavaTek, Its First Digital Case Data Assistant for Lawyers

**The Turkish Bar Association (TBB)** announced the launch of **DavaTek**, a digital data assistant designed to give lawyers more effective access to case data. The platform is among the first officially bar-backed tools aimed at supporting data-driven practice in Turkish law.

The adoption of AI-assisted tools promoted by bar associations raises questions of service quality and professional liability. Errors or outdated content in such systems can expose lawyers to malpractice risk. It is anticipated that the TBB will update its ethical guidance to address this; practitioners should assess whether their professional indemnity cover extends to AI-assisted work product.

### Istanbul Bar Issues Justice Declaration: Defence and Liberty Violations Named

The Istanbul Bar Association published a **"Justice Declaration"** at the judicial year opening. The declaration specifically identified violations in the areas of the right to a fair trial, judicial independence, freedom of defence, personal liberty and security, and the rights of detained persons.

Such declarations do not generate direct legal obligations, but they serve to document systemic concerns in a form usable in **ECtHR applications** and domestic proceedings. For practitioners dealing with human rights violation claims, bar declarations provide contextual support for establishing a pattern of systemic practice.

### Justice Ministry Opens 135 Legal Information Helpline

The Ministry of Justice's Information Technologies Directorate launched the **"135 Alo Adalet"** (135 Legal Justice) telephone helpline, giving citizens a direct channel to access information about court proceedings and justice services.

The helpline may prove practically useful for litigants unfamiliar with court procedures and for trainee lawyers handling basic procedural queries. The quality and frequency of information updates will be the key indicator of the service's long-term value.

## Official Gazette and Parliament

### Medium-Term Programme in Force: Single-Digit Inflation Targeted for 2029

The **Medium-Term Programme (2027-2029)** entered into force, published in the supplementary edition of the **Official Gazette dated 6 September 2026** (Decision No. **11752**). The programme sets inflation targets of **21% for 2027**, **13.5% for 2028** and **9% for 2029**, with an export target of **USD 316 billion** by the end of the period.

The OVP is the primary document steering statutory reforms across areas from taxation to the judiciary. It specifically provides for **AI-assisted digital audits** to combat the informal economy, a development that creates new compliance dimensions for businesses and taxpayers. The planned nationwide roll-out of subsidised rental housing — piloted in Istanbul — is a development worth monitoring for practitioners in landlord-tenant law.

### HSK Redraws Court Jurisdictions: Eceabat Transferred to Gelibolu Court

**The Supreme Council of Judges and Prosecutors (HSK)** reorganised the geographic jurisdictions of a number of courts on grounds of location and caseload. The most notable change: **Eceabat** district (Çanakkale) was removed from the Çanakkale Court circuit and reassigned to the **Gelibolu Court** circuit.

Jurisdiction changes require careful attention in ongoing proceedings. Under **Article 6 of the Code of Civil Procedure (HMK)**, the competent court for cases filed after the change takes effect must be determined by reference to the new circuit boundaries. Additionally, enforcement judges and labour and consumer courts in **Gaziosmanpaşa, Sandıklı, Serik, Suşehri and Ortaca** were aligned with the circuits of the nearest criminal or civil courts of first instance in those districts.

### 14 New Substances Added to Controlled Drugs List: Sale and Possession Now Criminal

By Presidential Decree, **14 new substances** were added to the controlled substances list under **Law No. 2313 on the Control of Narcotic Substances**. Every form of production, import, export, sale and possession of these substances is now entirely prohibited and subject to the same strict controls and criminal penalties as existing scheduled drugs.

The expansion of the controlled substances list under **Turkish Penal Code Article 188** directly affects both criminal defence lawyers and businesses handling chemical or pharmaceutical products. For criminal defence, the date from which each substance entered the schedule is a material fact in establishing **intent** (kasıt). Conduct predating the inclusion of a substance is governed by **TPC Article 7** on the retroactive application of more favourable law.

### VUK Communiqué No. 595: Digital Platforms Must Report to Tax Authority Monthly

**General Communiqué No. 595 of the Tax Procedure Law (VUK)** amended Communiqué No. 538 to separate the reporting obligations of **hosting providers** and **social network providers**. These providers must now notify the **Revenue Administration (GİB) monthly** with their domain addresses, information for taxpayer identification of their users, and listing details relating to sales and rental transactions on their platforms.

Turkey is continuing to close revenue gaps in the digital economy. The measure directly affects e-commerce marketplaces and social media providers that generate advertising or sales revenue through listings. For merchants operating through these platforms, the risk of tax authority identification has increased materially. The confidentiality exceptions under **VUK Article 148** will regain relevance. Tax advisers are encouraged to inform digital-commerce clients of this new compliance obligation.

### Withholding Rate Update: 10% Rate Set for Money Market Funds

Under **Provisional Article 67 of the Income Tax Law (GVK)**, the withholding rates applied to gains that certain corporate taxpayers earn from investment fund and partnership participation shares were updated. For **money market funds** — including free funds whose title includes the phrase "money market" — the withholding rate on participation share gains was set at **10%**.

Withholding rates on capital market instruments directly affect investment decisions. The change may require certain institutional investors to revisit portfolio structure and tax planning. Accounting and tax advisers should update their clients' portfolios to reflect the new rate from its effective date.

### CMB Allows Remote Identity Verification of Foreign Clients via NFC Passport

The **Capital Markets Board (CMB / SPK)** published a communiqué amendment permitting brokerage firms, portfolio management companies and crypto-asset service providers to onboard **non-Turkish clients** remotely using **ICAO 9303 standard NFC-enabled passports**. Clients onboarded through this method will be classified in the **high-risk category** and may only transfer funds to their accounts via **SWIFT** from a bank account opened abroad in their own name.

The measure must be read in the context of **Law No. 5549 on Prevention of Laundering Proceeds of Crime** and FATF compliance requirements. Client data and portfolio sizes will be reported to **MASAK** (Financial Crimes Investigation Board) every three months. Compliance officers and financial services lawyers must integrate this communiqué into their clients' know-your-customer (KYC) and anti-money-laundering frameworks.

### CBRT Regulation Codifies Biometric Remote Onboarding for Payment Institutions

The **Central Bank of the Republic of Turkey (CBRT)** published an amendment to the **Regulation on Payment Services and Electronic Money Issuance**, explicitly codifying the use of **biometric methods** or **electronically authenticable identity documents** in remote customer onboarding and identity verification processes.

The change requires payment institutions to review their biometric data processing procedures for compliance with both the **Personal Data Protection Law (KVKK)** and banking regulation. The wider adoption of remote biometric verification increases data diversity while also strengthening protection against fraud and identity theft. Practitioners in payment services law should brief clients on the new formal requirements.

### Tourism Facility Regulation Grants Conditional Flat-Ownership Exception

An amendment to the **Regulation on the Qualities of Tourism Facilities** introduced an exception to the flat-ownership (kat mülkiyeti) prohibition. Facilities located in areas designated solely for **commercial use under zoning plans dated before 1 June 2019**, where all accommodation units belong to a single owner, may now establish **flat ownership (kat mülkiyeti) or construction servitude (kat irtifakı)**.

The general prohibition on establishing flat ownership in tourism facilities arises from the intersection of the **Flat Ownership Law No. 634** and tourism sector regulation. The exception should facilitate property transfers and financing arrangements for qualifying older facilities. Identifying qualifying facilities requires a review of zoning plan dates and land registry records.

### Forest Fire Regulation Updated: Volunteers Now Entitled to Martyrdom Rights

A new **Regulation on Forest Fire Fighting Operations** was published (5 September 2026, Official Gazette No. 33361), replacing the previous regulation. Key provisions: district governors may now ban entry to forests during meteorologically high-risk periods; agricultural machinery operators must carry **fire-suppression equipment**; volunteers who die or are injured fighting fires may now benefit from **martyrdom and veteran status** under relevant legislation.

The extension of martyrdom rights to volunteers represents a concrete expansion of the scope of **Law No. 2330 on Lump-Sum Compensation and Pension Entitlements**. The provision that property damage to third-party equipment sustained during firefighting operations will be compensated by the forestry administration clarifies the government's liability exposure in compensation claims. Environmental and agricultural law practitioners should review the full updated text.

### Three Major Motorways Added to Privatisation Programme by Presidential Decree

A Presidential Decree added the **Niğde-Pozantı Motorway**, the **Gaziantep Ring Road** and the **Çağlayan-Yenişehir section of the Bursa Ring Road** to the privatisation programme. These may be privatised individually or together with the **15 July Martyrs' Bridge** and **Fatih Sultan Mehmet Bridge**, which were added to the programme earlier.

Motorway privatisations are governed by **Privatisation Law No. 4046**. The transfer of long-term operating rights, user-fee regulatory frameworks, and potential public-private partnership (PPP) structures all generate issues worth monitoring from an infrastructure law and competition law perspective.

## Court of Cassation Rulings

### Court of Cassation 5th Civil Division: Mediation Does Not Apply to Insurer's Subrogation Action Against Flat Owners

The **Court of Cassation 5th Civil Division** quashed, for the benefit of law, a lower court's procedural dismissal in a case where an insurer pursued flat owners through enforcement for a payment it had made to its insured. The lower court had dismissed the **action to set aside the objection to enforcement** (itirazın iptali) on the ground that **mandatory mediation** had not been attempted.

**Previous practice:** Some courts were applying the mandatory mediation requirement to insurance subrogation claims and dismissing files without examining the merits.

**What this means:** Insurers may bring an action to set aside an objection to enforcement against flat owners without first going to mediation. For insurance and flat-ownership law practitioners, this is a ruling that directly eliminates a potential procedural defence in similar cases.

### Istanbul Regional Court 12th Civil Division: Scraping Data with Fake API Keys Is Unfair Competition

The **Istanbul Regional Court of Appeal (BAM) 12th Civil Division** held that the bulk extraction of data from a mobile news application using **fake user keys (API keys)** constitutes **unfair competition** under the **Turkish Commercial Code (TTK)**.

The ruling clarifies a significant point in digital law: unauthorised API access does not only give rise to liability under **TPC Arts. 243-244** (computer offences) — it also triggers civil commercial consequences under **TTK Article 54** on unfair competition. For technology companies and their legal advisers, this decision underscores the need to review API terms of use through a commercial law lens, not only a criminal one.

### Court of Cassation 3rd Civil Division: Previous Owner Must Prove Deposit Was Transferred

**Court of Cassation, 3rd Civil Division** (File No. E. 2026/3664, K. 2026/2960, 12 May 2026):

> Where the previous owner cannot prove that the tenancy deposit (güvence bedeli) was transferred to the new owner, the obligation to return the deposit remains with the **previous owner** who originally received it.

**Previous position:** On the sale of rented property, questions about who bears the deposit repayment obligation when no transfer was documented had remained contentious.

**What this means:** The **burden of proof** rests on the previous owner who took receipt of the deposit. A written transfer document — preferably notarised — at the time of the property sale is therefore essential for both buyers and sellers. This ruling reinforces the need for preventive due diligence in conveyancing transactions involving tenanted property.

### Access to Consumer Court Without Arbitration Committee — Claim Value Is Decisive

**Court of Cassation, 3rd Civil Division** (File No. E. 2025/5731, K. 2026/2980, 12 May 2026):

> A claimant who bypasses the **consumer arbitration committee** and files a partial or unliquidated claim directly in the consumer court, asserting that the claim falls within the court's jurisdictional threshold, must state a claim value of **at least the court's minimum threshold**.

**Legal context:** Under **Consumer Protection Law No. 6502**, disputes below defined monetary thresholds must be resolved through **consumer arbitration committees** before a court action can be brought. The ruling closes the route of circumventing this obligation by pleading an unliquidated claim. Lawyers filing consumer cases must carefully establish claim value from the outset to pre-empt a jurisdictional challenge.

### Lawyers Under Physical Attack During Enforcement May Invoke Self-Defence

**Court of Cassation, 1st Criminal Division** (File No. E. 2024/7693, K. 2025/9713, 30 December 2025):

> A lawyer subjected to a physical attack at an enforcement (haciz) site who responds with force **proportionate to the attack** is acting within the scope of lawful self-defence.

**TPC Article 25** on self-defence requires the presence of a threat and proportionality in the response. This ruling explicitly confirms that enforcement lawyers facing physical interference at an enforcement site have a legal right of proportionate self-defence. Practitioners at risk of such incidents now have a direct authority to cite if criminal proceedings are brought against them.

### Court of Cassation 8th Civil Division: Rent Determination Judgment Cannot Ground Enforcement Proceedings

**Court of Cassation, 8th Civil Division** (File No. E. 2016/4721, K. 2016/6720, 13 April 2016):

> Because rent determination judgments **do not contain an order to perform** (eda hükmü) in respect of rent arrears, they cannot serve as the basis for enforcement proceedings as enforceable court orders (ilamlı icra) — except for ancillaries such as litigation costs and attorneys' fees.

A rent determination judgment fixes the rent level; it is not, in itself, an order directing payment. To access enforcement as an enforceable court order, a separate **judgment ordering payment of rent arrears** must be obtained. Failure to appreciate this distinction risks a procedural refusal of the enforcement application — a costly mistake in contested tenancy cases.

### Court of Cassation 12th Civil Division: Interest Runs from Judgment Date Even Where Not Awarded

**Court of Cassation, 12th Civil Division** (File No. E. 2000/14229, K. 2000/15555, 20 October 2000):

> Where a judgment provides that the debt becomes due on the date of the judgment, the creditor may claim interest from the **date of the judgment** in enforcement proceedings, even if the judgment contains no interest award — or even if interest was expressly rejected.

The starting date for default interest in judgment enforcement is an area where Turkish law and case law admit more than one reading. This ruling makes clear that the absence of an interest award in the judgment does not extinguish the creditor's right to claim interest from judgment date in enforcement. Practitioners bringing enforcement applications on the basis of court orders should note this as a direct authority for the interest claim.
