The defining development in **Turkey's legal landscape on 17 August 2026** is the parliamentary adoption of the **National Solidarity and Social Cohesion Law** — widely known as the "Framework Law." In the same week, the Turkish Bar Association announced the end of SGK set-off practices against attorney fees, the Constitutional Court found a union rights violation for private security personnel, and the Official Gazette published significant amendments on vehicle rentals, fuel excise, and building demolition rules.

## National Solidarity Law Adopted by Parliament

### Framework Law Passes 468-88 — Legal Foundation for Peace Process

On **10 August 2026**, Turkey's Grand National Assembly (TBMM) adopted the **12-article Law on Strengthening National Solidarity and Social Cohesion** with 468 votes in favor, 88 against, and 6 abstentions. The ruling AKP, coalition partner MHP, the pro-Kurdish DEM Party, New Party, and New Road voted in favor; the İYİ Party largely opposed; the New Welfare Party abstained.

The law — designed as the legal framework for a renewed peace process — provides for conditional suspension of criminal investigations and prosecutions in specified offense categories, with suspension periods of 5-10 years depending on sentence length. **Premeditated murder** convictions and offenses committed **before 1 June 2005** are excluded. A supervisory council chaired by the Vice President will oversee implementation; the prerequisite is that the PKK/KCK cease armed activities.

**Practical significance:** Courts may suspend qualifying proceedings upon fulfillment of conditions; if no new offense occurs during the suspension period, the case will be automatically dismissed. At the application stage, numerous procedural questions are expected to arise before Turkish courts.

[Source →](https://www.tbmm.gov.tr/Haber/Detay?Id=123d7eab-530b-4e93-bcb6-019fed833511)

### Parliament Recesses Until 1 October 2026

TBMM Plenary Session resolved at its 125th sitting on **10 August 2026** to suspend legislative work. Parliament will reconvene on **Thursday, 1 October 2026** at 14:00. The National Solidarity Law was the last major legislation of the spring-summer legislative period.

## Developments in Legal Practice and Bar Associations

### Bar Association Ends SGK Set-Off Against Attorney Fees

**Turkey's Union of Bar Associations (TBB)** announced that the practice of offsetting court-ordered opposing party attorney fees and fees paid to attorneys assigned under the Criminal Procedure Code (**CMK**) against Social Security Institution (SGK) premium debts has been terminated.

Under the former practice, SGK invoked its collection authority under Law No. 6183 to intercept attorney fee payments — effectively blocking lawyers from receiving the fees they had legally earned. With the practice ended, **CMK legal aid fees** and court-awarded opposing party fees will be paid to attorneys in full without deduction.

### TBB Updates Attorney Secretary Cards

TBB published an announcement on the validity periods and renewal timeline for Attorney Secretary Cards, which are issued to secretarial staff employed in law offices. Attorneys should consult their local bar for current renewal procedures.

### Istanbul Bar: Separate Fee Sought for Detention Review Hearings

The Istanbul Bar Association announced the outcome of its petition to the Ministry of Justice's Criminal Affairs Department, requesting that attorneys attending **detention review hearings** receive a **full mandatory defense fee** separate from interrogation and interview fees. Because a detention review constitutes an independent judicial proceeding, the bar argues a distinct fee entitlement arises — a position that directly affects attorneys handling legal aid assignments.

## Official Gazette: Legislation This Week

### Vehicle Rental: Damage Liability Now Tied to Fault Percentage

The **Regulation on Motor Vehicle Rental** entered into force via the Official Gazette on **15 August 2026**, fundamentally reshaping Turkey's car rental industry:

- **Previous practice:** Rental companies passed uninsured damage costs directly to renters, often invoking "coverage exclusion" clauses to charge fault-free drivers the full repair bill.
- **New rule:** A renter who bears **zero fault** in an accident faces **zero damage liability**. In shared-fault accidents, recovery is limited to the damage portion proven by an official accident report. Normal wear and tear is fully exempt throughout the rental period.
- **Who is affected:** Every consumer who rents a vehicle in Turkey now has legal protection against arbitrary damage billing; the burden of proving fault shifts decisively to rental companies.

[Source →](https://www.resmigazete.gov.tr/eskiler/2026/08/20260815-1.htm)

### Motor Diesel Excise Tax Temporarily Zeroed

A Presidential Decree (CBK) zeroed the per-liter **Special Consumption Tax (ÖTV)** on motor diesel from **13 to 31 August 2026**, ending the sliding-scale mechanism in place for diesel since March 2026. The sliding-scale mechanism continues for petrol and LPG until 30 September 2026.

**Phased reinstatement:**
- September 2026: TRY 3 / liter
- October 2026: TRY 6 / liter
- November 2026: TRY 9 / liter
- December 2026: TRY 12 / liter
- From 1 January 2027: TRY 13.9006 / liter

[Source →](https://www.resmigazete.gov.tr/eskiler/2026/08/20260813-0.pdf)

### High-Rise Buildings Receive a Legal Definition for the First Time

Amendments to the **Regulation on Building Demolition** introduced dedicated demolition rules for high-rise structures — for the first time in Turkish law. Buildings exceeding **21.50 meters in building height** or **30.50 meters in structural height** are now legally classified as "high-rise" and subject to additional technical and safety requirements for demolition. The definition has direct implications for urban renewal projects, building valuations, and construction permits.

[Source →](https://www.resmigazete.gov.tr/eskiler/2026/08/20260812-2.htm)

### End-of-Life Tyre Collection Rights Expanded

Amendments to the **Regulation on Control of End-of-Life Tyres** extended collection rights beyond manufacturers and authorized organizations to include licensed waste tyre **recovery facilities** and **pyrolysis plants**, provided they obtain ministry authorization. The change is expected to increase competition in waste management and expand recycling capacity.

[Source →](https://www.resmigazete.gov.tr/eskiler/2026/08/20260813-1.htm)

### Customs: Overseas Retirement Certificate Must Now Be Notarized

Amendments to the **Customs General Communiqué on Temporarily Imported Road Vehicles** redefined the "retirement certificate" requirement. Certificates proving overseas retirement must now be issued under the relevant country's **social security legislation** and accompanied by a **Turkish translation certified by a consulate, embassy, or notary**. Turkish nationals residing abroad who drive foreign-registered vehicles into Turkey should obtain a properly certified document before their next border crossing.

[Source →](https://www.resmigazete.gov.tr/eskiler/2026/08/20260814-5.htm)

### Grain Licensed Warehouses: Minimum Capacity and Capital Requirements Raised

The **Communiqué on Licensed Warehouses for Grains, Pulses, and Oilseeds** was amended to require a minimum storage capacity of **35,000 tonnes** for licensed depots. Capital requirements are now tiered:

| Capacity | Minimum Paid-in Capital |
|---|---|
| Up to 35,000 tonnes | TRY 20 million |
| 35,001–70,000 tonnes | TRY 30 million |
| 70,001–110,000 tonnes | TRY 40 million |
| Above 110,000 tonnes | +TRY 5 million per 30,000 tonnes |

[Source →](https://www.resmigazete.gov.tr/eskiler/2026/08/20260814-6.htm)

### Higher Education Fees Increased by 40 Percent

Presidential decrees set student contribution fees and tuition for distance learning and second-shift programs **40 percent higher** than the previous year for the **2026-2027 academic year**. Students and families should factor the increase into their annual budget planning.

## Constitutional Court Rulings

### Blanket Union Ban on Private Security Personnel Is Unconstitutional

The **Constitutional Court (AYM)** ruled that a former legal provision categorically **prohibiting private security personnel employed at public institutions from forming or joining trade unions** violated the constitutional right to organize under **Article 51 of the Turkish Constitution**.

- **Previous state:** The prohibition was absolute — no private security officer could exercise union rights regardless of the nature of their duties or employer.
- **Ruling:** The court found the blanket ban failed the proportionality test; it ordered **TRY 50,000 in non-pecuniary damages** to the applicant union and retrial.
- **Significance:** The decision marks a turning point for collective labor rights in the private security sector. Individuals whose union rights were similarly obstructed may now pursue individual applications before the Constitutional Court.

[Source →](https://www.resmigazete.gov.tr/eskiler/2026/08/20260810-15.pdf)

### Women's Family Registry After Marriage: Court Upholds Existing Rule

The AYM reviewed Article 23(2), first sentence, of **Population Services Law No. 5490** — which requires a woman's family registry to be transferred to her husband's household upon marriage — and **rejected the constitutional challenge by majority vote**, finding no violation. Dissenting opinions invoked equality and non-discrimination principles; the constitutional debate is expected to continue in legal scholarship.

[Source →](https://www.resmigazete.gov.tr/eskiler/2026/08/20260810-13.pdf)

## Court of Cassation Rulings

### Erroneous Overpayments to Employees Are Recoverable Under Unjust Enrichment

**Turkey's Court of Cassation, 9th Civil Chamber**, held that erroneous overpayments made by an employer or administration to an employee can be reclaimed under the **unjust enrichment provisions of the Turkish Code of Obligations** (Arts. 77-82).

- **Issue contested:** Whether employers could recover inadvertent salary overpayments from good-faith employees had been unsettled, particularly in public employment.
- **Ruling:** A payment made without legal obligation and without donative intent qualifies for restitution once the payer proves error — regardless of the employee's good faith.
- **Who is affected:** Public and private sector employers that have made payroll, bonus, or incentive payment errors now have clear legal authority to seek recovery proceedings.

[Source →](https://www.alomaliye.com/2026/08/13/yargitaydan-emsal-karar-isciye-yapilan-hatali-odeme-geri-alinabilecek/)

### Renewed Lease: Fair Rent Review Possible Before Five-Year Threshold

**Court of Cassation, 3rd Civil Chamber** (E. 2025/3450, K. 2026/1141, 03.03.2026): where the rent in a renewed lease is found to diverge materially from market comparables, the court **may determine a fair rent by equity** even when five years have not elapsed — calculating the baseline from the original lease's start date. The ruling clarifies that the five-year period in Turkish Code of Obligations Art. 344 is not an absolute bar when the agreed rent is demonstrably off-market.

### Inheritance Rejected by All Heirs Triggers Bankruptcy Liquidation

**Court of Cassation, 12th Civil Chamber** (E. 2026/2623, K. 2026/2921, 29.04.2026): when **all nearest statutory heirs** reject an inheritance, the estate must be liquidated by the civil court (sulh mahkemesi) under **bankruptcy law rules**. The ruling clarifies creditor priority and procedural steps following a collective inheritance renunciation, providing certainty for third-party creditors.

### Dual-Maturity Promissory Note Lacks Negotiable Instrument Status

**Court of Cassation, 12th Civil Chamber** (E. 2010/8588, K. 2010/21582): a promissory note bearing **two different maturity dates** is not a valid negotiable instrument under the Turkish Commercial Code, barring the expedited **kambiyo takibi** (negotiable instrument enforcement) procedure. Holders of such notes must resort to ordinary enforcement proceedings.

### Second Appellate Petition May Raise New Factual Grounds Within Deadline

**Court of Cassation, 8th Civil Chamber** (E. 2018/15518, K. 2019/9846, 23.02.2023): within the appellate (istinaf) filing period, a party may submit a **second petition raising factual grounds not included in the first petition**. The ruling confirms that litigants can supplement — not merely clarify — their appellate arguments through a follow-up filing as long as the statutory period has not expired.
