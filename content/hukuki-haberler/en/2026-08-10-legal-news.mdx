## Judiciary and Bar News

### HSK Dismisses 84 Judges and Prosecutors for Professional Misconduct in 2026

The Second Chamber of Turkey's **Supreme Board of Judges and Prosecutors (HSK)** announced that **84 judges and prosecutors** were dismissed from their positions in 2026 under the charge of "violating the honor of law and the profession" — the most severe disciplinary sanction under **Law No. 6087**.

Dismissal proceedings at HSK follow a structured process: the relevant chamber recommends, and the full Board confirms. These decisions are subject to review in administrative courts. For legal practitioners, understanding this mechanism is essential when advising on judicial conduct complaints or challenging the legality of disciplinary outcomes. The scale of this year's dismissals signals active enforcement of professional standards.

### Istanbul Bar Association Issues Guidance Note on 12th Judicial Reform Package

The **Istanbul Bar Association's Legislation Research and Development Commission** published a comprehensive guidance note on the amendments introduced under Turkey's **12th Judicial Reform Package**. The package targets shorter trial durations, reduced litigation costs, and broader access to justice.

The **Ankara Bar Association No. 2** issued a concurrent notice to its members on the Constitutional Court's recent ruling in expropriation cases — covered in detail in the Judicial Decisions section below. Bar guidance notes remain the fastest practical reference for lawyers navigating reform-driven procedural changes.

### 52 Forest Fires Under Criminal Investigation: 9 Arrested, 34 Detained

Justice Minister Akın Gürlek disclosed that, since **June 1, 2026**, criminal proceedings have been initiated in connection with **52 forest fires**. Of the **34 suspects** detained, **9 were formally arrested** and 14 placed under judicial supervision measures.

Criminal liability for forest fires under Articles 170 and 174 of the **Turkish Penal Code** turns on proof of intent. Where deliberate arson is established, penalties increase substantially. The evidentiary burden — forensic analysis, on-site inspection, expert testimony — makes these cases procedurally demanding. Environmental lawyers, criminal defense counsel, and insurers managing fire-related property damage claims should monitor the investigation's progress.

### BDDK Restricts Investment Instruments for Savings Finance Companies

Turkey's **Banking Regulation and Supervision Agency (BDDK)** issued new rules on the investment instruments available to **savings finance companies** — the participation-based housing finance sector. The new framework steers these companies toward **liquid, low-risk assets** while capping exposure to higher-risk instruments.

These companies collect consumer savings through housing accumulation contracts and lease certificates. Any shift in their investment profile affects the financial security underlying consumer commitments. Consumer protection attorneys and financial regulators should track how this interacts with deposit protection rules and any pending disputes over contract performance or fund adequacy.

### Competition Authority Grants Conditional Clearance to A101-CARREFOURSA Deal

Turkey's **Competition Authority (Rekabet Kurumu)** gave conditional approval to **A101 Yeni Mağazacılık A.Ş.**'s acquisition of **CARREFOURSA Turkey** under **Law No. 4054** on the Protection of Competition. Conditional clearance binds the buyer to structural or behavioral remedies designed to prevent market dominance.

This merger reshapes Turkey's grocery retail sector. The specific conditions imposed — typically store divestitures or supply commitment obligations — are binding and enforceable; non-compliance triggers investigation and potential fines. Suppliers, rival chains, and consumer advocates should review the conditions as they become public.

### SEDDK Issues Circular on Motor Vehicle Insurance Arbitration Commission

The **Insurance and Private Pension Regulation and Supervision Authority (SEDDK)** published **Circular 2026/02** on applications to the **Insurance Arbitration Commission** for motor vehicle insurance disputes.

The Commission provides a mandatory alternative dispute resolution channel for traffic accident compensation claims against insurers. This circular addresses procedural requirements, fee schedules, or jurisdictional scope. Attorneys handling motor liability cases should review the updated requirements before filing new arbitration claims, as procedural non-compliance can delay or block admissibility.

## Legislation and Official Gazette

### Higher Education Law Amendment: Students Who Lost Enrollment Status May Return

An amendment to Turkey's **Higher Education Law (Law No. 2547)** was published in the Official Gazette, granting students who lost their **enrollment status** — across preparatory, associate, bachelor's completion, bachelor's, and graduate levels — the right to resume their studies.

Students who voluntarily withdrew are also covered, subject to statutory exceptions. This change creates a new actionable right for former students whose reinstatement requests have been refused. Administrative law attorneys should monitor whether universities comply or attempt procedural workarounds, and be ready to pursue administrative or judicial remedies on behalf of clients whose applications are denied.

### Presidential Decree No. 200: Power to Freeze Public Positions Centralized

**Presidential Decree No. 200** transferred the authority to place vacant positions from the **(I) List** of public institutions into "reserved" status — effectively freezing them — directly to the **Presidency**.

This centralization removes discretion from individual ministries in personnel administration. Under Turkish administrative law, position freezes affect recruitment pipelines and can be challenged in administrative courts by civil servants with legitimate expectations of appointment. Public sector employment lawyers and trade unions should assess the decree's scope and monitor its implementation.

### Regulation on Local Climate Change Action Plans Published

A new regulation on **Local Climate Change Action Plans and Provincial Climate Change Coordination Councils** entered into force via the Official Gazette, establishing the procedural and technical framework for provincial-level climate planning.

The regulation aligns with Turkey's **net-zero emissions commitment** and its **Nationally Determined Contribution (NDC)** under the Paris Agreement. For environmental and administrative lawyers, this regulation will increasingly feature in land-use permit disputes, urban redevelopment challenges, and reviews of municipal decisions that conflict with provincial climate action plans.

### 12-Article "Terror-Free Turkey" Framework Law Clears Justice Committee

A **12-article framework law** tied to the "Terror-Free Turkey" peace process — formally the **Law on Strengthening National Solidarity and Social Cohesion** — was approved by the **Grand National Assembly's Justice Committee**.

The law is conditional: it takes effect only after security agencies formally certify that **PKK-KCK** and affiliated organizations have ceased active operations and surrendered arms and munitions. Given its potential implications for counterterrorism law, criminal enforcement, and transitional justice, criminal defense lawyers and human rights practitioners should track the law's passage through the full Assembly and any subsequent implementing regulations.

## Constitutional Court and Judicial Rulings

### Constitutional Court Strikes Down Expropriation Law: State Cannot Block Enforcement Without Security

Turkey's **Constitutional Court (AYM)** struck down **paragraph 2 of supplementary Article 4 of Law No. 2942** (the Expropriation Law) by a majority vote.

**The old rule:** In unlawful expropriation (el atma) damage suits, the state could apply for a stay of enforcement without posting any security deposit. This allowed the government to delay payments to citizens whose land it had already occupied.

**The ruling:** The Court held that this provision imposed a disproportionate restriction on the right to property under Articles **13, 35, and 46** of the Constitution. Requiring citizens to endure further delays — after already losing use of their property — without any financial guarantee from the state was found unconstitutional.

**Practical impact:** Courts must now reject unsecured stay-of-enforcement applications in expropriation cases. Attorneys with pending cases should raise this ruling in ongoing enforcement proceedings, and examine whether it applies retroactively to cases where enforcement is already stayed.

### AYM: Refusing to Reissue Diploma After Legal Gender Change Violates Privacy

The **Constitutional Court** ruled that denying a person's request to have their university diploma **reissued to reflect their updated legal name and gender** — following a court-approved identity change — violated the right to **respect for private life** under Article 20 of the Constitution.

Universities had refused to update diploma records despite valid court orders changing the applicant's registered identity. The Court found that this perpetuated a systematic institutional interference with the person's legally recognized private identity.

Attorneys representing clients with gender registry changes who encounter institutional resistance should cite this ruling as direct precedent in administrative proceedings and as the basis for individual applications to the Constitutional Court.

### AYM: Courts Must Give Reasons When Modifying Enforcement Penalty Rates

The **Constitutional Court** ruled unanimously that a court's use of the **correction (tashih) mechanism** to raise the enforcement non-compliance penalty rate from **20% to 40%** without adequate reasoning violated the applicant's right to a reasoned judgment under Article 36 of the Constitution.

Courts are constitutionally required to explain the factual and legal basis for decisions that modify parties' rights. A correction order that silently alters a material figure — without explanation — cannot satisfy this standard. Litigators should challenge enforcement correction orders lacking explicit reasoning, both at the trial level and through constitutional complaint.

### Council of State: Criminal Conviction Must Be Final Before Civil Servant Can Be Dismissed

**Council of State (Danıştay), Second Chamber** — E. 2025/739, K. 2025/5453, 25.11.2025: a civil servant facing active criminal proceedings cannot be **dismissed** from public service on disciplinary grounds until the criminal court's judgment becomes **final**.

Under **Law No. 657** (Civil Servants Law), dismissal linked to criminal conduct requires finality of the criminal verdict. Administrative dismissals issued prematurely — before finality — are subject to annulment. Affected public employees should seek immediate annulment of the administrative act in administrative courts, presenting this decision as controlling precedent.

### Court of Cassation: Rent Determination Cannot Rest Solely on Online Listings

**Court of Cassation, Third Civil Chamber** — E. 2026/1265, K. 2026/3231, 20.05.2026: setting a rent value in **rent determination proceedings** using only the average of **online listings**, without examining actual comparable rents and without physically inspecting the property, constitutes reversible error.

Under Articles 344–346 of the **Turkish Code of Obligations**, proper rent determination requires comparison of real market rents, on-site inspection of the property, and expert assessment. Online listing prices systematically overstate market values and lack the contextual detail required for valid comparison. Expert reports relying solely on such sources should be challenged at trial and on appeal.

### Reading the Payment Order via UYAP Citizen Portal Is Not Legal Service

**Sakarya Regional Court of Justice, Eighth Civil Chamber** — E. 2026/558, K. 2026/1024, 12.06.2026: a debtor's act of reading a **payment order (ödeme emri)** through the **UYAP Citizen Portal** does not constitute legal notification of that order.

Statutory notification under **Law No. 7201** (Notification Law) requires specific delivery mechanisms — postal service, the official e-tebligat system, or other prescribed methods. UYAP portal access is an informational service, not a substitute for statutory service. Enforcement attorneys must not calculate objection deadlines from the date of UYAP viewing; defense counsel can challenge enforcement timelines calculated on that basis.

### Court of Cassation: Mandatory Defense Counsel Persists After Juvenile Defendant Turns 18

**Court of Cassation General Assembly on Criminal Law** — E. 2024/7-19, K. 2025/114, 05.03.2025: the mandatory defense counsel guarantee for **juvenile defendants** (suça sürüklenen çocuk) does not terminate when the defendant turns 18 during proceedings — neither automatically nor at the defendant's election.

Under Article 150 of the **Code of Criminal Procedure (CMK)**, the mandatory representation right attaches to the defendant's status as a juvenile and must continue throughout the trial. Removal of appointed counsel upon reaching majority constitutes procedural error and grounds for reversal. Criminal defense attorneys should audit ongoing cases involving the transition from juvenile to adult status and raise this ruling where counsel was withdrawn.
