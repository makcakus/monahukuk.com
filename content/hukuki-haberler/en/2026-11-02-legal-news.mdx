This week Turkish law was shaped by the AI-powered DOLUNAY anti-fraud project, **Law No. 7596** granting compensation to defrauded investment fund holders, and a new regulation on electronic informed consent in healthcare. The Capital Markets Board (SPK) raised the qualified investor threshold fivefold while revoking Tera Yatırım's entire license. Five Court of Cassation rulings addressed topics ranging from administrative liability to inheritance law, with direct implications for legal practice.

## Legal Agenda

### DOLUNAY Project: AI-Powered Real-Time Detection of Cyber Fraud

Justice Minister **Akın Gürlek** announced the **DOLUNAY Project** following talks with Interior Minister Mustafa Çiftçi, marking a new era in Turkey's fight against cyber fraud.

The project rests on three pillars: **real-time AI-driven monitoring** of suspicious financial flows, accelerated legal intervention before criminal proceeds reach digital wallets or crypto assets, and the establishment of a **Fraud Prevention Centre** within the Ministry of Interior. This substantially expands Turkey's monitoring capacity under **Law No. 5549 on Prevention of Laundering Proceeds of Crime**. For practitioners, AI-driven account freezes will likely generate new challenge procedures; proactive client defence planning is now advisable for any matter touching large or digital asset transfers.

### Illegal Betting Crackdown: 150 Suspects Detained in Three Separate Investigations

Justice Minister Akın Gürlek announced that **150 suspects** were detained across three simultaneous operations targeting illegal betting networks and new-generation criminal organisations.

Illegal betting in Turkey is regulated under **Law No. 7258 on Sports Betting** and **Law No. 5651 on the Regulation of Internet Broadcasts**. The scale of these operations demonstrates that prosecutors can now coordinate large multi-defendant investigations. Defence lawyers in multi-accused criminal proceedings should monitor developments closely, particularly regarding joinder of charges and evidence-sharing between investigations.

### Insurance Arbitration Appeals Now Filed Through UYAP Digital System

The Insurance Arbitration Commission announced the completion of integration work allowing appeals against arbitral awards to be submitted through **UYAP** (National Judiciary Network).

Insurance arbitration is a specialised dispute resolution mechanism under Article 30 of **Law No. 5684 on Insurance**. The UYAP integration removes the previous paper-based submission requirement and digitises deadline tracking and evidence submission. Insurance lawyers must now route all temyiz applications through UYAP rather than physical filing.

### SPK Revokes All Tera Yatırım Licences: Winding Up and Market Manipulation Charges

The **Capital Markets Board (SPK)** revoked all operating licences and authorisation certificates of Tera Yatırım Menkul Değerler AŞ, ordering investor compensation and the **gradual winding up** of the broker.

In the same decision, SPK filed criminal complaints against 50 individuals for market manipulation involving shares of **PASEU, SELEC and MANAS**, and imposed **two-year trading bans** on 51 individuals and four companies, including Aura Portföy. This action is a significant enforcement precedent under Article 101 of **Law No. 6362 on Capital Markets** concerning market manipulation sanctions.

### SPK Raises Qualified Investor Threshold Fivefold to TRY 50 Million

By SPK decision, the total financial asset threshold for **qualified investor status** has risen from **TRY 10 million to TRY 50 million**, while the transaction volume threshold for professional client classification has increased from **TRY 5 million to TRY 25 million**.

**Previous rule:** Investors holding TRY 10 million in financial assets qualified for access to higher-risk capital market instruments. **New rule:** The fivefold increase will significantly reduce the number of qualified investors. **Who is affected:** Those previously classified as qualified investors who have been investing in non-public instruments now need to reassess their status and existing holdings. Capital markets lawyers should audit client portfolios against the new thresholds.

[Source →](https://spk.gov.tr/duyurular/basin-duyurulari/2026/basin-duyurusu_2)

## Official Gazette

### Law No. 7596: Investment Fund Victims to Receive Compensation and Up to TRY 1 Million Advance Payment

**Law No. 7596 on Certain Investment Funds Subject to Liquidation Orders** was published in the **Official Gazette on 27 October 2026** and entered into force immediately.

**Previous position:** Following SPK's liquidation decisions of 17 September 2026, there was no concrete compensation mechanism for defrauded fund unit holders. **New framework:** Net investment amounts will be updated using the **arithmetic average of CPI and PPI** changes, protecting against inflation erosion. Unit holders may receive advance payments of up to **TRY 1 million**. Recovered funds will be held in separate accounts per fund and **cannot be attached**. **Who is affected:** Unit holders of funds subject to the September 2026 SPK liquidation orders. Secondary regulations from SPK will clarify the scope and timing of payments.

### Electronic Informed Consent Regulation: e-Nabız and SMS OTP Now Legally Valid

The **Regulation on Obtaining Informed Consent in Electronic Form** entered into force upon publication in the **Official Gazette on 27 October 2026**.

**Previous rule:** Patient informed consent was obtained through handwritten signatures, a time-consuming paper-heavy process. **New rule:** Healthcare facilities may obtain patient consent via **e-Nabız** (Turkey's national health portal) or the facility's own electronic system using a **one-time SMS password**. **Exception:** Where the legal representative of a minor or incapacitated patient cannot be digitally verified, electronic consent remains unavailable and physical signature is still required. **Who is affected:** Hospitals and clinics must adapt their consent workflows to a digital infrastructure. Health law practitioners will encounter a new layer of analysis in disputes over consent validity.

[Official Gazette →](https://www.resmigazete.gov.tr/eskiler/2026/10/20261027-4.htm)

### Short-Term Rental Permit Cancellation Now Requires Prior 30-Day Cure Notice

The Ministry of Culture and Tourism amended the **Regulation on Short-Term Residential Rentals for Tourism Purposes**.

**The change:** Where documentation submitted for licensing is found to be deficient or non-compliant, the permit holder must now be given **30 days** to remedy the deficiency before the permit is cancelled. If the deficiency is not cured within this period, the **permit will be revoked**. Permit revocation triggers a halt to operations and administrative fines. Property owners in tourism-heavy regions such as Antalya should maintain ongoing document compliance to avoid cancellation exposure.

[Official Gazette →](https://www.resmigazete.gov.tr/eskiler/2026/10/20261027-2.htm)

### Presidential Decrees 201–203: New IT Directorates and Cyber Security Company Powers

Three Presidential Decrees published in the **Official Gazette on 31 October 2026** restructured several public bodies.

**Decree 201:** A **Directorate General for Information Technology** was established within the Ministry of Energy and the Ministry of Industry and Technology. **Decree 202:** A **Directorate General for 112 Emergency Call and Coordination** was created within the Ministry of Interior. **Decree 203:** The **Cyber Security Presidency** was granted authority to incorporate companies, take equity stakes and acquire companies, enabling public-private partnership models in the cyber security sector. IT companies and lawyers advising on public procurement must identify their new counterpart administrative units under these structures.

[Official Gazette →](https://www.resmigazete.gov.tr/31.10.2026)

### Parliamentary Committee Approves Mecca Defence Pact with Pakistan and Saudi Arabia

The **Grand National Assembly Foreign Affairs Committee** approved the bill authorising ratification of the **Mecca Agreement for Joint Defence**, signed between Turkey, Pakistan and Saudi Arabia.

Defence treaties are submitted to the Grand National Assembly for ratification under **Presidential Decree No. 9**. Following committee approval the bill will proceed to the plenary for final adoption. International public law and defence procurement lawyers should monitor the treaty's entry into force and scope once published in the Official Gazette.

## Court Decisions

### Failing to Answer Official Correspondence Constitutes Abuse of Office — Court of Cassation 5th CC

**Court of Cassation 5th Criminal Chamber** (E. 2021/7403, K. 2024/2686, **11 March 2024**):

> A public official's failure to respond to official correspondence (*müzekkere*) in a timely and proper manner constitutes **abuse of office by omission** under Turkish Criminal Code Article 257.

**Legal context:** Article 257 of the **Turkish Criminal Code** criminalises both intentional acts and negligent omissions amounting to abuse of office. **Practical significance:** Lawyers seeking information from public bodies now have a stronger legal basis: the threat of criminal liability under this ruling may accelerate official responses to formal correspondence. In administrative litigation, this precedent is a useful tool for accelerating document discovery against public authorities.

### Withdrawal from Case Possible While Resistance Ruling Awaits HGK Review — Court of Cassation HGK

**Court of Cassation General Assembly of Civil Chambers** (E. 2026/62, K. 2026/478, **9 September 2026**):

> Withdrawal from a case is permissible against a **resistance ruling** (direnme kararı) that has been appealed but has not yet been deliberated and decided by the General Assembly of Civil Chambers.

**Legal context:** A resistance ruling occurs when a first-instance court maintains its original judgment after a reversal on appeal. This clarification resolves uncertainty about case management at the HGK stage. **Practical significance:** Parties may now settle, conciliate or file a withdrawal at the HGK stage without waiting for the final ruling, making it a realistic cost-reduction strategy in protracted appeal proceedings.

### BAM's HAGB Orders in Serious Crimes Are Open to Cassation — Court of Cassation CGK

**Court of Cassation Criminal General Assembly** (E. 2026/81, K. 2026/478, **16 September 2026**):

> In the exceptional offences exhaustively listed in Article 286/3 of the **Code of Criminal Procedure (CMK)**, orders of **deferral of pronouncement of judgment (HAGB)** issued by Regional Courts of Appeal (BAM) are also subject to cassation review.

**Legal context:** CMK Article 286/3 preserves cassation review for specific serious offences such as intentional homicide, intentional injury, torture and sexual assault. **New rule:** BAM-level HAGB decisions in these offences are no longer final — they are open to Yargıtay scrutiny. Criminal lawyers defending or prosecuting such cases should factor this route into appellate strategy.

### Where BAM Re-Decides the Merits, Party That Did Not Appeal Still Benefits from Current AAÜT — Court of Cassation 9th HD

**Court of Cassation 9th Labour Chamber** (E. 2026/3494, K. 2026/6507, **2 July 2026**):

> When a Regional Court of Appeal (BAM) quashes the first-instance judgment and issues a fresh decision on the merits, the **Minimum Attorney Fee Schedule (AAÜT)** in force at the **date of the BAM decision** applies to the attorney fee awarded in favour of the party that did not appeal.

**Legal context:** The applicable tariff date for attorney fee orders has significant financial consequences given annual schedule revisions. **Practical significance:** A party who did not appeal but benefits from a BAM merits ruling will have attorney fees calculated under the current AAÜT, not the one in force at the original hearing. Lawyers should account for this when estimating adverse cost exposure and when claiming attorney fees in appellate proceedings.

### Statute of Limitations for Enforcement of Will Suspended During Will-Contestation Proceedings — Court of Cassation 7th HD

**Court of Cassation 7th Civil Chamber** (E. 2023/5462, K. 2024/2289, **30 April 2024**):

> Where an action to contest the validity of a will has been filed, the **limitation period** for enforcement of the will begins to run only from the **date on which the rejection judgment in the contestation proceedings becomes final**.

**Legal context:** Under the Turkish Civil Code, the limitation period for enforcing a will would previously continue to run during concurrent contestation proceedings, creating a risk that the enforcement right could become time-barred before the contestation was resolved. **New rule:** The limitation period is suspended for the duration of the contestation action and restarts once a final rejection judgment is issued. Inheritance lawyers can now advise clients that electing the contestation route does not jeopardise the concurrent enforcement right.
