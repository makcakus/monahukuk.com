## Employment Law

### Turkey's Remote Work Regulation Gains a Hybrid-Work Clause

An amendment to the **Remote Work Regulation** (originally published 10 March 2021) added a new paragraph to **Article 9**, providing an explicit legal framework for hybrid working arrangements. The change was published in the Official Gazette on **25 September 2026** (Issue No. **33381**).

The new provision reads: *"(2) Work duties may be performed partly at the workplace and partly through remote work. In such cases, the days and working hours allocated to each mode shall be determined in the employment contract."*

**Background:** The previous regulation assumed an employee would work either entirely remotely or entirely on-site. There was no express provision governing how a mixed arrangement should be structured contractually, creating ambiguity in flexible-working arrangements where employees came into the office on specific days.

**New rule:** Hybrid working must now be grounded in a written employment contract that specifies which days are office-based and which are remote.

**Effective:** 25 September 2026 (date of publication).

**Who is affected:** Employers operating or planning hybrid-work policies should review standard employment contract templates to reflect this change. Working-hours calculation, overtime thresholds, and workplace accident insurance coverage are particularly contested areas under hybrid models. Labour counsel should assist clients in updating their HR documentation accordingly.

## Financial Regulation and Payment Services

### MASAK Circular 34: Transactions with UN-Listed Entities Must Be Frozen Immediately

**MASAK** (Financial Crimes Investigation Board) published Circular No. 34 in the Official Gazette on **23 September 2026**. The circular establishes urgent procedures for transactions involving persons, entities, or organisations that are subject to **UN Security Council listing decisions** related to terrorism financing or the financing of the proliferation of weapons of mass destruction.

**Mandatory obligations:**
- Attempted transactions involving listed persons or entities must be **immediately reported to MASAK headquarters**
- Such transactions must be **suspended** (transaction-freeze obligation)
- These obligations apply to all obligated entities under **Law No. 5549 on the Prevention of Laundering of Crime Proceeds** and related legislation

**Legal context:** Turkey's FATF membership and participation in the UN sanctions regime make compliance with listing decisions a statutory requirement under domestic law. Circular 34, read alongside the earlier Amendment to Circular No. 5, reinforces existing reporting mechanisms.

**Who is affected:** Banks, payment institutions, electronic money institutions, foreign exchange bureaux, portfolio management companies, and brokerage firms — all obligated entities under the MASAK framework — are directly subject to this circular. Non-compliance risks severe administrative fines and potential suspension of operations under Law No. 5549. Compliance teams should integrate this circular into internal control procedures without delay.

### Papel E-Money Operating Licence Revoked

A decision published in the Official Gazette on **23 September 2026** revoked the operating licence of **Papel Elektronik Para ve Ödeme Hizmetleri A.Ş.** (Papel Electronic Money and Payment Services Inc.).

**Legal context:** Operating licences for electronic money and payment service providers are granted and supervised by the Central Bank of the Republic of Turkey (CBRT) under **Law No. 6493 on Payment and Securities Settlement Systems, Payment Services and Electronic Money Institutions**. Article 14 of that law sets out the conditions under which a licence may be revoked following supervisory findings.

**What it means in practice:** Active Papel users should immediately verify the status of their accounts, balances, and transfer capabilities. Businesses integrated with Papel for collections or payments should assess the impact and arrange alternative payment infrastructure. More broadly, this development underscores the tightening regulatory environment for fintech companies in Turkey; payment-services entities should audit their CBRT compliance obligations in light of recent enforcement activity.

## Public Property Management

### National Property Circular Extends Asset Allocation to Wealth Fund-Linked Entities

The Ministry of Environment, Urbanisation and Climate Change published **General Circular No. 426** in the Official Gazette on **24 September 2026**, amending **General Circular No. 324** on National Property. The change expands the list of institutions eligible to receive public real-property allocations.

**Two new eligibility categories added:**
- **Paragraph (j):** Ministry-affiliated and related institutions and their subsidiaries, for purposes within their founding-legislation mandates
- **Paragraph (k):** Legal entities established by special law or presidential decree, and entities in which **Turkey Wealth Fund or the Treasury holds at least 15% equity**, for purposes aligned with their statutory objects

**Who is affected:** Companies within the Turkey Wealth Fund and public-equity entities gain a broader legal basis for national-property allocation requests. Counsel advising on privatisation or public property matters should review this circular against their clients' asset-acquisition plans.

## International Trade Law

### New Anti-Dumping Communiqué and Amendment Published

Two separate import-regulation instruments were published in the Official Gazette on **23 September 2026**:

1. **Communiqué No. 2026/29 on Preventing Unfair Competition in Imports** entered into force as a new measure
2. **Amendment to Communiqué No. 2021/19** updated a Chinese company name from *Zhejiang Juba Welding Equipments Manufacturing Co., Ltd.* to *Zhejiang Juba Mechanical & Electrical Co., Ltd.*

**Legal context:** Investigations into unfair competition in imports are conducted under **Law No. 3577 on the Prevention of Unfair Competition in Imports** and its implementing regulations. New communiqués typically introduce anti-dumping or countervailing duties on specific products and origin countries.

**Who is affected:** Companies importing goods falling within the scope of Communiqué No. 2026/29 should review the full text to determine whether additional customs obligations apply. Trade counsel should brief clients promptly on any new tariff or quota requirements.
