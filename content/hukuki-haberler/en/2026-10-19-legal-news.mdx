## Legal Profession

### Simultaneous Fraud Operation in 39 Provinces: 159 Detained

Justice Minister **Akın Gürlek** announced that a coordinated investment fraud investigation led by the **Ankara Chief Public Prosecutor's Office** resulted in simultaneous operations across **39 provinces**. Of **215 suspects**, **159 were detained**, with the financial flow linked to suspects exceeding **6.656 billion Turkish lira**.

Under **Article 157 of the Turkish Penal Code** (aggravated fraud), investigations of this scale often involve multiple aggravating factors — the organized nature of the scheme, the large number of victims, and the financial volume all point to sentences at the upper end of the statutory range. For victims, early engagement with prosecutors on asset tracing and precautionary attachment orders is critical to improving the odds of actual recovery.

### Alo Adalet 135 Hotline Extended to All 81 Provinces

Justice Minister Gürlek announced that the **Alo Adalet 135 hotline**, which receives complaints about prolonged cases and investigations, is now operational at courthouses in all 81 Turkish provinces. The line centralises complaints about delays in judicial proceedings and routes them to the relevant units.

While the hotline addresses bureaucratic bottlenecks, its legal binding force is limited. Where the problem persists, the right to a fair trial within a reasonable time under **Article 6 of the European Convention on Human Rights** provides the applicable legal remedy via individual application to the **ECtHR**.

### TBB Files Annulment Lawsuit Against SEDDK Insurance Circular

The **Union of Turkish Bar Associations (TBB)** filed a lawsuit before the **Council of State (Danıştay)** seeking the suspension and annulment of a SEDDK (Insurance and Private Pension Regulation and Supervision Agency) circular on applications to the Insurance Arbitration Commission for motor vehicle insurance claims (Circular 2026/02).

The TBB argues that the circular effectively restricts access to the courts by nudging claimants toward arbitration and limiting the role of attorneys in traffic accident compensation proceedings — raising concerns under **Article 36 of the Turkish Constitution** (right to petition courts). If the Council of State issues a stay, the circular's application would be suspended pending the final judgment. Practitioners in insurance and tort law should monitor this case closely.

### MASAK Publishes Suspicious Transaction Guide on Rented Accounts

**MASAK** released a new guidance document: the "Suspicious Transaction Reporting Guide on Combating Rented Accounts." A rented bank account is one whose holder transfers de facto control to a third party — typically to move criminal proceeds while maintaining a layer of nominal legitimacy, making it a common vehicle for money laundering.

The guide operationalises the suspicious transaction reporting (**ŞİB**) obligation under **Law No. 5549** on the Prevention of Laundering of Crime Proceeds for financial institutions. It specifies transaction patterns that should trigger a report. Criminal defence lawyers representing clients charged in connection with account activity should review the guide's criteria — the indicators it lists may also form the basis for arguments distinguishing a client from a knowing participant.

[Source →](https://masak.hmb.gov.tr/duyuru/kiralik-hesaplarla-mucadele-supheli-islem-bildirimi-rehberi-yayimlandi)

## Legislation and Parliament

### Deadline for Companies to Meet New Minimum Capital Extended to 31 December 2027

A communiqué published in the Official Gazette extended the deadline for joint-stock and limited liability companies to comply with updated minimum capital requirements to **31 December 2027**. The **Turkish Commercial Code** sets minimum share capital at **TRY 250,000** for joint-stock companies (Article 332) and **TRY 10,000** for limited liability companies (Article 580); the transition deadline has been extended multiple times to ease the adjustment burden.

Failure to comply by the extended deadline carries severe consequences: a company may be struck from the commercial register, face forced dissolution, or expose shareholders to personal liability. Corporate counsel should audit their client portfolios against this deadline.

### 31,054 Contracted Healthcare Workers Assigned to Underserved Regions for 2027

A Presidential Decree designated service units where a total of **31,054 contracted healthcare personnel** will be employed in regions facing staffing shortages, effective **1 January 2027**.

In Turkish labour law, **contracted public employees** operate under a distinct legal regime from civil servants — they are not subject to the same dismissal protections, face different rules on transfer and contract renewal, and may not access certain collective bargaining rights. Healthcare professionals in this category should have their contracts reviewed carefully before signing, particularly regarding non-renewal clauses and geographic restrictions.

### Instalment Period Regulation Revised: Ministry Takes Over Limit-Setting Authority

The **Ministry of Trade** removed the fixed **12-month general instalment cap** and product-specific time limits from the consumer credit regulation, replacing them with a framework that grants the Ministry direct authority to set and update instalment limits by administrative decision.

**Previous rule:** Specific instalment ceilings were hard-coded in the regulation (12 months for most goods; shorter periods for selected categories). **New rule:** Limits will be set dynamically by Ministerial decision — the static thresholds are gone. Consumer finance practitioners, retail sector counsel, and banks advising on credit card agreements must monitor upcoming Ministerial decisions for the applicable constraints. The change creates regulatory uncertainty in the short term while granting the executive greater flexibility to respond to economic conditions.

## Court of Cassation Rulings

### Separate Attorney Fees Required for Each Co-Plaintiff in Optional Joinder

**Court of Cassation, 1st Civil Chamber**, Case No. **E. 2020/589**, Decision No. **K. 2021/1102**, **01.03.2021**:

> Where plaintiffs are optional co-parties and each is represented by counsel, it is erroneous to fail to award separate attorney fees to each plaintiff based on their respective shares.

**Legal context:** **Optional joinder** (CCP Article 57) allows multiple claimants to proceed in the same lawsuit while retaining independent claim status — distinct from mandatory joinder, where parties form an indivisible legal unit. In optional joinder, each plaintiff's share is assessed independently, and attorney fees must follow suit.

**Practical significance:** Attorneys representing multiple plaintiffs in property, inheritance, or shared-ownership disputes now have clear appellate authority to seek separate fee awards for each co-plaintiff proportional to their individual claims. Courts that lump co-plaintiffs together on fees should be challenged.

### Contractor's Loss from Delayed Payment Must Be Calculated Using Basket Method

**Court of Cassation, 6th Civil Chamber**, Case No. **E. 2025/2406**, Decision No. **K. 2026/2915**, **01.07.2026**:

> The consequential loss suffered by a contractor unable to collect the outstanding project balance on time must be determined using the basket (sepet) calculation method.

**Legal context:** **Consequential loss** (Code of Obligations, Article 122) covers actual damages to creditors that exceed the statutory interest rate when a debt is paid late. The **basket method** tracks purchasing power against a foreign-currency index, providing inflation-adjusted compensation in a way that pure TRY interest rates may not.

**Practical significance:** In construction and subcontracting disputes where project balances are paid late, this ruling confirms that contractors can claim damages well above statutory interest, particularly during periods of high inflation. It is a useful precedent for quantum calculations in contract disputes.

### Failure to Name Neighbour in Article 21/2 Service Renders Notice Invalid

**Court of Cassation, 12th Civil Chamber**, Case No. **E. 2021/1453**, Decision No. **K. 2021/2304**, **02.03.2021**:

> Under Article 21/2 of the Notification Law, the name of the neighbour from whom a statement is taken must be recorded on the notice. Without this, it cannot be established that the process server actually attended the address and found the addressee absent.

**Legal context:** **Article 21/2 of the Notification Law** permits service on a neighbour when the addressee is not at home, but imposes strict formal requirements — the neighbour's identity must be explicitly recorded. This is not a technicality but a condition of evidence: the record must demonstrate that the server actually visited the address.

**Practical significance:** A notice missing the neighbour's name is procedurally defective: no time period starts to run, no decision becomes final, and the document is treated as unserved. Parties who received a notice in enforcement, tax, administrative, or criminal proceedings should verify whether the neighbour's name is on the notice document. If absent, a timeliness objection may be available.

### Ignoring a Valid Excuse Note Violates the Right to Defense

**Court of Cassation, 8th Criminal Chamber**, Case No. **E. 2021/10908**, Decision No. **K. 2021/19980**, **01.11.2021**:

> Disregarding the excuse petition filed by defence counsel citing adverse weather conditions and health problems — requesting additional time for oral submissions and an adjournment — constitutes a restriction on the right to defense and requires reversal.

**Legal context:** **Article 36 of the Constitution** and **Article 151 of the Code of Criminal Procedure (CMK)** guarantee the right to a defense. The Court of Cassation held that proceeding without addressing a legitimate excuse deprives the defendant of effective representation and constitutes a substantive basis for reversal.

**Practical significance:** Defence attorneys should submit excuse petitions in writing, with supporting documentation (medical certificate, travel disruption evidence), and file them before the hearing begins whenever possible. A court that proceeds over a properly documented excuse provides grounds for reversal on appeal — this ruling gives that argument explicit appellate authority.
