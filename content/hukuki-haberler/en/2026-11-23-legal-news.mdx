Last week the Official Gazette carried a Presidential Decision granting three- and five-year residence permits to foreign investors who do not work in Türkiye, new rules for electing a mayor when the office falls vacant, and the transfer of Adil Katılım Bank to the Savings Deposit Insurance Fund. The Constitutional Court annulled the provision of a 1930 law that allowed prostitution under state supervision. At the Court of Cassation, rulings addressed mediation in claims to show a rented property, lawyers' fees after a SIM card cloning case, and fee calculation where a claim is dismissed for lack of standing.

## Official Gazette and Parliament

### Three- and Five-Year Residence Permits for Foreign Investors

The **Decision on Residence Permits to Be Issued to Foreign Investors**, Presidential Decision No. 11994 of 17 November 2026, was published in Official Gazette No. 33434 on 18 November 2026 and took effect on publication. It rests on Article 31 of Law No. 6458 on Foreigners and International Protection and covers an investor who does not work in Türkiye, a foreign spouse, and the minor or dependent foreign children of the investor or spouse.

An investment of at least USD 150,000 (or the equivalent in foreign currency) earns a three-year permit; at least USD 200,000 earns five years. The money can go into bank deposits, government debt instruments, real estate or venture capital investment fund units, or the private pension system. In every case the investment must be held for half of the permit period; verification falls to the Banking Regulation and Supervision Agency, the Ministry of Treasury and Finance, the Capital Markets Board and the Insurance and Private Pension Regulation and Supervision Agency respectively. These permits are not counted under Article 12 of the Turkish Citizenship Law, and the Ministry of the Interior will set the procedures.

### Electing a Mayor: Combined Ballot and Preference Stamp

The Ministry of Environment, Urbanisation and Climate Change added Article 19/A to the Regulation on the Working of Municipal Councils, published in Official Gazette No. 33436 on 20 November 2026 and in force from that date. When the office of mayor or acting mayor falls vacant, candidacies are submitted at the first session of the voting meeting that the governor sets within ten days; no applications are accepted afterwards, though a candidate may withdraw before any voting session.

Voting is secret, on a single ballot listing the candidates alphabetically, with the member stamping a preference seal in the chosen candidate's box. The presiding board must set up at least two closed booths and place a transparent ballot box where everyone can see it. Members who are candidates may vote but cannot chair the sessions or serve on the board. Each political party group and the independent members may appoint an observer. If the box holds more sealed envelopes than there were voters, that round is repeated; ballots are kept until the period for challenging the result expires, or until a lawsuit is finally decided.

### Anti-Dumping Duties on Staple Fibre Yarn Continue for Five More Years

The Ministry of Trade's Communiqué No. 2026/32, published in the Official Gazette of 18 November 2026, keeps in place anti-dumping duties on imports of yarn of synthetic or artificial staple fibres originating in China, Indonesia, India, Malaysia, Pakistan, Thailand and Vietnam, following an expiry review. The duties were first imposed by Communiqués 2020/8 and 2020/9; the review found that dumping and injury would likely continue or recur if the measures lapsed. The legal basis is Law No. 3577 on the Prevention of Unfair Competition in Imports.

Rates vary by country and company: USD 0.49 to 0.80 per kilogram for China, USD 0.29 to 0.39 for India, and between 19.48 and 26.25 per cent of the CIF value for Vietnam. The measure expires five years after entry into force, and if a new expiry review opens before then it stays in force until that review ends.

### Pilot Licence Criminal-Record Conditions Rewritten

The Directorate General of Civil Aviation replaced Article 16 of the Pilot Licence Regulation (SHY-1) in Official Gazette No. 33435 of 19 November 2026, effective on publication. An applicant must not have been sentenced to two years or more in prison for an intentional offence, even where the periods in Article 53 of the Turkish Criminal Code have elapsed. Even if amnestied, the applicant must also be free of convictions for offences against state security, the constitutional order and national defence, espionage, migrant smuggling and human trafficking, sexual offences, drug trafficking, money laundering, organised crime, intentional homicide, bid rigging, bribery, embezzlement, theft, fraud and forgery, and for violations of specified smuggling, firearms and counter-terrorism laws. A licence will be cancelled if these conditions are lost after issue or false documents are found to have been submitted.

### Pharmaceutical Promotion Regulation: New Notice Periods and a New Ban

The Turkish Medicines and Medical Devices Agency amended the Regulation on Promotional Activities for Medicinal Products for Human Use, published in Official Gazette No. 33437 on 21 November 2026 and effective on publication. The Agency must now be notified of a meeting's content, likely attendees, expense items and events at least five working days ahead for domestic meetings and at least ten working days ahead for meetings abroad. Promoting products that are licensed or authorised but not on the Turkish market is now prohibited, and visits to licence holders' production facilities in Türkiye are listed among permitted promotional methods.

## Court Decisions

### Constitutional Court: Provision Allowing State-Supervised Prostitution Annulled

The Constitutional Court unanimously annulled the first sentence of Article 128(1) of the Public Health Law No. 1593. The decision (**File 2025/144, Decision 2026/164**), dated 16 July 2026, joined referrals from the Erzurum Regional Administrative Court 3rd Administrative Chamber, the Mardin 3rd Administrative Court and the 4th Chamber of the Council of State, and was published in Official Gazette No. 33433 on 17 November 2026. The provision had the Ministries of Health and the Interior issue a joint regulation setting the rules for registered women and brothels; the referrals arose from cases challenging the sealing of homes and workplaces.

The Court noted that this provision is the only legal basis in Turkish law for prostitution under state supervision, and reviewed it under Articles 5 and 17 of the Constitution. Placing prostitution in a legal framework, it said, does not make it compatible with human dignity even if the aim is public health and safety, and regulating an area in which people's vulnerability is exploited conflicts with the state's positive duty to protect physical and moral existence. The second sentence, which could no longer apply, was annulled under Article 43(4) of Law No. 6216. The decision contains no provision delaying the annulment's entry into force.

### Court of Cassation 3rd Civil Chamber: Mediation Is a Precondition for Claims to Show a Rented Property

The Adana Regional Court of Appeal 5th Civil Chamber and the Ankara Regional Court of Appeal 15th Civil Chamber had issued conflicting final rulings on whether claims under Article 319(2) of the Code of Obligations, seeking permission to show a rented property to buyers, require mediation first. The Ankara chamber treated such claims as urgent matters outside the mediation requirement.

The **3rd Civil Chamber** (File 2026/4277, Decision 2026/3806, 15 June 2026) resolved the conflict under Article 35 of Law No. 5235 and held, unanimously and finally, that applying to a mediator is a precondition to suit. In the Chamber's view, Article 18/B of the Mediation Law No. 6325 has covered every dispute arising from a lease, except eviction proceedings through enforcement without a court judgment, since 1 September 2023, and the law makes no exception for these claims. A claim filed without prior mediation is dismissed on procedural grounds.

### Court of Cassation 3rd Civil Chamber: SIM Card Cloned and Crypto Account Emptied; Lawyers' Fees Corrected

A consumer in Kayseri sued a mobile operator after the SIM card of his line was cloned at one of its authorised dealers without his knowledge and 7,990.71 USDT left his crypto account the same day. The first-instance court found the operator at fault and awarded USD 7,990.71 plus TRY 25,000 in non-pecuniary damages; the regional court of appeal rejected the appeals.

The **3rd Civil Chamber** (File 2025/5307, Decision 2026/2209, 15 April 2026) rejected the substantive grounds of appeal and corrected only the attorney's fees. Because the operator skipped the first mediation meeting without a valid excuse, Article 18/A(11) of Law No. 6325 entitles it, for the rejected part of the pecuniary claim, to half of the fee under the Attorneys' Minimum Fee Tariff (TRY 1,516.97). The Constitutional Court had annulled Article 326(2) of Law No. 6100 on 25 December 2024 (File 2024/29, Decision 2024/226), with effect from 14 December 2025, so no fee could be awarded to the operator for the rejected non-pecuniary claim. The judgment was affirmed as corrected.

### Court of Cassation 10th Civil Chamber: One Lump-Sum Fee When a Claim Fails for Lack of Standing

A worker in Kocaeli suffered a 40.20 per cent permanent loss of working capacity in a traffic accident involving a service vehicle while on temporary assignment, and sued both the employer that insured him and the business where he worked for pecuniary and non-pecuniary damages. The regional court dismissed the claim against the insuring employer for lack of fault and held the other business liable for pecuniary damages plus TRY 60,000 in non-pecuniary damages.

The **10th Civil Chamber** (File 2025/7260, Decision 2026/4604, 14 April 2026) found it wrong to award the dismissed defendant separate fees for the pecuniary and non-pecuniary claims. Where a claim is rejected for lack of standing, Article 7(2) of the Attorneys' Minimum Fee Tariff allows a single lump-sum fee, so the Chamber set it at TRY 30,000 and left a TRY 20,000 fee for the dismissed non-pecuniary claim against the other defendant. The worker's appeal against the rejected TRY 80,000 non-pecuniary claim fell below the TRY 544,000 appeal threshold and was dismissed on amount. The judgment was affirmed as corrected.

### Court of Cassation 12th Criminal Chamber: Acquittal of School Manager Who Installed Cameras in Staff Room Affirmed with Correction

A school manager was tried for violating privacy after security cameras were installed in the staff room and classrooms. The first-instance court sentenced him to two years and one month in prison. The regional court quashed that and acquitted him, reasoning that a staff room shared by all teachers and classrooms are not areas of the complainants' private life.

The **12th Criminal Chamber** (File 2024/2326, Decision 2026/6429, 22 June 2026) upheld the acquittal but corrected its legal basis: because the defendant did not act with awareness of unlawfulness, the acquittal should rest on Article 223(2)(a) of the Code of Criminal Procedure instead of sub-paragraph (c). The judgment was affirmed as corrected, unanimously.

### Union of Turkish Bar Associations Disciplinary Board: Filing a Power of Attorney Only to Collect a Fee After the Prosecutor Seeks Acquittal Breaches Professional Ethics

The Disciplinary Board of the Union of Turkish Bar Associations (File 2025/610, Decision 2025/654, 5 September 2025) held that a lawyer who, in a criminal case handled by a colleague appointed as mandatory defence counsel, filed a power of attorney solely to collect a fee after the prosecutor's opinion requested acquittal acted contrary to professional ethics and the dignity of the legal profession.
