## Legal News

### KVKK Rules Biometric Attendance Tracking Unlawful Even With Employee Consent

Turkey's **Personal Data Protection Authority (KVKK)** issued a public notice responding to inquiries about its **Principle Decision No. 2026/921** (dated 29 April 2026). The decision clarifies that processing employees' biometric data — including fingerprints and palm scans — solely for attendance-tracking purposes lacks statutory authorization under Turkish law.

**Key legal basis:** Under **Law No. 6698** (Turkish PDPL), Article 6, biometric data is classified as special category personal data requiring an explicit statutory basis to process. The board held that employee consent does not cure this deficiency when the sole purpose is attendance monitoring, as proportionality requirements are not met. High-security facilities serving genuine security functions beyond simple attendance monitoring are treated differently.

**Practical impact:** Any employer using biometric scanners purely for time-and-attendance must reconfigure or replace these systems. Continued use creates KVKK administrative sanction exposure. Employers should conduct a data processing inventory and migrate to card-based or alternative attendance methods without delay.

### Two Major Bills Head to Parliament in October: Alimony Duration Cap and Social Media ID

Justice Minister Akın Gürlek announced that two significant legislative reforms will reach Turkey's **Grand National Assembly in October 2026**. The first would grant judges authority to set time limits on alimony (nafaka) payments, ending the current indefinite-duration default established by case law. The second would require identity verification for social media account access.

**On alimony:** Under current Turkish family law, alimony payments following divorce can continue indefinitely; the **Court of Cassation** has consistently upheld open-ended alimony. The proposed amendment would allow judges to cap payment duration based on specific circumstances, aligning Turkey with several European jurisdictions that apply time-limited spousal support.

**On social media identity verification:** Mandatory identity verification raises significant questions under freedom of expression, the right to anonymous speech, and personal data protection frameworks. Legal practitioners and civil society organizations are expected to engage actively during the parliamentary process.

### Turkish Bar Association Publishes AI Usage Advisory Guide for Lawyers

The **Turkish Bar Association (TBB)** released a comprehensive advisory guide on the ethical and safe use of artificial intelligence tools in legal practice. The guide addresses client confidentiality, data security, and professional due diligence obligations when using AI-assisted legal research, document drafting, and case law analysis tools.

This marks the first comprehensive TBB policy document on AI governance for lawyers. Attorneys using AI tools must ensure they are not transferring client data to third-party systems without appropriate safeguards — a requirement intersecting directly with KVKK obligations under Law No. 6698.

### TBB: Requiring Criminal History Certificate in Internship Applications Violates KVKK

The Turkish Bar Association announced that certain bar associations have been requesting annotated civil registry records (vukuatlı nüfus kayıt örneği — a document disclosing criminal history) as a mandatory internship application document, in violation of the TBB Internship Regulation and the Personal Data Protection Law. TBB clarified this requirement has no legal basis.

Applicants who have been asked for this document can first appeal to the relevant bar association. If the requirement persists, a formal complaint to KVKK is available as a remedy.

### Alo Adalet 135: New Helpline for Lengthy Court Cases Launches 1 September

The **Alo Adalet 135** helpline launches on **1 September 2026** at the Ankara West, İzmir, and Kayseri courthouses. Citizens experiencing unreasonable delays in court proceedings or investigations can call 135 to file complaints quickly and securely with the Ministry of Justice.

The service is particularly relevant for parties asserting violations of the right to a fair trial within a reasonable time under **ECHR Article 6**. Nationwide rollout is planned following the pilot phase.

## Official Gazette: New Regulations

### Concordat Expense Advance Schedule Published — Retroactive to March 2018

The **Concordat Expense Advance Schedule** was published in the Official Gazette and entered into force. The schedule applies to all concordat requests filed after **15 March 2018**, meaning it has retroactive effect on a significant number of pending proceedings.

Concordat (Turkish Code of Enforcement and Bankruptcy, Articles 285 ff.) is a court-supervised financial restructuring process allowing a debtor to negotiate a repayment plan with creditors. The expense advance covers court and procedural costs at the outset of the proceeding. Insolvency practitioners should review the itemized schedule to update client cost estimates and fee letters.

### 2026 Notary Fee Schedule Updated

The **2026 Notary Fee Schedule** amendment was published in the Official Gazette. Turkey's notary fees are updated periodically in line with inflation and official tariff reviews. The revised schedule applies to all notarial acts including certification, authenticated copies, power of attorney preparation, and valuations. Legal practitioners relying on notarial acts should confirm the applicable fees before proceeding.

### Turkey Emissions Trading System (TR ETS) Regulation Enters Into Force

The **Turkey Emissions Trading System (TR ETS) Regulation** was published in the Official Gazette and entered into force, establishing the legal framework for monitoring, reporting, and verification (MRV) of greenhouse gas emissions. The previous Greenhouse Gas Emissions Monitoring Regulation has been repealed.

**TR ETS** follows the model of the EU Emissions Trading System and is designed to create a functioning carbon market mechanism in Turkey. Large industrial facilities, energy producers, and aviation operators subject to greenhouse gas emission permits are in scope. The regulation details obligations for preparing monitoring plans, submitting annual emission reports, and undergoing independent third-party verification.

Beyond environmental compliance, TR ETS creates significant new advisory demand for legal professionals working in energy law, corporate sustainability, regulatory affairs, and carbon market transactions.

### Halkbank SME Credit: Businesses With Overdue Tax or Social Security Debt Now Eligible

An amendment to the **Treasury-Subsidized Investment and Working Capital Credit Programme** administered through **Türkiye Halk Bankası** expanded eligibility. Small businesses with overdue tax or social security obligations may now apply for additional credit specifically to repay those arrears, capped at **300,000 TL per year**. The programme has been extended through **31 December 2027**.

This amendment provides a structured path out of the debt accumulation cycle many small businesses face. Borrowers should carefully assess repayment capacity before taking on additional credit to service existing liabilities.

### Employment Protection Support Programme Extended to Large Companies With Higher Limits

Amendments to the **Employment Protection Support Programme Regulation** extended the programme through **31 December 2028** and brought large enterprises into scope alongside SMEs. Credit limits were revised upward: **50 million TL** for SMEs and **150 million TL** for large enterprises. A new payment channel — "Go Dijital Cüzdan" — was added. Large enterprises seeking to benefit should verify their industrial sector classification based on their Social Security workplace registration code.

### New Requirements for Tourism Facilities: Reception Desks, Pool Safety, Transfer Restrictions

The **Simple Accommodation Facilities and Beach Establishments Certification Regulation** was amended. Facilities with **25 or more rooms** must now maintain a reception desk. Pool and sunbathing areas must feature non-slip surfaces, safety steps, and depth indicators.

A critical legal restriction: **tourism operation certificates** may no longer be transferred except through inheritance (testamentary or intestate succession). This restriction significantly affects hotel acquisitions, business transfers, and equity transactions in the tourism sector. Legal advisors working on Turkish tourism M&A must account for this change when structuring deals.

## Court Decisions

### Court of Cassation 12th Chamber: Reposting Another Person's Video Without Consent Is a Criminal Offence

*Yargıtay 12. Ceza Dairesi (12th Criminal Chamber of the Court of Cassation)*

The **Court of Cassation** upheld an **18-month prison sentence** for a social media user who reposted — on their own account — a video that the victim had previously shared publicly, without the victim's renewed consent.

**Previous position:** Courts had inconsistently treated republication of already-public content, sometimes accepting the "it was already public" defence. **Decision:** Original publication does not create an open licence for redistribution by third parties. **Practical impact:** Social media users in Turkey face criminal liability for resharing content — including photos, videos, and recordings — without the subject's explicit consent, even if the original post was publicly accessible on another account.

### Council of State 8th Chamber: No Enforcement Order Before Traffic Fine Appeal Is Final

*Danıştay 8. Daire, E. 2025/6734, K. 2026/675, 10 February 2026*

The **Council of State (Danıştay)** ruled that enforcement orders for traffic fines cannot be served while an appeal of the underlying fine is still pending before the courts. A fine that has not yet become final and binding cannot lawfully be referred to enforcement.

This decision provides meaningful protection for drivers who appeal traffic penalties. Those who received enforcement orders while an appeal was pending can challenge the enforcement action and seek suspension of proceedings on this basis.

### Court of Cassation 5th HD: Constitutional Court Interest Rate Ruling Does Not Authorize Courts to Award Advance Interest Sua Sponte

*Yargıtay 5. Hukuk Dairesi, E. 2025/2119, K. 2025/13301, 22 October 2025*

The **5th Civil Chamber of the Court of Cassation** clarified that the **Constitutional Court's** annulment of statutory interest rate provisions does not confer on courts or arbitral tribunals the authority to award advance interest (avans faizi) without a specific claim from a party. An express request remains required.

**Practical impact:** Parties in expropriation, commercial contract, or damages proceedings who do not expressly claim interest in their pleadings risk losing that right entirely. This reinforces the importance of precise and complete pleading in Turkish civil litigation.

### Court of Cassation 12th HD: A Final Negative Declaratory Judgment Binds the Enforcement Court

*Yargıtay 12. Hukuk Dairesi, E. 2025/9772, K. 2026/2131, 1 April 2026*

Although an enforcement court is not required to stay proceedings pending a separate negative declaratory judgment action, once that action has resulted in a **final judgment** in the debtor's favour, the enforcement court must take it into account as a procedural bar under **CCP Article 114/1-i** (Code of Civil Procedure, condition of action).

This ruling provides strong protection for debtors who have obtained a finalized judgment confirming no debt exists: the enforcement court is bound to treat that judgment as determinative.

### Court of Cassation 11th HD: No Decision Without a Hearing During Judicial Recess

*Yargıtay 11. Hukuk Dairesi, E. 2023/3887, K. 2023/6222*

For cases not among those statutorily permitted to proceed during Turkey's judicial recess (20 July – 31 August), issuing a decision without holding a hearing violates procedural rules. The Court of Cassation sets aside such decisions on appeal.

Attorneys should scrutinize decisions issued during the recess period in non-exempt proceedings and raise procedural violation arguments in cassation filings where applicable.
