A busy week for Turkish law: **Law No. 7589** (the 12th Judiciary Package) entered force on 31 July, reworking deferred-sentencing rules and bank-account fraud penalties. The Court of Cassation Grand Chamber held that UYAP petitions lacking an electronic signature cannot be voided on that ground alone, while the **Personal Data Protection Board (KVKK)** classified online exam-result lists as regulated personal-data processing. These developments, alongside new specialist courts for internet and criminal-procedure matters, define this week's **Turkey legal news**.

---

## Legal Profession Updates

### Bar Exam Applications Open 11–19 August 2026

The **Legal Profession Entrance Examination (HMGS)** and the **Administrative Law Preliminary Exam (İYÖS)** accept applications from **11 to 19 August 2026**. Candidates unable to apply in that window may submit a late application on **26 August**. Both exams run simultaneously on **27 September 2026**.

A notable methodological change accompanies this cycle: scoring will apply **standard-deviation normalisation**, adjusting raw scores against the statistical spread of results. Law graduates seeking admission to the bar, judiciary, or administrative courts must not miss these deadlines.

### New Judicial Efficiency Bureaux for Court Docket Management

Justice Minister **Akın Gürlek** announced the creation of **Judicial Efficiency Bureaux** within first-instance criminal courts and regional appeal courts, including affiliated courthouses. The bureaux will monitor case timelines, file management, and intra-court coordination.

Practitioners should watch for new electronic-notification channels and potential procedural timeline shifts as this institutional layer matures over coming months.

---

### Bar Associations Move Green Passport Applications Online

The Istanbul, Ankara, and Izmir bars announced that **green passport** (service passport) applications for licensed lawyers are shifting to an **e-government platform**, removing the requirement for in-person submission. Lawyers should check their home bar's website for updated application steps and the rollout date, as timing may differ across bars.

## Official Gazette and Parliament

### 12th Judiciary Package: Deferred Sentencing and Fraud Rules Amended

**Law No. 7589** ("On Amendments to Various Laws for Effective and Efficient Judiciary") was published in the **Official Gazette on 31 July 2026**.

Key changes:

- **Bank-account misuse**: persons who allow others to use their account or card details may receive up to a **50 % sentence reduction**, creating new plea-negotiation dynamics in financial fraud cases.
- **HAGB (deferred sentencing) reformed**: **torture and ill-treatment offences are permanently excluded** from the deferred-sentencing mechanism. A conviction in these cases now results in a directly enforceable custodial sentence.
- **Notarial records digitalised**: paper notarial documents may be converted to and maintained in digital form.
- **Inherited-property auctions**: the first auction of real property passing through inheritance is now open **only to co-heirs**, limiting speculative third-party bidding at the initial round.

For criminal-defence practitioners, the HAGB change is most consequential in proceedings against public officials. Clients charged with torture or ill-treatment can no longer benefit from suspended sentences on conviction.

### Specialist Judges Designated for Internet Law and Criminal Procedure

**HSK (Council of Judges and Prosecutors) First Chamber**, through **Decision No. 1547**, designated specialist judges effective **1 August 2026** for two areas:

1. Access-blocking and content-removal requests under **Law No. 5651** (the Internet Law)
2. Protective measures under the **Code of Criminal Procedure (CMK)** — searches, seizures, wiretaps

In courts with eight or more **Peace Criminal Judges (SCH)**, the **No. 1 SCH** becomes the designated specialist bench. New cases filed from 1 August are routed accordingly.

The result is doctrinal consistency: internet-law and criminal-investigation requests concentrate before experienced specialist judges rather than rotating panels. Lawyers practising in digital investigations or content regulation should update their case-routing assumptions immediately.

### PDPA Ruling: Online Exam Lists Require Legal Basis

The **Personal Data Protection Board (KVKK)** published **Decision No. 2026/1301** (dated 01.07.2026) in the **Official Gazette on 28 July 2026**.

The Board held that posting exam and lottery results on publicly accessible websites constitutes **personal-data processing** governed by **KVKK No. 6698**. Practical consequences:

- Lists lacking a lawful processing basis must be **taken offline**.
- Government agencies must shift to **authenticated access systems** such as e-Devlet rather than posting names and ID numbers on open websites.
- Violations are subject to KVKK's administrative-fine regime, which can reach several million Turkish lira.

Legal departments of universities, district governorships, and public bodies that routinely publish selection or admission lists online should treat this decision as an immediate compliance obligation.

### Digital Precious Metals: Blockchain-Backed Gold Set to Trade on Exchange

A **29 July 2026** amendment to the **Communiqué on Precious Metal Standards and Refineries** introduced the category of **"digital precious metal"** — physically backed gold, silver, or platinum tokenised on a blockchain and tradeable on exchange. These instruments are legally distinct from crypto-assets regulated under the Capital Markets Board (SPK).

Refineries must register with the Precious Metals and Stones Stock System (KMTS) and obtain Treasury and Finance Ministry approval before listing such assets. For capital-markets, fintech, and crypto-assets lawyers, this communiqué creates a novel regulatory category that is likely to generate future litigation around custody, redemption, and exchange liability.

### Foreign-Registered Vehicles Face Border Exit Block for Toll Debts

The **Regulation on Transition Fees and Administrative Fines for Foreign-Registered Vehicles on Toll Roads**, published on **29 July 2026**, introduces a direct enforcement mechanism: vehicles with foreign licence plates that carry **outstanding toll or administrative-fine debts** will be **blocked from exiting Turkey** at customs checkpoints until the debt is cleared.

This directly affects international road-haulage operators, tourists, and foreign vehicle owners. Lawyers advising transport companies or individuals in cross-border contexts should incorporate this rule into compliance guidance.

### Hydrogen Gas Added to Turkish Mining Regulatory Scope

The **Regulation on Mining** was amended on **31 July 2026** to add **hydrogen gas (H₂)** to the list of gases within the mining regulatory framework. The same amendment removed the phrase "finally decided" from licence and permit proceedings, potentially streamlining approval timelines.

Bringing hydrogen under mining law signals Turkey's intent to regulate green-hydrogen production through existing mineral-resource infrastructure. Energy and natural-resources lawyers should expect secondary legislation on licensing, royalties, and environmental-compliance requirements.

### Higher Education Law: Student Amnesty and Fake-Diploma Criminal Penalties

Parliament enacted amendments to the **Higher Education Law**, introducing a **student amnesty** reinstating eligibility for millions of students. The law also imposes strict financial audits on **private (foundation) universities**, provides **criminal imprisonment** for issuing or using fake diplomas, and mandates **dismissal from profession** for academics who have theses or papers written on their behalf.

These provisions open new litigation areas: criminal proceedings for diploma fraud, administrative challenges to university audits, and disciplinary appeals by dismissed academics.

## Court of Cassation and Constitutional Court Rulings

### Constitutional Court: PDPA Fine Does Not Open Direct Path to AYM

**Background**: KVKK administrative fines are challenged through the administrative court system — annulment action, appeal to a Regional Administrative Court, and review by the Council of State.

**Ruling**: The **Constitutional Court (AYM)** declared **inadmissible** a petition from a sports facility fined by KVKK for operating a **palm-print biometric scanner**. The applicant alleged violation of the right of access to court without first exhausting administrative-court remedies.

**Significance**: AYM reaffirmed that ordinary legal channels must be fully exhausted before an individual constitutional application is entertained. KVKK enforcement targets cannot bypass the administrative judiciary by filing directly with the Constitutional Court.

---

### Court of Cassation Grand Chamber: E-Signature-Free UYAP Petition Is Valid

**Background**: Some judicial panels had refused or treated as deficient petitions filed through UYAP that lacked an attached electronic signature.

**Ruling**: The **Grand Chamber of the Court of Cassation (CGK)** held that a petition submitted through UYAP **cannot be declared invalid solely because it lacks an electronic signature**.

**Significance**: This ruling protects lawyers when system errors, connectivity failures, or technical malfunctions prevent attaching a valid e-signature during submission. UYAP system delivery itself confers formal legal existence on the filing. Practitioners should document technical incidents to support any procedural challenge.

---

### Court of Cassation General Assembly: Expropriation Notice Must Be Served via UETS

**Ruling**: The **Court of Cassation General Assembly (HGK)** held that the warning notice for depositing expropriation compensation must be served to the plaintiff agency's registered address in the **National Electronic Notification System (UETS)**. (**E. 2024/570, K. 2025/569, 24.09.2025**)

**Significance**: This closes a gap by which government agencies argued that physical mail sufficed in expropriation proceedings. Lawyers representing property owners in compulsory-purchase cases should verify whether the expropriating authority has a UETS address and whether the notice was delivered there — a service defect could invalidate procedural timelines.

---

### Regional Administrative Court: Lawyer VAT Base Cannot Fall Below Minimum Tariff

**Ruling**: The **Izmir Regional Administrative Court, 2nd Tax Chamber**, held that even if a lawyer agrees to charge **below the mandatory minimum fee schedule (AAÜT)**, the **VAT base must be calculated on at least the AAÜT rate**. The sub-tariff agreement does not reduce the lawyer's VAT liability. (**E. 2019/300, K. 2019/428, 18.04.2019**)

**Significance**: Lawyers who discount fees for commercial reasons must still report VAT calculated on the official tariff rate. Historical VAT filings should be reviewed against AAÜT benchmarks in consultation with a financial advisor to identify any exposure.

### Court of Cassation 1st Civil Chamber: Party's Death Mandates Sua Sponte Action

**Ruling**: The **Court of Cassation 1st Civil Chamber** held that ensuring proper **party standing** following the death of a litigant is a matter of **public order**. The presiding judge must address it **of their own motion** at any stage, regardless of which party is appealing. (**E. 2026/1598, K. 2026/2390, 30.03.2026**)

**Significance**: In long-running property, succession, or commercial disputes, a party's death can go undetected. Lawyers should implement protocols to check party status before each hearing and notify the court immediately upon learning of a death, requesting joinder of heirs. Failure to reconstitute the parties can vitiate the entire proceeding.

### Court of Cassation 9th Labour Chamber: Overtime Threshold in 24-Hour Shift Work

**Ruling**: The **Court of Cassation 9th Labour Chamber** confirmed that in 24-hour shift arrangements, maximum productive working hours are treated as **14 hours per day**. Daily work **exceeding 11 hours** — a 3-hour band — qualifies as **overtime** for statutory compensation purposes. (**E. 2026/1068, K. 2026/2977, 02.04.2026**)

**Significance**: Workers on 24-hour shifts in security, healthcare, and maintenance sectors are entitled to overtime pay for those daily hours between 11 and 14. Labour lawyers handling compensation claims for shift workers should apply this formula when calculating unpaid overtime accruals, as it can substantially increase award amounts in back-pay litigation.
