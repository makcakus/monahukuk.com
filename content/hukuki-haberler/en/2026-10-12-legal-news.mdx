The week of 12 October 2026 brought three landmark developments in Turkish law. The **Personal Data Protection Authority (KVKK)** issued binding rules on how employers may monitor corporate email accounts. A **social media age-verification regulation** banning accounts for under-15s was published in the Official Gazette. And the **Constitutional Court (AYM)** handed down a pilot ruling finding that systematic publication bans in prisons violate freedom of expression, ordering Parliament to legislate within one year.

## Legal Profession News

### KVKK Issues Binding Rules on Workplace Email Monitoring

The **Personal Data Protection Board (KVKK)** published its **Principle Decision No. 2026/2035, dated 16 September 2026**, setting out the procedural requirements employers must follow when monitoring corporate email accounts and other communication channels assigned to employees.

Under **Law No. 6698 on the Protection of Personal Data (KVKK Law)**, employees' personal data are protected even within the workplace; the employer's monitoring authority is not unlimited. This decision operationalises the core principles of "proportionality" and "legitimate purpose" in the context of internal corporate surveillance.

Practical implications for lawyers and HR departments: employees must be informed before any monitoring takes place; the scope of monitoring must be limited to work-related content; and personal communications must be excluded from the monitoring perimeter. Employment law disputes involving dismissed employees are expected to reference this decision going forward.

## Official Gazette

### Regulation Banning Social Media for Under-15s Published — In Force 1 November

The **Regulation on Age-Separated Services for Children on Social Networks and Age Verification** was published in the Official Gazette on **10 October 2026** and enters into force on **1 November 2026**.

Key provisions:

- Social network accounts may **not be opened for children under 15**.
- Users aged **15–18** will receive a default private account with live-streaming and infinite scroll disabled; personalised advertising is prohibited.
- **Age verification** will be carried out via an e-Government token that reveals only whether the user meets the age threshold — no other personal data is disclosed.
- Existing accounts must be verified by **31 March 2027**; accounts belonging to under-15s will be closed.

Legal framework: This regulation operates under **Law No. 5651** on the regulation of internet publications. Foreign platforms such as Meta, TikTok and X are required to comply through their Turkish representatives. Non-compliance may result in bandwidth throttling sanctions.

### TTK Article 376 Communiqué: Capital Loss Exemption Extended to 2028

**Article 376 of the Turkish Commercial Code (TTK)** governs the general assembly and management obligations that arise when a company loses two-thirds of its capital or becomes insolvent. An amendment to the communiqué on its application extended the following exemption:

- The option to **exclude foreign currency exchange losses** and **half of certain rent, depreciation and personnel costs from 2020-2021** when calculating capital adequacy has been **extended from 1 January 2027 to 1 January 2028**.

Companies with foreign currency liabilities that have suffered technically large exchange-rate losses may appear balance-sheet insolvent without reflecting their genuine operating position. This extension postpones the obligation to trigger Article 376 procedures — including capital increases or liquidation — giving boards and their counsel additional time to restructure.

[Communiqué text →](https://www.resmigazete.gov.tr/eskiler/2026/10/20261010-19.htm)

### Anti-Money Laundering Regulation: Identity Verification Threshold Raised to TRY 370,000

Significant amendments were made to the **Regulation on Measures Against Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing**:

- The identity verification threshold for individual transactions was raised from **TRY 185,000 to TRY 370,000**.
- The threshold for EFT and crypto-asset transfers increased from **TRY 15,000 to TRY 30,000**.
- Where a customer's identity has previously been verified, the requirement to obtain a **specimen signature for subsequent transactions** is lifted, provided the identity can be confirmed through secure channels such as internet or mobile banking.

These changes, made in the context of Turkey's **FATF compliance obligations**, update the know-your-customer (KYC) requirements applicable to banks, crypto-asset service providers, and professionals — including lawyers and notaries — who handle high-value commercial transactions.

[Regulation text →](https://www.resmigazete.gov.tr/eskiler/2026/10/20261007-9.pdf)

## Constitutional Court and Court of Cassation Rulings

### AYM Pilot Ruling: Prison Publication Bans Violate Freedom of Expression

The **Constitutional Court (AYM)** ruled that the practice of withholding books, newspapers and periodicals sent from outside to persons held in detention facilities — as well as confiscating their diaries, letters and similar documents — violates the right to freedom of expression.

**Previous situation:** Prison regulations allowed management discretion to withhold certain publications and documents on security grounds. The Court found these interferences lacked **"a sufficient and foreseeable legal basis"**.

**The pilot ruling mechanism:** Rather than deciding the individual case in isolation, the Court applied the **pilot ruling procedure** (Türkiye's adaptation of the ECtHR pilot judgment approach). This suspends examination of similar applications and gives the Grand National Assembly a deadline — **one year from Official Gazette publication** — to enact corrective legislation.

**Practical significance:** Inmates' family members and defence lawyers may rely on this ruling to support individual applications in respect of ongoing publication bans. The ruling also creates a binding framework for the legislative amendments now required under Turkish constitutional law.

### Court of Cassation Grand Chamber: Silence on Expert Report Is Not Acceptance

In a personal injury compensation claim arising from a workplace accident, the failure of the employee's counsel to contest an expert report does **not** constitute acceptance of the report.

**Court of Cassation Grand Chamber (Yargıtay HGK), File No. E. 2026/10-129, Decision No. K. 2026/299, 6 May 2026**

Some first-instance courts had interpreted counsel's failure to contest an expert report as implied consent. This ruling clarifies that challenging an expert report is an elective right, not a procedural obligation; silence does not amount to acceptance. Lawyers handling workplace accident claims should note that while contesting a report within the two-week post-service window remains the safest approach, failure to do so no longer carries the same procedural risk.

### Court of Cassation 3rd Chamber: Enforcement Applications Must State the Cause of Debt

The cause of the debt must be stated in an enforcement application. Failure to do so renders the application and the payment order procedurally defective, and a subsequent action to annul the debtor's objection will be dismissed.

**Court of Cassation 3rd Civil Chamber, E. 2023/5182, K. 2024/170, 15 January 2024**

This ruling confirms a formal requirement under **Article 58/3 of the Enforcement and Bankruptcy Code (Law No. 2004)**: the cause of the claim must be clearly stated in the enforcement application. Creditors' lawyers are reminded to describe the basis of the claim precisely when initiating enforcement proceedings.

### Court of Cassation Grand Chamber: Enforcement Officers Must Verify Judgment Status ex officio

The enforcement officer receiving an application must verify **on their own motion** whether the document submitted by the creditor qualifies as a judgment or equivalent document, and whether the parties to the proceedings correspond to the creditor and debtor named in the document.

**Court of Cassation Grand Chamber, E. 2025/286, K. 2026/145, 11 March 2026**

This ruling establishes that enforcement officers are not passive recipients of applications; they are independently required to assess the legal status of the document submitted. Since proceedings initiated under an invalid or mischaracterised document remain at risk of annulment, creditors' lawyers should pre-verify the document's qualifying status before filing.

### Court of Cassation 11th Chamber: Multiple Repair Visits Count as Timely Defect Notice

Where a vehicle is brought to an authorised service centre multiple times due to recurring defects, the **defect notification must be considered to have been made within the statutory period**.

**Court of Cassation 11th Civil Chamber, E. 2025/4983, K. 2026/1691, 25 March 2026**

Under **Law No. 6502 on Consumer Protection**, consumers bear a notification obligation in respect of defective goods. This ruling holds that repeated service visits for persistent faults collectively satisfy the notification requirement. Consumers who have purchased a new vehicle and experienced recurring unresolved defects will find this ruling directly relevant: the risk of missing the notification deadline is effectively removed where there is a documented repair history.

### Turkish Bar Association Disciplinary Board: Reporting Informal Collection Does Not Require Waiving Criminal Complaint

A creditor's lawyer who reports an informal out-of-court collection to the enforcement file is **not required** to formally waive the criminal complaint before the enforcement criminal court, and failure to do so does not give rise to disciplinary liability. The obligation to waive the criminal complaint before that court rests with the complaining debtor, not the creditor's lawyer.

**TBB Disciplinary Board, File E. 2023/243, Decision K. 2023/339, 2 April 2023**

For lawyers acting for creditors: recording the informal collection in the enforcement file is sufficient. Managing the criminal complaint process before the enforcement criminal court is the debtor's responsibility.
