Last week the Official Gazette carried a regulation that rewrites the rules for insurance sold alongside goods and services, the ratification law for the Mecca Joint Defence Agreement, and an amendment that changes how Turkish schools run exams. At the Court of Cassation, the Grand Civil Chamber set a notable standard for serving notices on companies, while two civil chambers ruled on marital property and condominium disputes.

## Official Gazette and Parliament

### Embedded Insurance Regulation Enters into Force

The **Regulation on Embedded Insurance**, prepared by the Insurance and Private Pensions Regulation and Supervision Agency (SEDDK), was published in Official Gazette No. 33428 on 12 November 2026 and took effect on publication. It governs insurance offered in the physical or electronic setting where a good or service is sold, limited to risks directly tied to that good or service, and rests on Insurance Law No. 5684.

Permitted cover includes breakage, loss or theft of goods, illness, theft or loss of a pet, inability to use travel, accommodation or subscription services, and the insurance required for residence permit and visa applications under Law No. 6458. Compulsory insurance, home and workplace, health, compulsory earthquake, legal protection and cargo insurance cannot be sold this way. Annual gross premium may not exceed the monthly gross minimum wage in force in the first half of the year in which cover starts; the Board may halve or double that ceiling. Cover lasts at most two years, with longer terms allowed for extended-warranty products.

A seller may not make the insurance a condition of buying the good or service, nor worsen the price or other sale terms for a buyer who declines it. If the buyer cancels, rescinds or withdraws from the underlying contract, the insurance ends automatically without compensation or penalty. Authorised institutions must notify the Agency one month before launching a product.

### Law Ratifying the Mecca Joint Defence Agreement Published

**Law No. 7597 approving the ratification of the Mecca Agreement for Joint Defence between the Republic of Türkiye, the Islamic Republic of Pakistan and the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia** was published in Official Gazette No. 33430 on 14 November 2026 and took effect on publication. The Grand National Assembly adopted it on 5 November 2026. The three-article law approves ratification of the agreement signed in Mecca on 7 August 2026.

### Ministry of Education Amends Assessment Regulation: No Exams in Pre-School and Primary School

The Ministry of National Education (MEB) published amendments to its 9 September 2023 Assessment and Evaluation Regulation in the Official Gazette of 14 November 2026, effective on publication. Exams of any kind can no longer be held in pre-school and primary school. The regulation now separates nationwide common written exams from school-wide common exams, and the Ministry will announce at the start of each school year which grades and subjects get a nationwide exam.

Assessment results may not be used to rank or compare students, classes, schools or provinces. Artificial intelligence tools approved by the Ministry may be used to support question writing and evaluation. The amendment also adds a separate chapter on national and international monitoring studies such as ABİDE, PISA and TIMSS.

### University Hospitals Coordination Committee Established

Presidential Decision No. 11971 of 13 November 2026 created a coordination committee for hospitals owned by state and foundation universities. The Head of the Council of Higher Education (YÖK) chairs it, with members from the Presidential General Secretariat, the Ministry of Treasury and Finance, the Ministry of Health, the Strategy and Budget Office and the Social Security Institution, plus one rector and one chief physician chosen by YÖK. The committee will monitor hospital finances, prepare mandatory financial improvement programmes for centres whose sustainability deteriorates, and recommend one of three candidates selected by the rector as chief physician. It meets in the first week of January, April, July and October.

### Post-Disaster Building Damage Assessment Regulation Amended

The Ministry of the Interior amended the Regulation on General Rules for Building Damage Assessment after Disasters. The amendment, published in the Official Gazette of 12 November 2026, replaces the phrase "under the supervision of the evacuation, placement and planning group" in Article 15(2) and Article 21(1) with "under the supervision of the security and traffic group, by the owners of the belongings"; Annexes 1 and 2 are also replaced. It takes effect on 22 December 2026.

## Court Decisions

### Court of Cassation HGK: Second Notice to a Company's Registered Address Is Valid Even if the First Came Back "Not Known"

In an enforcement proceeding in Eskişehir based on a promissory note, the payment order was sent to the company's address in the trade registry and returned with the note "addressee not known at the address." The notice was then served at the same address under Article 35 of the Notification Law. The 12th Civil Chamber found the service defective and reversed; the lower court resisted.

The **Grand Civil Chamber** (File 2025/629, Decision 2026/385, 18 June 2026) upheld the resisting decision by majority. The Chamber reasoned that a legal entity's address is never "unknown" when it is registered, and that Article 35(4) applies once the first notice to the registry address has come back undelivered, for whatever reason. The dissenting view held that a notice returned as "not known" should make later service under Article 35 invalid; the majority did not adopt it.

### Court of Cassation 2nd Civil Chamber: Car Registered in a Sibling's Name Brought into Marital Property Liquidation

In a participation-in-acquired-property claim before the Gaziosmanpaşa 4th Family Court, the claim regarding a car acquired on 14 July 2020 in the name of the husband's brother had been dismissed. The **2nd Civil Chamber** (File 2025/5281, Decision 2026/7924, 10 September 2026) unanimously reversed. Weighing witness statements, WhatsApp messages between the spouses, loan documents, credit card spending on the car and HGS-OGS toll payments together, the Chamber held that the car belonged to the husband and had to be treated as an added-back value under Article 229 of the Turkish Civil Code. The parties married on 8 July 2019, and the divorce action was filed on 16 September 2020.

### Court of Cassation 5th Civil Chamber: Clarification Cannot Change a Judgment; Parking Area Shared by Land Share

In Kayseri, a claim sought to cancel the title to a 430 m² covered car park attached to one unit and to designate it as a common area. The regional court first described it as common space to be used "in proportion to the defendants' land shares," then, on a clarification request, changed the wording to "in proportion to the land shares of the unit owners."

The **5th Civil Chamber** (File 2025/13155, Decision 2026/11155, 15 June 2026) unanimously set aside the supplementary decision and reversed the main ruling. It held that clarification or correction cannot add a new obligation or grant a new right, and that a judge cannot change the judgment after relinquishing the file. As for use of common areas, the extent of each owner's right is proportional to the land share unless agreed otherwise, and the management plan contained no provision in the defendants' favour.
