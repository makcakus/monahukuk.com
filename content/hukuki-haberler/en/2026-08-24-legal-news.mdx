This week in Turkish law brought significant legislative and judicial developments. The Official Gazette published a **Child Protection Act amendment**, the **Turkey Artificial Intelligence Action Plan 2026-2030**, and ended DASK earthquake insurance policy transfers. The Court of Cassation issued landmark rulings on labor mediation, e-mail resignations, and consumer protection for new vehicle defects.

## Lawyer's Agenda

### Judicial Efficiency Offices: Operational Guidebook Published

Justice Minister Akın Gürlek announced the publication of the **Judicial Efficiency Offices Guidebook**, developed under the **Fourth Judicial Reform Strategy**. These offices monitor docket delays, flag stalled cases, and report on judicial performance — with the guidebook standardizing how they operate across all courts.

For practitioners, this means complaints directed to efficiency offices will now follow a consistent procedural framework. Ad hoc treatment of efficiency-related submissions should diminish, and attorneys have a clearer administrative channel when systemic delays are encountered.

### Forest Fire Criminal Investigations: 46 Suspects, 12 Detained

Minister Gürlek reported that criminal investigations into **73 forest fires** across **25 provinces** since 1 June 2026 are ongoing. Of 46 identified suspects, 12 have been remanded in custody and 21 placed under judicial supervision.

Under Turkish criminal law, arson-related offenses may be prosecuted under **Turkish Criminal Code (TCK) Articles 170 and 179** (creating general danger and negligent fire-setting). If intent is established, substantially heavier penalties apply. Defense counsel representing suspects should note that judicial supervision orders are appealable.

### İzmir Bar Association Warns Companies on Mandatory In-House Counsel

The **İzmir Bar Association** sent formal written notices to companies operating in İzmir and its districts, reminding them of their obligation under **Article 35 of the Lawyers Act (Law No. 1136)** to retain in-house counsel. Companies meeting certain scale thresholds — primarily joint-stock and limited liability companies — are legally required to have a salaried attorney on staff.

Failure to comply exposes companies to administrative sanctions and creates enforceability risks in legal transactions concluded without counsel. Corporate lawyers advising clients on governance and operational compliance should verify whether this obligation applies.

### Supreme Electoral Council Restricts Voter Data Sharing on Digital Platforms

The **Supreme Electoral Council (YSK)** issued a decision limiting political parties' ability to share voter registry data with third parties via external digital platforms and websites, restricting voter query functions, and mandating enhanced data security measures.

This decision sits at the intersection of **KVKK** (Turkey's personal data protection law) and electoral legislation. Voter registry data can qualify as sensitive personal data under KVKK, making unauthorized sharing subject to penalties under both electoral and data protection regimes. Parties conducting voter outreach during election periods now face tighter constraints on how they process and disseminate voter information.

[YSK decision (PDF) →](https://www.ysk.gov.tr/doc/karar/dosya/58015706/2026-315.pdf)

## Official Gazette

### Child Sentencing Reform: Reduced Leniency for Ages 15-18 in Serious Crimes

**Law No. 7593 amending the Child Protection Act** was published in the Official Gazette on **18 August 2026**. The key changes:

**Sentencing latitude narrowed:** For juveniles aged **15-18**, the sentence reduction brackets previously available under **TCK Article 31** were tightened. For serious crimes such as intentional homicide, courts may now **entirely eliminate** the sentence reduction for this age group.

**Parental criminal liability:** Parents who violate family law obligations and thereby enable a child to commit a serious crime face aggravated penalties.

**Digital child protection:** A mandatory **social investigation report** is now required before trying children under 15; new cybersecurity-linked protective measures for children are introduced.

Criminal defense attorneys handling juvenile cases must recalibrate sentencing calculations under the new framework. The discretion available to courts on reductions has narrowed, and the range of outcomes for ages 15-18 in serious offenses now varies more significantly with the specific facts.

[Official Gazette →](https://www.resmigazete.gov.tr/eskiler/2026/08/20260818-1.htm)

### Turkey AI Action Plan 2026-2030: Presidential Circular Published

A **Presidential Circular on the Turkey Artificial Intelligence Action Plan (2026-2030)** was published in the Official Gazette on **18 August 2026**. The 44-page strategic plan is organized around four axes — **"Perceive, Utilize, Produce, Govern"** — and contains **16 critical actions**.

From a legal standpoint, the "Govern" pillar is most immediately relevant: it addresses ethical oversight, liability frameworks, and regulatory architecture for AI systems deployed in public services, the judiciary, and private enterprise. As Turkey aligns with the **EU AI Act**, new compliance requirements for tech companies and data processors are likely to emerge from this plan in the coming legislative cycle.

### DASK Earthquake Insurance Transfer Permanently Abolished

A communiqué amending the **Compulsory Earthquake Insurance (DASK) General Conditions** was published on **21 August 2026**. The change permanently ends the practice of automatically transferring DASK policies to new owners upon property sale.

**Before:** When a property changed hands, the existing DASK policy transferred automatically to the buyer, with remaining coverage and premiums carrying over.

**After:** Buyers must independently obtain a new DASK policy after property transfer. The transaction itself no longer carries insurance continuity.

For real estate and property lawyers, this is an immediate client advisory obligation: buyers must be informed that completing the title deed transfer does not provide earthquake insurance coverage and that a new policy must be issued independently.

[Official Gazette →](https://www.resmigazete.gov.tr/eskiler/2026/08/20260821-11.htm)

### Free Transit Support Authority Transferred to Ministry of Environment

A regulatory amendment published on **22 August 2026** transferred all authority and execution responsibilities for the **free transit revenue support payment** scheme — compensating public transport operators for passengers entitled to free travel — from the **Ministry of Family and Social Services** to the **Ministry of Environment, Urbanization and Climate Change**.

## Court of Cassation Rulings

### Court of Cassation: KVKK Penalty on Doctor Voided Without Expert Evidence

The **Court of Cassation, 7th Criminal Chamber** (E. 2025/6580, K. 2026/4354, 13 April 2026) quashed a lower court ruling that had upheld the **Personal Data Protection Authority (KVKK)**'s administrative fine against a physician for failing to implement adequate data security measures.

The chamber held that the fine could not stand without expert evaluation of: (1) whether the technical and administrative measures taken were adequate; (2) how many patients were affected by the breach; and (3) the physician's financial circumstances. Deciding the appeal without obtaining an expert panel report was a reversible procedural error.

For healthcare lawyers, this ruling provides a concrete defensive argument: KVKK penalty proceedings must be backed by expert testimony before courts can uphold them. Failing to request expert evaluation may constitute appealable grounds.

### Court of Cassation: Email Venting Workplace Frustration Counted as Resignation

The **Court of Cassation, 9th Labor Chamber** ruled that an email in which a senior manager who had worked at the company for three years stated they felt "spiritually worn down and exhausted" — following a year of what they described as adverse treatment and unauthorized reduction of bonuses — constituted a **resignation** under Turkish labor law. The employee received no severance.

**Lower court:** Found no clear resignation intent in the email and awarded severance and notice pay.

**Court of Cassation:** Evaluated the email together with the employer's reply, witness statements, and all other evidence holistically. The resignation intent could be derived from the totality of circumstances.

The ruling is a clear warning for employees and managers: emotionally charged emails expressing dissatisfaction, exhaustion, or intent to leave can function as resignation declarations in Turkish labor law, even without the word "resign."

### Court of Cassation: Employer's Lawyer Cannot Mediate in Labor Disputes

The **Court of Cassation, 9th Labor Chamber** ruled that an attorney in an ongoing retainer relationship with an employer cannot serve as mediator in a dispute between that employer and one of its employees — and that the parties' consent does not cure this disqualification.

**Background:** An employee alleged being coerced into signing a resignation letter and a voluntary mediation record. First instance and appellate courts rejected the claim on limitation grounds. The Court of Cassation overturned, holding the ongoing legal representation relationship **categorically eliminated** the mediator's impartiality — a mandatory requirement under the **Mediation in Civil Disputes Act**.

For both employees entering mediation and labor attorneys structuring dispute resolution processes: the identity and potential conflicts of interest of the proposed mediator must be scrutinized at the outset. An improperly conducted mediation does not bind the parties.

### Court of Cassation: Validity of Computer Evidence in Divorce Cannot Rest on Content Alone

The **Court of Cassation, 2nd Civil Chamber** (E. 2024/9995, K. 2025/5421, 28 May 2025) held that the admissibility of evidence obtained from a spouse's computer in divorce proceedings cannot be determined solely by examining the content of the evidence — the manner in which it was obtained must also be assessed.

Digital evidence extracted from a spouse's device without consent may constitute a criminal offense under **TCK Articles 136 and 243** (data theft and unauthorized computer access) and may therefore be excluded as illegally obtained evidence. Family courts must evaluate the full circumstances of collection, not merely the evidentiary value of the content.

For family law practitioners, this is an important procedural note: advising clients on how to gather digital evidence before initiating proceedings is now a legal risk management question, not merely a tactical one.

### Court of Cassation: Factory Defect in New Car Entitles Buyer to Full Replacement

The **Court of Cassation, 11th Civil Chamber** (E. 2025/6373, K. 2026/3122, 2 June 2026) ruled that a consumer who purchased a new vehicle that exhibited recurring, persistent factory defects affecting the engine during the warranty period is entitled to a **replacement with a defect-free equivalent vehicle** — not merely a repair — and that both the manufacturer and the dealer are **jointly and severally liable** for the defect.

**Previous practice:** Many courts accepted repair as sufficient even for high-value vehicles with serious recurring defects.

**Court of Cassation reasoning:** Given the high purchase price of the vehicle and the gravity of defects affecting the engine, requiring the consumer to accept repairs is unreasonable. The consumer's right to replacement under **Law No. 6502 on Consumer Protection** takes precedence.

For consumer protection lawyers, this ruling significantly strengthens the legal footing for replacement claims in new vehicle purchases where factory defects recur within the warranty period.
