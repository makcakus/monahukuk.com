The week of 20 July 2026 brought significant legislative and judicial developments in Turkish law. **Judicial Reform Package No. 12** passed the Grand National Assembly, introducing provisions on digital fraud via IBAN lending, electronic notarial documents, genetic data protection, and mandatory UYAP-based property sales. The **Constitutional Court** handed down three individual-application rulings finding violations of property rights and the right of access to court. **The Court of Cassation** and **Council of State** issued notable procedural and substantive decisions across tax, tenancy, and administrative law.

## Legal News

### All-Province Meeting Reviews Criminal Justice Practices

**Justice Minister Akın Gürlek** chaired the **Judicial Assessment Meeting** for first-instance criminal courts, attended by chief public prosecutors and judicial commission presidents from all 81 provinces. The agenda focused on practical problems in criminal proceedings and progress toward targets set under Turkey's **Judicial Reform Strategy**.

These gatherings translate reform policy into operational guidance. When procedural or technical problems are raised at this level, follow-up circulars or ministerial directives tend to follow quickly. Lawyers practising in criminal courts should monitor forthcoming ministerial communications for changes to case management and scheduling practices.

### TBB Files Complaint Over Reverse-Handcuff Treatment

The **Union of Turkish Bar Associations (TBB)** announced it has filed a criminal complaint concerning the application of reverse handcuffs to **Deniz Göktaş** and the public release of footage showing the restraint.

The legal stakes are clear. The practice of reverse handcuffing, when it causes unnecessary pain or injury, falls within the scope of **Article 17 of the Turkish Constitution** (prohibition of torture and degrading treatment) and **Article 3 of the European Convention on Human Rights**. The TBB's formal complaint lifts the matter from a disciplinary discussion to a criminal law question, and may later provide grounds for an ECtHR application.

### İzmir Bar Association: Data Protection Officer Role Lacks Statutory Basis

In proceedings brought by the **İzmir Bar Association** to annul a Data Protection Officer (DPO) certification programme, the court identified a significant statutory gap: the concept of a "data protection officer" does not appear anywhere in **Personal Data Protection Law No. 6698 (KVKK)**. Creating the role through secondary regulation alone was found to lack legal authority. Moreover, allowing any four-year university graduate to act as a DPO was held to conflict with **Article 35 of the Attorneys' Act**, which reserves certain legal advisory functions for lawyers.

For Turkish data protection practitioners: this decision creates uncertainty around DPO appointment frameworks built purely on regulatory guidance. Law firms advising clients on KVKK compliance should assess whether their DPO structures rest on a defensible statutory foundation.

### İzmir Bar: Assignment Letter Sufficient for CMK Defence Fee Payment

The **İzmir Bar Association** confirmed — in line with an earlier announcement by the TBB — that lawyers appointed under the **Code of Criminal Procedure (CMK)** as mandatory defence counsel or upon suspect request may claim their fees using only the **assignment letter**, without additional documentation.

This removes a common administrative barrier for duty lawyers. Under **CMK Articles 149–157**, court-appointed lawyers have a right to be remunerated at the Ministry of Justice tariff rate; the confirmation that an assignment letter suffices streamlines the claims process in practice.

### MASAK Publishes 2026–2030 Anti-Money Laundering Strategy

**MASAK** (Financial Crimes Investigation Board) published its updated **2026–2030 strategy document** on combating money laundering and terrorist financing.

This five-year roadmap shapes how Turkish obliged entities — banks, financial institutions, lawyers, notaries, real estate agents, and precious metal dealers — will face supervisory scrutiny going forward. Turkey's alignment with **FATF** (Financial Action Task Force) recommendations is a core driver of the document. Entities subject to AML/CFT obligations should review the strategy early; forthcoming regulatory changes to customer due diligence, beneficial ownership disclosure, and reporting thresholds are likely to follow.

### Insurance Arbitration Commission Fee Schedule Updated

The **Insurance Arbitration Commission** fee schedule was updated under **Article 30 of Insurance Law No. 5684**. The new schedule applies to applications lodged on or after **16 July 2026**.

Insurance arbitration offers a faster and cheaper route than litigation for resolving insurance disputes. The revised fees affect case economics for claimants in traffic accident, life insurance, and health insurance disputes. Lawyers advising clients on whether to choose arbitration or litigation should recalibrate their cost-benefit assessments using the updated schedule.

## Legislation / Official Gazette

### 12th Judicial Reform Package: Four Key Changes

The torba (omnibus) bill known as **Judicial Reform Package No. 12** was adopted by the **Grand National Assembly of Turkey (TBMM)**. Four provisions stand out for legal practice:

**1. IBAN Lending and Digital Fraud:** Individuals who participate in fraud schemes by allowing their payment accounts or IBANs to be used by others — a practice known as "IBAN kiralama" — may now receive reduced sentences proportionate to their degree of fault. The provision acknowledges the structural role of financial account facilitators in organised digital fraud.

**2. Genetic Data Protection:** Following a Constitutional Court annulment ruling, the statutory framework governing the processing of genetic data has been revised. Genetic data is among the most sensitive categories of personal data; the revision brings the law into compliance with the Constitutional Court's guidance.

**3. Notarial Documents via Secure Electronic Signature:** Notaries will now be able to transmit certified true copies of documents to official bodies using a **secure electronic signature**. This removes the need for physical delivery in many property registration, company formation, and power-of-attorney workflows.

**4. Mandatory UYAP Portal for Property Transactions:** Sales of movable and immovable property affecting ownership rights must now be conducted through an electronic portal integrated with the **UYAP** (National Judiciary Informatics System). For real estate lawyers and enforcement practitioners, this introduces new formalities and may affect the timing and documentation of property transfers.

### Capital Markets Board Amends Investor Compensation Centre Rules

The **Capital Markets Board of Turkey (SPK)** amended the **Investor Compensation Centre (YTM) Regulation**, updating rules on asset protection, the return of time-barred assets, and Takasbank's responsibilities. Under the revised rules, claims for the return of assets transferred to YTM due to limitation periods must be submitted through **e-Government (e-Devlet)**.

YTM provides a safety net for investors when a licensed intermediary fails. Migrating the claims process to e-Government is designed to reduce paperwork, but also means that digitally unassisted investors may face practical barriers. Capital markets lawyers should note that the new channel may also affect the procedural framing of disputes about asset recovery.

### Turkish-Flagged Vessel Scrapping Incentive Regulation Amended

The regulation on **incentives for building new vessels to replace scrapped Turkish-flagged ships** was amended. Changes include a broader definition of eligible contractors, a **vessel registration requirement** for the scrapped ship, and the establishment of a **minimum tonnage threshold** for the newly built vessel.

These adjustments tighten eligibility criteria and reduce the risk of abuse. Shipping lawyers advising on maritime incentive applications must ensure clients' scrapped vessels appear on the relevant ship registry before applying.

### TBMM School Violence Commission Questions Digital Platforms

The **Parliamentary Commission on School Attacks** heard testimony from representatives of **TikTok, X, and Meta** on children's digital risks and the online dimension of school violence. Platforms described measures they have taken to remove violent content and protect minors.

This parliamentary scrutiny signals that Turkey's digital platform regulation — centred on **Law No. 5651** — may evolve further. Content removal obligations and the liability standards for platforms hosting violent or harmful content are likely to be revisited in forthcoming amendments.

## Constitutional Court and Court Decisions

### Constitutional Court: Forfeiting Security Deposit From Annulled Procurement Violates Property Rights

The **Constitutional Court** ruled that failing to compensate the applicant for losses arising from a security deposit (letter of guarantee) forfeited to the Treasury in reliance on a procurement debarment order — where the underlying procurement decision had already been **annulled by a court** — constitutes a violation of the right to property under **Article 35 of the Constitution**.

The logic is straightforward: once the procurement decision is void, the legal basis for the associated debarment order collapses with it. A security forfeiture that persists despite the annulment lacks legal justification. Contractors challenging forfeiture of guarantees following court-annulled procurement procedures should cite this ruling as direct authority.

---

### Constitutional Court: GIB "Restricted CPA List" Blocked Court Access

The **Constitutional Court** found that the right of access to a court was violated in the case of an applicant whose challenge to the **Revenue Administration's (GİB)** "restricted Certified Public Accountant (YMM) list" was dismissed without examination.

The prior position was that the administrative listing of a CPA as "restricted" — effectively preventing them from practising on behalf of taxpayers — could not be meaningfully reviewed by courts, since courts refused to hear such cases on the merits. The Constitutional Court held this refusal itself violated **Article 36 of the Constitution** (right to a fair hearing). Tax lawyers advising CPAs affected by such listings now have authority for the proposition that these decisions are subject to full judicial review.

---

### Constitutional Court Upholds Non-Complaint Status for Data Offences

The **Constitutional Court** dismissed a constitutional challenge to **Article 139 of the Turkish Criminal Code (TCK)**, holding that making the offence of "unlawfully giving or obtaining data" **non-complaint-dependent** (i.e., allowing public prosecutors to initiate proceedings without a victim's complaint) does not violate the Constitution.

TCK Article 139 covers unlawful access to or disclosure of personal data. Keeping it as a non-complaint offence means prosecutors can act where victims are unaware, unwilling, or unable to complain. The Court found this legislative choice falls within the permissible margin of regulatory discretion. For data protection lawyers: this confirms that the criminal channel under TCK Article 139 and the administrative channel under KVKK can run in parallel.

### Council of State: T3-Tractor Excise Tax Requires Expert Classification

The **Council of State's Tax Chamber (VDDK)** ruled that the **excise tax (ÖTV)** classification of vehicles registered as **T3-Tractors** must be determined by reference to the **customs tariff explanatory notes** and, where necessary, expert testimony — before any tax assessment can be finalised.

The boundary between a T3-tractor (lower tax rate) and a commercial vehicle (higher rate) turns on technical characteristics. The Council of State held that this determination cannot rest solely on a vehicle registration certificate; expert examination is required in contested cases. Tax lawyers handling disputes involving agricultural machinery, construction equipment, or special-purpose vehicles should ensure that classification disputes are fully evidenced before the administrative stage.

### Court of Cassation: UYAP Read-Status Cannot Substitute Valid Service

The **Court of Cassation's 3rd Civil Division** ruled that the appearance of a document as "read" in the **UYAP** electronic case management system does not constitute legal service (tebligat) or oral pronouncement (tefhim). It quashed a lower court decision that had treated a case as having been filed out of time on that basis — citing it as a **ruling in the interest of the law (kanun yararına bozma)**.

The principle is fundamental: under Turkey's **Notification Law** and related procedural rules, legal service triggers time limits only when carried out by the prescribed method. A digital "read" marker in a case file management system is not that method. Lawyers whose clients have had cases dismissed as time-barred on the basis of UYAP read-status should re-examine whether service was validly effected.

### Council of State: Flat Attorney Fee Applies in Administrative Cancellation

The **Council of State's 8th Division** ruled — again as a **ruling in the interest of the law** — that in cancellation cases **not involving a monetary claim** (such as the cancellation of a payment order), courts must award the **flat-rate attorney fee** prescribed for such cases. Applying the proportional fee cap in **Article 13/2 of the Attorney Fee Schedule** to non-monetary administrative cases is unlawful.

The practical distinction matters: proportional fees vary with the amount in dispute, while flat fees are fixed. Using the wrong basis understates the award owed to the winning party's lawyer. Administrative lawyers seeking fee awards after successful cancellation proceedings should use this ruling to contest any proportional-fee calculation by the first-instance court.

### Court of Cassation: Written Lease Renewal Voids Prior Vacation Undertaking

The **Court of Cassation's 3rd Civil Division** (E. 2026/2877, K. 2026/2766, 05.05.2026) held that where a **vacation undertaking** (tahliye taahhüdü) has been given and the parties subsequently **renew the lease agreement in writing**, the undertaking is rendered void.

A vacation undertaking is a written commitment by the tenant to vacate the property by a specified date; under Turkish tenancy law it can be used to initiate eviction proceedings if not honoured. The Court's position is that a later written lease renewal supersedes the undertaking, reflecting the parties' subsequent shared intention to continue the tenancy. Landlords relying on an older undertaking should check whether any written renewal was signed after its date — if so, the undertaking cannot form the basis of eviction proceedings.

### Highest Interest Rate Applied Ex Officio in Expropriation Compensation Cases

The **Court of Cassation General Assembly** (E. 2025/114, K. 2026/226, 08.04.2026) ruled that in cases determining **expropriation compensation**, courts must apply the **highest interest rate applicable to public receivables** without waiting for the respondent party to specifically request it.

Under **Expropriation Law No. 2942**, interest on delayed compensation is a recurring source of dispute. The General Assembly resolved this by confirming that the rate is applied ex officio — the court applies it automatically, regardless of whether the claimant has explicitly requested the higher rate. Property owners and their lawyers in expropriation compensation cases should ensure pleadings do not inadvertently restrict the rate claimed.
