Turkey's legal week was dominated by three landmark developments. The Constitutional Court ruled on the 1993 Madımak massacre — in an individual application decided 12 years after it was filed — finding that the judicial proceedings had violated the right to life. The Justice Minister confirmed that the new alimony bill will reach parliament in October following the Court's earlier annulment ruling. The Court of Cassation, meanwhile, published two consumer-law decisions striking down a 90-day timeshare refund delay and clarifying when lenders may pass bank transaction taxes on to borrowers.

## Legal Profession and Bar Associations

### New Alimony Bill Due in Parliament This October

Justice Minister **Akın Gürlek** announced on live television that a new alimony bill is scheduled to reach the Grand National Assembly in **October 2026**, following the Constitutional Court's ruling that struck down open-ended alimony provisions. The ministry says the bill will replace indefinite duration with a needs-based, flexible framework.

Since the Court's annulment, lower courts have been applying alimony provisions inconsistently. Family-law practitioners should note that until the new text is enacted, judicial discretion effectively fills the gap — making comprehensive fact-gathering on spouses' economic circumstances at trial especially important.

### Specialist Terror Investigation Units Launched in 81 Provinces

The Ministry of Justice directed **175 serious criminal prosecution offices** across **81 provinces** to establish dedicated **Terror Crimes Investigation Bureaux**. The aim is to centralise terrorism-related investigations within specialist units rather than general prosecution teams.

For defence counsel representing terror-charge clients, this means a new institutional counterpart. Document requests, access to case files, and witness examinations will now be channelled through these bureaux rather than the general prosecutor's office. Anticipating different procedural rhythms will matter in early case management.

[Source →](https://www.adalet.gov.tr/adalet-bakanligindan-teror-sorusturmalarinda-uzmanlasma-ve-koordinasyon-adimi)

### Bar President Visits Jailed Lawyers, Challenges Pretrial Detention

**Turkish Bar Association (TBB) President Av. Erinç Sağkan** visited **Av. Hüseyin Can Güner** and **Av. Emre Doğan**, both held on remand at Kırşehir Prison, and issued a public statement afterwards. Sağkan argued that pretrial detention is an exceptional measure of last resort and that detention orders unsupported by concrete evidence violate both personal liberty and the right of democratic representation.

For lawyers handling detention review applications, the TBB's formal position provides an institutional reference point alongside Article 5 of the European Convention on Human Rights and Article 19 of the Turkish Constitution.

### Judicial Recess Begins; Green Passport Applications Go Digital

The **judicial recess** (adli tatil) runs from **20 July to 1 September 2026**. Urgent proceedings and statutory time limits continue to run; practitioners need to monitor deadlines that fall within this window.

The TBB also announced that **green passport applications** — a benefit available to practising Turkish lawyers — are now processed **electronically**, replacing the previous paper-based procedure. Application steps are published on the TBB's official website.

### Court Suspends Tax Directive on Notarised Termination Notices

The **TBB** announced that the Council of State (Danıştay) has suspended enforcement of the Revenue Administration's directive that imposed **stamp tax** on certain termination notices sent through a notary public. The suspension applies pending a final decision on the merits.

In practice, parties who terminate employment or lease contracts via notary are currently not required to pay the stamp tax in question. Should the directive ultimately be annulled, claims for taxes already paid may become viable — making it worth recording any payments made under the directive.

[Source →](https://www.barobirlik.org.tr/Haberler/noter-araciligiyla-gonderilecek-bir-kisim-fesih-ihbarlarinin-ve-cayma-hakkina-iliskin-ihbarlarin-dam-86600)

### KVKK Warns on Using Third-Party Data for Advertising

The **Personal Data Protection Authority (KVKK)** published a public notice confirming that using personal data **purchased from or obtained through third parties** for advertising and marketing purposes requires **explicit consent** under Article 5 of Law No. 6698. Operating targeted campaigns on acquired data without documented consent is a compliance risk.

Companies running data-brokered marketing campaigns should treat this notice as both a legal warning and a signal of forthcoming audits. Those without consent records should pause third-party-data campaigns until proper consent structures are in place.

[Source →](https://www.kvkk.gov.tr/Icerik/8830/ucuncu-kisilerden-elde-edilen-kisisel-verilerin-reklam-ve-pazarlama-amacli-kullanilmasina-iliskin-kamuoyu-duyurusu)

### Single Hotline Alo 193 Now Handles All Insurance Claims

The **Insurance and Private Pension Regulatory Authority (SEDDK)** issued **Circular No. 2026/21**, making a unified **Alo 193** hotline the mandatory channel for reporting traffic and motor insurance losses. Policyholders can now call 193 without knowing in advance which insurer holds the relevant policy.

For traffic accident cases, clients should be advised of the new reporting procedure immediately after an incident. The first notification step has changed, and the insurer is reached through the central hub rather than a company-specific line.

[Circular →](https://www.seddk.gov.tr/UploadContent/Documents/2026-21%20Say%C4%B1l%C4%B1%20Genelge.pdf)

### Istanbul Regional Court Upholds Bar's Challenge to Protest Ban

The **Istanbul Bar Association** won a definitive ruling in its challenge to a four-day blanket ban on meetings, marches, and press statements issued by the **Istanbul Governor's Office** on **19 March 2025**. **The Istanbul Regional Administrative Court, 10th Chamber**, dismissed the Governor's appeal and confirmed the ban's annulment as final.

This judgment reinforces the principle, well established in Turkish administrative law, that general and abstract justifications are insufficient to support blanket restrictions on assembly. The Istanbul Bar's 2025–2026 Annual Report identifies this as one of the year's leading strategic cases.

[Source →](https://istanbulbarosu.org.tr/haber/istanbul-barosundan-emsal-zafer)

### Law Faculty Intake Drops 13 Percent to 8,745 Places

Publication of the **2026 university placement guide (YKS)** confirmed a **13 percent cut in law faculty capacity** nationwide, reducing total intake to **8,745 places**. The Turkish Bar Association welcomed the reduction as consistent with its longstanding position that overexpansion of law schools has diluted educational quality.

Mid-term, a shrinking graduate pool may tighten labour supply for law firms. The broader debate over lawyer density versus quality of representation will take several years to resolve empirically.

## Official Gazette and Parliament

### New Administrative Courts Open in Kayseri and Edirne

A Presidential decree published in **Turkey's Official Gazette** established new **administrative courts** in **Kayseri** and **Edirne**, with their respective jurisdictional areas defined. The measure is designed to reduce caseloads at existing administrative courts and shorten timeframes for access to administrative justice.

Lawyers bringing actions against government acts in these provinces should review the new jurisdictional boundaries before filing. A misidentified court is a procedural error that can cost a client their statutory limitation period.

[Official Gazette →](https://www.resmigazete.gov.tr/eskiler/2026/07/20260725-6.pdf)

### Solar and Wind Energy Licences Moved to Energy Ministry

A new **Regulation on Planning and Licensing of Wind and Solar Power Plants** published in **Turkey's Official Gazette** transfers planning approval, building permits, and operating licences for renewable energy facilities from municipalities and provincial special administrations to the **Ministry of Energy and Natural Resources**.

The change fundamentally restructures the permit process for renewable energy projects. Administrative challenges and permit applications that previously went through local government now route to central government. Practitioners in the energy sector need to update their procedural maps accordingly.

[Official Gazette →](https://www.resmigazete.gov.tr/eskiler/2026/07/20260724-16.htm)

### Framework Law Heads to Committee, Stops Short of Amnesty

Parliament Speaker **Numan Kurtulmuş** stated that a **framework law** prepared as part of the new resolution process will go to committee next week, with an aim to pass before parliament's summer recess. Kurtulmuş emphasised that the legislation will carry **no amnesty provisions** — general or personal.

The distinction matters legally: an amnesty extinguishes criminal liability entirely, whereas a framework law operates on different grounds. Until the bill text is public, managing client expectations carefully is essential.

## Constitutional Court and Court of Cassation

### Constitutional Court: Madımak Trial Violated Right to Life

The **Constitutional Court of Turkey** held that judicial proceedings into the **Madımak massacre of July 1993**, in which **35 people were killed**, were not conducted effectively — constituting a violation of the **procedural limb of the right to life**. An individual application filed by survivors and victims' relatives in **2014** was decided **12 years later**. The Court ordered the payment of **non-pecuniary damages** to the applicants.

The ruling reinforces the principle that the state's obligation under **Article 17 of the Constitution** (mirroring Article 2 ECHR) includes the duty to conduct an adequate investigation into deaths. It is expected to be cited in future applications concerning unresolved historical events.

[Source →](https://t24.com.tr/gundem/aymden-madimak-katliami-karari,1337880)

### Court of Cassation: 90-Day Timeshare Refund Wait Is Unlawful

The **Court of Cassation, 3rd Civil Chamber**, held that timeshare sellers cannot make consumers wait **90 days** before refunding payments following a valid withdrawal from the contract. The Chamber ruled that refunds must be made **immediately** upon withdrawal and quashed a consumer court decision that had accepted the delay.

The ruling, published as a cassation in the interest of law (kanun yararına bozma) in Turkey's Official Gazette, sets a binding precedent. Consumers who paid and waited beyond the legal period may be entitled to claim default interest on the delayed refund.

[Official Gazette →](https://www.resmigazete.gov.tr/eskiler/2026/07/20260723-6.pdf)

### Banks May Pass BSMV Tax to Borrowers If Disclosed

The **Court of Cassation, 3rd Civil Chamber**, ruled that a bank's practice of charging the **Banking and Insurance Transactions Tax (BSMV)** through to consumer-loan borrowers does **not constitute an unfair contract term** — provided the borrower expressly accepted the obligation in the pre-contractual information form and the loan agreement itself.

Where no explicit acceptance appears in the documentation, the analysis remains open. Practitioners advising clients on loan-related disputes should examine both the information form and the loan agreement before taking a position on a BSMV refund claim.

[Official Gazette →](https://www.resmigazete.gov.tr/eskiler/2026/07/20260722-3.pdf)

### Consumer Arbitration Referral Does Not Block Parallel Enforcement Action

The **Court of Cassation, 3rd Civil Chamber**, ruled that initiating enforcement proceedings on a real estate agent's commission claim — where the amount falls within the consumer arbitration committee's monetary threshold — does not create *lis pendens* (derdestlik) with a pending committee referral. A consumer court decision that dismissed the case without examining the merits was quashed.

The practical consequence: creditors in consumer disputes falling within the arbitration threshold need not wait for committee proceedings to conclude before launching enforcement. Both tracks can run simultaneously without blocking each other.

[Official Gazette →](https://www.resmigazete.gov.tr/eskiler/2026/07/20260724-21.pdf)

### Antalya Court: Landlord's Need Must Persist Throughout Proceedings

**Antalya Regional Court of Appeals, 15th Civil Chamber** (**Case No. 2026/1244, Decision No. 2026/1422**, **21 May 2026**) reaffirmed that for an eviction claim based on the landlord's personal need to succeed, the need must be genuine, sincere, necessary, and — crucially — **continuous throughout the proceedings**. Temporary, anticipated, or remote needs do not qualify. It is not enough that the need existed when the case was filed; it must persist at judgment.

For landlords pursuing eviction on personal-need grounds, this means filing is only the first step. Evidence that the need remains alive at the close of the hearing is essential — otherwise the court will dismiss regardless of how compelling the initial need was.

### Threatening to Eject Beachgoers Amounts to Unlawful Imprisonment

**Court of Cassation General Criminal Board** (**Case No. 2022/82, Decision No. 2025/98**, **26 February 2025**) held that beach operators who use **threats to force people who are not their customers to leave a coastal area** commit the offence of **unlawfully depriving a person of liberty** (Turkish Penal Code, Article 109). The Board confirmed that coastal zones are public property and operators have no legal basis to restrict access.

For consumer rights practitioners, this ruling gives weight to criminal complaints in cases where beach operators have used intimidation to control access. It also signals that civil liability claims accompanying such incidents stand on solid ground.
