One of the most attractive aspects of Turkish citizenship application is that, alongside the applicant, **the spouse and children** can also become Turkish citizens by the same decision. Family members applying as a single file provides significant ease both legally and financially. This guide explains the process of including spouse and children in Turkish citizenship and key considerations.

## Legal Framework of Family-Based Application

Prevailing Turkish citizenship legislation directs the following close relatives of an applicant being admitted to citizenship to also acquire citizenship by the same decision:

- **Spouse** — The foreign-national spouse may be included in the principal applicant's file.
- **Minor (under 18) children** — naturally included.
- **Dependent adult children** — under specific conditions; e.g., adult children with permanent physical or mental disability dependent on family.

## Family Members in Investment-Based Citizenship

In Turkish citizenship application through investment, only the principal applicant (investor) needs to meet the investment threshold. **Separate investment** for spouse and children is not required. Family members:

- Are processed under the same file,
- May be subjected to independent interviews,
- Pass criminal record and health checks in their own names.

This makes the investment route extremely advantageous for foreigners conducting family planning — one investment makes the entire household Turkish citizens.

## Process and Documents for Spouse

Documents needed to include the spouse in the application:

- Passport and apostilled identity document,
- Apostilled birth certificate,
- Apostilled marriage certificate (registered in Turkey),
- Criminal record (home country and Turkey),
- Health report,
- Biometric photographs,
- Additional documents proving the marriage with the principal applicant is genuine (joint photos, joint bank account, etc.).

**Important:** If the spouse has children from a previous marriage, the relationship between the principal applicant and those children must be clarified for them to be included.

## Process and Documents for Children

Typical documents for minor children:

- Passport and apostilled identity,
- Apostilled birth certificate,
- Custody certificate (if parents are divorced),
- Documents showing the **other parent's** consent to Turkish citizenship — this is the most often overlooked and delaying point.

### Other Parent's Consent

If the child was born outside marriage or parents have separated, the other parent's **notarised consent** or, where consent cannot be obtained, a court ruling thereon must be submitted. Since this procedure depends on the other parent's cooperation in the home country, preparation should begin well before the application.

## Custody Agreements and Other Legal Issues

Including children of divorced families in citizenship:

- In **sole custody**, the custodial parent's application is accepted,
- In **joint custody**, the other parent's consent is required,
- If custody is contested, this matter must be resolved first.

For Turkey to recognise the home-country custody decision, a **recognition and enforcement** lawsuit may need to be filed.

## Children Becoming Turkish Citizens by Birth

If the principal applicant becomes a Turkish citizen and a child is born subsequently, the child becomes a Turkish citizen **at birth**. This child does not need a separate citizenship application; once birth is registered with civil registration, citizenship is automatically acquired.

The same applies to children born of foreigners married to Turkish citizens; the child is a Turkish citizen from birth because mother or father is Turkish.

## Cases Where Spouse Is Rejected, Principal Applicant Is Accepted

Some files show the principal applicant accepted while the spouse is rejected. Common causes:

- Obstacles in spouse's criminal record or background check,
- Sham-marriage suspicion,
- Document deficiency,
- Allegation of false declaration.

Spouse's rejection is open to **administrative judicial review**; the principal applicant's citizenship is not affected. The path remains open for the spouse to acquire citizenship through judicial process.

## Cases Where Child Is Rejected

Decisions rejecting children are extremely rare; typically:

- Other parent's consent missing,
- Custody relationship contested,
- Sham-adoption suspicion

can lead to rejection. In this case, citizenship acquisition by the family's other members is not blocked; for the child, a new application or cancellation lawsuit is pursued separately.

## Strategic Recommendations for Family-Based Application

1. **Run document processes in parallel.** Apostille and translation processes for family members take time; preparation should begin months before application.
2. **Prepare spouses for interview together.** A natural, genuine narrative is the strongest defence.
3. **Get other parent's consent early.** This is the biggest bottleneck for divorced families.
4. **Keep criminal record documents current.** Expired documents are rejection grounds.
5. **Get professional support.** In family files, deficiency in one person can set back the whole file; professional coordination saves time and money.

## After Family Acquires Citizenship

Once all family members become Turkish citizens:

- They can obtain Turkish passports,
- Children can join the Turkish education system in non-foreigner status,
- Spouse and children can be employed **without need for work permit**,
- Home-country citizenships (within home-country law allowance) are retained.

If the family has long-term residence plans in Turkey, these status changes will create ease in every area — school registration, insurance, property acquisition.
