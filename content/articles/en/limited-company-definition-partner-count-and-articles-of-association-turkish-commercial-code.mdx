The limited şirket is Turkey's most widely used business vehicle — the default choice for most small and mid-sized enterprises. TTK Art. 573-579 set its foundations.

## One Founder Is Enough, and Liability Stops at Capital

Art. 573 allows a single real or legal person to form a limited company with defined capital divided into capital shares. Unlike the pre-2012 regime, no minimum of two partners applies. Critically, partners bear no personal liability for company debts — they owe only their committed capital contribution, plus any additional payment or ancillary performance obligations specifically written into the articles. Turkish courts have confirmed this repeatedly: any general assembly resolution imposing extra payment obligations beyond what the articles provide requires unanimous consent, not majority vote — a resolution passed by majority alone is void.

## Fifty-Partner Ceiling and the Single-Partner Notice

Art. 574 caps the number of partners at fifty — beyond that, the joint-stock form is the intended vehicle. If the partner count drops to one, this must be reported to the managers within seven days, who then have seven more days to register and publish the single-partner status; failure exposes the responsible parties to liability for resulting damage.

## What the Articles Must Contain

Art. 575 requires the articles of association to be signed in written form before authorized personnel at the trade registry directorate — Turkey centralized this into a single procedural channel, unlike the joint-stock company's notary-or-registry alternative. Art. 576 lists five mandatory contents: trade name and registered office, business purpose, capital structure, managers' identities, and the method of announcements.

## The Thirteen "Opt-In" Provisions

Art. 577 lists thirteen categories of arrangements — restrictions on share transfer, pre-emption and buy-back rights, additional payment or ancillary obligations, veto rights, penalty clauses, non-compete terms, special voting arrangements, withdrawal and exclusion rights, and more — that only bind partners if specifically written into the articles. This list reflects the limited company's greater contractual flexibility compared to the joint-stock company, blending partnership-style customization with corporate-form liability protection.

## Practical Takeaway

Foreign investors drafting or reviewing articles of association for a Turkish limited company should treat Art. 577's list as a checklist — rights around exit, veto, and additional obligations mean nothing unless explicitly incorporated.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the maximum number of shareholders in a limited liability company?

Under TTK Article 574/1 <b>the number of shareholders may not exceed fifty.</b> The lower limit is one: under Article 573/1 a limited liability company may be formed by <b>one or more natural or legal persons</b>. Under Article 574/2, if the number of shareholders falls to one, that fact must be <b>notified in writing to the managers within seven days</b> of the transaction producing that result, and the managers must have it registered and announced.

### Is a shareholder in a limited liability company liable for the company's debts?

As a rule, no. Under TTK Article 573/2 <b>shareholders are not liable for the company's debts and are obliged only to pay the capital shares they have subscribed and to perform the additional payment and ancillary obligations provided for in the articles of association.</b> The most important exception concerns public claims: under Article 35 of Law No. 6183, shareholders are liable with their personal assets, <b>in proportion to their capital shares</b>, for public debts that cannot be collected from the company.

### Are the articles of a limited liability company executed before a notary?

Not any more. Under TTK Article 575/1 <b>the articles of association must be made in writing and signed by the founders before authorised staff at the trade registry directorate.</b> Following the amendment made by Law No. 7099, the requirement of notarial certification was abolished and the principle of signing at the trade registry directorate was adopted. The aim of this rule is to reduce the cost and duration of incorporation.
