TTK Art. 803-807 provide two related fraud-prevention tools originating in Geneva Convention practice: the crossed cheque and the account-payee cheque. Both let the drawer or holder restrict how a cheque can be cashed, reducing loss and theft risk by keeping the payment inside the banking system.

## Crossed Cheques

Under Art. 803, either the drawer or a holder may cross a cheque by drawing two parallel lines on its face. A general crossing (nothing, or just "bank," between the lines) lets the drawee pay only a bank or its own customer; a special crossing (naming a specific bank between the lines) restricts payment to that bank or its customer (Art. 804). General crossing can be converted to special, but never the reverse, and erasing a crossing is legally ineffective — the restriction survives attempted tampering. A bank that violates these rules is liable for resulting damage up to the cheque's face value. Turkish criminal case law has held that because crossed cheques receive this special statutory treatment, they qualify as "official documents" rather than "private documents" for forgery-offense purposes — a classification with real consequences in criminal proceedings.

## Account-Payee Cheques

A step further than crossing, Art. 805 lets the drawer or holder write "to be credited to account" (or similar) on the cheque, barring cash payment entirely — the bank may only settle it through book entry, clearing, or account transfer, which then substitutes for payment. This notation cannot be effectively erased either, and a bank violating it is likewise liable up to the cheque's face value.

## Holder's Fallback Rights

Two situations let the holder bypass the account-payee restriction. First, if the drawee bank goes bankrupt, suspends payments (even without a court judgment), or a levy against it proves fruitless, the holder may demand cash payment directly and pursue recourse if unpaid (Art. 806). Second, if the bank refuses to credit the amount unconditionally to the holder's account, or the local clearing house declares the cheque cannot be offset against the holder's debts, the holder may again pursue recourse (Art. 807). Together these provisions confirm that the account-payee restriction governs only the form of payment — once that form becomes practically unworkable, the holder reverts to the ordinary kambiyo protections.

## Practical Takeaway

Crossed and account-payee cheques remain less common than ordinary endorsable cheques in Turkish banking practice, but they offer a meaningful fraud-reduction tool for high-value transactions, with clear fallback rights if the restriction cannot be honored.
