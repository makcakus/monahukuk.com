TTK Art. 1228 finally defines the bill of lading (*konişmento*) — a document referenced throughout the freight-contract chapters but never until now formally pinned down.

## The Core Definition: Delivery Only Against Surrender

Under Art. 1228(1), a bill of lading proves a carriage contract was made, shows the cargo was received or loaded, and — decisively — means the carrier owes delivery only against surrender of the document itself. That third feature is what elevates a bill of lading above an ordinary receipt: possession of the paper, not identity, controls who can claim the cargo. This surrender-against-delivery principle is the doctrinal foundation for everything that follows regarding negotiability and the protection of downstream holders. Turkish courts have repeatedly enforced it strictly, including holding an agent liable for releasing cargo to a third party without the bill of lading being presented.

## Received-for-Shipment vs. On-Board Bills

A "received-for-shipment bill" can be issued once cargo is received for carriage but not yet loaded; once actually loaded, the carrier must issue a full "on-board bill of lading" in as many copies as the shipper requests, against surrender of the earlier document. A simple annotation on a received-for-shipment bill noting the loading date and vessel converts it directly into an on-board bill, without reissuance. The document may be signed by the captain, the carrier, or an authorized agent, always on the carrier's behalf.

## Straight, Order, and Bearer Bills

Three negotiability forms exist: straight (naming a specific consignee, the least transferable), order (transferable by endorsement, similar in spirit to bill-of-exchange endorsement), and bearer (transferable simply by physical handover, the most liquid form). Absent contrary agreement, a bill issued "to order" defaults to the shipper's own order — a default favoring the negotiable form that underpins letters of credit and documentary collections, since international trade financing depends on being able to endorse and resell a bill while goods are still at sea. Turkish courts examine the actual wording of a given bill — including any "non-negotiable" notation — to classify it correctly, rather than relying on labels alone.

## Multiple Originals

Where multiple originals are issued, all must carry identical text and state the total number issued, and the shipper must provide the carrier a signed copy on request — formalities that support the surrender principle when several originals exist in circulation.
