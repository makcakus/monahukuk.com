TTK Art. 1221-1223 round out the grounds for terminating a freight contract, covering scenarios structurally different from loss, government action, or war: a ship needing repair, an unlisted delay-causing event, and a dispute over who actually holds the termination right.

## Ship Needs Repair Mid-Voyage: A Real Choice, Not an Automatic Outcome

Under Art. 1221, if the ship needs repair during the voyage, cargo interests may either take the cargo off — provided full freight and all other accrued claims are paid or secured — or simply wait for repairs to finish. Time-based freight simply stops accruing during the repair period, consistent with the recurring "freight follows actual service" principle applied elsewhere in the Code. For partial voyage charter and general cargo (kırkambar) contracts specifically, if cargo was unloaded during repairs, the shipper may reclaim it by paying full freight and other claims.

## Unlisted Natural Events and Delays: Generally No Effect, With a Safety Valve

Art. 1222 functions as a catch-all: delay from a natural event or other unforeseen circumstance not otherwise addressed in this part of the Code generally does not change either party's rights — unless the delay defeats the contract's specific purpose entirely. Where a lengthy delay is expected, the shipper may unload already-loaded cargo at its own risk and cost, against adequate security and a commitment to reload on time; failing to reload means owing full freight plus any damage caused by the unloading. Where the delay stems from government action specifically, time-based freight again does not run. For partial charter and general cargo (kırkambar) contracts, this temporary-unloading right requires the other shippers' consent — since one shipper's unilateral choice could disrupt a voyage shared among several parties.

## Who Exercises the Termination Right When the Shipper Lacks Control

Art. 1223 resolves a practical question: if the shipper no longer has disposal power over the cargo — for example, after a sale mid-transit or an endorsement transferring control — the termination right belongs to whoever currently holds that disposal power, not the original shipper. The logic follows economic interest: whoever stands to gain or lose from the cargo's fate should be the one deciding whether to wait or walk away.

## Practical Note

Turkish courts have encountered fact patterns resembling both mid-voyage repair and unlisted delay (e.g., a vessel forced back to port by engine trouble or civil unrest at the destination), but reported decisions have so far resolved these on statute-of-limitations or standing grounds rather than reaching the substance of Art. 1221-1223.
