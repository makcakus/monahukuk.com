Turkish joint-stock companies can raise capital or reward stakeholders through instruments that fall short of full share ownership. TTK Art. 502-506 cover two such tools: profit-sharing certificates ("intifa senetleri") and debt instruments such as bonds, commercial paper, and convertible notes.

## Profit-Sharing Certificates Carry No Shareholder Rights

Under Art. 502, the general assembly may issue profit-sharing certificates in favor of holders of shares cancelled for cause, creditors, or others connected to the company for similar reasons — including, notably, founders. The defining limitation, set out in Art. 503, is that certificate holders can never receive shareholder rights (voting, attendance, information rights); they may only be granted economic entitlements such as a share of net profit, liquidation proceeds, or new shares.

The most common use is the "founder's certificate" rewarding those who contributed effort or services at incorporation. Turkish Court of Cassation case law treats the relationship between a founder-certificate holder and the company as contractual rather than corporate: the certificate must be provided for in the original articles of association (a later amendment cannot create one retroactively), and it cannot be cancelled, redeemed, or restricted unilaterally without the holder's consent — even in a corporate restructuring such as a demerger, where holders who lose equivalent rights in a successor company may claim compensation.

## Debt Instruments Require a General Assembly Decision

Art. 504 requires that bonds, commercial paper, asset-backed securities, and convertible/exchangeable instruments be issued by general assembly resolution (subject to the qualified majority for articles amendments), with the resolution specifying all terms and the board handling implementation. Payment must be in cash and paid in full on delivery — no in-kind or deferred consideration is allowed.

## Delegation to the Board and the Ceiling on Issuance

Art. 505 allows the general assembly to delegate this issuing authority to the board of directors for up to fifteen months, subject to the same qualified-majority requirement. Art. 506 caps the total amount of such debt instruments at the sum of the company's capital and balance-sheet reserves (plus any permitted revaluation funds), ensuring debt issuance stays proportionate to the company's equity base; capital markets legislation applies in parallel for publicly traded companies.

## Why This Matters for Foreign Investors

Investors structuring incentive plans for Turkish founders or evaluating a Turkish counterparty's bond issuance should check that founder certificates were provided for in the original articles — not added later — and that any debt instrument program stayed within the statutory ceiling and received proper general assembly authorization.
