One of the practical matters foreigners settling in Türkiye must resolve in their first months is how long they may keep driving on their existing licence. The answer is set out plainly in Article 88 of the Highway Traffic Regulation, and it differs for foreigners and for Turkish citizens. Missing the deadline brings not only an administrative fine but serious exposure on the insurance and liability side.

## The Six-Month Period for Foreigners

Subparagraph (b) of Article 88 of the Highway Traffic Regulation is unambiguous: foreigners may drive on licences obtained abroad **for a maximum of six months from the date of entry into the country**. At the end of six months, driving in Türkiye requires that the foreign licence **be exchanged for a Turkish licence**.

That the period runs from the "date of entry" matters in two respects. First, it is tied to the entry stamp in the passport, not to the start date of the residence permit. Second, leaving and re-entering the country does not restart it; the provision is designed to move drivers who have become established in Türkiye onto the national document, and treating exits as resetting the clock is not a defensible reading. Where residence exceeds six months, conversion is unavoidable.

These periods apply **subject to the provisions of the relevant laws and of the bilateral and multilateral agreements to which Türkiye is a party**. International road traffic conventions and mutual recognition agreements may provide a different regime for particular document types, so the assessment must be made by nationality and document type.

## The Two-Year Period for Turkish Citizens and Dual Nationals

Subparagraph (a) of the same article sets a different rule for Turkish citizens: licences obtained abroad may be used **for a maximum of two years** from the date of entry. At the end of two years the document must be exchanged for a Turkish licence.

This subparagraph carries an additional intermediate duty: Turkish citizens **present in Türkiye for more than six months** must carry, together with the licence, a **Turkish translation** certified by a Turkish mission abroad or by a notary. This rule is frequently overlooked in practice by dual nationals and [Blue Card holders](/en/articles/former-turkish-citizens-blue-card-rights); a person holding Turkish citizenship who passes the six-month mark does not discharge the obligation merely by carrying the foreign document.

## Where the Conversion Application Is Made

The second part of Article 88 governs the procedure in two sentences: **the application may be made to any civil registry office** (*nüfus müdürlüğü*), and the information and documents that must be produced are determined by the General Directorate of Civil Registration and Nationality.

Two practical consequences follow. First, there is no restriction tying the application to the district of residence; any civil registry office may be approached. Second, because the required documents are not itemised in the Regulation, the administration may update them; the current list should be confirmed before applying. In practice, the original foreign licence with a certified Turkish translation, passport, valid residence permit, foreigner identification number, a medical report, biometric photographs, a blood group declaration, and the fee receipt are typically requested. On completion, the foreign document is as a rule surrendered to the administration; whether it is returned to the holder or sent to the issuing state's authority follows the procedure the administration sets.

The class of the foreign licence is assessed against the corresponding Turkish licence classes. Where no equivalent exists, or where the class requires a separate qualification in Türkiye, direct conversion may not be possible; the relevant class must then be obtained under the general rules.

## A Little-Known Limit: The Ban on Commercial Driving

Article 92 of the Regulation imposes a restriction often overlooked. While the foreign licences of persons with diplomatic immunity serving in Türkiye, of foreign technical and administrative staff, and of **foreign nationals and stateless persons residing under a residence permit** are exchanged for the corresponding Turkish document under Article 88, those persons **may not drive vehicles registered in Türkiye for gain or for commercial purposes**.

The provision shows that conversion alone does not enable a foreign driver to work in Türkiye as a taxi, shuttle, or haulage driver. Because commercial driving is a professional activity, a [work permit](/en/articles/work-permit-application-process-turkey) and the professional competence certificates required by the relevant legislation come into play as well. Overlooking this distinction exposes both the driver and the employer to sanctions.

## The Penalty for Overrunning

Driving on a foreign licence after the six-month period has run is treated in law as driving without a licence. Article 36 of Highway Traffic Law No. 2918 provides an administrative fine for driving a motor vehicle without holding a licence; in the third paragraph, as amended by Law No. 7574 of 12 February 2026, that amount is set at 40,000 Turkish lira. The same paragraph imposes a fine of the same amount, assessed against the registration plate, on the operator who permits the vehicle to be driven. Because the amounts in the Law are revalued at the beginning of each calendar year, the current figure should be verified against the tariff in force.

The insurance dimension is more serious than the fine. Driving without a valid licence is typically an exclusion under comprehensive motor policies, and under compulsory motor third-party liability insurance the insurer may recover from the driver after indemnifying the injured party. In the event of an accident, the financial consequence can far exceed the administrative fine.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does the six-month period restart each time I leave and re-enter the country?

No. The Regulation counts the period "from the date of entry into the country" and is aimed at moving drivers established in Türkiye onto the national document. Treating short exits as resetting the clock is not a safe reading; where residence exceeds six months, conversion should be carried out.

### If I hold an international driving permit, must I still convert?

Article 36 of Law No. 2918 allows driving by persons holding a licence under multilateral agreements or a valid international driving permit. But the time limits in Article 88 of the Regulation apply subject to those agreements. For long-term residence, conversion is the safe course rather than reliance on an international permit.

### Is a residence permit required for conversion?

The Regulation leaves the required documents to the General Directorate of Civil Registration and Nationality, and in practice a valid residence permit and foreigner identification number are requested from foreigners. Conversion is not practically available to someone in tourist status — and the six-month rule means the need does not arise.

### I have converted my licence — can I now drive commercially?

No, conversion alone is not sufficient. Article 92 of the Regulation provides that foreign nationals residing under a residence permit may not drive vehicles registered in Türkiye for gain or for commercial purposes. Commercial driving additionally requires a work permit and professional competence.
