Almost everyone who applies to mediation asks the same first question: "what is this going to cost me?" The answer is not a single figure, because the fee depends on three variables: whether a settlement is reached, how long the session lasts, and the type of dispute.

This article explains the rule by which the fee is calculated and the situations in which you pay nothing at all.

## How Is the Fee Determined?

The mediation fee is not a freely negotiated price; it is set by the **Mediation Minimum Fee Tariff**, prepared each year by the Ministry of Justice and approved by the Mediation Board. The Tariff is published in the Official Gazette at the beginning of each year and current amounts should be checked there.

The statutory rule is this: unless otherwise agreed, the mediator's fee is determined according to the Tariff **in force on the date the activity ends**, and the fee and expenses are borne equally by the parties. It is not the tariff at the date of application but the tariff at the date the final minutes are drawn up that applies — a difference that matters for processes running close to the turn of the year.

The Tariff has two parts, and the outcome of the process determines which applies:

- **Part One — hourly fee.** Applies where no settlement is reached. Different hourly rates are provided according to the type of dispute (employment, commercial, consumer, lease and others).
- **Part Two — proportional fee.** Applies where a settlement is reached and is calculated **as a percentage of the amount agreed**; the rate falls in bands as the amount rises.

The practical consequence of this distinction surprises most people: **settling costs more than not settling.** In a high-value claim the proportional fee can reach a significant figure once a settlement is reached. That cost should be calculated before entering the negotiation and taken into account in fixing the settlement amount.

## If You Do Not Settle: In Most Cases You Pay Nothing

This is the least known feature of mandatory mediation. The Law places the fee on the **budget of the Ministry of Justice** in three situations:

- where the parties cannot be reached,
- where no session can be held because the parties did not attend,
- where there is a failure to settle after sessions lasting **less than two hours**.

In these cases the amount of a two-hour fee is paid from the Ministry's budget according to Part One of the Tariff. Since in practice most mandatory mediation sessions do not exceed two hours, parties who fail to settle usually pay nothing at all.

If the session exceeds two hours, only the fee relating to the **excess** is, unless otherwise agreed, borne equally by the parties.

## If You Settle: The Proportional Fee and a Two-Hour Floor

Where a settlement is reached the fee is calculated under Part Two of the Tariff and, unless otherwise agreed, is borne **equally** by the parties. The Law also sets a floor: in that case the fee **cannot be less than the amount of the two-hour fee** determined under Part One.

The phrase "unless otherwise agreed" matters. The parties may agree in the settlement document that the whole fee is borne by one side. Putting the mediation fee on the table while discussing the amount to be paid prevents later argument.

## The Fee Paid Counts as a Litigation Cost

Whether paid from the Ministry's budget or borne by the parties, the mediation fee **counts as a litigation cost**. The 10th Chamber of the Council of State has stated the purpose of this rule expressly: to ensure that, in an action brought after mediation has produced no result, the mediation fee is added to the litigation costs in the calculation.

The practical consequence: if you win the case, the mediation fee you paid is also charged to the other side. The 9th Civil Chamber of the Court of Cassation treats the failure to rule on this point as a ground for reversal, precisely because the mediation fee is a litigation cost.

By contrast, awarding a mediation fee in a file where no application to mediation was ever made is erroneous.

## No Fee Arises in a Dispute Not Capable of Mediation

If the mediator ends the process without holding any meeting, having found that the dispute is not capable of mediation, they are not entitled to a fee. The 9th Civil Chamber accepts this result on the basis of the relevant provision of the Mediation Minimum Fee Tariff.

## Necessary Expenses Are Separate

Fee and expenses are not the same thing. Items such as service of documents, travel, accommodation, room hire and interpreter costs are independent of the fee, and the mediator may claim them separately; they may also request an advance.

In mandatory mediation, the necessary expenses that the office must incur are covered from the budget of the Ministry of Justice — to be paid by the parties in accordance with the settlement if one is reached, and to be collected from the party who is ultimately unsuccessful if no settlement is reached.

## Special Protection for Consumers

In consumer disputes the regime is weighted in the consumer's favour. Under Article 73/A of the Turkish Law on the Protection of Consumers, in **all** situations — parties who cannot be reached, non-attendance, **settlement** and failure to settle — the mediation fee payable by the consumer is covered from the budget of the Ministry of Justice. The limit is the amount of the two-hour fee.

If the case is decided in the consumer's favour, the fee covered from the budget is collected from the defendant under the provisions of Law No. 6183 and recorded as budget revenue.

## If You Cannot Afford It: Legal Aid

A party who needs legal aid to meet the mediation fee may benefit from it by decision of **the civil court of peace at the place where the mediation office is located**. Articles 334 to 340 of the Turkish Code of Civil Procedure apply by analogy.

The request is directed not to the court in which the action will be brought but to the civil court of peace of the place where the mediation office is located, and must be supported by documents showing the applicant's financial position.

## The Mediator May Not Take a Fee for Referrals

The Law prohibits a mediator from receiving a fee in return for acting as an intermediary for, or recommending, particular persons, and treats transactions contrary to this prohibition as **void**. A mediator who recommends a lawyer, expert, appraiser or consultant to you and derives a benefit in return acts unlawfully — and this also constitutes a circumstance casting doubt on their impartiality.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q: Who pays the mediation fee, and when?

Where a settlement is reached the fee is as a rule paid equally by the parties at the end of the process. Where no settlement is reached, the portion up to two hours is covered from the budget of the Ministry of Justice and is not collected from the parties. The mediator is also entitled to request an advance for fees and expenses.

### Q: I made the application — must I pay the whole fee?

No. Who made the application does not affect the sharing of the fee; the statutory rule is that, unless otherwise agreed, the fee and expenses are borne **equally** by the parties.

### Q: Does the fee rise as the settlement amount rises?

Yes. Where a settlement is reached the fee is calculated proportionally on the amount agreed under Part Two of the Tariff; although the rate falls in bands, the absolute amount rises. For high-value settlements the fee should therefore be discussed as part of the negotiation.

### Q: Is my lawyer's fee included in the mediation fee?

No. The mediation fee is paid to the mediator; the attorney's fee is entirely separate and is determined by the Minimum Attorney Fee Tariff and the retainer agreement. In addition, half of the attorney's fee determined under that Tariff is awarded against a party who fails to attend the first meeting without excuse, in favour of the other party.

### Q: What happens if I do not pay the mediation fee?

The fee is a claim the mediator may assert against the parties and may be pursued under the general rules. In mandatory mediation, where no settlement is reached, an unpaid fee is in addition ruled on as a litigation cost and collected from the unsuccessful party.
