TTK Art. 1068-1075 govern the "gemi müdürü" (ship manager) — the officer through whom a joint shipping ownership actually functions day to day, since gathering every co-owner for routine decisions is impractical.

## Appointment and Removal

A ship manager is appointed by the value-weighted majority described in our companion article — but appointing someone who is *not* a co-owner requires unanimity, reflecting how significant it is to hand daily control to an outsider with no ownership stake (Art. 1068). The manager can always be removed by majority vote, though any contractual claims arising from that removal (e.g., early-termination compensation) survive it (Art. 1068(2)). Both appointment and removal must be entered on the trade and ship registries.

## Scope of Authority

The manager's authority covers "ordinary business" broadly — outfitting and maintaining the ship, entering into carriage contracts, and arranging insurance (Art. 1070). Notably, the captain answers only to the ship manager's instructions, not to any individual co-owner acting alone — a rule that keeps operational command unified and prevents conflicting instructions from paralyzing the vessel at sea. But four categories require special authorization regardless: bills of exchange, borrowing money, and selling or mortgaging the ship or shares in it (Art. 1070(4)) — all high-risk, often irreversible acts. Extraordinary repairs and appointing or dismissing the captain likewise require a prior co-owner resolution rather than the manager's unilateral decision (Art. 1069).

## Protecting Third Parties, Protecting Co-Owners

Rights and obligations from the manager's authorized acts belong to the ownership entity, not the manager personally (Art. 1071). Internal restrictions on the manager's statutory authority bind third parties only if they actually knew of the restriction at the time (Art. 1072) — a classic good-faith-reliance rule keeping commerce with the entity fast and safe. In exchange, the manager owes a "prudent shipowner" standard of care (Art. 1073), must keep the entity's books separately from any other business (Art. 1074), and must give any co-owner, on request, access to accounts and records — with a further nuance: majority approval of the manager's accounting does not extinguish a dissenting co-owner's individual right to object (Art. 1075).

## Practical Takeaway

For lenders or counterparties dealing with a Turkish joint shipping ownership, verifying the registered manager's signing authority — solo or joint — before contracting is essential, since undisclosed internal limits will not protect a good-faith counterparty from being bound.
