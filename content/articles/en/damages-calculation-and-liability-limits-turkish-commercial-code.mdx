Once a Turkish carrier is found liable under Art. 875-879, the next and often more consequential question is how much it actually owes. TTK Art. 880-887 answer that with a valuation method, a hard kilogram-based cap, and one important exception that removes the cap entirely.

## Valuing the Loss

Art. 880 uses a replacement-value method for total loss (the goods' value at the time and place of pickup) and a diminution-in-value method for damage (the gap between undamaged and damaged value at pickup) — critically, a later invoice for replacement goods is not the correct measure; Turkish courts have reversed awards that used post-loss repair or replacement costs instead. Art. 881 adds the cost of establishing the loss (inspection, expert fees) on top of the base award.

## The 8.33 SDR Cap

Art. 882 caps the total award under Art. 880-881 at 8.33 Special Drawing Rights per kilogram of gross weight — a figure borrowed directly from the CMR Convention — regardless of how much higher the actual loss is. Delay-only damages are capped separately at three times the freight (Art. 882(3)). Beyond this, Art. 883 lets the sender recover the freight itself and related duties, and Art. 884 sets a distinct three-times-total-loss-value cap for consequential losses unrelated to the physical loss, damage, or delay of the goods (misdelivery, for instance). Art. 885 extends all of these caps to tort-based claims as well — a claimant cannot sidestep the SDR cap simply by pleading negligence instead of breach of contract — and extends carrier protection to third-party tort claims too, subject to narrow exceptions.

## When the Cap Disappears

Art. 886 removes every exemption ground and every liability cap where the carrier (or an Art. 879 auxiliary) caused the loss through intent or through reckless conduct undertaken with awareness that harm was likely. Turkish courts read this threshold narrowly — gross negligence alone, even a driver's clear fault in causing an accident, has repeatedly been held insufficient; the claimant must show the carrier consciously disregarded a known risk. Art. 887 extends the same protection (and the same narrow exception) to auxiliaries sued directly in tort.

## Practical Takeaway

A foreign cargo owner should expect that, absent proof of genuinely reckless conduct, a Turkish carrier's exposure is capped at roughly 8.33 SDR per kilogram — a figure that can fall well short of a high-value shipment's actual worth, making supplemental cargo insurance the more reliable protection.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How is the carrier's liability in damages limited?

Under Article 882/1 TTK the damages payable for total loss of or damage to the consignment are limited to <b>an amount corresponding to 8.33 Special Drawing Rights (SDR) per kilogramme of the gross weight of the consignment</b>. For partial loss or damage the limit is calculated on the weight of the affected part. The lira equivalent of the SDR is determined at the rate on the day of payment.

### On what value are the damages calculated?

Under Article 880/1 TTK the damages are calculated by reference to <b>the value of the goods at the place and time at which they were taken over for carriage</b> — not their value at the place of delivery or on the date of the action. The value is determined by the exchange price, failing which the market price, failing which the ordinary value of goods of the same kind and quality. The carrier also bears, under Article 881, the expenses necessarily incurred in establishing the loss and, under Article 883, the freight and the taxes and duties relating to the carriage.

### When can the carrier not rely on the limit of liability?

Under Article 886/1 TTK a carrier — and the persons referred to in Article 879 — who is <b>proved</b> to have caused the loss by an act or omission committed <b>intentionally</b> or <b>recklessly and with knowledge that such loss would probably result</b>, cannot rely on the exonerations and limits of liability provided in this Part. The burden of proof lies on the claimant who seeks to displace the limit.
