Employee claims are the field where mandatory mediation was first applied and where it remains most heavily used. Since 1 January 2018, almost every employee claim — from severance pay to overtime — must pass through the mediation stage before going to court.

This guide explains how the process should be managed on both the employee's and the employer's side, the most frequent mistakes, and which settlements the Court of Cassation treats as invalid.

## Which Claims Are in Scope?

Article 3 of Labour Courts Law No. 7036 keeps the scope broad: actions brought for **employee or employer claims and compensation** based on the law or on an individual or collective employment contract, and for **reinstatement**. With the 2023 amendment, actions for **removal of objection, negative declaration and restitution** relating to those claims were added.

Typical items within scope:

- severance pay and notice pay;
- wages, bonuses, premiums and additional payments;
- overtime, weekly rest and national/public holiday pay;
- annual leave pay;
- bad-faith compensation and discrimination (unequal treatment) compensation;
- reinstatement claims;
- the employer's claims against the employee (training costs, damages, contractual penalty).

The scope is also clarified as to persons: journalists subject to Press Labour Law No. 5953 and seafarers subject to Maritime Labour Law No. 854 are likewise treated as employees for the purposes of this article.

## What Falls Outside the Scope

**Occupational accident and occupational disease compensation.** Article 3/3 of Law No. 7036 expressly excludes them: pecuniary and non-pecuniary damages arising from an occupational accident or occupational disease, and the related declaratory, objection and recourse actions, are not within the scope of mandatory mediation. Such actions may be brought directly.

The parties may nonetheless go to **voluntary** mediation in these disputes as well, and any settlement reached is fully binding.

**Actions for the determination of service.** These actions, to which the Social Security Institution is a party and which concern public policy, are not amenable to mediation.

## The Timetable of the Process

The mediator must conclude the process within **three weeks** of the date of appointment; in compulsory circumstances this may be extended by a maximum of one week.

In the period from the application until the date the final minutes are drawn up, **limitation is suspended and no forfeiture period runs**. This is decisive for employee claims subject to the five-year limitation period, and particularly for the one-month application period in reinstatement claims.

## Before the Session: Do Not Sit Down Without the Calculation

A decision taken at the mediation table cannot be undone; so there is only one thing that must be done before the session: **a documentary calculation of every item.**

Documents needed for the calculation:

- the employment contract and any annexes;
- entry and exit notifications and the SSI service record (to establish actual length of service);
- payslips and bank account movements (to establish the gross "all-inclusive" wage);
- attendance records and workplace entry–exit records (for overtime);
- the leave register or leave request forms;
- the notice of termination and proof of service.

The critical point is the correct calculation of the **gross all-inclusive wage**: severance pay is calculated not on the bare wage but on the all-inclusive wage, which adds continuing payments such as transport, meals and bonuses. Where the wage shown on the payslip differs from the wage actually paid, proving that difference is a separate matter.

The distribution of the burden of proof also shapes the negotiation: for items such as termination, overtime and annual leave, a significant part of the burden lies on the employer. For the employee's side, that is negotiating power.

## The Grave Consequence of Settling: The Release Effect

Where a settlement is reached in mediation, **no action may be brought again concerning the matters agreed**. In employee claims this produces, in practice, the effect of a release.

The most technical task in the settlement document is therefore the drafting of its scope:

- a general phrase such as "all employee claims" also closes off an item not thought of at the time;
- if the items are enumerated one by one, the judicial route remains open for those not listed;
- where items are deliberately excluded (occupational accident compensation, for instance), this must be stated **expressly** in the document.

A frequently seen arrangement in practice is settling on severance and notice pay while overtime and leave claims are never mentioned in the document. Whether an action may then be brought for the unmentioned items turns on the wording of the document — and the only way to prevent that argument from the outset is to enumerate the items separately.

## Settlements the Court of Cassation Treats as Invalid

The 9th Civil Chamber of the Court of Cassation consistently treats a group of "mediation settlements" drawn up in employee claims as invalid. Their common features are:

**Timing.** Minutes drawn up on the day the employment contract ended, or immediately afterwards. In one case it was established that the minutes appointing the mediator, of the first session and of the final session had **all been drawn up on the same day**.

**Absence of a genuine dispute.** Documents drawn up when no dispute had yet arisen and without any application by the employee.

**Release character.** Texts that are not the product of negotiation, prepared unilaterally and put to the employee for signature.

The Chamber treats such documents as not amenable to mediation, on the ground that they are drawn up at a time when the parties **cannot freely dispose of their rights**. The result favours the employee: the document does not benefit from the bar on bringing an action, and the amount paid is merely **set off** against the claim awarded.

Another ground of invalidity concerns the mediator's conduct. The Chamber has held that, in a case where the mediator acted against a party, the text drawn up did not qualify as a valid mediation settlement document and had to be assessed under the conditions in Article 420 of the Turkish Code of Obligations or the provisions on performance.

## Fees and Costs

Where no settlement is reached and the session did not exceed two hours, the two-hour fee is paid **from the budget of the Ministry of Justice**; it is not collected from the parties. Where a settlement is reached, the fee is calculated proportionally on the agreed amount and, unless otherwise agreed, met equally by the parties.

This is a cost to watch in employee claims: in a high-value settlement the proportional fee can reach a significant figure and reduce the net amount received. Who bears the fee should be part of the negotiation.

## Notes for the Employer's Side

**Attendance is compulsory.** An employer who fails without valid excuse to attend the first meeting is liable, even if successful in the action, for half of the other side's litigation costs and half of the attorney's fee determined under the Minimum Attorney's Fee Tariff.

**The representative's authority.** If a settlement is to be signed, the attending person must hold a power of attorney including the powers **to settle and to grant a release**.

**Have the documents ready.** For items where the burden of proof lies mainly on the employer (overtime, annual leave, grounds for termination), collating the documents before the session makes it possible to set a realistic negotiating limit.

**Watch the timing.** Documents drawn up on the same day as the termination and functioning as a release are treated as invalid on review by the Court of Cassation, and the amount paid is merely set off. For the employer, that means paying the money and facing the action as well.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q: I settled in mediation — can I later bring an action for overtime?

It depends on the scope of the settlement document. If overtime pay is expressly enumerated in the document, no action may be brought. If only severance and notice pay were listed and overtime was never mentioned, the judicial route may remain open; but where the document contains a general phrase such as "all claims", that claim too is regarded as closed.

### Q: What happens if the employer does not come to the mediation?

Where no session could be held because the parties did not attend, the mediator terminates the activity and draws up the final minutes. Those minutes satisfy the condition of action; the non-attending employer is identified in them and faces the sanction as to litigation costs and attorney's fees.

### Q: If I increase my claim by amendment, must I go back to mediation?

No fresh application is needed to increase, by amendment, the amount of a claim item already advanced in the mediation application. By contrast, if a **new claim item** never advanced in the mediation is to be added, the condition of action must be separately satisfied for that item.

### Q: Must I apply again for a claim already discussed in voluntary mediation?

No. In a case where the severance-pay claim appeared in the minutes of a voluntary mediation settlement, the 9th Civil Chamber of the Court of Cassation stated that the **mediation application condition must be regarded as satisfied** for that claim, and that dismissing the action on procedural grounds by requiring a further mandatory-mediation application was erroneous.

### Q: Must I go to mediation for occupational accident compensation?

No, it is not a condition of action; you may bring the case directly. The parties may nonetheless resort to voluntary mediation and any settlement reached is binding — so the same care is required when attending such a session.

### Q: Do mediation minutes count as a release?

A valid mediation settlement document is stronger than a release: no action may be brought on the matters agreed, and in most cases the document can be put directly to enforcement. By contrast, in the cases the Court of Cassation treats as invalid, the document is not regarded as a mediation settlement and is assessed under Article 420 of the Turkish Code of Obligations and the provisions on performance — that is, like a release or a payment document.
