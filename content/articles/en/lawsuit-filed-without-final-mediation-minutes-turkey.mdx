The most common reason why actions within the scope of mandatory mediation are dismissed without the merits ever being examined is not a defect touching the substance of the dispute: it is **the failure to file the final minutes in due form**. Even a party who has run the process faultlessly, attended the session and obtained the minutes recording the failure to settle can lose the case because those minutes were filed in the wrong form.

## Two Different Defects, Two Different Outcomes

The Law distinguishes two situations and their sanctions differ.

**If no mediator was applied to at all:** without any further step, and **without the statement of claim being served on the other party**, the action is dismissed on procedural grounds for want of the condition precedent. The court grants no time and does not allow the defect to be cured.

**If a mediator was applied to but the final minutes were not attached to the statement of claim:** the court sends the claimant a summons warning that the final minutes must be produced within a **final one-week period**, failing which the action will be dismissed on procedural grounds. If the warning is not complied with, the action is dismissed without the statement of claim being served on the other party.

That one-week final period is **mandatory** and cannot be altered by the court.

## Why Is a Photocopy Not Enough?

The Law defines the document to be produced expressly: **the original** of the final minutes recording the failure to settle, or **a copy certified by the mediator**. A simple photocopy does not fall within that definition.

A serious divergence of view had arisen on this point between regional courts of appeal. On one view, where the action was filed through the national judicial IT system (UYAP) and the mediation documents were accessible through it, submitting a duplicate of the minutes should suffice; the judge could reach the document with a single click anyway. The opposing view relied on the wording of the statute.

The 9th Civil Chamber of the Turkish Court of Cassation ended the debate with its decision resolving the conflict of case law. Its reasoning has three steps:

**Step one:** sending a document through UYAP produces **the same legal effects** as physical submission by hand. The problem is not the electronic medium but the character of the document sent.

**Step two:** in the files at issue, the minutes sent through UYAP were **photocopies/duplicates** and therefore did not meet the statutory requirements.

**Step three:** the fact that the judge can access the document through UYAP has no bearing on the outcome. The duty to produce the document rests on **the parties and their lawyers**; since the principle of investigation of the court's own motion does not apply in employment proceedings, the burden of investigation cannot be placed on the judge. The contrary view would mean that judges must gather every piece of information and every document reachable through UYAP, which is incompatible with the existing system of adjudication.

The result: **the original of the final minutes or a copy certified by the mediator** must be filed, whether by hand or through UYAP; otherwise the action is dismissed on procedural grounds for want of the condition precedent.

## Can the Defect Be Cured Later?

Two separate questions are being conflated here.

The defect of **never having applied to mediation** cannot be cured later. The 11th Civil Chamber accepts that having applied to a mediator is by its nature a **special condition precedent that cannot be completed subsequently**, and that applying to mediation after the action has been filed has no effect on the outcome. The same Chamber has held that, where the mediation condition precedent has not been satisfied, the curable conditions in the file cannot be examined, and that this condition must be examined first and a procedural dismissal given **without even requiring the court fee to be completed**.

The defect of **not producing the final minutes**, by contrast, can be cured within limits: if the original or a certified copy is produced within the final one-week period granted by the court, the action continues. Once that period is missed there is no remedy.

## The One Softening: Actions Filed in a Court Lacking Subject-Matter Jurisdiction

The only flexibility developed by case law concerns subject-matter jurisdiction. According to the practice of the 9th Civil Chamber, even if the mediation condition precedent was not satisfied before the action was filed in a court lacking jurisdiction, the condition is deemed satisfied where a mediator was applied to and the process concluded with minutes recording a failure to settle **before the file was registered with the court having jurisdiction**.

This provides a practical rescue in files in which a decision declining jurisdiction has been given.

## A Special Safeguard in Reinstatement Claims

For reinstatement claims the Law provides a separate safety valve. Under Article 20/1 of Turkish Labour Law No. 4857: where the action is dismissed on procedural grounds because it was brought directly without applying to a mediator, **the decision is served on the parties of the court's own motion**, and a mediator may be applied to **within two weeks** of that service of the final dismissal decision.

This provision removes the risk of missing the one-month application period and applies only to reinstatement claims.

## The Content of the Minutes Is Also Reviewed

Filing the final minutes is not always enough; the minutes themselves must also have been drawn up in due form.

In a case where the date on which the final minutes were drawn up conflicted with the date of the signature on them, the 9th Civil Chamber found the minutes not to be in due form and stressed that where there is doubt about a preclusive period it must be construed **in favour of the person against whom the period runs**. In a dispute before the 3rd Civil Chamber, annulment of the minutes was sought on the grounds that the lawyer's surname was misspelt and that the file had been closed without signatures being taken and without the deficiencies being remedied.

On signature the Law allows flexibility: if the document is not signed by the parties, their legal representatives or their lawyers, **it is signed by the mediator alone, stating the reason**. The other party's refusal to sign therefore does not invalidate the minutes.

## Where Can You Obtain the Original?

The mediator must keep the documents relating to the activity and the final minutes for **five years**. They also send a copy of the final minutes to the General Directorate within one month of the termination of the activity.

For that reason, even if you have lost the original or a different lawyer attended the session, a certified copy can be requested from the mediator. Article 2/3 of the Turkish Attorneyship Law gives lawyers the right to request information and documents; the fact that a different lawyer attended the session is not accepted as a valid excuse for failing to obtain the document.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q: I could not attach the original to my statement of claim — can I file it later?

You can file it within the final one-week period granted by the court. That period is mandatory and cannot be extended by the court; if it is missed, the action is dismissed on procedural grounds.

### Q: Is it enough if the mediator sent the minutes with an electronic signature?

Sending, through UYAP, a document that meets the statutory requirements produces the same result as physical submission by hand. What is decisive is not the channel of transmission but whether the document sent is **the original or a copy certified by the mediator**.

### Q: Does a procedural dismissal create res judicata?

No. The dismissal is on procedural grounds and does not create res judicata on the merits; the action can be brought again once the mediation process has been completed. But the time lost, the court fees and — for claims subject to preclusive periods — irrecoverable losses are real.

### Q: Are minutes still drawn up if the other party never attended?

Yes. Where the parties cannot be reached or no session can be held because they did not attend, the mediator likewise ends the activity and draws up the final minutes. Those minutes satisfy the condition precedent; the non-attending party is also named in them.

### Q: Can I challenge a dismissal given in an action brought without mediation?

An appeal may be brought against a procedural dismissal. But if no application to mediation was made at all, the outcome will not change on appeal either; the condition is regarded as a special condition precedent that cannot be completed subsequently.
