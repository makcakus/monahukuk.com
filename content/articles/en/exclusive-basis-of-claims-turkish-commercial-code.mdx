A single sentence, TTK Art. 1268, functions as the keystone that makes every protection built in Art. 1256–1267 actually work in practice.

## The Exclusivity Rule

Art. 1268 provides that a compensation claim for a passenger's death, injury, or baggage loss/damage against the carrier or actual carrier may be brought *only* under this Chapter's provisions. The word "only" is doing real work: a claimant cannot plead the claim as an ordinary tort claim or under general contract principles to escape the Chapter's carrier-protective mechanics — the shipping-incident defenses, fault standards, SDR caps, and forfeiture threshold this series has covered all apply regardless of the legal label the claimant chooses.

## Comparison to the Cargo-Context Technique

Interestingly, the cargo-context equivalent (Art. 1190, covered earlier in the series) uses a different technique to reach the same practical result: rather than forbidding alternative legal theories outright, Art. 1190 simply *extends the same defenses and limits* to whatever theory the claimant chooses. Art. 1268 goes one step further — the alternative theory itself is foreclosed. Put simply, Art. 1190 says "plead whatever theory you like, but the outcome will not change"; Art. 1268 says "you cannot plead an alternative theory at all." Both achieve the same end — preventing an artful choice of legal theory from unraveling the carrier-liability architecture — through different drafting techniques.

## Scope: Not Unlimited

The exclusivity rule is confined to claims for death, injury, or baggage loss/damage. A claim outside those three categories — for example, damage to a vehicle carried under a separate freight contract, which Art. 1250 excludes from the "baggage" definition — may fall outside Art. 1268's reach and remain subject to its own separate regime. Similarly, a claim by someone who does not meet the Art. 1249 definition of "passenger" may not be covered either. No confirmed case law testing these specific boundary scenarios has been identified, and this is flagged transparently.

## Case Law

The 2026 Istanbul Regional Court of Appeal decision (12th Civil Chamber, 2026/420-2026/757, not yet final) cites Art. 1268 directly while rejecting a standing objection, confirming that the underlying bodily-injury claim against the carrier, operator, captain, and actual carrier had to proceed under this Chapter's own regime rather than general tort law.

## Practical Takeaway

For claimants and their counsel, framing a claim in tort or another alternative theory offers no escape from the SDR caps and defenses this Chapter establishes — case strategy should be built directly around Art. 1256 and following provisions.
