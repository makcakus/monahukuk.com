TTK Art. 1174-1177 close out the loading-and-discharge chapter by addressing what happens when discharge simply doesn't happen, and by extending the discharge rules to partial charters and layered general cargo arrangements.

## Why Discharge Failure Isn't Handled Like Loading Failure

Unlike a shipper's failure to load — which triggers the carrier's contractual cancellation and damages rights under Art. 1158-1160 — a consignee's failure to take delivery is routed to the general creditor-default provisions of the Turkish Code of Obligations (TBK Art. 107-109). Art. 1174 explains why: the consignee is often a third party who acquired delivery rights through an endorsed bill of lading, not the carrier's actual contracting counterparty. "Cancelling the contract" against someone who was never a party to it makes no conceptual sense, so the law instead gives the carrier general deposit/consignment remedies that work regardless of any contractual relationship.

## The Carrier's Obligations When the Consignee Won't Cooperate

Under Art. 1174, if the consignee — having said it's ready to collect — still fails to take full delivery within discharge time (and demurrage time, if any), the carrier can invoke TBK's creditor-default remedies after notifying the consignee. The same applies if the consignee refuses delivery outright, fails to respond to a readiness notice, or can't be located — but here the carrier must also notify the shipper, since the shipper remains the party with the real contractual stake. Financially, the carrier can claim demurrage for delay within discharge time (payable by the shipper, per the prior article) and full damages for delay beyond it.

## Partial Charters and Layered General Cargo Contracts

Art. 1175 applies the discharge rules (Art. 1168-1174) separately to each contract when several shippers charter distinct parts of a ship — but drops the cross-consent requirement found on the loading side, since one shipper's discharge doesn't risk delaying another's voyage. Art. 1176 extends the general cargo loading rule to discharge: the consignee must take delivery without delay on notice, with public notice by local custom substituting when the consignee is unknown — a practical accommodation for cargo whose bill of lading may have changed hands. Art. 1177 confirms that when a charterer sublets space through general cargo contracts with third parties, the carrier's rights and obligations toward the head charterer remain governed by Art. 1168-1174 regardless — privity of contract is preserved up the chain.
