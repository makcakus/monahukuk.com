*Article 8 – (1) The mediator may meet and communicate with each of the parties separately or together. (Second sentence repealed: 12/10/2017-7036/20) (…)*

Reduced to a single sentence, this provision forms the backbone of mediation technique. The mediator's ability to meet the parties **separately** — known in practice as the private session or *caucus* — is the most distinctive procedural feature separating mediation from litigation and arbitration. A judge cannot meet one party in the absence of the other; a mediator can, because the mediator will render no decision.

The second sentence of the provision was repealed in 2017 by Law No. 7036. The repealed sentence concerned the mediator's duty to inform the parties and to observe equality in the process; since those duties are already regulated in Articles 9 and 11, it was a repetition.

## The Function and Rules of the Private Session

The private session allows a party to tell the mediator what they could not say in the presence of the other side: their real bargaining limit, the weak points of the case as they see them, and the commercial or personal factors that make settlement difficult. The mediator uses that information to see whether there is a genuine zone of overlap between the parties' positions.

The private session has two rules, both flowing from the general principles of the Law.

**The confidentiality rule.** Information learned in a private session cannot be conveyed to the other party without the express permission of the party concerned. This is the natural consequence of the confidentiality duty imposed on the mediator by Article 4/1. In practice the mediator asks at the end of each private session, "which part of this information may I share with the other side?"; counsel must have thought through the answer to that question in advance.

**The equality rule.** Under Article 9/3 the mediator is obliged to observe equality between the parties. Holding repeated private sessions with one party without offering the same opportunity to the other damages the appearance of impartiality. In practice mediators take care to keep the number and length of private sessions balanced.

## The Power to Meet Separately Is Not an Obligation

Article 8/1 confers a **power** on the mediator; it does not impose a method. Depending on the nature of the dispute, the mediator may conduct the entire process in joint session, or conduct it from start to finish through private sessions. That choice is the mediator's own under Article 15/3: where the parties have not agreed a procedure, the mediator conducts the activity having regard to the nature of the dispute, the parties' wishes and the procedure and principles required for a swift resolution.

By contrast, under Article 15/2 the parties may freely agree the mediation procedure, provided it does not conflict with mandatory rules of law. They may also agree that no private sessions will be held; this follows from the default character of Article 8.

## The Power to Communicate in Mandatory Mediation

The expression "communicate" in Article 8 is given concrete form in mandatory mediation by Articles 18/A/6-7 and turns into an **obligation** rather than a power.

The applicant party gives the mediation bureau every kind of contact information relating to itself and, if available, to the other side; the bureau is also empowered to research the parties' contact information in official records, and the relevant institutions and organisations are obliged to provide it. That information is given by the bureau to the appointed mediator. The mediator takes it as a basis and, where necessary, **may also research on their own initiative**; using every means of communication available on the basis of the information at hand, the mediator informs the parties of the appointment and invites them to the first meeting.

A sentence added in 2023 by Law No. 7445 introduced an important safeguard: the mediator **also informs the party themselves even where they have a lawyer**. That addition aims to prevent processes conducted through a representative of which the party is unaware.

The final sentence is decisive as to the burden of proof: the mediator **documents** the steps taken as to information and invitation. Proving that the invitation was made lies with the mediator; where there is no invitation document in the file, the sanction of litigation costs and attorney's fees in Article 18/A/11 cannot be awarded on the ground that the party did not attend the meeting.

## Who Bears the Risk of Not Being Invited

A defence frequently encountered in practice is the other side's assertion that "I was not aware of the mediation session, I was not duly invited". The consequence of that defence differs according to who raises it.

The 9th Civil Chamber of the Court of Cassation has stated that even where it is accepted that the employer was not duly invited to the meeting by the mediator, reaching the parties and inviting them to the meeting is **the mediator's responsibility**; accordingly, no fault attributable to an employee who applied to the mediation bureau in accordance with the statutory provision can be spoken of. That case law is decisive as to whether the condition of action is regarded as fulfilled: a party who made the application in due form cannot be held responsible for a defect in the mediator's invitation procedure, and their action cannot be dismissed on procedural grounds for that reason.

By contrast, the result differs for the party asserting that they were not invited: if it cannot be documented that the invitation was made in due form, the non-attendance sanction cannot be applied to that party.

## Case Law of the Court of Cassation and the Council of State

The following decisions show the limits of the mediator's power and duty to communicate with the parties.

**1. Court of Cassation, 9th Civil Chamber, Case No. 2022/3398, Decision No. 2022/5294, dated 26.04.2022**

The Chamber stated that the mediator informs the parties of the appointment and invites them to the first meeting using the contact information provided by the bureau or established by the mediator; and emphasised that, even if it is accepted that the employer was not duly invited, reaching the parties and making the invitation is the mediator's responsibility, so that no fault attributable to the employee who applied in due form can be spoken of.

**2. Court of Cassation, 11th Civil Chamber, Case No. 2021/8659, Decision No. 2023/2668, dated 03.05.2023**

The decision addresses, together with the provisions of the Law and the Regulation, the mediator's obligation to inform the parties and invite them to the first meeting using every means of communication and to document these steps, and establishes that the contact information is provided to the mediator by the courthouse mediation bureau.

**3. Court of Cassation, 9th Civil Chamber, Case No. 2023/16551, Decision No. 2023/19863, dated 19.12.2023**

The Chamber stated that the mediator takes as a basis the contact information provided by the bureau, may research on their own initiative where necessary, and informs the parties and invites them to the first meeting on the basis of the information at hand. The decision assesses the powers of the bureau and of the mediator together as regards obtaining contact information.

**4. Court of Cassation, 9th Civil Chamber, Case No. 2024/7293, Decision No. 2024/9150, dated 27.05.2024**

In the dispute, assessing together the letters received from the mediation bureau and the Mediation Department, it was established that the claimant and the comparator employees had themselves selected the mediator, had attended the sessions in person, and that the claimant had exercised their will as to the mediator's selection. The decision shows the method of proving attendance and choice through official records.

**5. Council of State, 10th Chamber, Case No. 2018/3449, Decision No. 2021/1714, dated 08.04.2021**

In an annulment action against the mediation legislation, the Chamber addressed Article 8 on meeting and communicating with the parties together with the principles of confidentiality and the evidentiary bar, and set out the statutory framework of the mediator's power of communication.
