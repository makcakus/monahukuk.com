TTK Art. 1006-1011 close out the ownership chapter by extending its rules to two special cases: fractional ownership of a registered ship, and ownership of a vessel that is not yet complete.

## Shares Follow the Ship's Own Rules

Art. 1006 and 1009 apply the same rules governing acquisitive prescription and loss of a whole ship to a fractional share or a co-ownership interest in a shipping partnership (donatma iştiraki) — a share is, after all, a mathematical fraction of the same ownership right. Art. 1007 requires the same notarized written agreement for transferring a registered ship share, but drops the separate delivery-of-possession requirement, since a fractional interest cannot be physically handed over the way a whole ship can. Notably, a shipping-partnership co-owner may freely transfer their interest without the other partners' consent — a deliberate departure from typical partnership consent restrictions — except where the transfer would cause the ship to lose Turkish flag rights, in which case all partners' approval is required.

## The Dissenting Partner's Opt-Out

Art. 1008 gives shipping-partnership co-owners a distinctive escape hatch: a partner who votes against a decision to undertake a new voyage, repair the ship, or pay a maritime claim may avoid the resulting financial obligation entirely by abandoning their share — without compensation — to the other partners. The abandoning partner must notify the others by notary within just three days of the decision (or of being informed of it), and ownership of the abandoned share passes proportionally to the remaining partners the moment the notification is sent.

## Vessels Still Under Construction

Art. 1010-1011 apply a straightforward mirror rule: ownership of an unfinished ship or share follows the unregistered-ship regime if it was never entered on the special under-construction registry, and follows the full registered-ship regime — including the formal transfer requirements and prescription rules — if it was. Turkish courts have confirmed this applies even in public-tender construction disputes: partial payment or a hand-over of possession alone does not transfer ownership of a registered under-construction vessel without the formal transfer instrument required for registered ships.

## Practical Takeaway

For co-owners and shipyard financiers, the three-day window in Art. 1008 is unusually tight by Turkish contract-law standards — a dissenting partner who wants to avoid a costly voyage or repair decision has almost no time to act once the decision is made.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is transfer of possession required for the transfer of a ship share?

No. Under TTK Article 1007/1, ownership of a registered share in a vessel passes to the acquirer <b>upon agreement between the owner and the acquirer to that effect</b>. Unlike Article 1001, which governs the transfer of ownership of a vessel, <b>transfer of possession is not required</b> here, because it is conceptually impossible to establish actual control over a mere share. Agreement alone suffices.

### Can a co-owning shipowner abandon their share in the partnership?

Yes, for certain resolutions. Under TTK Article 1008/1, where the joint shipowning partnership resolves upon <b>a new voyage</b>, or upon <b>having the vessel repaired at the end of a voyage</b>, or upon <b>the payment of a maritime claim for which the partnership is liable</b>, each co-owning shipowner <b>who did not participate in the resolution</b> may abandon their share in the partnership <b>without claiming any compensation</b>. This is an exit right granted to the minority against being placed under a new financial burden by a majority resolution.

### Can ownership be established over a vessel under construction?

Yes. TTK Articles 1010 and 1011 divide vessels under construction into two categories according to whether they are registered in the special registry. Acquisition and loss of ownership over <b>structures and shares in structures not registered</b> in the special registry for vessels under construction are subject to the provisions applicable to unregistered vessels; <b>registered</b> structures and shares in structures are subject to the provisions applicable to registered vessels. Furthermore, under Article 1013, a shipyard owner may request registration of a mortgage over the structure for claims arising from its construction and repair.
