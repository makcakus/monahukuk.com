TTK Art. 780-787 open Turkey's third negotiable instrument: the cheque. Like a bill of exchange, a cheque is an order (havale) directing a third party to pay — but where bills and notes are credit instruments, a cheque is a payment instrument, and three special rules flow from that function: only a bank can be drawee (Art. 782), a cheque cannot be accepted (Art. 784), and an interest clause on a cheque is void (Art. 786).

## Mandatory Elements

Art. 780 requires the word "cheque," an unconditional order to pay, the drawee bank's trade name, the place of payment, the date and place of issuance, the drawer's signature, and — added in 2016 — a bank-issued serial number and a QR-style karekod. The karekod lets a payee check a bank database for the drawer's identity and recent history of unpresented, paid, or dishonored cheques before accepting one — a market-level transparency tool for reducing dishonored-cheque risk. Missing elements are largely fatal (Art. 781), though the payment place and place of issuance default to locations shown near the drawee's or drawer's name, and foreign-bank cheques are exempt from the serial-number/karekod requirement.

## Only a Bank Can Be Drawee

Art. 782 is the defining rule separating cheques from bills: in Turkey, only a bank can be drawee, requiring a prior "cheque agreement" between drawer and bank. A cheque drawn on anyone else is not a cheque at all — merely an ordinary payment order without any of kambiyo law's protections. Art. 783 governs the underlying "cover" (karşılık): while lack of sufficient funds does not affect the cheque's formal validity, a drawer who issues a cheque without adequate cover owes 10% of the unpaid amount plus the holder's actual damages — separate from criminal liability for dishonored cheques under the Cheque Law (5941), which can carry fines and a cheque-account ban.

## No Acceptance, No Interest

Art. 784 bars acceptance outright — any acceptance notation on a cheque is disregarded — because a cheque is meant to be payable on demand, not turned into a deferred credit instrument. Art. 786 similarly voids any interest clause, since a cheque is not meant to accrue value over time.

## Payee Designation and Place of Payment

A cheque may be drawn to a named payee (with or without "to order"), to a named payee marked "not to order," or to bearer (Art. 785) — three distinct transfer regimes covered in a companion article. Art. 787 allows payment through a third-party location, provided that third party is also a bank.

## Practical Takeaway

Anyone accepting a Turkish cheque should check the karekod where possible, confirm the drawee is genuinely a bank, and understand that a missing mandatory element strips the instrument of cheque status entirely, closing off kambiyo-specific enforcement.
