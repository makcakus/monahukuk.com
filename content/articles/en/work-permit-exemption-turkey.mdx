As a rule, a foreign national may work in Türkiye only after [obtaining a work permit](/en/articles/work-permit-application-process-turkey). Law No. 6735 on International Labour Force, however, provides a second route for certain categories of activity: the work permit exemption. A common misconception is that "exemption" means no formality at all. Article 13 of the Law says otherwise — foreigners within the scope of the exemption may work **provided they obtain a work permit exemption document**. The exemption is not an absence of authorisation; it is a distinct type of document.

## What the Exemption Document Actually Does

The Implementing Regulation of the International Labour Force Law defines the exemption as a document issued by the Ministry that grants the foreigner the right to work and reside in Türkiye without obtaining a work permit, for the duration of its validity. Two elements matter in practice. First, the document confers the authority to work; second, it removes the need to obtain a separate residence permit while it remains valid. For foreigners arriving for short assembly, training, trade-fair, or artistic assignments, this eliminates one of two application processes.

In return, the Law draws a significant boundary. Under Article 13(6), **time spent under a work permit exemption is disregarded when calculating statutory work permit or residence permit periods**. This excludes exemption periods entirely from the calculations underlying a [long-term residence permit](/en/articles/long-term-residence-permit-turkey-requirements) or a citizenship application. For a foreigner aiming at a permanent status in Türkiye, the exemption is convenient in the short term but accumulates no qualifying time.

## Who Falls Within the Exemption?

Article 48 of the Implementing Regulation lists the exempt categories by type of activity, each with its own maximum duration. The principal ones are:

- Foreigners working within scientific, cultural, or artistic activities — **up to one month**,
- Those arriving for the assembly, maintenance, or repair of machinery and equipment imported into Türkiye, for training in its use, or to repair vehicles that have broken down in Türkiye — **up to three months**,
- Cross-border service providers — **up to three months**,
- Non-resident board members of joint stock companies incorporated under the Turkish Commercial Code, non-managing partners of other companies, and persons authorised at the highest level to represent and bind such companies without being partners — **up to three months**,
- Persons notified by the Ministry of Interior or Foreign Affairs as being of Turkish descent — **up to three months**,
- Foreigners working within sporting activities — **up to four months**,
- Seasonal agricultural and livestock workers, and those working at fairs and circuses operating outside certified tourism establishments — **up to six months**,
- Tour operator representatives — **up to eight months**,
- Interns under international student exchange, new graduate trainee exchange, or youth exchange programmes — **up to twelve months**,
- Those coming to universities and public institutions to conduct research or broaden their experience — limited to the training period and in any event **up to two years**,
- Foreigners notified by the relevant public institutions as capable of making a significant contribution to Türkiye in economic, socio-cultural, technological, or educational fields — **up to three years**.

Further groups are exempt for the duration of their contracts: professional foreign athletes and coaches together with sports physicians, physiotherapists and similar sports personnel; foreign seafarers serving on vessels registered with the Turkish International Ship Registry operating outside cabotage lines; staff of schools, cultural and religious institutions attached to foreign diplomatic and consular missions in Türkiye; students enrolled in formal education in Türkiye who must complete a compulsory internship; and those undergoing medical or dental specialty training.

Article 13(7) of the Law separately treats as exempt the **cross-border service provider whose activities in Türkiye do not exceed ninety days within any one hundred and eighty days**.

## Where the Application Is Filed

Applications are made within Türkiye directly to the Ministry of Labour and Social Security through the electronic system, and abroad to the Turkish mission in the country of which the foreigner is a national or in which they are lawfully present. Applications filed with a mission are forwarded to the Ministry through the system. Filing through an authorised intermediary institution is also possible.

Duration operates as a threshold in the assessment: exemption applications made for three months or longer are evaluated by the Ministry. A parallel distinction applies to fees — no fee is charged for exemption applications requested for a validity period shorter than three months, leaving only the document charge.

## No Extension, and a Waiting Period Before Reapplying

This is the sharpest difference between an exemption and a work permit. Under Article 51 of the Regulation, **no extension application may be made for an exemption**. The exemption is issued for the period requested by the foreigner, within the ceilings in Article 48, and for a term sixty days shorter than the validity of the passport.

Nor is immediate reapplication permitted once the term expires. Article 53 imposes a waiting period: for exemptions granted under subparagraphs (b) and (c) of Article 48(1) — assembly, maintenance and repair, and cross-border service provision — a new application within the same exemption cannot be made until **six months** have passed from the date of issue; for all other exemptions the period is **twelve months**. Where activity in Türkiye becomes continuous, the correct route is not to repeat the exemption but to move to a work permit.

## Grounds for Refusal and Cancellation

Under Article 52, applications are refused where they are inconsistent with international labour force policy, are found to have been made with false or misleading information or documents, concern professions reserved by other laws to Turkish citizens, relate to a foreigner who is found not to fall within the exemption, or are made for a foreigner who is not to be permitted entry into Türkiye.

Cancellation is governed by Article 56: failure to enter Türkiye within six months of the exemption's start date, or failure to extend the validity of the passport, may result in cancellation. The gravest consequence appears in Article 57 — where the exemption becomes invalid for any reason, **the residence right attached to it also ends**. A foreigner present in Türkiye on the strength of an exemption document falls into irregular status once it is cancelled, unless a separate residence permit has been obtained.

The foreigner and the employer must also notify the Ministry **within fifteen days** of the start and end of work under the exemption and of any circumstance requiring cancellation. Breach of this notification duty exposes [the employer to administrative sanctions](/en/articles/employer-obligations-hiring-foreign-nationals-turkey).

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I need a separate residence permit while holding an exemption document?

No. The work permit exemption confers a right of residence alongside the right to work for its period of validity. But that residence right ends when the exemption expires or is cancelled; if you intend to remain in Türkiye, plan a separate residence permit application before the term runs out.

### Does time spent under an exemption count toward a citizenship application?

It does not. Article 13(6) of Law No. 6735 expressly provides that time spent under an exemption is disregarded in calculating statutory work permit or residence permit periods. Where an application depends on uninterrupted residence in Türkiye, a work permit or a suitable residence permit is the appropriate route.

### Can I reapply under the same exemption as soon as it expires?

No. For assembly, maintenance and repair, and cross-border service provider exemptions, six months must pass from the date of issue; for every other exemption category the waiting period is twelve months.

### I am a foreign board member of a joint stock company — do I need a work permit?

If you are not resident in Türkiye, you fall within the exemption and may operate on an exemption document for up to three months. If you reside in Türkiye and actually work in the company, a work permit rather than an exemption is required.
