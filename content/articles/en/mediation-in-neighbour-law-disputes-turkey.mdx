Neighbour disputes are one of the oldest and most stubborn fields of law. A judgment vindicates one of the parties, but the neighbourhood continues — and more often than not the dispute ends not when the judgment is enforced, but as the parties go on living side by side. Since 1 September 2023, applying to a mediator before bringing an action in these disputes has been compulsory.

## Scope: The Neighbour Law Provisions of the Civil Code

Article 18/B/1(ç) of Law No. 6325: **disputes arising from neighbour rights** fall within mandatory mediation.

The scope consists of disputes arising from the neighbour law provisions in Article 737 et seq. of the Turkish Civil Code:

- **Excessive use** (Art. 737): creating noise, vibration, odour, smoke, soot, dust, vapour and similar harmful effects in a manner that disturbs neighbours;
- **Excavation and structures** (Art. 738): excavating or erecting structures so as to damage the neighbouring property, or removing the support of neighbouring land;
- **Plants** (Art. 740): branches and roots extending onto the neighbouring land;
- **Natural flow and water** (Arts. 742–747): the obligation to tolerate flow, the right to take water and the right of conduit;
- **Rights of way** (Arts. 747–748): necessary rights of way and other passage rights;
- **Boundaries and boundary markers** (Arts. 744–746);
- claims for prevention of interference and restoration to the former state, together with the associated compensation.

## Overlap with Condominium Law

In practice a significant part of neighbour disputes also arises from Condominium Law No. 634: noise from the flat above, interference with a common area, conversion of an independent unit into a workplace.

This overlap creates no practical problem. Both types of dispute fall within Article 18/B and the result is the same: applying to a mediator before bringing an action is compulsory. The question of characterisation matters only in determining the competent court and the substantive rules to be applied. ## Why Mediation Suits This Field

Three features of neighbour disputes make mediation markedly advantageous compared with litigation.

**The relationship continues.** The parties remain neighbours after the case. A judgment vindicates one side but does not repair the relationship; often it damages it further.

**The dispute is continuous.** Noise, odour or excess is not a single event but a recurring condition. A judgment produces compensation for the past and a prohibition for the future; mediation makes it possible to establish **practical and workable** arrangements such as time restrictions, insulation works, fitting a filter or pruning a tree.

**The standard is flexible.** Article 737 defines the limit of tolerance as harmful effects "exceeding the degree that may be tolerated between neighbours according to the location and character of the property and to local custom". In court this flexible standard becomes a matter of expert reports and lengthy proceedings; in negotiation the parties can give it concrete content according to their own understanding.

## What Can Be Agreed in Mediation

Solutions a court could not order but the parties can agree upon:

- time and day restrictions for the disturbing activity;
- carrying out sound or odour insulation and sharing the cost;
- a pruning schedule for overhanging branches and who bears the cost;
- protective measures and security to be provided during excavation or construction;
- the route, width, conditions of use and price of a right of way;
- sharing the construction of a party wall, boundary marker or fence;
- compensation for past damage and a payment schedule;
- a penalty clause applicable if the breach is repeated.

The last item is particularly important: a penalty clause inserted into the mediation settlement creates, upon repetition of the breach, a directly enforceable claim and makes bringing a fresh action unnecessary.

## Drafting the Settlement Document: Amenability to Enforcement

Under Article 18/B/3, obtaining the annotation as to the enforceability of the settlement document **is compulsory**; for documents relating to immovable property the annotation is obtained from the civil court of peace of **the place where the property is located**, and for others from that of **the place where the mediator performed the duty**.

In examining the annotation, the court reviews whether the content of the settlement is amenable to mediation and to **enforcement**. That calls for particular care in neighbour settlements, because most obligations in this field are not payments of money but obligations **to do or to refrain from doing**.

Three rules for correct drafting:

**Make it concrete.** Not "I shall not disturb my neighbour", but "no renovation work shall be carried out between 22.00 and 08.00."

**Make it measurable.** Not "the tree shall be pruned", but "branches crossing the boundary line shall be pruned within 30 days and the pruning shall be repeated once a year in October."

**Attach a sanction.** Obligations to do or to refrain from doing can be enforced under Articles 30 and 343 of the Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law; but adding a penalty clause to the settlement allows proceedings based directly on a monetary claim and is far more effective in practice.

The warning of the 12th Civil Chamber of the Court of Cassation applies here too: a debtor cannot be compelled to conclude a contract on the basis of a settlement document that merely evidences a promise and contains no constitutive judicial pronouncement. In arrangements requiring the creation of a right in rem, such as a right of way, the settlement must be drafted in a form capable of registration in the land registry.

## Rights of Way: A Special Case

The necessary right of way (Art. 747) is the most technical sub-heading of neighbour disputes and offers a particular advantage in mediation.

A court determines the right of way over the route causing "the least damage" and by way of expert examination; the process is long and costly. In mediation, by contrast, the parties can agree directly on the route, the width, the conditions of use and the price.

However, a right of way is a **limited right in rem** and must be registered in the land registry. Article 17/B comes into play here: disputes concerning the creation of a limited right in rem over immovable property are amenable to mediation; once the enforceability annotation has been obtained, either party may apply to the land registry directorate for registration, and the registration is carried out **without an official deed being drawn up**.

In addition, during the process, if the parties so agree in writing and the mediator records it in the minutes, a restriction on the power of disposal may be annotated in the land register for a period not exceeding three months.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q: My neighbour makes constant noise — can I sue directly?

No. In disputes arising from neighbour rights, applying to a mediator before bringing an action is a condition of action. An action brought without satisfying this condition is dismissed on procedural grounds without an examination of the merits.

### Q: Can I also complain to the municipality or the police about noise?

Yes, and those routes are independent of the mediation requirement. The administrative sanction process for the noise misdemeanour under Article 36 of the Misdemeanours Law and the mediation process for the private-law dispute do not obstruct each other; administrative records may also be used as evidence in the later civil action.

### Q: What if my neighbour does not come to the mediation?

Where no meeting could be held because the parties did not attend, the mediator terminates the activity and draws up the final minutes; those minutes satisfy the condition of action. The non-attending neighbour is identified in the minutes and, even if successful in the action to be brought, is liable for half of the other side's litigation costs and half of the attorney's fee determined under the Minimum Attorney's Fee Tariff.

### Q: We settled but my neighbour is not keeping their word — what can I do?

A settlement document bearing the enforceability annotation is a document having the nature of a judgment and can be put to enforcement as such. Obligations to do or to refrain from doing can be enforced under Articles 30 and 343 of the Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law; if a penalty clause was included in the settlement, proceedings can be brought directly on the monetary claim.

### Q: Can I seek an interim injunction?

Yes. No mediation requirement applies to a request for an interim injunction; it may be sought directly from the court. If an injunction was obtained before the action was brought, the period for bringing the action under Article 397/1 of the Code of Civil Procedure does not run from the application to the mediation bureau until the date the final minutes are drawn up.
