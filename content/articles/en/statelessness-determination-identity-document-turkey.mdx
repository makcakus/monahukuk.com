A person who is not considered a national of any state exists in law but is invisible in practice: no passport, no identity document, no protection of any state. Turkish law has produced a specific answer to this situation. Article 50 of Law No. 6458 on Foreigners and International Protection governs the determination of statelessness and the document to be issued to the person so identified. That document provides far more than an ordinary identity card.

## Why Determining Statelessness Is a Separate Procedure

Statelessness is often confused with international protection, but it is a different concept. An [international protection](/en/articles/international-protection-asylum-application-turkey) application rests on a threat of persecution or serious harm in the country of which the person is a national — that is, a bond of nationality exists but protection does not. In statelessness, the problem is the absence of the bond itself.

The distinction has practical consequences: a stateless person is in need of protection merely because they are the national of no state, even without any claim of persecution. For that reason the law establishes the determination of statelessness as an independent procedure.

## The Legal Nature of the Stateless Person Identity Document

The second paragraph of Article 50 of Law No. 6458 defines the nature of the document by three elements.

**It is subject to no fee.** The article expressly states that the document is *subject to no fee whatsoever*. This is the legal reflection of the fact that a stateless person is often without a source of income.

**It takes the place of a residence permit.** The document does not require a separate residence permit to be obtained; it confers the right of stay itself. In this respect it differs from the [temporary protection identity document](/en/articles/temporary-protection-to-residence-permit-transition-turkey) — the temporary protection document does not take the place of a residence permit, whereas the Stateless Person Identity Document does.

**It is renewed every two years.** The power to renew rests with the governorates. The document is not open-ended; whether the condition of statelessness continues is reviewed periodically.

The same paragraph provides that the document also carries the **foreigner identification number**. The holder thereby obtains, through the document, the [foreigner identification number](/en/articles/foreigner-identification-number-turkey) required for banking, health, title deed, and tax transactions.

## Counting the Periods: The Most Important Provision of the Article

The third paragraph of Article 50 lays down the rule of greatest practical importance: **periods spent in Türkiye while holding a Stateless Person Identity Document are taken into account in the aggregation of residence periods.**

This provision is the fundamental difference separating the stateless person from holders of protection statuses. Time spent under temporary protection does not count towards the residence total, whereas time spent holding a Stateless Person Identity Document does. As a result, a stateless person can accumulate the period required for a [long-term residence permit](/en/articles/long-term-residence-permit-turkey-requirements) and ultimately for [Turkish citizenship](/en/articles/citizenship-long-term-residence).

This reveals the legislation's approach to statelessness: the aim is not to hold the person in uncertainty but to bring them to a permanent status. It is the domestic reflection of the international law principle of reducing statelessness.

## When the Document Ceases to Be Valid

The fourth paragraph governs termination: **the Stateless Person Identity Document loses its validity upon the person's acquisition of the nationality of any country.**

The provision is logical — once statelessness ends, the basis for the document falls away. But it calls for care in practice: if the person acquires Turkish citizenship the document ceases to be valid automatically; if they acquire the nationality of another country, the right of stay in Türkiye must be placed on a new basis. Where this transition is not planned, a person acquiring a nationality may briefly fall into a gap in their right of stay.

## Procedure and Principles

The fifth paragraph of the article provides that the procedure and principles concerning the determination of statelessness and the Stateless Person Identity Document are **to be determined by regulation**. The law establishes the framework and leaves the detail to administrative regulation.

The practical meaning of this is that the documents required and the method of determination may be updated by the administration. Proving a claim of statelessness is often difficult: the person is expected to establish that the states of which they might be a national do not treat them as such. This is a technical exercise in proof, conducted through letters obtained from the authorities of the states concerned, birth records, and documents evidencing previous status.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Will I pay a fee for the Stateless Person Identity Document?

No. Article 50 of Law No. 6458 expressly provides that the document is subject to no fee whatsoever.

### Does time spent holding this document count towards a citizenship application?

Yes. The third paragraph of the article provides that periods spent in Türkiye while holding the document are taken into account in the aggregation of residence periods. This is the fundamental difference separating the stateless person from a person under temporary protection.

### Do I also need to obtain a residence permit?

You do not. The document takes the place of a residence permit. However, because it must be renewed by the governorates every two years, the renewal date must be tracked.

### What happens to my document if I acquire Turkish citizenship?

The law provides that the document loses its validity upon the person's acquisition of the nationality of any country. Where Turkish citizenship is acquired the person moves to a citizen's identity card in any event, so no gap arises; where the nationality of another country is involved, the right of stay in Türkiye must be re-established.
