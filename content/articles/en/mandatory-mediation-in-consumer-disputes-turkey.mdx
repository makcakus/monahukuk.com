*LAW NO. 6502, ARTICLE 73/A – (Added: 22/7/2020-7251/59)*

*(1) In disputes heard before the consumer courts, having applied to a mediator before bringing an action is a condition of action. However, the provisions on mediation as a condition of action do not apply to the following matters: a) disputes within the competence of the consumer arbitration committee; b) objections to decisions of the consumer arbitration committee; c) the actions specified in the sixth paragraph of Article 73; d) the actions specified in Article 74; e) disputes which are in the nature of a consumer transaction and arise from a right in rem over immovable property.*

*(2) The eleventh paragraph of Article 18/A of the Law on Mediation in Civil Disputes No. 6325 is not applied against the consumer.*

*(3) Where, at the end of the mediation activity, the parties cannot be reached, no session could be held because the parties did not attend, or the parties settle or fail to settle, the mediation fee payable by the consumer is met from the budget of the Ministry of Justice. (…)*

*(4) Where the action brought at the end of the mediation activity concludes in the consumer's favour, the mediation fee is collected from the defendant under the provisions of Law No. 6183 and recorded as budget revenue.*

Added by Law No. 7251 of 22/7/2020 and in force since **28 July 2020**, Article 73/A of the Consumer Protection Law is the third link in the chain of mandatory mediation. It differs markedly from the other two links — labour and commercial disputes: its scope is defined by reference to the consumer courts' jurisdiction, and it introduces three separate balancing provisions protecting the consumer.

## Scope: The Court Criterion, Not the Type of Action

Article 73/A/1 is structurally different from Article 5/A TCC. Whereas Article 5/A TCC lists particular types of action, Article 73/A brings **all disputes heard before the consumer courts** within scope and then sets out five exceptions. The criterion is not the nature of the claim but whether the dispute falls within the jurisdiction of the consumer court.

Accordingly, under Article 73/1 of the Consumer Protection Law, every dispute that may arise from consumer transactions and from practices directed at consumers — defective goods and services, instalment sales, timeshares, package tours, subscriptions, consumer credit, housing finance, distance selling — is as a rule subject to the mediation condition.

## The Five Exceptions

**(a) Disputes within the competence of the consumer arbitration committee.** Where the value falls below the monetary threshold and application to the consumer arbitration committee is compulsory, mediation is not used. There is already another compulsory preliminary stage there.

**(b) Objections to decisions of the consumer arbitration committee.** No mediation is required for an objection lodged with the consumer court against a committee decision.

**(c) The actions in Article 73/6 of the Consumer Protection Law.** Actions for prevention, cessation and declaration brought by consumer organisations, the relevant public institutions and organisations and the Ministry, in cases which concern consumers generally and where there is a risk of a situation contrary to the Law arising.

**(d) The actions in Article 74 of the Consumer Protection Law.** Actions concerning the determination and prevention of unfair terms.

**(e) Disputes which are in the nature of a consumer transaction and arise from a right in rem over immovable property.** This is the most debated exception in practice. Where a dispute arising from the sale of a dwelling concerns the **right in rem** over the property — such as cancellation of title and registration — mediation is not required; by contrast, a claim for payment or damages arising from the same sale (delay compensation, price reduction for defects) is not regarded as concerning the right in rem and is subject to the mediation condition. The 3rd Civil Chamber of the Court of Cassation assesses the condition of action by drawing this distinction in disputes arising from real-estate sale contracts in the nature of a consumer transaction.

## Three Balancing Provisions in the Consumer's Favour

What distinguishes Article 73/A from the other mandatory-mediation regimes are three special provisions protecting the consumer.

**The non-attendance sanction is not applied against the consumer (Art. 73/A/2).** The sanction in Article 18/A/11 of Law No. 6325 — under which a party who fails without excuse to attend the first meeting is liable for half of the litigation costs even if successful, and an attorney's fee is awarded against them — cannot be operated against the consumer. For the seller or supplier, however, the sanction applies as usual. That asymmetry is deliberate: the legislature did not wish to pressure the economically weaker party with the risk of non-attendance.

**The mediation fee is met from the Ministry's budget (Art. 73/A/3).** In all cases — the parties cannot be reached, there is no attendance, the parties **settle** or **fail to settle** — the mediation fee payable by the consumer is met from the budget of the Ministry of Justice. It should be noted that this protection is broader than the general rule in Article 18/A/13 of Law No. 6325: there, the Ministry steps in only where no settlement is reached and only up to the two-hour fee, whereas here the consumer's share is met from the budget even where a settlement is reached. The ceiling is again the amount of the two-hour fee.

**Collection of the fee from the defendant (Art. 73/A/4).** Where the action brought at the end of the mediation concludes in the consumer's favour, the mediation fee met from the budget is collected from the defendant under the provisions of Law No. 6183 and recorded as budget revenue.

## The Rest of the Procedure and the Strictness of the Condition

Apart from the scope and protective provisions, the procedure is subject to Article 18/A of Law No. 6325: the application is made to the mediation bureau at the place of the competent court — since under Article 73/5 of the Consumer Protection Law consumer actions may also be brought before the consumer court of the consumer's domicile, the consumer may apply to the bureau at their own domicile; the mediator concludes the process within three weeks (four in compulsory circumstances); and from the application to the bureau until the date the final minutes are drawn up, limitation is suspended.

There is no difference as to the strictness of the condition. The 6th Civil Chamber of the Court of Cassation has stated that, under Article 73/A of the Consumer Protection Law, having resorted to mandatory mediation before bringing an action in monetary claims heard before the consumer court is regulated as a **special condition of action**. An action brought without fulfilling the condition is dismissed on procedural grounds under Articles 114/2 and 115/2 of the Code of Civil Procedure and Article 18/A/2 of Law No. 6325.

## Case Law of the Court of Cassation

The following decisions show the application of mandatory mediation in consumer disputes.

**1. Court of Cassation, 3rd Civil Chamber, Case No. 2023/504, Decision No. 2023/2329, dated 20.09.2023**

The Chamber stated that, under Article 73/A added after Article 73 of Law No. 6502, having applied to a mediator before bringing an action is a condition of action in disputes heard before the consumer courts, and carried out its assessment of scope by enumerating the exceptions in the first paragraph.

**2. Court of Cassation, 3rd Civil Chamber, Case No. 2022/7326, Decision No. 2022/9352, dated 12.12.2022**

The dispute arises from a real-estate sale contract in the nature of a consumer transaction. The decision assesses that the consumer court has jurisdiction under Article 73/1 of the Consumer Protection Law and considers, within the framework of the exception for disputes arising from a right in rem over immovable property, whether applying to a mediator before bringing the action was required under Article 73/A added by Law No. 7251.

**3. Court of Cassation, 6th Civil Chamber, Case No. 2024/167, Decision No. 2024/438, dated 31.01.2024**

The Chamber concluded that the action fell within the scope of the mediation condition, stating that the dispute was a consumer transaction, that it is to be resolved by the consumer court under Article 73/1 of the Consumer Protection Law, and that under Article 73/A having resorted to mandatory mediation before bringing an action in monetary claims heard before the consumer court is regulated as a special condition of action.

**4. Court of Cassation, 3rd Civil Chamber, Case No. 2022/6069, Decision No. 2022/7384, dated 05.10.2022**

The decision addresses the practice of dismissing an action on procedural grounds for lack of the mediation condition under Article 73/A of the Consumer Protection Law, Article 18/A-2 of Law No. 6325 and Articles 114/2 and 115/2 of the Code of Civil Procedure, and the assessment made by the Ministry of Justice against that decision.

**5. Court of Cassation, 3rd Civil Chamber, Case No. 2025/821, Decision No. 2025/6003, dated 09.12.2025**

By enumerating the exceptions in the first paragraph of Article 73/A — chiefly disputes within the competence of the consumer arbitration committee and objections to committee decisions — the Chamber sets out in up-to-date form the cases in which the condition of action is not required.
