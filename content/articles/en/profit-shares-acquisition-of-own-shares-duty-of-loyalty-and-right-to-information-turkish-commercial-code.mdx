TTK Art. 608-615 close out the "Rights and Obligations of Partners" chapter of the limited company regime, covering profit distribution, share buybacks, loyalty duties, and information rights.

## Profit Distribution and the Interest Ban

Under Art. 608, profit may only be distributed from net annual profit and reserves set aside for that purpose, after statutory and articles-mandated reserves are funded; a partner has no automatic entitlement to a share of profit absent a general assembly resolution so deciding — Turkish courts confirm this consistently. Art. 609 bars paying interest on capital or additional payments (a corollary of the repayment ban), with a narrow exception for "preparation-period interest" during a company's startup phase, governed by the joint-stock company rules. A partner or manager who received an unjustified profit distribution must return it under Art. 611, though a good-faith recipient's liability is capped at what creditors actually need, with a five-year (or two-year, if in good faith) limitation period.

## Buying Back Own Shares

Art. 612 allows a limited company to acquire its own capital shares only if it has freely available equity to cover the price and the nominal value acquired does not exceed 10% of capital — raised to 20% for shares acquired following a partner's withdrawal or expulsion, provided any excess over 10% is disposed of or cancelled through a capital reduction within two years. Voting and related rights on shares the company holds itself are frozen for as long as it holds them.

## Loyalty, Non-Competition, and Information Rights

Art. 613 imposes an inalienable duty on all partners to keep company secrets and avoid harming the company's interests; a broader non-competition duty binds only if the articles provide for it (managers face a separate, automatic non-competition rule under Art. 626). Art. 614 gives every partner a right to request information and inspect the company's affairs from managers — a critical tool for minority partners — subject to a three-tier resolution process (manager, then general assembly, then court) if information is wrongfully withheld.

## Practical Takeaway

A foreign investor should not expect automatic dividends from profitable operations — a formal distribution resolution is always required — and should factor the 10%/20% buyback caps into any exit or redemption planning involving the company repurchasing a departing partner's stake.
