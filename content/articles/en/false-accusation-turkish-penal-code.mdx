Foreign nationals who become entangled in Turkish criminal proceedings — whether as the person wrongly accused or as someone facing a complaint they believe is fabricated — are often unfamiliar with how Turkish law treats false accusation as a standalone offense against the administration of justice, not merely as a civil wrong. Articles 267 through 269 of the Turkish Penal Code (Türk Ceza Kanunu, "TCK") set out a detailed framework: the core offense of falsely attributing an unlawful act to someone, a related offense of using another person's identity to dodge one's own prosecution, and a graduated system of sentence reductions for those who retract their false claim before the damage compounds. Understanding this framework matters both for defending against a fabricated complaint and for assessing exposure if one has made — or is considering making — a report to the authorities.

## What the Offense Protects and Why It Is Prosecuted Automatically

False accusation sits in the section of the Turkish Penal Code dealing with offenses against judicial administration, and it is designed to protect two distinct interests at once. The first is the individual target's reputation and their right to be presumed innocent until proven otherwise. The second, equally important, interest is the integrity of the investigative and administrative apparatus itself — prosecutors, police, and administrative bodies should not have to expend resources chasing claims their author already knows to be false. Because the offense implicates the functioning of public institutions and not only a private grievance, it is prosecuted by the state on its own initiative rather than depending on the victim filing and maintaining a complaint, and the victim's consent to the accusation does not by itself erase the offense. This dual character is central to how Turkish courts approach the crime: it is treated as an affront to the justice system as much as to the individual who was wrongly implicated.

## How a False Accusation Is Committed

Under Article 267(1), the offense requires someone to attribute an unlawful act to another person — while knowing that person did not commit it — through a report or complaint to a competent authority, or through the press or broadcast media. Three elements stand out. First, the false claim must target an identifiable individual; a vague or anonymous accusation naming no one in particular does not qualify. Second, the act being falsely attributed must be "unlawful" in a broad sense — it need not be a crime; an act that would only trigger an administrative penalty can also form the basis of the offense. Third, the accusation must describe something concrete and plausible enough to be investigated, rather than a bare insult or generalized slur, which may fall under different offenses entirely. The law is also specific about the channel: a report or complaint to an authority with jurisdiction, or a statement carried by the press or broadcast media. This list is exhaustive, so a false claim made only in a private conversation, without more, typically will not satisfy this particular offense, even though it might expose the speaker to liability elsewhere. It is not necessary that nothing at all happened — what matters is that the accused knowingly pinned a fabricated set of facts on someone else, exposing that person to the concrete risk of an investigation or an administrative sanction.

## The Knowledge Requirement: Why Genuine Mistakes Are Not Covered

The defining feature of this offense is its mental element. The statute requires that the person act "knowing" the accused party did not commit the act — language that Turkish criminal doctrine reads as requiring direct intent. The accuser must be certain the claim is false and proceed with the report, complaint, or public statement regardless. As a result, a complaint based on a genuine misunderstanding, an honest but mistaken belief, or a good-faith interpretation of ambiguous facts that later turns out to be wrong does not amount to false accusation — there is no negligence-based version of this crime. The provision also embeds a specific purpose requirement: the accusation must be made in order to trigger an investigation, prosecution, or administrative sanction against the target. Whether a merely reckless or doubtful accusation (as opposed to one made with certain knowledge of its falsity) can satisfy the offense is debated among commentators, but the prevailing reading of the statutory phrase "knowing [it] not to be true" requires the stronger, direct form of intent.

## Aggravating Factors: A Scaled System of Increased Penalties

The law increases the penalty in stages as the consequences of the false accusation escalate. Fabricating physical evidence or material proof to support the claim increases the sentence by half. If the falsely accused person is later acquitted or has proceedings against them discontinued, and in the meantime a protective measure other than police custody or pre-trial detention was applied against them because of the accusation, the sentence again increases by half. Where the wrongly accused person was actually taken into police custody or pre-trial detention as a result of the false claim, the accuser additionally becomes liable as an indirect perpetrator of the separate offense of unlawfully depriving someone of their liberty — a rule that recognizes the direct causal link between a false accusation and the innocent person's loss of freedom. If the false accusation results in the victim being convicted to aggravated life imprisonment or ordinary life imprisonment, the accuser faces a prison sentence of twenty to thirty years, increased further by half if enforcement of that sentence has already begun. A conviction for false accusation carried out through the press or broadcast media must be publicized through an equivalent outlet, at the convicted person's expense. A further paragraph of the article dealing with judicial fees was struck down by the Turkish Constitutional Court in a 2011 ruling and no longer applies.

## Using Someone Else's Identity to Escape Prosecution

Article 268 addresses a specific variant of the same wrong: a person who, in order to prevent an investigation or prosecution against themselves for a crime they committed, uses another individual's identity or identity information is punished under the same provisions as false accusation. The classic scenario is someone who is stopped or questioned by the police and presents another person's civil registry details as their own. Turkish law treats this not as a separate, narrower offense but folds it directly into the false-accusation framework, because the underlying harm is the same: the authorities are misled, and an innocent third party's identity is dragged into a criminal matter without their knowledge or consent. This closes a potential gap between identity misuse and false accusation, ensuring the conduct does not escape adequate sanction merely because it takes the form of borrowing an identity rather than making an explicit verbal accusation.

## Retraction: A Sliding Scale of Sentence Reductions

Article 269 rewards early retraction with meaningful reductions in sentence, calibrated to how far the case has already progressed. Retracting before any investigation begins earns the largest reduction — four-fifths of the sentence. Retracting after an investigation but before prosecution begins reduces the sentence by three-quarters. Once the case reaches trial, retraction before judgment cuts the sentence by two-thirds; retraction after conviction but before enforcement begins cuts it by half; and retraction after enforcement of the sentence has started still allows a one-third reduction. Where the false accusation concerned conduct that could only ever attract an administrative penalty rather than a criminal one, retracting before the administrative sanction is imposed allows a reduction of one-half, and retracting afterward allows one-third. For a false accusation made through the press or broadcast media, the retraction itself must be published through the same channel to qualify for any reduction at all. The clear purpose behind this graduated scheme is to give the accuser a continuing incentive to correct the record, even at a late stage, rather than letting an innocent person's situation deteriorate further.

## Limitation Period and Related Procedural Points

Because the falsity of an accusation may remain hidden for a long time, the statute of limitations for prosecuting the false accusation does not begin to run from the date the accusation was made — it begins only once it is established that the wrongly accused person did not commit the act attributed to them, typically the date an acquittal or non-prosecution decision becomes final. The offense is considered complete once the false report reaches the competent authority, and an attempt is conceptually possible where the conduct can be broken into distinct steps. If a single false claim implicates several people, a separate offense arises for each victim. False accusation should not be confused with simple insult, nor with statements protected as part of a legitimate claim or defense in litigation — courts are expected to scrutinize, in each case, whether the accuser actually knew the claim to be false.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can someone be prosecuted for false accusation if their complaint turns out to be mistaken but made in good faith?**
No. The offense requires the accuser to know with certainty that the claim is false. A complaint based on a genuine, even if ultimately incorrect, belief does not meet this standard, because Turkish law does not recognize a negligent version of this crime.

**Does giving a false name to the police to avoid one's own prosecution count as false accusation?**
Yes, if it is done to prevent an investigation into a crime the person actually committed and involves using another identifiable person's identity or identity details. Article 268 punishes this conduct under the same rules as ordinary false accusation, even though no explicit verbal accusation was made.

**What happens if the falsely accused person was taken into custody or detained because of the accusation?**
The accuser faces an aggravated penalty under Article 267 and separately becomes liable as an indirect perpetrator of the offense of unlawfully depriving a person of their liberty, reflecting the direct link between the false claim and the loss of freedom suffered by the victim.

**Is there any benefit to retracting a false accusation after the fact?**
Yes. Turkish law provides substantial, graduated sentence reductions for retraction, ranging from four-fifths of the sentence if withdrawn before any investigation starts down to one-third if withdrawn after the sentence has begun to be enforced — though a retraction made through the press must be published through the same medium to count.
