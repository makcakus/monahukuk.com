Having covered who owes the salvage award and when a consignee becomes personally liable (Arts. 1306–1307), this article turns to how the award itself is calculated and litigated when more than one salvor is involved. Art. 1308 opens the "distribution of the award" section with a single-award rule (para. 1) and a case-consolidation rule (para. 2).

## One Award for the Whole Operation

Under Art. 1308(1), a single salvage award is fixed for all salvage activity from the moment the triggering danger began until the point the property owner or master is entitled to request return of the salved goods. Multiple salvors may have taken part along the way; the rule that matters is that none of them calculates or claims a separate award — everyone entitled to a share draws it out of this one combined total. This connects directly to the rule (covered earlier in this series) that a salvage award may never exceed the salved value of the property: if each salvor could claim its own award independently, the sum of separate claims could easily exceed what the salvaged property is actually worth, defeating the owner's guarantee of a net benefit from salvage. Pooling everything into a single award lets that value cap function as intended.

## Consolidating Multiple Lawsuits: The Priority Hierarchy

If more than one lawsuit is filed to claim a share of the award, Art. 1308(2) does not leave it to chance which case absorbs the others. It sets a four-step hierarchy: the action of a salvor holding an exclusive right takes priority; failing that, the salvor who entered into the salvage contract; failing that, the salvor who contributed the most vessels; failing that, the salvor who contributed the most equipment. The logic runs in two stages — the first two tiers favor the party legally closest to the operation (the party with contractual or exclusive standing), while the last two fall back to an objective, measurable proxy (scale of resources committed) when no such contractual priority exists.

## Arbitration and Court Proceedings Working Together

Where one of the competing claims is pending before an arbitral tribunal rather than a court, Art. 1308(2) still requires consolidation: every case is folded into the first lawsuit filed in court, and that court stays its own proceedings pending a final arbitral award fixing the total salvage award amount. Once the arbitral award becomes final, the court applies that figure and decides — within the same consolidated case — how the total is to be distributed among all salvors. This respects the binding force of the arbitration agreement (the court cannot recalculate the award itself) while still avoiding fragmented, potentially inconsistent outcomes across different fora.

## Practical Takeaway

For salvors, this means a claim for a share of the award should generally be pursued within whichever proceeding already carries priority under the Art. 1308(2) hierarchy, rather than filed as a freestanding action — a separately filed case is liable to be consolidated (and control ceded) regardless. For owners facing multiple salvor claims, the single-award and consolidation rules together are a safeguard against paying out more than one properly capped total.
