TTK Art. 1044-1053 close out the ship mortgage regime by cataloguing every route to termination, grouped into three categories.

## Termination Tied to the Underlying Claim

As an accessory right, a mortgage generally falls with its secured claim (Art. 1044) — including where creditor and debtor merge into the same person. Where a non-owner debtor partially repays, the mortgage share that passes to that debtor ranks *behind* whatever the original creditor still holds — the unsatisfied creditor stays senior. A related rule, Art. 1045, provides that merger of mortgage and ownership in the same person also extinguishes the mortgage — unless the debtor is someone else, or the claim itself carries a separate pledge or usufruct, in which case the mortgage survives but loses its foreclosure and interest-securing functions. Art. 1046-1047 extend subrogation logic to multi-ship mortgages: a paying owner steps into the mortgage on a co-liable ship to the extent of its recourse right against that ship's owner.

## Termination of the Mortgage Alone

Three routes end only the mortgage, leaving the debt itself intact: mutual agreement between owner and creditor followed by deregistration (Art. 1049); unilateral creditor waiver, which an owner can even affirmatively demand if it holds a permanent defense against the mortgage (Art. 1050); and simple expiry where the mortgage was created for a fixed term (Art. 1051).

## Court-Ordered Termination Where the Creditor Is Unknown

For old or transferred ships where nobody can identify the current mortgage creditor, Art. 1052 allows a court, after ten years of registry inactivity with no interruption of limitation, to summon the unknown creditor by public notice and declare the mortgage extinguished if nobody appears. Art. 1053 gives owners an even more proactive path: depositing the debt amount (with strict limits on how much back-interest must be included) for the unknown creditor, followed by the same public-notice procedure.

## Practical Takeaway

These "phantom mortgage" cleanup mechanisms matter for owners refinancing or selling older vessels where a decades-old, fully repaid loan was never formally deregistered — Art. 1052-1053 provide a lawful path to a clean registry without needing the original, now-untraceable lender's cooperation.
