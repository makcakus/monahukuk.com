TTK Art. 917-922 define the freight forwarding commission contract — the legal position of the intermediary who arranges carriage rather than performs it, commonly known internationally as a "forwarder."

## Organizer, Not Carrier

Under Art. 917, the forwarder undertakes to have goods carried, not to carry them — a distinction with real consequences. Where a carrier performs the transport itself, a forwarder organizes it: selecting the route and vehicle, choosing and contracting with the actual carrier, and securing the sender's compensation rights, as detailed in Art. 918. Forwarding is classified as a commercial enterprise activity, subjecting the forwarder to the prudent-merchant standard, and general carriage-contract rules apply to any point this chapter leaves open.

## The Sender's Duties and the Forwarder's Fee

Art. 919 places the same packaging, labeling, documentation, and hazardous-goods disclosure duties on the sender that apply in ordinary cargo carriage — breach triggers strict liability for resulting costs, subject to the same SDR caps and consumer carve-outs. Critically, under Art. 920 the forwarder's commission becomes due the moment goods are handed to the actual carrier — not upon final delivery — because the forwarder's organizational obligation is complete at that point.

## When the Forwarder Becomes a Carrier

Art. 921 creates an important trap for the unwary: if the parties agree on a single all-inclusive fee covering both commission and carriage costs, the forwarder automatically takes on a carrier's rights and duties for that shipment — no separate election is needed. This has real teeth in practice: Turkish courts have repeatedly held forwarders who issued their own freight invoices and undertook carriage as a whole liable exactly as carriers, with no benefit of any forwarder-only defense.

## Recovering From the Actual Carrier

Under Art. 922, because the forwarder contracts with the actual carrier in its own name, the sender cannot sue that carrier directly unless the forwarder has formally assigned the claim — though the receivable is shielded from the forwarder's own creditors in insolvency.

## Practical Takeaway

For foreign shippers and forwarders alike, the recurring litigation question is not whether Turkish law recognizes forwarding as distinct from carriage — it does — but whether the pricing structure, invoicing practice, or degree of control quietly converted the forwarder into a carrier with full cargo liability.
