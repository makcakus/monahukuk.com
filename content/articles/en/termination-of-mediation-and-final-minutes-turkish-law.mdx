*Article 17 – (1) The mediation activity ends in the following cases: a) the parties reach a settlement; b) the mediator determines, after consulting the parties, that further effort at mediation is unnecessary; c) one party notifies the other party or the mediator that they are withdrawing from the mediation activity; ç) the parties agree to end the mediation activity; d) (Amended: 12/10/2017-7036/23) it is determined that the dispute is not capable of mediation.*

*(2) At the end of the mediation activity, whether the parties settled, failed to settle, or how the activity concluded is documented in minutes. This document, drawn up by the mediator, is signed by the mediator, the parties, their legal representatives or their lawyers. If it is not signed by the parties, their legal representatives or their lawyers, it is signed by the mediator alone, stating the reason.*

*(4) Upon termination of the mediation activity, the mediator must retain for five years the notifications made to them, the documents entrusted to and held by them, and the minutes drawn up under paragraph two. The mediator sends a copy of the final minutes drawn up at the end of the activity to the General Directorate within one month of the termination of the mediation activity.*

The final minutes are the shortest but legally the most heavily loaded document of the mediation process. This single document determines when the limitation period starts running again, whether the condition precedent has been satisfied, which party failed to attend and therefore how litigation costs and attorney's fees are apportioned. In practice, the most frequent reason for an action being dismissed on procedural grounds is not a defect touching the merits, but the improper drafting of this document or its improper submission to the court.

## The Five Grounds of Termination

Article 17/1 sets out the termination of the activity in five subparagraphs, and the list is exhaustive.

**Settlement by the parties (a).** The most desirable outcome. Where a settlement is reached, a settlement document is drawn up under Article 18, and under Article 18/5 no action may be brought on the matters agreed.

**The mediator's determination that further effort is unnecessary (b).** The mediator may make this determination only **after consulting the parties**; they cannot end the process unilaterally without hearing them.

**Notification of withdrawal by a party (c).** A direct consequence of the principle of voluntariness. Notification may be given to the other party or to the mediator; no reason need be given.

**Termination by agreement of the parties (ç).** Even if the dispute is not resolved, the parties may close the process together.

**A finding of non-mediability (d).** Where it emerges that the dispute is not capable of mediation, the process ends. Under the Mediation Minimum Fee Tariff no fee is payable to the mediator in that case.

In mandatory mediation, Article 18/A/10 makes a practical addition to this list: the mediator also ends the activity, draws up the final minutes and immediately notifies the mediation office where **the parties cannot be reached** or **no session can be held because the parties did not attend**.

## Content and Signature of the Final Minutes

Article 17/2 fixes the **minimum** content of the minutes: whether the parties settled, failed to settle, or how the activity concluded. Article 17/3 leaves anything beyond that to the parties — apart from the conclusion of the activity, it is for the parties to decide what is recorded. The mediator gives the parties the necessary explanations about the minutes and their consequences, and where a party is not present, informs those absent using any means of communication.

The signature regime is flexible, and this is often misunderstood in practice: the document is signed by the mediator, the parties, their legal representatives or their lawyers; **if the parties, their legal representatives or their lawyers do not sign, it is signed by the mediator alone, stating the reason**. A party's refusal to sign therefore does not invalidate the minutes; minutes drawn up with the mediator's signature alone still satisfy the condition precedent. On the other hand, the reason for the failure to sign must be stated in the minutes.

The limit of this flexibility is the **accuracy of the content**. The 9th Civil Chamber of the Turkish Court of Cassation held, in a case where the date on which the final minutes were drawn up conflicted with the date of the signature on them, that the minutes were not in due form, and stressed that where there is doubt about a preclusive time limit it must be construed in favour of the person against whom the limit runs. Likewise, in a dispute before the 3rd Civil Chamber, annulment of the minutes was sought on the grounds that the lawyer's surname was misspelt and that the file had been closed without signatures being taken and without the deficiencies being remedied.

## Filing the Minutes with the Court: A Photocopy Is Not Enough

In mandatory mediation the claimant must attach to the statement of claim **the original of the final minutes recording the failure to settle, or a copy certified by the mediator** (Article 18/A/2; for employment disputes, Article 3/2 of Law No. 7036). If this requirement is not met, the court sends a summons warning that the minutes must be produced within a final one-week period, failing which the action will be dismissed on procedural grounds.

A divergence of view that arose between regional courts of appeal on this point was resolved by the decision of the 9th Civil Chamber on the resolution of a conflict of case law. According to that decision, sending a document meeting the statutory requirements through the national judicial IT system (UYAP) has the same legal effect as physical submission by hand; but if the document sent is a **photocopy/duplicate**, it does not meet the statutory requirements. Nor does it matter that the judge can access the minutes through UYAP, because the duty of production rests on the parties and their lawyers, and the principle of investigation of the court's own motion does not apply in employment proceedings. In sum: **the original or a copy certified by the mediator** must be filed, whether by hand or through UYAP.

The one-week final period is mandatory and cannot be altered by the court.

## Can the Invalidity of the Minutes Be the Subject of a Separate Action?

Judicial practice answers this clearly: **minutes recording a failure to settle cannot be the subject of a separate action.**

The 11th Civil Chamber upheld a decision dismissing on procedural grounds an annulment action brought by a company claiming that the mediation invitation letter had never been served on it and that it had been unaware of the sessions — on the ground that such objections **can be raised in the action to be brought against it and assessed by the court of its own motion**, so that there is no legal interest in a separate action.

The 9th Civil Chamber likewise endorsed the assessment that, in a dispute stemming from the mediator's error as to the constitution of the parties, that error could not be attributed to the other side, and that bringing a separate annulment action instead of resorting to settlement or post-action mediation in an employee claims case is incompatible with the principle of good faith. The dissenting opinion stressed that a request to annul minutes recording a failure to settle is in the nature of a declaratory action, and that the protection sought could be obtained by raising it as a defence in the main action, so that no current legal interest existed.

The position differs for **settlement** minutes — because a settlement closes the road to litigation under Article 18/5, establishing its invalidity does require a separate action.

## Duties of Retention and Notification

Article 17/4 imposes two distinct duties on the mediator. The first is **retention**: the notifications made to them concerning the activity, the documents entrusted to and held by them, and the final minutes must be kept for five years. This duty enables a party's lawyer to apply later if the original of the minutes is needed — indeed Article 2/3 of the Turkish Attorneyship Law gives lawyers the right to request information and documents; even if a different lawyer attended the session, a party can obtain from the mediator the original or a certified copy of documents relating to them.

The second is **notification**: the mediator sends a copy of the final minutes to the General Directorate within one month of the termination of the activity. These copies are recorded and kept by the Department of Mediation under Article 30/1-g; in practice, letters obtained from the mediation office and from the Department become decisive evidence of how the process actually unfolded.

## Decisions of the Court of Cassation

The following decisions illustrate the practice on drawing up and filing the final minutes.

**1. Court of Cassation, 9th Civil Chamber, File No. 2020/932, Decision No. 2020/5773, 17.06.2020 (Resolution of Conflicting Case Law)**

Resolving a divergence between chambers of the Antalya and Istanbul regional courts of appeal, the Chamber held that sending a document meeting the statutory requirements through UYAP produces the same effect as submission by hand, but that these requirements are not met where the document submitted is a photocopy/duplicate. That the judge can access the document through UYAP has no bearing on the outcome; the duty of production rests on the parties and their lawyers, and since the principle of investigation of the court's own motion does not apply in employment proceedings, the burden of investigation cannot be placed on the judge.

**2. Court of Cassation, 9th Civil Chamber, File No. 2023/12641, Decision No. 2023/13520, 02.10.2023**

The Chamber addressed the conflict between the date on which the final minutes were drawn up and the date of the signature on them; since the alleged signature date fell after the date the minutes were drawn up, it held that the minutes recording the failure to settle were not in due form, and stated that where there is doubt as to a preclusive period it must be construed in favour of the person against whom the period runs.

**3. Court of Cassation, 3rd Civil Chamber, File No. 2024/2979, Decision No. 2025/2584, 05.05.2025**

The claimant sought annulment of the mediation minutes, alleging that their lawyer's surname was misspelt, that an incorrect reference had been made to the labour courts, and that the mediation file had been closed without their signature and without the deficiencies in the minutes being remedied. The decision serves as an example of the review of formal defects in the final minutes.

**4. Court of Cassation, 11th Civil Chamber, File No. 2023/5637, Decision No. 2024/7178, 08.10.2024**

The claimant companies sought annulment of the minutes, alleging that the mandatory mediation invitation letter had not been sent to them and that the meeting had been held and the final minutes drawn up without any service at all. The Chamber upheld the decision dismissing the action on procedural grounds, accepting that such allegations may be raised in the action to be brought against them and assessed by the court of its own motion, and that **there is therefore no legal interest in making the annulment of minutes recording a failure to settle the subject of a separate action**.

**5. Court of Cassation, 9th Civil Chamber, File No. 2025/3354, Decision No. 2025/5339, 23.06.2025**

The employer sought annulment of the final minutes recording a failure to settle in mandatory mediation, alleging that a person without authority to represent it had attended the session. The Chamber upheld the dismissal of the action, holding that a drafting or **party-constitution error attributable to the mediator cannot be held against the employee**, and that bringing a separate annulment action instead of resorting to settlement or post-action mediation in an employee claims case is incompatible with the principle of good faith. The dissenting opinion stated that the request was in the nature of a declaratory action and that no current legal interest existed.
