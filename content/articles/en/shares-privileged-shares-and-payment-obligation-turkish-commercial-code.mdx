TTK Art. 476-483 cover two foundational building blocks of Turkish joint-stock companies: the nature of the share itself, and the shareholder's core obligation to actually pay for it — including what happens when that obligation is not met.

## Minimum Value, Indivisibility, and Privileges

Under Art. 476, a share's nominal value must be at least one kuruş, raisable only in increments of one kuruş (with presidential authority to raise the floor up to a hundredfold); shares issued below this are void, though payments already made remain protected and issuers face joint liability for resulting harm. Art. 477 treats each share as indivisible against the company — co-owners must act through a single joint representative — while letting the general assembly split or consolidate shares (within the minimum-value rule) via an articles amendment, though consolidation specifically requires every affected shareholder's consent.

Art. 478 defines a privileged share broadly: any superior right attached to a share regarding dividends, liquidation proceeds, preemptive rights, or voting, or even an entirely new right not otherwise found in the law. Turkish courts have read this expansively — a contractual "joint signature representation right" granted to one share class, though not a statutorily defined right type, was still recognized as a privilege requiring the privileged shareholders' special-meeting approval before removal.

## The Fifteen-Vote Ceiling

Art. 479 allows voting privileges by granting unequal votes to equal-value shares, but caps any single share at fifteen votes — an emphatic limit against concentrating control. Exceptions require court approval of a genuine institutionalization project or another justified reason, and remain subject to ongoing court oversight; Turkish courts have been notably strict about what counts as sufficient justification, rejecting bare claims of "vested rights" absent a concrete institutionalization plan. Voting privileges are further barred outright from articles amendment votes and from votes on discharge or filing a liability suit — areas where letting privileged votes control the outcome would undermine accountability itself.

## The Duty to Pay and the Consequences of Default

Art. 480 confirms that a shareholder's only obligation, beyond narrow statutory exceptions, is paying the share price (plus any premium) — the articles cannot impose additional debts. Art. 481 governs how that debt becomes due (typically via board announcement), while Art. 482-483 set out the consequences of default: automatic default interest without need for formal notice, and — following a strict, sequential warning procedure with a one-month grace period — forfeiture (ıskat), where the board strips the defaulting shareholder of rights and resells the share. Turkish courts enforce this procedure with unusual rigor: registered-shareholder notices must go by registered mail, forfeiture decisions require proper board quorum, and any procedural shortcut renders the forfeiture void or non-existent.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### At most how many votes can be granted to a single share?

Under TTK Article 479/2, <b>at most fifteen votes may be granted to a single share.</b> This limitation does not apply <b>where corporatisation so requires or a justified cause is proven</b>; however, this requires the commercial court of first instance in the place of the company's headquarters to examine the corporatisation plan or the justified cause and issue a decision granting the exception. Every change to the plan is likewise subject to a court decision.

### For which resolutions can a voting privilege not be exercised?

Under TTK Article 479/3, a voting privilege cannot be exercised on two matters: <b>(a) amendment of the articles of association</b> and <b>(c) release of the board from liability (ibra) and the initiation of a liability lawsuit.</b> The purpose of this prohibition is clear: to prevent control over amendments to the articles of association — the company's constitution — and over the accountability of the managers, from being left solely to a privileged minority. On these two matters, all shares carry equal voting power.

### What happens to a shareholder who fails to pay their capital debt?

Under TTK Article 482/1, <b>a shareholder who fails to fulfil their capital contribution debt within the time limit is obliged to pay default interest without the need for a warning.</b> Under Article 482/2, the board of directors is authorised to deprive the defaulting shareholder of the rights arising from their subscription commitment and any partial payments made, and to admit another person in their place. This procedure of <b>forfeiture (ıskat)</b> is regulated in Article 483 and requires a call by announcement granting a period of one month.
