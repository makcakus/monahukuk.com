TTK Art. 870-874 close out the general goods-carriage rules by covering payment, delivery, and — most practically — what happens when a shipment simply never arrives.

## Freight and the Consignee's Position

Under Art. 870, freight becomes due on delivery, and a carrier forced to end carriage early due to an obstacle is entitled only to a proportional share of freight (or less, if the obstacle was in its own risk sphere). Once goods reach the delivery point, Art. 871 lets the consignee demand delivery against payment, and — importantly — also lets the consignee assert the sender's claims against the carrier for loss, damage, or delay, without displacing the sender's own standing to do the same. In exchange, the consignee owes the freight shown on the consignment note (or, absent one, a reasonable amount matching what sender and carrier agreed).

## Cash-on-Delivery

Art. 872 lets delivery be conditioned on cash (or cash-equivalent) payment collected by the carrier — the collected sum is treated as passed to the sender even against the carrier's own creditors — and makes the carrier strictly liable, capped at the amount that should have been collected, for delivering goods without collecting.

## Transit Time and the Loss Presumption

Art. 873 sets a reasonable-transit-time standard wherever no specific period was agreed. Art. 874 then solves the classic evidentiary problem in carriage claims: if goods are not delivered within twenty days of that transit period (thirty days for cross-border shipments), the rights-holder may simply treat them as lost — without proving actual loss. If the goods later turn up after compensation is paid, the rights-holder can request notice and, within thirty days of learning of it, repay the compensation and reclaim the goods; otherwise the carrier may dispose of them freely.

## Practical Takeaway

For a shipper or consignee dealing with an overdue Turkish shipment, the loss presumption under Art. 874 is a genuinely useful tool — it converts an uncertain and hard-to-prove disappearance into a simple, date-driven claim once the twenty- or thirty-day window has passed.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### When is the carriage charge payable?

Under TTK Article 870/1 <b>the carriage charge is payable on delivery of the goods</b>. In addition to the carriage charge, the carrier may also claim the <b>expenses</b> incurred for the goods that were necessary in the circumstances. The obligation to pay rests as a rule on the consignor; however, under Article 871/2 a consignee who requests delivery of the goods thereby also assumes the payment obligations arising from the consignment note or the contract.

### When are undelivered goods deemed lost?

Under TTK Article 874/1, if the goods are not delivered <b>within twenty days following the transit period</b>, the person entitled may <b>treat them as lost</b> and claim compensation for total loss. <b>In cross-border carriage this period is thirty days.</b> Thanks to this presumption, the person entitled need not wait indefinitely where the fate of the goods remains uncertain.

### What happens if goods deemed lost are later found?

Under TTK Article 874/2 the person entitled may, when the compensation is paid, request to be notified immediately should the goods later be found. Under Article 874/3 they may, <b>within thirty days of being notified that the goods have been found</b>, demand delivery of the goods by repaying the compensation, less expenses where appropriate. If the person entitled did not request notification or did not assert this right in time, the carrier may deal freely with the goods under Article 874/4.
