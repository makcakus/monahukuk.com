TTK Art. 794-802 cover the operational core of cheque payment: aval, presentment deadlines, revocation, and what happens if the drawer dies, loses capacity, or is declared bankrupt.

## Aval — With One Key Exclusion

Cheque aval works like bill-of-exchange aval (imported via Art. 818): a third party or existing signatory may guarantee the cheque, in whole or part, with a simple signature. The one structural difference: the drawee bank itself can never give aval (Art. 794/2) — since a cheque is a payment tool, not a credit instrument, the bank has no business assuming an independent kambiyo undertaking.

## Payable on Demand — Always

Art. 795 makes a cheque payable at sight regardless of any contrary notation, which is simply disregarded. Even a cheque presented before its stated issuance date must be paid on the day it is presented (Art. 795/2) — the statutory basis for Turkey's widespread post-dated cheque practice, though under the Cheque Law a post-dated cheque presented before its stated date can only be dishonored for insufficient funds and pursued through enforcement once its stated issuance date and the applicable presentment period have actually passed.

## Presentment Windows

A cheque payable where it was drawn must be presented within ten days; elsewhere in Turkey, within one month; internationally, within one or three months depending on the continents involved (Art. 796) — periods that start the day after the stated drawing date. Missing this window carries serious consequences: loss of recourse rights, the ability to revoke, and conversion of any later endorsement into a mere assignment. Presentment to a clearing house counts as presentment to the drawee (Art. 798).

## Revocation Only After the Window Closes

Art. 799 permits revocation only after the presentment period has expired — a rule protecting the cheque's function as a reliable payment instrument, since allowing revocation during the window would let any drawer block payment freely. Before the period expires, the bank may still pay even without express instruction. This differs from "stop payment" instructions based on involuntary loss of possession, which remain available during the presentment window. The drawer's death, loss of legal capacity, or bankruptcy after the cheque enters circulation does not affect its validity (Art. 800) — reflecting the instrument's abstract, self-contained character.

## Limited Bank Duty on Endorsements

The drawee bank paying an endorsable cheque must check that the chain of endorsements is formally consecutive, but need not verify each endorser's signature is genuine (Art. 801) — the same formal-only standard applied to holder status generally.

## Foreign-Currency Cheques

Where a cheque is payable in a currency not legal tender at the payment location, Art. 802 lets payment be made in local currency at the presentment-day rate, shifting the choice of rate to the holder if payment is refused — paralleling the bill-of-exchange foreign-currency rules.

## Practical Takeaway

Tracking the presentment deadline is the single most consequential step for a cheque holder in Turkey: it determines whether recourse rights, revocation exposure, and full kambiyo protection remain available.
