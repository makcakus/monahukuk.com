Articles 211 through 216 of the Turkish Commercial Code (TTK) open the Code's regulation of the general partnership (kollektif şirket) — the first specific company type the Code addresses after its general provisions on commercial companies. A general partnership is where the concept of unlimited personal liability is at its most direct.

## Definition

Under Art. 211, a general partnership is formed among natural persons to operate a commercial enterprise under a joint trade name, where no partner's liability toward creditors is limited. This is the defining feature separating it from capital companies, and it shapes the internal partner relationships and third-party dealings addressed elsewhere in this part of the Code. Along with the limited partnership, it is classified as a "person company" under Art. 124.

## Formal Requirements for the Partnership Agreement

The partnership agreement must be in writing, with signatures either notarized or executed in person before the commercial registry director (Art. 212) — a heightened formality reflecting that partners are entering unlimited personal liability. The agreement must include the partners' identities, the fact that the entity is a general partnership, its trade name and registered office, a defined business purpose, each partner's committed capital contribution, and the signing authority of the persons representing the company (Art. 213).

## Defects in the Agreement: Default to an Ordinary Partnership

Where the agreement fails the required written form or omits a mandatory term, the entity is not automatically void — instead, it is treated as an ordinary (adi) partnership under the Code of Obligations, a pragmatic solution that avoids unwinding a functioning business over a formal defect (Art. 214). This softer outcome, however, is qualified where the entity has begun operating before registration.

## Registration and the Consequence of Skipping It

Founders must file the notarized partnership agreement with the commercial registry within fifteen days of notarization (Art. 215). Where the entity begins operating under the partnership's name before this registration is completed, the partners become jointly and severally liable to third parties for the transactions undertaken — a liability regime more severe than the default ordinary-partnership treatment, and one that applies even where no formal partnership agreement exists at all, so long as the parties operated under a joint name (Art. 216). Turkish courts have confirmed that a trade name including one partner's personal name does not, by itself, make that individual personally the debtor in enforcement proceedings — liability attaches to the partnership as a legal entity.

Foreign investors considering a Turkish general partnership structure should weigh the unlimited personal liability exposure carefully against the more common capital-company alternatives, and ensure registration is completed promptly to avoid the heightened pre-registration liability regime.
