TTK Art. 1136-1137 close the Code's time charter chapter by fixing how hire gets paid and who answers for what goes wrong.

## Hire Payment and Security

Under Art. 1136, hire is paid monthly in advance once commercial management is actually handed to the charterer, and no hire is owed for any period the ship is off-hire for 24 hours or more due to being commercially unusable — a rule that closely mirrors the bareboat charter's 24-hour threshold. Art. 1136(3) gives the disponent owner the same three-layer security package seen in bareboat charter: a lien over the charterer's movable property, a pledge over freight receivables, and a further lien under Art. 1201 — again, third-party freight debtors are only bound once notified.

## Liability for Commercial Decisions and Late Return

Art. 1137(1) closes the loop opened by the commercial-management split in the earlier provisions: the charterer is liable for losses the disponent owner suffers arising from commercial management decisions — a bad port choice, accepting cargo contrary to the contract, or mishandling fuel procurement — while losses from technical management or crew navigational fault stay with the disponent owner, who retains possession. Art. 1137(2) requires redelivering the ship at the agreed place and condition; late redelivery triggers double the hire rate for the entire delay period (no fifteen-day grace period as in bareboat charter), unless the disponent owner proves higher actual loss.

## Practical Takeaway

For time charterers, Art. 1137's liability rule means commercial decisions carry real financial exposure if they cause the disponent owner loss — and Turkish courts have confirmed that whether a party actually holds "charterer" status for costs like discharge-port expenses is a question of legal characterization the court can resolve from the documentary record, without necessarily needing expert appraisal.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### When is hire paid under a time charter?

Under TTK Article 1136/1, <b>hire is payable monthly and in advance, starting from the day the commercial management of the vessel is actually placed at the disposal of the charterer under the terms of the contract</b>. That is, hire runs and is paid in advance not from the date the contract is signed, but from the moment the vessel actually comes under the charterer's commercial management.

### For which damage is the charterer liable?

Under TTK Article 1137/1, <b>the charterer is liable for damage suffered by the disponent owner arising from the commercial management of the vessel</b>. Liability is confined to the sphere of <b>commercial management</b>: voyage instructions, port selection, cargo type and loading decisions. The <b>technical management</b> of the vessel, on the other hand, remains with the disponent owner under Article 1133; the risk relating to the machinery, the hull and the crew belongs to them.

### What is the fundamental difference between a time charter and a bareboat charter?

The scope of control. Under a <b>bareboat charter</b>, the vessel is handed over to the charterer unmanned; the charterer employs the crew themselves and is treated as the shipowner under Article 1061/2. Under a <b>time charter</b>, on the other hand, the disponent owner under Article 1131 hands over <b>the commercial management of a manned vessel</b>; technical management, the crew and the vessel's seaworthiness remain with the disponent owner. This distinction determines the entire allocation of liability and expenses.
