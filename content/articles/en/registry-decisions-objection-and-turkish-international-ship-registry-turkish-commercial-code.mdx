TTK Art. 993-995 close out the Ship Registry chapter with three short but structurally important provisions: how registry decisions are challenged, where the technical detail comes from, and how the ordinary registry relates to Turkey's separate international registry.

## Judicial, Not Administrative, Review

Art. 993 routes challenges to registry office decisions through the general commercial-registry objection procedure (Art. 34) — meaning disputes go to the commercial court supervising the registry office, not an administrative appeals body. This follows naturally from the registry's structure: since registry offices already operate under judicial supervision, judicial review of their decisions is the consistent next step. Turkish courts have confirmed this route only becomes available once the registrar has actually issued a decision — silence alone, without a formal refusal, is not enough to trigger an objection case.

## Technical Rules Left to Regulation

Art. 994 confirms that the Code sets the framework — the "what" — while a regulation issued by the President covers the "how": registry setup, staff qualifications, documentation formats, and correction procedures. That regulation cannot override or narrow the Code's substantive rules, such as the reliance principle or the mortgage-consent requirement for deregistration.

## The Parallel International Registry

Art. 995 preserves a separate regime entirely: the Turkish International Ship Registry (TUGS), created by a 1999 law to offer a competitive, tax-advantaged alternative to "flag of convenience" registries abroad, aimed at keeping Turkish and foreign owners from flagging out to Panama, Liberia, or similar jurisdictions. TUGS operates in parallel with — not as a substitute for — the ordinary registry covered throughout this chapter. Turkish courts have upheld the administration's power to move a vessel from TUGS back to the ordinary registry ex officio where it never actually met TUGS eligibility criteria, even after many years of TUGS registration, while confirming that accrued tax benefits during that period remain protected.

## Practical Takeaway

For owners weighing TUGS versus the ordinary Turkish registry, the choice carries real tax and administrative consequences, but eligibility is strictly defined — long-standing registration on the wrong registry does not itself create a vested right to stay there.
