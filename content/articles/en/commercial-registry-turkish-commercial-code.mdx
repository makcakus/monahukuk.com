Articles 24 through 38 of the Turkish Commercial Code (TTK) establish the institutional framework for the commercial registry — the mechanism through which a merchant's identity, representation authority, and capital structure become publicly verifiable. Far from being mere paperwork, the registry has direct substantive legal effects: it determines when facts about a merchant become binding on third parties and when good-faith reliance on registered information is protected.

## Structure and Registration Procedure

Commercial registry offices operate under the supervision of chambers of commerce and industry, with the State and the relevant chamber jointly and severally liable for damage caused by errors in keeping the registry (Art. 24-25). Registration is generally made on request, via petition, within fifteen days of the triggering event (one month for those outside the registry's jurisdiction) (Art. 27-30).

## The Registrar's Review Duty and Provisional Registration

Under Art. 32, the registrar must substantively review whether legal requirements for registration are met — including, for legal entities, whether the company agreement complies with mandatory provisions. Where a matter's registrability depends on a pending court decision, provisional registration is available, but it is automatically struck if the applicant fails to prove within three months that court proceedings have been initiated. Turkish courts have confirmed that the power to order provisional registration belongs exclusively to the registrar — a court cannot order it directly as an interim measure.

## The Consequences of Registration and Publication: Reliance Protection

The most consequential rule is Art. 36: registered facts take legal effect against third parties only from the day following their publication in the Turkish Trade Registry Gazette — registration alone is not enough. After publication, third parties cannot claim ignorance of the registered fact. Conversely, a fact that should have been but was not registered or published can only be invoked against a third party if that party actually knew or should have known of it. Art. 37 adds a "reliance on appearance" principle: where the registered record and the published notice diverge, third parties acting in good faith are protected in relying on the published version.

Foreign investors should note that changes in a Turkish company's signing authority only bind third parties from the date of Trade Registry Gazette publication — a document signed by an outgoing representative before that date can still validly bind the company.
