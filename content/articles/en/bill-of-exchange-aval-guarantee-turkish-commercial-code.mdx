TTK Art. 700-702 regulate "aval," the personal guarantee unique to negotiable-instrument law that secures payment of a bill of exchange. Aval resembles suretyship under general contract law in substance, but it is a unilateral, form-bound kambiyo undertaking rather than a written contract, and — critically — its validity does not depend on the underlying debt.

## Who Can Give Aval and How It Must Be Shown

Under Art. 700, payment of a bill may be guaranteed in whole or in part by a third party unconnected to the instrument, or by someone who has already signed it (such as an endorser), who may then hold more than one role on the same instrument. Art. 701 sets the form: aval is written on the bill or an allonge with the words "for aval" or an equivalent, signed by the guarantor. Turkish courts read this loosely in the holder's favor — any signature on the face of the bill other than the drawee's or drawer's counts as an aval, even without the word "aval" itself, and even a mislabeled notation such as "guarantor" (kefil) is recharacterized as aval. If the aval does not state on whose behalf it was given, it is deemed given for the drawer (Art. 701/4).

## Independence From the Underlying Debt

The defining feature of aval, confirmed by a 2018 Court of Cassation Grand General Assembly decision, is that the guarantor's undertaking remains valid even if the guaranteed debt is void for any reason other than a defect in form — the guarantor can only raise defenses tied to formal deficiencies of the instrument itself, not personal defenses (such as want of consideration) available to the party they guaranteed. This makes aval a materially stronger security device than ordinary suretyship. A guarantor who pays acquires, by operation of law, the rights of the person they guaranteed against everyone liable to that person (Art. 702/3) — a statutory subrogation requiring no separate assignment.

## Practical Relevance

Aval applies equally to promissory notes and checks via TTK Art. 778 and Art. 818, and in practice most Turkish case law on aval arises from note and check disputes rather than bills of exchange themselves — particularly disputes over whether a company director had authority to guarantee their own personal debt using the company as avalist. Courts have held such self-dealing invalid absent clear authorization, applying the duty of care owed by company managers.

## Practical Takeaway

Anyone extending credit against a Turkish bill, note, or check with an aval guarantee should verify the aval's form and confirm on whose behalf it runs, since the guarantor's liability tracks the guaranteed party's liability almost without exception — making aval a highly reliable, but also highly binding, credit enhancement tool.
