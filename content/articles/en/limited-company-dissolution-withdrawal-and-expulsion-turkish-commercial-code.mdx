TTK Art. 636-640 open the chapter on dissolution and departure, reflecting how deeply personal trust between partners still matters even in a capital company like the Turkish limited company (limited şirket).

## How a Limited Company Dissolves

Art. 636 lists a closed set of grounds: a cause specified in the articles, a general assembly resolution, bankruptcy, or other statutory grounds — including a court-ordered dissolution where a mandatory organ has long been missing or the assembly cannot convene. Most significant in practice is Art. 636/3: any partner may seek court-ordered dissolution for just cause, but the court is not bound to dissolve the company — it may instead order the claimant partner's exit against payment of the fair value of their share, or another appropriate remedy. Turkish courts define "just cause" as a breakdown of trust between partners severe enough to make continuing the relationship unconscionable under good-faith principles, and in two-partner companies have accepted that the case can proceed without separately naming the company as defendant.

## Voluntary Withdrawal and Expulsion

Art. 638 lets the articles grant a contractual withdrawal right, and independently gives every partner a right to sue for withdrawal on just cause even absent such a clause. Other partners learning of a withdrawal request may join it within one month (Art. 639), subject to equal treatment among departing partners. Expulsion works differently: the articles may specify grounds for expulsion by general assembly resolution, subject to a three-month deadline for the expelled partner to challenge it (Art. 640/1-2); alternatively, the company itself may seek court-ordered expulsion for just cause (Art. 640/3) — but only after the general assembly first adopts a qualified-majority resolution authorizing the lawsuit, a procedural prerequisite Turkish courts enforce strictly. Notably, courts have held that merely exercising one's own legal rights (e.g., suing for unpaid manager fees) does not by itself constitute just cause for expulsion, since constitutional protections for access to courts and fair compensation apply.

## Practical Takeaway

A foreign investor in a Turkish limited company with a deteriorating partner relationship should recognize that Turkish courts favor keeping the company alive: rather than dissolving the company, a judge can order the departing partner bought out at fair value — a remedy worth proposing early in any dispute rather than only litigating for full dissolution.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can a shareholder in a limited liability company bring an action for dissolution on just grounds?

Yes. Under TTK Article 636/3 <b>where just grounds exist, every shareholder</b> may ask the court to dissolve the company. Unlike the position in joint stock companies (Article 531), <b>no capital-share threshold is required</b> here: even a single shareholder may bring the action. The court is not bound to order dissolution: it may decide <b>that the plaintiff shareholder be paid the true value of their share and be excluded from the company, or adopt another appropriate and acceptable solution</b>.

### How does one withdraw from a limited liability company?

There are three routes. <b>Contractual withdrawal</b> (Article 638/1): the articles of association may confer a right of withdrawal on the shareholders and subject it to conditions. <b>Withdrawal on just grounds</b> (Article 638/2): where just grounds exist, any shareholder may ask the court to order their withdrawal. <b>Joining a withdrawal</b> (Article 639): when one shareholder brings a withdrawal action, the others may join on the same terms. In every case a right to a <b>departure payment</b> arises under Article 641.

### Can a shareholder be expelled from the company?

Yes. Under TTK Article 640/1 <b>the articles of association may specify grounds on which a shareholder may be expelled from the company by a general assembly resolution.</b> A shareholder may bring an action to annul an expulsion resolution taken on one of those grounds <b>within three months</b> of its notification to them through a notary. Expulsion based on a just ground not provided for in the articles is possible only <b>by a court decision</b>.
