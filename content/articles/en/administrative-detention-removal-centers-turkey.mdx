Not every foreigner subject to a deportation order is taken to a removal centre. Law No. 6458 on Foreigners and International Protection (YUKK) treats administrative detention — a deprivation of liberty — as a separate decision, restricts the grounds on which it may be ordered, and provides an expedited judicial review. The 2019 amendment introduced by Law No. 7196 added "alternatives to administrative detention" to the system. This article sets out the legal framework, the time limits, the appeal routes, and the alternatives.

## Apprehension and the Forty-Eight Hour Decision Period

Article 57 of YUKK requires that foreigners falling within Article 54 be reported to the governorate immediately upon apprehension by law enforcement, so that a decision may be taken. Where a deportation order is considered necessary, the governorate issues it, and **the assessment and decision period may not exceed forty-eight hours**. That limit is placed on the interval of uncertainty between apprehension and the determination of administrative status.

Foreigners against whom a detention order is issued are taken to removal centres by the apprehending law enforcement unit **within forty-eight hours**. The two periods are independent: the first concerns the decision, the second the transfer.

## When May Detention Be Ordered?

The existence of a deportation order is not in itself a ground for detention. Article 57(2) permits a detention order only in the enumerated situations:

- A risk of absconding or disappearing,
- Breach of the rules on entry into or exit from Türkiye,
- Use of forged or false documents,
- Failure, without an acceptable excuse, to leave within the period granted for departure,
- Constituting a threat to public order, public security, or public health.

Absent any of these grounds, a detention order is unlawful. The Law also gives the governorate a second option: instead of ordering detention for such persons, it may impose **alternatives to administrative detention** under Article 57/A. Indeed, Article 57/A(3) makes the imposition of one or more of those obligations **mandatory** for foreigners who fall within Article 57(2) but are not detained.

## The Maximum Period: Six Months and Extension

Administrative detention in removal centres **may not exceed six months** as a rule. Where removal proceedings cannot be completed because the foreigner refuses to cooperate or fails to provide accurate information or documents about their country, the period **may be extended by a maximum of six further months**. The confinement of the extension grounds to these two situations matters: administrative slowness or protracted consular correspondence is not, by itself, a ground for extension.

Alongside the ceiling there is a regular review mechanism. Under paragraph four, the governorate assesses **every month** whether continued detention remains necessary; where required, it does not wait for the thirty-day interval. Detention is **terminated immediately** for foreigners whose continued detention is not considered necessary, and alternatives under Article 57/A are imposed instead.

## Notification and Information Duties

The detention order, any extension, and the results of the monthly reviews are notified **with reasons** to the foreigner or their legal representative or lawyer. Where the detained person is not represented by a lawyer, they or their legal representative are informed of the outcome, the appeal procedures, and the applicable time limits. In practice, a failure to give this information can be decisive in any dispute over whether an appeal period ever began to run.

## Appeal to the Magistrate Judge

The route against a detention order does not lie in the administrative courts. Under Article 57(6), the detained person, their legal representative, or their lawyer may apply to the **magistrate judge in criminal matters** (*sulh ceza hâkimi*). The essential features are:

- No separate time limit is prescribed; an application may be made for as long as detention continues.
- **The application does not suspend the detention.**
- If the petition is submitted to the administration, it is transmitted to the competent judge immediately.
- The judge concludes the review **within five days** and the decision is **final**.
- A **fresh application** may be made on the ground that the conditions for detention have ceased or changed. A first refusal therefore does not close the route; every change in circumstances opens a new application.

Paragraph seven further provides that those challenging administrative detention who cannot meet lawyers' fees are, upon request, provided with legal services under Law No. 1136 on Attorneyship. This is the direct statutory basis for the bar association's legal aid mechanism.

The route against the deportation order itself is different; that decision is [challenged before the administrative court](/en/articles/deportation-order-appeal-turkey). The two are not alternatives but parallel forms of review.

## Alternatives to Administrative Detention

Article 57/A, added in 2019, lists seven obligations that may be applied instead of detention: residence at a specified address, reporting obligations, family-based return, return counselling, voluntary service in works of public benefit, a financial guarantee, and electronic monitoring. Where one or more of these is applied, the period **may not exceed twenty-four months**.

Notification and information duties apply to the alternatives as well. The appeal route, however, is narrower: only a foreigner subject to **electronic monitoring**, their legal representative, or their lawyer may apply to the magistrate judge; the application does not suspend the obligation, and the judge concludes the review within five days with a final decision.

Two consequences deserve particular attention. First, foreigners who fail to comply with the alternatives **may be placed in administrative detention**. Second, where a guarantee has been imposed and the foreigner does not leave Türkiye within the guarantee period — save in cases of force majeure or where court proceedings have not concluded — the guarantee is **forfeited to the Treasury**.

## Detention Time Is Not a Residence Breach

A frequent practical question is how time in a removal centre affects the foreigner's residence status. Article 26 of YUKK resolves the point expressly: time spent in removal centres under administrative detention **does not count as a breach of the residence permit period**. Any existing residence permits may be cancelled; those without a foreigner identification number may be issued one without the requirement of a residence permit.

Article 57(8) further provides that electronic and communication devices of detained foreigners may be examined **for the purpose of establishing their nationality**, and that data obtained may not be used for any other purpose. Purpose limitation is the essential test of the lawfulness of such an examination.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Should I apply to the administrative court against a detention order?

No. A detention order is challenged before the magistrate judge in criminal matters, who decides within five days. The administrative court is the forum for an action to annul the deportation order. The two applications are made separately.

### My first appeal was rejected — can I apply again?

Yes. The Law expressly allows a fresh application to the magistrate judge on the ground that the conditions for detention have ceased or changed. A change in health, family circumstances, or the practical impossibility of removal can each support a new application.

### Can detention exceed one year?

No. The maximum is six months, extendable by up to six further months only where proceedings cannot be completed because the foreigner refuses to cooperate or withholds accurate information or documents. The overall ceiling is twelve months.

### I cannot afford a lawyer — what can I do?

Article 57(7) of YUKK provides that those challenging administrative detention who cannot meet lawyers' fees are, on request, provided with legal services under the Attorneyship Law. You may apply to the legal aid unit of the bar association in your province.
