Mediation in lease disputes has been compulsory since 1 September 2023. But in this field the real question is not "whether an application was made" but **when it was made** — because the Court of Cassation does not treat an application made before the right to bring an action has arisen as satisfying the condition of action.

This article focuses on the timing question in eviction actions and on the limits of scope. For the general framework of lease mediation, see also our article on [mandatory mediation in rental disputes](/articles/mandatory-mediation-rental-disputes-turkey).

## Scope: Everything Arising from the Lease Relationship

Article 18/B/1(a) of Law No. 6325 keeps the scope broad: **disputes arising from a lease relationship**. Because no types of action are enumerated, the scope covers almost every claim connected with the lease relationship:

- actions for determination of rent and for adaptation;
- collection of rent arrears;
- eviction actions (need, undertaking to vacate, reconstruction and development, two justified notices, the tenant having another dwelling);
- return of the deposit (security payment);
- compensation for damage and defects to the leased property;
- useful and necessary expenses incurred by the tenant.

No distinction is drawn between residential and roofed-workplace leases; **commercial leases are also within scope**.

## The Only Exception: Eviction Through Enforcement Without Judgment

The only exception written into the provision is: **the provisions on eviction of leased immovable property through enforcement without judgment under Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law No. 2004.**

That is, no application to a mediator is required for **eviction proceedings without judgment** commenced before the enforcement office under Articles 269 et seq. of that Law for non-payment of rent. The landlord can proceed directly by serving a payment order.

By contrast, debate continues where the court is seised following an objection to the proceedings. In practice there is a tendency to require the mediation condition for every claim taken to court; it is therefore prudent not to skip the mediation stage where the court is to be approached for removal of the objection and eviction.

## The Timing Trap: Do Not Apply Before the Right of Action Arises

This is the most critical and least known rule of lease mediation.

In its decision resolving a divergence between chambers of the regional courts of justice under Article 35 of Law No. 5235, the 3rd Civil Chamber of the Court of Cassation laid down the following criterion: in **eviction actions based on need and on reconstruction/development** brought under Article 350 of the Turkish Code of Obligations, the mandatory mediation application must be made **after the right to bring the eviction action has arisen — that is, within the period for bringing the action**.

The concrete result: in fixed-term contracts, if the landlord applies to mediation before the lease term expires, the condition of action is not regarded as satisfied; the application is treated as "not made" and the process must be started afresh. The reasoning emphasised that the period for bringing an action concerns public policy, that an application made before any dispute has arisen would become a formality, and that a process whose cost is met from the budget of the Ministry of Justice would be set in motion unnecessarily.

The decision's scope is limited to eviction actions under Article 350; in claims such as determination of rent or collection of rent arrears, an application may be made as soon as the dispute arises. But since the Chamber stated that the criterion accords with its own settled practice, it is prudent to observe the same approach for other eviction grounds whose limitation period is set by statute.

Combined with the narrow time regime of eviction actions, this rule creates a serious risk. For eviction based on need, for example, the action must under Article 350 be brought **within one month** of the end of the term in fixed-term contracts; under Article 353, if the landlord has notified the tenant in writing within that period that an action will be brought, the period for bringing the action is deemed extended by one lease year. A mediation application made before the lease period ends can waste that period too.

The practical rule: **first establish the date on which the right of action arose, then apply.**

## Protection of Limitation and Forfeiture Periods

An application made at the right time provides strong protection: in the period from the application to the mediation bureau until the date the final minutes are drawn up, **limitation is suspended and no forfeiture period runs**.

Thanks to this protection, the one-month periods in eviction actions freeze for the duration of the mediation. Since the mediator must conclude the process within three weeks (four in compulsory circumstances), the delay is limited.

## Transitional Provision: Actions Before 1 September 2023

Under Provisional Article 3 of Law No. 7445, the provisions of Article 18/B on mediation as a condition of action do not apply to **actions pending** before the first-instance courts, the regional courts of justice and the Court of Cassation as at the date the article entered into force.

The 3rd Civil Chamber applies that provision by comparing the date the action was brought with 01.09.2023: the mediation condition is not required in actions brought before that date.

## If You Settle: The Annotation Is Compulsory

If a settlement is reached in a lease dispute, under Article 18/B/3 **obtaining the annotation** as to the enforceability of the settlement document **is compulsory**. This is an exception to the general rule in Article 18/4 (no annotation required where the document is signed with lawyers).

The competent court is divided in two: for settlement documents relating to immovable property, the civil court of peace of **the place where the property is located**; for other settlement documents, that of **the place where the mediator performed the duty**.

The point to watch in drafting the settlement document is that the obligation must be **amenable to enforcement**. The 12th Civil Chamber of the Court of Cassation has stated that a debtor **cannot be compelled to conclude a lease contract** through enforcement proceedings based on a mediation settlement document that merely evidences a promise and contains no constitutive judicial pronouncement; an enforcement office has no constitutive power such as the formation of a contract.

Accordingly, an eviction settlement should contain a directly enforceable obligation such as "the tenant shall vacate the property on [date]", and expressions requiring a constitutive result, such as "the parties shall conclude a new lease contract", should be avoided.

## Practical Notes for Tenants and Landlords in Antalya

**The competent bureau is at the place where the property is located.** For a property in Antalya, the Antalya Mediation Bureau is competent. An application to the wrong bureau restarts the process if the other side objects at the first meeting.

**Sessions are conducted in Turkish.** The Law provides no right to a state-funded interpreter. For foreign tenants and landlords in Antalya, attending the sessions with bilingual counsel is decisive for understanding the content of the document signed.

**Include a jurisdiction clause in the lease.** Specifying the competent court in the contract clarifies the jurisdiction of both the mediation bureau and the court from the outset and prevents later arguments. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q: I want to evict my tenant — must I go to a mediator first?

If you are going to bring an eviction action before the court, yes — but only after your right to bring the action has arisen. If you are going to commence eviction proceedings without judgment under the Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law for non-payment of rent, no mediation is required.

### Q: I applied to mediation early — what should I do?

In eviction actions based on Article 350 of the Turkish Code of Obligations, an application made before the right of action arose does not satisfy the condition; the process must be restarted. Make a fresh application after the date the right of action arises and attach the final minutes of that second process to the statement of claim. Since the period for bringing the action does not run during the mediation, an application made at the right time causes you no loss in terms of time limits.

### Q: Is an action for determination of rent also within scope?

Yes. Actions for determination of rent and for adaptation are disputes arising from a lease relationship and fall within mandatory mediation.

### Q: I cannot get my deposit back — can I bring an action directly?

No. The return of the deposit (security payment) is a dispute arising from a lease relationship and you must apply to a mediator before bringing an action.

### Q: If the tenant does not come to mediation, does eviction become easier?

It does not produce eviction directly; but where no session could be held because the parties did not attend, the mediator draws up the final minutes and the judicial route opens. In addition, the non-attending party is identified in the minutes and, even if successful in the action to be brought, is liable for half of the other side's litigation costs and half of the attorney's fee determined under the Minimum Attorney's Fee Tariff.
