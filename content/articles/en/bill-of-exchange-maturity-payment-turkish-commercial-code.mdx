TTK Art. 703-712 govern how and when a bill of exchange must be paid — the practical core of the instrument's function.

## Four Maturity Types, No Others

Art. 703 recognizes only four maturity structures: at sight, at a fixed period after sight, at a fixed period after issuance, or on a fixed date. A bill stating maturity any other way, or showing successive maturities, is void — though Turkish courts hold that a maturity clause whose starting point cannot be determined (e.g., an ambiguous "90 days") is simply disregarded as unwritten, rather than voiding the whole instrument, provided the bill still qualifies as payable at sight under Art. 704.

## At-Sight Bills and Presentment Windows

A sight bill must be presented for payment within one year of issuance (Art. 704) — a period the drawer may shorten or lengthen and endorsers may only shorten. For bills payable a fixed period after sight, maturity runs from the acceptance date or, absent one, from the protest date, or — if neither exists — from the last day of the period allowed for presentment for acceptance (Art. 705). Art. 706 supplies detailed rules for computing month-based maturities (e.g., "half a month" means fifteen days), and Art. 707 addresses calendar mismatches between issuance and payment locations.

## Presentment, Partial Payment, and Timing Rules

The holder must present the bill for payment on the due date or within the following two business days (Art. 708); presentment to a clearing house counts as presentment to the drawee. The payer may demand the bill back marked "paid" (Art. 709); the holder cannot refuse partial payment, and the payer may require it to be noted on the instrument. Under Art. 710, a holder is never obliged to accept early payment, and a drawee who pays early bears the resulting risk — but a drawee who pays at maturity in good faith is discharged even against a forged endorsement, since the payer's duty is limited to checking the formal chain of endorsements, not verifying signatures' authenticity. Turkish courts apply this protection symmetrically to protect both holders and paying debtors.

## Foreign Currency and Deposit

Where a bill is payable in a currency not legal tender at the place of payment, Art. 711 lets the debtor pay in local currency at the maturity-date rate, but shifts the choice of rate to the holder if the debtor is in default. If the holder fails to present the bill within the deadline, Art. 712 lets the debtor deposit the sum with a bank at the holder's cost and risk — a practical exit confirmed in practice by Turkish courts ordering such deposits.

## Practical Takeaway

Selecting the correct maturity type, tracking the two-business-day presentment window, and understanding the debtor's deposit remedy are essential to preserving — or defending against — recourse rights on a Turkish bill of exchange.
