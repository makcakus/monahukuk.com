TTK Art. 1271 closes the Chapter on sea passenger carriage with a mandatory-law backstop, preventing the entire liability architecture built across Art. 1256–1270 from being quietly dismantled by fine print in a ticket.

## Voiding Pre-Incident Terms That Undercut Passenger Protection

Art. 1271(1) voids any term in a contract made *before* the incident causing death, injury, or baggage loss that either (i) eliminates a liable party's responsibility outright, (ii) reduces the Chapter's liability limits — except for the deductible mechanism specifically permitted under Art. 1263(4) — or (iii) shifts the burden of proof away from the carrier or actual carrier. This mirrors, in structure, the mandatory-law protections the series encountered in the cargo context (Art. 1243). The provision also confirms severability: voiding a problematic clause does not invalidate the passenger carriage contract as a whole.

## Voiding Pre-Claim Jurisdiction and Arbitration Agreements

Art. 1271(2) adds a distinct and stricter rule: jurisdiction (forum-selection) and arbitration agreements made *before a claim arises* are simply not valid. This closes off a specific abuse: without this rule, a carrier could embed a clause in every ticket routing any future dispute to a distant or inconvenient forum, or to arbitration under terms that would practically deter an ordinary passenger — or a grieving family — from pursuing a valid claim. Critically, the timing qualifier is the operative element: an agreement on jurisdiction or arbitration made *after* a specific dispute has already arisen remains valid, since by then the passenger genuinely understands what is at stake.

## Notably Stronger Than the Cargo-Context Rule

This provision is, on this specific point, even more protective than the equivalent cargo-context freedom given to sophisticated commercial parties (such as charterparty parties under Art. 1245, who may contract out of many mandatory protections). The difference reflects a real power imbalance: an ordinary ticket-buying passenger typically cannot negotiate carriage terms the way a commercial charterer can — they either accept the standard terms or forgo the journey.

## Practical Takeaway

For carriers, standard ticket terms attempting to cap liability below the statutory minimum, shift the burden of proof, or lock passengers into a pre-selected forum or arbitration mechanism are void and unenforceable — but the carriage contract itself remains intact. For passengers, any forum or arbitration clause encountered in ticket terms prior to an incident offers no binding effect; post-incident settlement or dispute-resolution agreements are a different matter entirely.

No confirmed case law applying Art. 1271's specific voiding mechanism has been identified — consistent with this type of mandatory-law backstop typically functioning as an unseen background protection rather than becoming its own separately litigated issue.
