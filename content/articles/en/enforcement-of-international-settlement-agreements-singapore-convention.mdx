*Article 17/A – (Added: 28/3/2023-7445/33)*

*(1) For the performance of settlement agreements drawn up as a result of mediation within the scope of the Convention adopted by Law No. 7282 of 25/2/2021 on the Approval of the United Nations Convention on International Settlement Agreements Resulting from Mediation, it is compulsory to obtain the annotation of enforceability from the commercial court of first instance.*

*(2) The annotation of enforceability may be requested from the court of the place agreed by the parties or, where no place has been agreed, successively from the court of the other party's domicile in Türkiye, the court of the place where they reside, or — if they have neither a domicile nor a place of residence in Türkiye — from one of the courts of Ankara, Istanbul or Izmir.*

*(3) The examination as to the granting of the annotation of enforceability is made on the file, in accordance with the provisions of the Convention and Article 18. The court may hold a hearing where necessary, stating its reasons.*

The long-known weak point of mediation in cross-border commercial disputes was the recognition and enforcement of the settlement. While established international mechanisms exist for the recognition and enforcement of foreign court judgments and for the enforcement of foreign arbitral awards, a settlement agreement signed as a result of mediation was treated like an ordinary contract; in the event of breach, a fresh action had to be brought. The United Nations instrument known as the **Singapore Convention** was prepared to close that gap; Article 17/A is the Convention's procedural counterpart in Turkish law.

## Türkiye's Accession Process

The Convention was signed on behalf of the Republic of Türkiye in Singapore on **7 August 2019**. Its approval was found appropriate by **Law No. 7282** of 25.02.2021, and the date of entry into force for Türkiye was set as **11 April 2022** by Presidential Decision No. 5235 of 24.02.2022.

The domestic procedure for the Convention was regulated a year later, by Article 17/A added to Law No. 6325 by Law No. 7445 of 28.03.2023. This two-stage structure shows that Article 17/A is not a rule of substantive law but entirely a rule of **procedure**: the Convention determines which settlements may be enforced, and Article 17/A determines where and how to apply.

## The Scope of the Convention: Which Settlements?

Article 17/A applies only to settlement documents drawn up "within the scope of the Convention". The Convention's scope is limited in two respects.

**Commercial and international character.** The Convention applies to written and **international** settlement agreements resulting from mediation which resolve a **commercial** dispute. The international character typically arises from the parties' places of business being in different States, or from a substantial part of the obligations, or the place with which the dispute is most closely connected, being in a State other than that of the parties' places of business.

**Exclusions.** Agreements arising from consumer transactions and agreements relating to family, succession and employment law fall outside the Convention. Also excluded are agreements approved by a court or concluded before a court and enforceable as a court judgment, and agreements recorded and enforceable as an arbitral award — for that second group, their own recognition and enforcement regimes already apply.

These limits matter in practice: a mediation settlement between a foreign employer and an employee working in Türkiye falls outside the Convention because it concerns employment law, and is subject not to Article 17/A but to [the general enforceability regime of Article 18](/articles/settlement-agreement-and-enforceability-annotation-turkish-law).

## The Competent Court

Article 17/A departs from the general regime of Article 18 on two points.

**Subject-matter jurisdiction.** While under the general rule the annotation of enforceability is obtained from the civil court of peace (Art. 18/2), for settlements within the scope of the Convention the annotation is obtained from the **commercial court of first instance**. That choice is consistent with the commercial character of the settlements covered.

**Territorial jurisdiction.** Article 17/A/2 establishes a tiered system. In order: the court of the place agreed by the parties; failing agreement, the court of the other party's domicile in Türkiye; failing that, the court of the place where they reside; and if they have neither a domicile nor a place of residence in Türkiye, one of the courts of **Ankara, Istanbul or Izmir**. At the final tier the applicant is given a choice among the three centres.

## The Examination: On the File, but by the Convention's Criteria

Under Article 17/A/3 the examination is as a rule made on the file; the court may hold a hearing where necessary, **stating its reasons**. Holding a hearing is exceptional and must be reasoned.

The scope of the examination is determined from two sources: the provisions of the Convention and Article 18. That dual reference means the review is broader than the narrow criterion in Article 18/3 (whether the content of the settlement is amenable to mediation and to enforcement). The Convention itself lists the situations in which enforcement may be refused; these include a party's incapacity, the settlement being null and void or incapable of being performed, the obligations having been performed or not being clear under the terms of the settlement, a serious breach by the mediator of the standards applicable to them, and a breach by the mediator of the duty to disclose circumstances relating to their impartiality, as well as contrariety to public policy and the dispute not being capable of settlement by mediation.

The criterion of "breach of the mediator's duty to disclose circumstances relating to impartiality" corresponds directly with Article 9/2 of Law No. 6325: conduct which in domestic law is a ground for the invalidity of the settlement document is, at the international level, a ground for refusing enforcement.

## Practice and Case Law

Article 17/A is a new provision that entered into force on 05.04.2023 with the publication of Law No. 7445, and the number of applications within the scope of the Convention is still limited; no settled case law of the Court of Cassation on the provision has therefore emerged at this stage. Two sources guide practice: on the one hand the text of the Convention itself and UNCITRAL's work, and on the other the settled case law developed around Article 18 on the annotation of enforceability.

The principal principles transferable from the Article 18 case law to Article 17/A are:

- Granting an annotation of enforceability is a **matter of non-contentious jurisdiction**, and the examination is as a rule made on the file (Court of Cassation, 9th Civil Chamber, Case No. 2019/5438, Decision No. 2019/15284, dated 05.09.2019).
- Even where the examination is made on the file, **service of the request on the other party is essential**; deciding without constituting the parties is a ground for reversal (9th Civil Chamber, Case No. 2016/31376, Decision No. 2016/21368, dated 01.12.2016).
- Where the content of the settlement is not amenable to mediation or to enforcement, no annotation may be granted; minutes drawn up immediately after termination and containing a blanket release declaration were held unsuitable on that ground (9th Civil Chamber, Case No. 2016/25300, Decision No. 2016/21744, dated 08.12.2016).
- Even a document bearing an annotation cannot be made the subject of judgment-based enforcement if the claim is made conditional and it contains no operative order (12th Civil Chamber, Case No. 2022/9143, Decision No. 2022/9318, dated 26.09.2022).
- Because the annotation request is a matter of non-contentious jurisdiction, no attorney's fee is awarded in favour of the requesting party; the litigation costs are left on that party (3rd Civil Chamber, Case No. 2024/971, Decision No. 2024/4021, dated 02.12.2024).
- The settlement document is a substantive-law contract in the sense of the law of obligations; invalidity claims are assessed under the provisions applicable to contracts (9th Civil Chamber, Case No. 2026/1306, Decision No. 2026/3216, dated 15.04.2026).

A further point to remember in international files is that Article 5/4 frames the evidentiary bar as applying "in civil litigation and arbitration, regardless of whether the matter relates to the subject of the mediation"; the content of the negotiations is therefore protected in an action later brought in Türkiye as well.
