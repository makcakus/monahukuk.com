Getting approved for Turkish citizenship is a milestone, but the process doesn't end with the decision. The official citizenship ruling — a presidential decree published in the Official Gazette — marks the beginning of a new set of steps. You need to register at the civil registry, obtain a national ID card, and apply for a Turkish passport. For foreign nationals completing naturalization in Antalya, knowing the right order makes the difference between a smooth transition and weeks of unnecessary back-and-forth.

## When the Citizenship Decision Takes Effect

Under Article 20 of Turkish Citizenship Law No. 5901, a citizenship decision takes legal effect from the date it is issued. Your rights as a Turkish citizen begin the moment the decision is made — not when you collect your ID card. In practice, however, those rights only become usable once you have documents to prove them. Article 36 of the same law confirms that your national ID card and passport serve as presumptive proof of citizenship, which is why obtaining them is the immediate priority after the decision. If you want a broader picture of what Turkish citizenship unlocks, our guide to [practical benefits of Turkish citizenship](/en/articles/turkish-citizenship-practical-benefits) sets out the key advantages.

## Registering at the Civil Registry (Nüfus Müdürlüğü)

The Civil Registration Directorate (Nüfus Müdürlüğü) in your district is the first office to visit. Before anything else, you need to be entered into Turkey's civil population records. The documents you typically need include the official notification of your citizenship decision, your current foreign passport, two biometric photographs, and any existing Turkish identity documents. A full breakdown of what applicants typically gather during the citizenship process is covered in our article on [documents required for a Turkish citizenship application](/en/articles/citizenship-application-documents-turkey).

Once registered, you are assigned a TC Kimlik Numarası — a permanent eleven-digit national identification number. Under Article 46 of Population Services Law No. 5490, this number is issued once and cannot be changed. It will be your identifier across all Turkish public services, from healthcare to tax filings and e-government transactions.

## Getting Your Turkish National ID Card

The Turkish national ID card — officially called the Türkiye Cumhuriyeti Kimlik Kartı — is a biometric chip card and your primary identity document inside Turkey. You apply for it at the same Civil Registration Directorate where you registered. There is no charge for the card, and production typically takes a few days once your population record is confirmed.

Keep your foreign passport and residence documents available throughout this period. Until your new Turkish ID is in hand, you will still need them for daily use and any official transactions.

## Applying for a Turkish Passport

Once you hold your national ID card, you can apply for a Turkish passport. Applications go to the Civil Registration Directorate or — in a city like Antalya — to the dedicated Nüfus Müdürlüğü offices that handle passport services. You will need your ID card, biometric photographs, and the applicable fee, which is set annually by the government.

Standard Turkish passports come in different validity periods. Processing usually takes one to two weeks, with expedited options sometimes available. Once you have the passport, you can use it for international travel. If you hold a second nationality, your Turkish passport may open additional destinations — our guide on [dual citizenship status in Turkey](/en/articles/turkish-dual-citizenship-status) explains how Turkey handles multiple nationalities.

## What Happens to Your Children

Under Article 20(2) of Turkish Citizenship Law No. 5901, minor children under the custody of the naturalizing parent acquire Turkish citizenship at the same time, provided the other parent consents. If both parents are naturalizing together, the children are automatically included. If the other parent does not consent, the matter is referred to the court of the country where the parent's habitual residence is located.

Children who were not included at the time of the parent's naturalization can apply independently after reaching adulthood, subject to the standard eligibility requirements. Registering children and obtaining their ID cards follows the same civil registry process, with the parent or guardian submitting documents on their behalf.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q: Should I cancel my old residence permit after getting Turkish citizenship?
Yes. Once you are registered as a Turkish citizen, your foreign residence permit becomes redundant. Holding both simultaneously can cause administrative confusion, particularly in public healthcare and social security systems. Notify the relevant migration authority and cancel the permit promptly.

### Q: Can I travel on my foreign passport while waiting for my Turkish passport?
Yes. The citizenship decision takes effect immediately under Article 20 of Turkish Citizenship Law No. 5901, but there is no obligation to use a Turkish travel document right away. Many newly naturalized citizens in Antalya and across Turkey continue using their foreign passports while their Turkish passport application is processed.

### Q: What if I need proof of citizenship before my ID card is ready?
Under Article 36 of Turkish Citizenship Law No. 5901, a civil registration record (nüfus kayıt örneği) issued by the Civil Registration Directorate also serves as presumptive proof of citizenship. You can request this printout immediately after registration, even before your physical ID card is produced.

### Q: Do minor children need to appear in person at the civil registry?
Generally yes, although practical requirements can vary by location. The parent or guardian submits the child's application paperwork, but the child may need to be present for the biometric photograph required for the ID card. It is best to confirm current requirements at your local Nüfus Müdürlüğü before visiting.
