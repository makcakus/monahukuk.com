Before you sign a property purchase agreement in Turkey, there is one document that deserves your full attention: the occupancy permit, known in Turkish as the *yapı kullanma izni* or more colloquially as the *iskan belgesi*. Many foreign buyers in Antalya and across Turkey focus closely on the title deed but overlook this permit entirely — and that oversight can create real problems down the line.

## What the Occupancy Permit Actually Is

The occupancy permit is an official certificate issued by the local municipality after a building has been inspected and found to comply with the approved architectural project and applicable construction standards. It is not the same as the building permit (*inşaat ruhsatı*), which is issued before construction begins. The occupancy permit comes at the end — once the building is complete and the authorities have verified that it matches what was approved.

Think of it as the government's formal sign-off that a building is safe, legal, and ready to be lived in. Without it, a building may be physically standing, but it exists in a kind of administrative limbo.

## Why It Matters When You Buy Property

The occupancy permit has practical and legal consequences that affect you from the moment you take ownership.

**Utility connections.** Turkish utility providers — electricity, water, and natural gas — generally require the occupancy permit before establishing permanent, individually registered connections in a buyer's name. Without it, residents often rely on temporary or shared arrangements, which are less stable and can create complications.

**Mortgage financing.** Turkish banks and most foreign lenders financing Turkish property will ask for the occupancy permit before approving a loan secured against the property. If you plan to borrow, the absence of iskan can block your financing entirely.

**Full condominium title.** Properties in apartment buildings or complexes typically begin with a construction easement title (*kat irtifakı*) during the building phase. Converting this to full condominium ownership (*kat mülkiyeti*) under Turkish law requires the occupancy permit. Until that conversion happens, your ownership interest is less complete than it should be. Our article on [condominium management for foreign owners](/en/articles/condominium-management-kat-mulkiyeti-foreign-owners) explains this distinction in detail.

**Resale and future transactions.** Any buyer you sell to — and their bank — will expect the permit to be in place. A property without iskan is harder to sell and typically commands a lower price.

## The Risks of Buying Without an Occupancy Permit

Some sellers present a property without iskan as a minor administrative issue that will be resolved "soon." Treat this description with caution. The reasons a building lacks an occupancy permit vary widely — and some of them are serious.

The building may have been constructed partly or entirely without following the approved project. It may have illegal extensions, extra floors, or structural elements that the municipality will not approve. In these situations, obtaining the permit may require costly modifications — or may be impossible without demolishing non-compliant portions.

In more serious cases, buildings that were constructed without proper permits under Turkish planning law can face enforcement action by the municipality, up to and including demolition orders. While enforcement in practice depends on many factors, the legal risk is real and should not be dismissed.

Even in less severe cases — where the permit is pending but achievable — the wait can stretch from months to years, leaving you without stable utilities and a legally incomplete title.

## Your Rights If the Developer Does Not Deliver Iskan

If you are buying a new property — or have bought one — and the developer promised to obtain the occupancy permit but has not, Turkish courts provide meaningful remedies. The Court of Cassation (*Yargıtay*) has consistently ruled that buyers cannot be compelled to accept delivery of a property that lacks the occupancy permit. Courts have ordered developers to pay compensation for delays and, in appropriate cases, have allowed buyers to terminate their contracts and reclaim their money.

The key is to have the developer's obligation to obtain iskan written into the purchase contract. If your contract is silent on this point, proving the obligation is harder — though not impossible. Before signing, always ensure the contract includes a clear commitment by the seller or developer to deliver the property with a valid occupancy permit.

## How to Check Whether a Property Has an Occupancy Permit

There are several reliable ways to verify this before committing to a purchase.

Ask the seller to provide the original occupancy permit certificate. A legitimate seller with nothing to hide will do so without hesitation. You can also visit the municipality's building affairs department (*yapı kontrol müdürlüğü*) where the property is located and request to check the records.

Look at the title deed itself. If it shows *kat mülkiyeti*, the property has completed the iskan process. If it shows *kat irtifakı* — or if the title is for land only — the building either does not yet have iskan or was built without proper permits.

A local lawyer familiar with [buying property in Turkey as a foreigner](/en/articles/buying-property-in-turkey-foreigner-guide) will routinely carry out this check as part of due diligence.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q: Can a property legally be sold in Turkey without an occupancy permit?
Yes — the absence of an occupancy permit does not automatically prevent a title transfer. This is precisely what makes the issue dangerous: you can become the legal owner of a property that has a serious administrative deficiency, and the problem becomes yours to manage.

### Q: I already bought a property and learned there is no iskan. What can I do?
The answer depends on why the permit is missing. If the building can legally obtain iskan — perhaps through minor corrections — you can work with the developer or a contractor. If the permit was promised in your contract and not delivered, you have grounds to seek compensation through Turkish courts.

### Q: Is kat irtifakı title less safe than kat mülkiyeti?
Kat irtifakı is a transitional title that exists before the building is completed and iskan is obtained. It is not necessarily unsafe, but it is incomplete. A property stuck on kat irtifakı indefinitely — because iskan cannot be obtained — is a clear warning sign.

### Q: Will my Turkish residence permit application be affected?
Property-based residence permits require ownership of a property in Turkey, but authorities may scrutinise the status of the title. A clear kat mülkiyeti title with valid iskan is the most straightforward basis for such an application.

### Q: Can the new owner be held responsible for illegal construction they did not know about?
In principle, buyers are not responsible for illegal construction carried out by a previous owner or developer. However, any outstanding enforcement orders or compliance requirements can still affect the property and the owner's use of it.
