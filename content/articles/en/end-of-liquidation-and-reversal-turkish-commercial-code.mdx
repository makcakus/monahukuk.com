Liquidation is normally a one-way street — but Turkish law provides mechanisms both for correcting a premature closure and for reversing dissolution entirely.

## Deregistration and Simplified Procedure

Once liquidation is complete, liquidators request deregistration of the company's trade name from the Trade Registry (Art. 545). Disputes between shareholders and liquidators follow simplified proceedings, with courts required to decide within thirty days; liquidator liability follows the general Art. 553 liability regime, and liquidation-related general assembly resolutions use ordinary (not qualified) quorum (Art. 546).

## Supplementary Liquidation: The "Revival" Procedure

Perhaps the most practically important tool here is Art. 547. If, after a company has been struck off, it turns out that further liquidation steps are actually necessary — a previously unknown claim, asset, or pending lawsuit surfaces — former liquidators, board members, shareholders, or creditors can ask the commercial court to re-register the company just long enough to complete those steps. This "revival" (ihya) procedure is most commonly used to give a deregistered company a legal counterpart in litigation, such as an outstanding labor claim. Turkish courts treat the appointment of a liquidator as a mandatory element of any such revival order — a revival granted without naming a liquidator is incomplete and must be corrected.

## Reversing Dissolution Before Distribution Begins

Art. 548 provides the sole exception to automatic liquidation: if a company dissolved because its term expired or by general assembly resolution — not by court order or bankruptcy — and distribution of assets to shareholders has not yet begun, the general assembly can vote to continue the company. This requires a heavy supermajority: at least 60% of capital. Separately, if a company dissolved through bankruptcy but the bankruptcy is later lifted or resolved through a composition agreement, the company automatically continues, subject to the liquidator confirming that no distribution has started.

## Practical Takeaway

Counterparties dealing with a deregistered Turkish company are not necessarily out of options — the revival procedure under Art. 547 exists precisely for cases where a legitimate claim was overlooked before the company closed its books.
