TTK Art. 788-793 govern how a cheque changes hands, tracking the endorsement rules for bills of exchange but adapted to a cheque's three possible designations — to order, "not to order," or to bearer.

## Three Transfer Regimes

A cheque payable to a named person, with or without "to order," transfers by endorsement plus delivery (Art. 788/1) — the default kambiyo transfer regime. A cheque marked "not to order" can only be transferred by an ordinary civil-law assignment of claim, carrying all the assignment's consequences — meaning the transferee is exposed to any defense the drawee could raise against the transferor, unlike the defense-free acquisition endorsement normally provides. Endorsement may run to the drawer or any obligor already liable on the cheque, who may re-endorse it further (Art. 788/3).

## General Endorsement Rules

An endorsement must be unconditional (conditions are simply disregarded, Art. 789); partial endorsement and endorsement by the drawee bank are void; an endorsement to bearer counts as a blank endorsement; and an endorsement in the drawee's favor is treated merely as a receipt unless made to a different branch of a multi-branch bank.

## Proving Holder Status

Under Art. 790, whoever holds an endorsable cheque is the rightful holder if their title is shown by an unbroken, consecutive chain of endorsements — even if the last endorsement is blank. This is a purely formal test: the holder need not prove each endorser's signature is genuine, only that the chain is unbroken. The Court of Cassation Grand General Assembly has confirmed that a bank merely presenting a cheque as agent (without an endorsement in its own favor) is not thereby a rightful holder, while a bank taking a cheque under its own blank or assignment endorsement is.

## Bearer Cheque Endorsements and Lost Cheques

An endorsement on a bearer cheque makes the endorser liable on recourse but does not convert the cheque into an order instrument — it still transfers by mere delivery (Art. 791). If a cheque leaves a holder's possession involuntarily, the new holder must return it only if they acquired it in bad faith or with gross negligence (Art. 792) — a strong pro-circulation rule reflecting the abstraction principle; Turkish courts place the burden of proving bad faith or gross negligence squarely on the party seeking return.

## Late Endorsement

An endorsement made after a protest (or equivalent) or after the presentment period has expired only has the effect of an assignment of claim (Art. 793) — stripping the transferee of defense-free kambiyo protection. An undated endorsement is presumed timely until proven otherwise.

## Practical Takeaway

Whether a cheque was validly endorsed, and when, often determines whether a holder can pursue kambiyo-specific enforcement or is limited to an ordinary assignment claim exposed to the drawer's defenses.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How is a cheque transferred?

Under TTK Article 788/1, a cheque made payable to a specified person, whether or not expressly bearing the words <b>'to order'</b>, is transferred <b>by endorsement and delivery of possession</b>. A cheque bearing the words 'not to order' or a similar expression, on the other hand, can only be transferred according to the rules on <b>assignment of a claim</b>, and such a transfer produces the effects of an assignment. A bearer cheque, however, is transferred by delivery alone.

### Is everyone holding a cheque a rightful holder?

No. Under TTK Article 790/1, a person holding a negotiable cheque is deemed a rightful holder — <b>even if the last endorsement is a blank endorsement</b> — provided that their right can be traced through <b>an uninterrupted and continuous chain of endorsements</b>. If the chain of endorsements is broken, the holder is not deemed rightful. In this respect, a cancelled endorsement is treated as if it had never been written.

### Is an endorsement made after the presentment period has expired valid?

It is valid, but its effect differs. Under TTK Article 793/1, an endorsement made after the drawing up of a protest, an equivalent determination, or <b>the expiry of the presentment period</b>, produces <b>only the effects of an assignment of the claim</b>. This means that the cambial-law regime under which defences are not enforceable against the transferee no longer applies: the debtor may also raise against the new holder the defences they had against the previous holder.
