*Article 13 – (1) The parties may agree to apply to a mediator before an action is brought or during the hearing of the case. The court may also inform and encourage the parties to apply to a mediator.*

*(2) Unless otherwise agreed, if no positive reply is given within thirty days to one party's proposal to apply to a mediator, that proposal is deemed rejected.*

*(3) (Added: 12/10/2017-7036/21) A party who needs legal aid to meet the mediation fee may benefit from legal aid by decision of the civil court of peace of the place where the mediation bureau is located. Articles 334 to 340 of the Code of Civil Procedure No. 6100 of 12/1/2011 apply by analogy.*

Article 13 has been overshadowed by mandatory mediation; yet it remains of great practical importance today. In every dispute falling outside the scope of mandatory mediation — from compensation for occupational accidents and diseases to actions for cancellation of title and registration, from tort damages to family-law claims concerning property — the parties may nonetheless resort to mediation. This is the voluntary route recognised by Article 13, and its outcome is by no means weaker than that of mandatory mediation: a settlement reached in voluntary mediation likewise extinguishes the right to sue under Article 18/5.

## Two Separate Moments of Application

Article 13/1 regulates two different moments for applying to mediation.

**Before an action is brought.** The parties may agree to apply to a mediator once a dispute arises, or beforehand through a mediation clause inserted in their contract. The 3rd Civil Chamber of the Court of Cassation has held valid a clause in an attorneyship contract providing that "disputes shall first be resolved by an attempt at mediation; if the attempt at mediation produces no result, the dispute shall be resolved by an arbitral panel", and found it lawful to go to mediation first in accordance with that expressed intention.

**During the hearing of the case.** The parties may also agree to apply to a mediator after an action has been brought. In that event Article 15/5 comes into play: where the parties declare that they will jointly apply to a mediator, the proceedings are stayed by the court for a period **not exceeding three months**; that period may be extended by up to three months upon the parties' joint application. In mediation during proceedings, the annotation of enforceability is requested under Article 18/2 not from the civil court of peace of the place where the mediator performed the duty, but **from the court hearing the case**.

## The Court's Power of Encouragement

The final sentence of Article 13/1 gives the court the power to inform and encourage the parties regarding mediation. That power is mirrored in the additions made to the Code of Civil Procedure by Article 35 of Law No. 6325: at the preliminary examination hearing the judge encourages the parties towards settlement **or mediation** (Arts. 137/1 and 140/2-3 CCP); the same obligation exists in simplified proceedings (Art. 320/2 CCP).

Encouragement is not compulsion; the court cannot order the parties to go to mediation, but only informs and suggests. A refusal produces no adverse consequence — the sanction in Article 18/A/11 applicable to mandatory mediation does not apply here.

## The Thirty-Day Reply Period and Implied Rejection

Article 13/2 introduces a rule critical to the practical operation of voluntary mediation: unless otherwise agreed, if **no positive reply is given within thirty days** to one party's proposal to apply to a mediator, the proposal is deemed rejected.

This provision has three consequences. First, silence counts as rejection; the other side need not expressly refuse. Second, the period is default in nature — the parties may agree a shorter or longer period by contract. Third, and most importantly, the deemed rejection of the proposal **does not suspend the limitation period**. The suspension in Article 16/2 depends on the mediation process having begun; and under Article 16/1 the process begins only where the parties are invited to the first meeting and agree to continue the process, this being recorded in minutes. An unanswered proposal therefore does not protect the creditor against the risk of limitation.

The position differs in mandatory mediation: under Article 18/A/15, limitation is suspended and no forfeiture period runs **from the application to the mediation bureau** until the date on which the final minutes are drawn up. The other party's complete non-attendance does not remove this protection.

## The Binding Force and Risk of Voluntary Mediation

Voluntary mediation is the institution most open to the misconception that "because it is not compulsory, its consequences are light too". Yet Article 18/5 applies without distinguishing between voluntary and mandatory: where a settlement is reached at the end of the mediation activity, the parties may not bring an action concerning the matters agreed upon.

The most contested manifestation of this has arisen in labour law. Minutes of "voluntary mediation" drawn up on the very day of termination of the employment contract or immediately afterwards, without any application by the employee and in reality functioning as a release, have been the subject of consistent review by the 9th Civil Chamber of the Court of Cassation. The Chamber holds that such minutes are not amenable to mediation, on the ground that they are drawn up at a time when the parties cannot freely dispose of their rights and in the absence of any genuine dispute. A document not held valid cannot benefit from the protection of Article 18/5; the payment it contains is set off against the employee's claims.

By contrast, voluntary mediation conducted in accordance with the proper procedure is fully binding. In that framework the Court of Cassation also addresses the question whether, for a claim item made the subject of a voluntary mediation settlement, a fresh mandatory-mediation application is required on the same matter.

## Legal Aid for the Mediation Fee

Article 13/3 was added in 2017 to ensure that financial weakness does not obstruct access to mediation. A party who needs legal aid to meet the mediation fee may benefit from legal aid by decision of **the civil court of peace of the place where the mediation bureau is located**; Articles 334 to 340 CCP apply by analogy.

Points to note in practice are the following: the request is addressed not to the court where the action will be brought, but to the civil court of peace of the place of the mediation bureau. Under Article 334 CCP, the applicant must lack the means to pay the required costs in whole or in part without significantly jeopardising the subsistence of themselves and their family, and the request must not be manifestly unfounded. Under Article 336 CCP, documents showing the applicant's situation must be attached to the request.

It should be noted that in mandatory mediation this route is often unnecessary, since under Article 18/A/13 the two-hour fee is paid from the budget of the Ministry of Justice where no settlement is reached. Legal aid functions mainly in voluntary mediation and in files where a settlement is reached and the proportional fee becomes burdensome.

## Case Law of the Court of Cassation

The following judgments show how application to voluntary mediation and its consequences appear in practice.

**1. Court of Cassation, 3rd Civil Chamber, Case No. 2025/5452, Decision No. 2026/129, dated 15.01.2026**

Recalling that Article 13 provides that the parties may agree to apply to a mediator before an action is brought or during the hearing of the case, the Chamber stated that stipulating mediation first and arbitration afterwards in a contract is valid, and that resorting first to mediation in accordance with that expressed intention was lawful.

**2. Court of Cassation, 6th Civil Chamber, Case No. 2023/4026, Decision No. 2024/1590, dated 16.05.2024**

The decision sets out that, under the heading "Mediation Activity" in the fourth chapter of Law No. 6325, Article 13 regulates application to mediation, while Article 16/2 provides that the time elapsing from the beginning to the end of the process is not taken into account in calculating limitation and forfeiture periods.

**3. Court of Cassation, 9th Civil Chamber, Case No. 2024/6077, Decision No. 2024/9495, dated 10.06.2024**

In the dispute, the invalidity and annulment were sought, on the ground of vitiated consent, of a voluntary mediation session held with a party alleged to have acted in breach of contract and with fraudulent intent, and of the minutes signed at the end of that session. The decision shows that documents drawn up in voluntary mediation are likewise subject to the invalidity review applicable to contracts.

**4. Court of Cassation, 9th Civil Chamber, Case No. 2024/1776, Decision No. 2024/2595, dated 19.02.2024**

The claimant sought a declaration of invalidity and the annulment of the settlement minutes and the other documents relating to the session, asserting that the voluntary mediation session had taken place while they were under the fear of not receiving their severance pay. The decision shows the scope of review as to the moment at which voluntary mediation is conducted.

**5. Court of Cassation, 9th Civil Chamber, Case No. 2025/8765, Decision No. 2025/10371, dated 24.12.2025**

The Chamber stated that where the severance-pay claim **appears in the minutes of a voluntary mediation settlement**, the mediation application condition must be regarded as satisfied for that claim, and that it was erroneous to dismiss the action on procedural grounds by requiring a further mandatory-mediation application for that claim (in the case at hand the judgment was upheld because the error had no effect on the outcome). The decision further confirms that defective consent cannot be relied upon in an annulment action brought more than a year after the minutes were drawn up, and that under Article 18/5 no action may be brought on the matters agreed.
