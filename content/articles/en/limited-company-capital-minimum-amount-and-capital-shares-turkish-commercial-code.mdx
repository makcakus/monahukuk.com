TTK Art. 580-584 set the capital rules for Turkey's most common business form, largely mirroring joint-stock company rules but scaled for smaller enterprises.

## Minimum Capital

Art. 580 sets the minimum share capital at TRY 10,000 — one-fifth of the joint-stock company's TRY 50,000 minimum — reflecting the limited company's orientation toward smaller-scale ventures. The President may increase this up to tenfold by decree. An existing company cannot reduce its capital below this floor.

## What Counts as Valid In-Kind Capital

Art. 581 allows contributing assets free of liens, attachments, or injunctions that can be valued in cash and transferred — including intellectual property rights and, notably, domain names and virtual environments, a modern addition reflecting the digital economy. Services, personal labor, commercial goodwill, and not-yet-due receivables cannot serve as capital. As with joint-stock companies, in-kind contributions must go through court-appointed expert valuation.

## Disclosure of Pre-Incorporation Benefits

Art. 582 requires founders to record in the articles any assets acquired for the company's account before incorporation, and any benefits granted to those who provided services in setting up the company. Turkish courts have held that such pre-incorporation transfers only bind the company if written into the articles — otherwise, the party claiming the benefit must prove it independently. This is not a mere formality: it ensures pre-incorporation dealings by founders remain auditable after the fact.

## Capital Shares

Art. 583 sets a minimum nominal value of TRY 25 per capital share (or multiples thereof), though this floor can be reduced to improve the company's financial position. A partner may hold multiple capital shares, and shares can be issued at a premium above nominal value. Payment can be made in cash, in kind, by offsetting a receivable, or by converting freely available equity into capital.

## Profit-Sharing Certificates

Art. 584 allows a limited company to issue profit-sharing certificates if the articles provide for it, applying joint-stock company rules by analogy — an infrequently used tool in practice.
