Because the articles of association function as the company's constitution, TTK Art. 421 sets heavier quorums for amending them than the general 25% bar under Art. 418 — and Art. 422-424 add the formalities and consequences that make resolutions actually stick.

## A Four-Tier Quorum System

Under Art. 421(1), the default rule for amending the articles requires 50% of capital represented at the first meeting, dropping to one-third for a second meeting held within a month — any articles clause that lowers these further, or substitutes a plurality rule, is void. Two matters require unanimous consent of all capital: imposing obligations on shareholders to cover balance-sheet losses, and relocating the company's registered seat abroad (Art. 421(2)). A 75% supermajority applies to three matters: a complete change of business purpose, creating privileged shares, and restricting the transfer of registered shares (Art. 421(3)) — and unlike the general rule, these heightened thresholds apply at every subsequent meeting, with no quorum relaxation for a "second meeting." A dissenting registered shareholder on a purpose-change or privilege-creation resolution is freed from transfer restrictions for six months following publication (Art. 421(6)).

## Minutes and Binding Effect

Art. 422 requires the minutes to record attendees, shareholdings, questions and answers, and the vote count for each resolution, signed by the chairmanship and the Ministry representative — without which the minutes are invalid. Art. 423 then states the blunt consequence of majority rule: a valid resolution binds shareholders who were absent or voted against it. This is not absolute — resolutions violating law, the articles, or good faith remain open to annulment or nullity actions under Art. 445 onward — but it is the mechanism that lets a joint-stock company function as a majority-governed collective.

## Approving the Balance Sheet Discharges Management

Art. 424 is a rule with real teeth: approving the balance sheet automatically discharges the board, management, and auditors from liability for that period — unless the resolution states otherwise. This "discharge by implication" fails, though, if the balance sheet knowingly omits or misrepresents matters that obscure the company's true condition. In practice, companies often keep balance sheet approval and discharge as separate agenda items precisely to control this automatic effect and to manage which board members can vote on which item.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What are the meeting and voting quorums for amending the articles of association?

Under Article 421/1 TTK the rule is a general assembly at which <b>at least half of the capital is represented</b>, deciding by <b>a majority of the votes present</b>. If that quorum is not met at the first meeting, a second may be held within one month, where the quorum is <b>at least one third of the capital</b>. Provisions in the articles that lower these quorums or provide for a relative majority are <b>void</b>.

### Which resolutions require unanimity?

Article 421/2 TTK names two: resolutions <b>imposing obligations and secondary obligations to cover balance-sheet losses</b>, and resolutions <b>transferring the company's head office abroad</b>. These require the unanimous vote of the holders or representatives of all shares constituting the capital. The three types of resolution in Article 421/3 (complete change of the business purpose, creation of privileged shares, restriction of the transfer of registered shares) require a <b>seventy-five per cent</b> affirmative vote.

### Does approval of the balance sheet discharge the board of directors?

As a rule yes. Under Article 424 TTK, the general assembly resolution approving the balance sheet, <b>unless the resolution provides otherwise</b>, <b>operates as a discharge</b> of the directors, managers and auditors. The same article sets an important limit: if certain matters are omitted or improperly stated in the balance sheet, or if the balance sheet contains items that prevent the company's true position from being seen and this was done deliberately, approval produces no discharge.
