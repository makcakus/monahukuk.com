TTK Art. 830-831 close out the section on instruments that resemble kambiyo paper without formally being kambiyo paper. Where the previous article in this series covered the bill's counterpart (the negotiable order-to-pay), this one covers the note's counterpart and a broad catch-all category — and both confirm the same theme: substance never buys access to Turkey's special enforcement track for bills, notes, and cheques.

## Payment Promise Treated as a Note — With One Carve-Out

Art. 830(1) mirrors the logic seen with negotiable orders-to-pay: a payment promise carrying every mandatory element of a promissory note under Art. 776, expressly drawn "to order," but not labeled "promissory note," is treated as a note for legal purposes. The one deliberate exception is intervention-payment rules, which — unlike most note provisions imported from bill-of-exchange law via Art. 778 — do not extend to this instrument. Art. 830(2) then draws the now-familiar line: being "equivalent to a note" is not the same as "being a note," so the special kambiyo enforcement track (available only for cheques, bills, and notes bearing the statutory wording) stays unavailable, and the holder must use ordinary attachment enforcement instead.

## The Residual Category: Any Instrument With a Certain Obligation

Art. 831 abandons any resemblance requirement to bills or notes altogether. Any instrument in which the signer undertakes to pay a sum certain as to place, time, and amount, or to deliver a defined quantity of fungible goods, becomes transferable by endorsement the moment it is expressly drawn "to order" — regardless of formal wording. For this broad category (and for named examples like warehouse receipts, warrants, and bills of lading), bill-of-exchange rules apply by analogy only to endorsement form, holder entitlement, and the duty to surrender the instrument, plus cancellation rules (except for warehouse receipts and warrants, which have their own regime). Recourse rules — protest, joint liability of endorsers, redress — are explicitly excluded under Art. 831(3) unless the law says otherwise elsewhere, which it later does for warehouse receipts and warrants.

## Practical Takeaway

For a foreign party negotiating or accepting an unconventional Turkish payment or delivery instrument, the recurring lesson across Art. 829-831 is the same: functional similarity to a bill or note secures the transferability and defense rules of kambiyo law, but never the summary enforcement remedy — that privilege stays reserved for instruments carrying the exact statutory label.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is an order promise of payment the same as a promissory note?

Largely, but not entirely. Under TTK Article 830/1, promises of payment that are not described in the text as promissory notes but are drawn <b>expressly to order</b> and contain the other elements required of a promissory note <b>have the effect of a promissory note</b>. There are two important differences: the provisions on payment by intervention do not apply, and under Article 830/2 the enforcement route reserved for bills of exchange is closed.

### Can an order promise of payment be enforced as a bill of exchange?

No. Under TTK Article 830/2 <b>the provisions of the Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law on the enforcement of cheques, bills of exchange and, among order instruments, promissory notes do not apply to promises of payment drawn to order.</b> This means that the attachment route reserved for bills of exchange (Articles 167 et seq. of the Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law) is closed; the creditor must proceed by the general attachment route or by enforcement without judgment.

### Is there a right of recourse in other endorsable instruments?

As a rule, no. Under TTK Article 831/1, instruments by which the signatory undertakes to make specified monetary payments as to place, time and amount and to deliver a specified quantity of fungible goods may be transferred by endorsement <b>provided they are expressly drawn to order</b>. However, under Article 831/3 <b>the provisions on recourse applicable to bills of exchange do not apply to these instruments unless the law expressly so provides</b>; the endorser bears no liability in recourse.
