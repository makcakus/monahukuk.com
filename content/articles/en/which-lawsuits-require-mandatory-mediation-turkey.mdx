The field in which applying to a mediator before bringing an action is compulsory has expanded step by step since 2018 through five separate statutory arrangements. As matters stand today, a significant portion of the actions brought before the civil courts of peace, civil courts of first instance, commercial courts, labour courts and consumer courts is subject to this condition.

Failure to fulfil the condition has a severe consequence: the action is dismissed on procedural grounds without the merits ever being reached, and the defect cannot be cured afterwards. Answering the question "is my case within scope?" correctly is therefore the first technical decision in the file.

## 1. Labour Disputes — Article 3 of Law No. 7036 (1 January 2018)

Scope: actions brought for **employee or employer claims and compensation** based on the law or on an individual or collective employment contract, and for **reinstatement**. With the 2023 amendment, actions for **removal of objection, negative declaration and restitution** relating to those claims and compensation were also brought within scope.

Typical claims within scope: severance pay, notice pay, overtime pay, annual leave pay, weekly rest and public holiday pay, wage claims, bad-faith compensation, discrimination compensation, reinstatement.

**Exception:** pecuniary and non-pecuniary damages arising from an occupational accident or occupational disease, and the related declaratory, objection and recourse actions, fall outside the scope (Art. 3/3). Such actions may be brought directly; the parties may go to voluntary mediation if they wish.

Journalists subject to Press Labour Law No. 5953 and seafarers subject to Maritime Labour Law No. 854 are also treated as employees for the purposes of this article. For detail, see our article on [mandatory mediation and the labour courts](/articles/mandatory-mediation-labour-courts-turkish-labour-law).

## 2. Commercial Disputes — Article 5/A TCC (1 January 2019)

Scope: among the **commercial actions** specified in Article 4 TCC and in other laws, actions for **payment, damages, removal of objection, negative declaration and restitution** whose subject matter is a sum of money.

Two criteria are required together: the dispute must be in the nature of a commercial action, and the claim must fall within one of those five types. Commercial actions whose subject matter is not the payment of money — annulment of a general meeting resolution, dissolution of a company, determination of unfair competition — are outside the scope.

**Difference in time limit:** in commercial disputes the mediator concludes the process within six weeks; in compulsory circumstances this may be extended by a maximum of two weeks.

**Temporal limit:** for negative declaratory and restitution actions, the condition applies, under Provisional Article 1 of Law No. 7445, to **actions brought on or after 1 September 2023**.

## 3. Consumer Disputes — Article 73/A of the Consumer Protection Law (28 July 2020)

Scope: **all disputes heard before the consumer courts**. Here the criterion is not the type of action but whether the dispute falls within the jurisdiction of the consumer court.

There are five exceptions:
- disputes within the competence of the consumer arbitration committee (those below the monetary threshold);
- objections to decisions of the consumer arbitration committee;
- the actions in Article 73/6 of the Consumer Protection Law (prevention/cessation/declaration actions brought by consumer organisations, public institutions and the Ministry);
- the actions in Article 74 of the Consumer Protection Law (determination of unfair terms);
- **disputes which are in the nature of a consumer transaction and arise from a right in rem over immovable property.**

The last exception is the most frequently confused point in practice: a claim for cancellation of title and registration arising from the sale of a dwelling is outside the scope, but a claim for delay compensation or price reduction arising from the same sale is within it.

There are two special protections in the consumer's favour: the non-attendance sanction is not applied against the consumer, and the mediation fee payable by the consumer is met from the budget of the Ministry of Justice.

## 4. Lease, Dissolution of Joint Ownership, Condominium and Neighbour Disputes — Article 18/B of Law No. 6325 (1 September 2023)

Four separate groups of dispute are listed in the same article:

**(a) Disputes arising from a lease relationship.** Determination of rent, rent claims, adaptation, eviction, return of the deposit, damage and defect claims. No distinction is made between residential and workplace leases. **Exception:** the provisions on eviction of leased immovable property through enforcement without judgment under the Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law.

**(b) Disputes concerning the division of movable and immovable property and the dissolution of joint ownership.** Partition actions fall within this scope, and the application must be directed at all co-owners.

**(c) Disputes arising from Condominium Ownership Law No. 634.** Dues and common-expense claims, the manager's liability, annulment of decisions of the general meeting of owners, prevention of interference with common areas, non-compliance with the project.

**(d) Disputes arising from neighbour rights.** Prevention of excessive use, noise, odour and smoke, damage from excavation and construction, tree branches and roots, and disputes over water flow and rights of way.

**Temporal limit:** under Provisional Article 3, these provisions do not apply to **actions pending** before the first-instance courts, the regional courts of justice and the Court of Cassation as at 1 September 2023.

**The timing trap:** in its decision resolving a divergence between regional courts of justice, the 3rd Civil Chamber of the Court of Cassation held that in **eviction actions based on need and reconstruction** brought under Article 350 of the Turkish Code of Obligations, the mediation application must be made after the right to bring the eviction action has arisen — that is, within the period for bringing the action. An application made before the lease term ends does not satisfy the condition.

## 5. Actions Arising from Agricultural Production Contracts — Article 13 of Agriculture Law No. 5488 (1 September 2023)

Under this little-known provision, having applied to a mediator is a condition of action in actions arising from an agricultural production contract. In such disputes, mediators who have **received specialist training in contract farming** are appointed, and where none are available, other mediators.

The time limit differs here too: the mediator concludes the application within **two weeks** of the date of appointment; in compulsory circumstances this may be extended by a maximum of one week. Under Provisional Article 5 of Law No. 5488, the provision applies to actions brought after 1 September 2023.

In agricultural regions such as Antalya, where contract farming is widespread, this provision applies directly to disputes between the purchasing company and the producer.

## The Principal Actions Outside the Scope

The mediation condition is **not required** in the following actions:

- actions for damages arising from occupational accidents and occupational diseases;
- actions for the determination of service (because the Social Security Institution is a party);
- bankruptcy and composition actions (they concern public policy and are not amenable to mediation);
- claims in family law relating to personal status, such as marriage, divorce, parentage and custody;
- actions concerning a right in rem over immovable property, such as cancellation of title and registration (immovable-property disputes are **amenable** to mediation but are not subject to the condition of action);
- commercial actions whose subject matter is not the payment of money;
- disputes heard in the administrative courts.

Furthermore, where special laws impose an obligation to resort to **arbitration or another alternative dispute-resolution method**, or where there is an **arbitration agreement** between the parties, the provisions on mediation as a condition of action do not apply. Insurance arbitration under Article 30 of the Insurance Law is the typical example.

## What Happens if the Condition Is Not Fulfilled?

Two different defects produce two different results.

**Where no application to a mediator has been made at all:** the action is dismissed on procedural grounds without any step being taken and without the statement of claim being served on the other side. The Court of Cassation holds that this is a **special condition of action that cannot be completed later**, and that applying to mediation after the action has been brought has no effect on the merits.

**Where an application has been made but the final minutes are not attached to the statement of claim:** the court grants a peremptory period of one week; if **the original of the minutes or a copy certified by the mediator** is not submitted within that period, the action is dismissed on procedural grounds.

The only relaxation concerns jurisdiction ratione materiae: where an action is brought before a court lacking jurisdiction, the condition is regarded as fulfilled if the mediator was applied to and the process ended with minutes of non-settlement before the file was registered on the docket of the competent court.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q: Do I also need to apply to mediation when bringing a counterclaim?

Yes. If the subject matter of the counterclaim is a claim within the scope of mandatory mediation, the condition must be separately satisfied for that claim too. The 9th Civil Chamber of the Court of Cassation did not accept the employer's defence that "the same matters were discussed in the mediation conducted for the principal action" and upheld the decision dismissing the counterclaim on procedural grounds for lack of a condition of action.

### Q: If I increase my claim by amendment, must I return to mediation?

The condition is regarded as fulfilled in respect of the claim items advanced in the mediation application. No fresh application is required to increase the amount of the same claim item by amendment; by contrast, if a **new claim item** never advanced in the mediation is to be added, the condition must be separately satisfied for that item.

### Q: Is the mediation condition also required for an interim injunction or interim attachment?

No. No application to mediation is required for requests for an interim injunction or interim attachment; these may be sought directly from the court. Moreover, where an injunction or attachment order was obtained before the action was brought, the period for bringing the action does not run from the application to the mediation bureau until the date the final minutes are drawn up.

### Q: What if the same action contains both in-scope and out-of-scope claims?

The mediation condition is required for the in-scope claim and not for the out-of-scope one. In practice the court may sever these claims and assess them separately; it is therefore advisable to set out clearly in the statement of claim which claim is subject to which regime.

### Q: If I skip the mediation condition, is my action definitively dismissed?

The dismissal is on procedural grounds and does not create res judicata on the merits. The action may be brought again once the mediation process has been completed. But the time and court fees lost in the meantime are real; and in claims subject to a forfeiture period (reinstatement, for example) an irreparable loss may arise.
