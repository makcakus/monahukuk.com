Foreign nationals facing minor criminal allegations in Turkey — a bar fight, a disputed identity document, an accusation of endangering traffic safety — are sometimes offered a fast-track resolution that promises a lower sentence in exchange for accepting the charge without a full trial. Turkish criminal procedure actually contains two distinct mechanisms of this kind, and they are frequently confused even by defendants who have already been through one of them. The Code of Criminal Procedure (CMK) provides for an "expedited trial procedure" that operates during the investigation stage, before any indictment is filed, and a separate "simplified trial procedure" that operates during the prosecution stage, after an indictment has already been accepted by the court. Both trade a reduced sentence for a streamlined process, but they apply to different offenses, are triggered by different actors, and produce different discount rates. Understanding which one applies — and what accepting it actually means — matters because both mechanisms directly shape the final sentence a person walks away with.

## The Expedited Trial Procedure: Scope and Timing

The expedited trial procedure under CMK Article 250 is available only during the investigation phase, before a public prosecution has been opened and before an indictment has been drafted. It does not apply to every minor offense — it is limited to a closed list of specific crimes named in the statute, including certain trespass offenses, intentionally endangering public safety, endangering traffic safety, causing disturbance through noise, currency counterfeiting, breaking an official seal, false statements in official documents, providing premises for gambling, and unauthorized use of another person's identity information, together with a handful of offenses under separate laws covering firearms, forestry, gambling equipment, and cooperatives. Even where the offense falls on this list, the procedure is unavailable if the case is one where the prosecutor could instead defer the filing of the public case altogether. The public prosecutor or law enforcement officers must first inform the suspect that this procedure exists, and the prosecutor's office then formally proposes it — but the proposal is only valid, and can only be accepted, in the presence of defense counsel. A suspect who is not represented by a lawyer at that moment cannot validly accept the offer, which means undocumented or unrepresented foreign suspects should not be pressured into a decision without first securing counsel.

## How the Prosecutor Calculates the Reduced Sentence

What makes the expedited procedure distinctive is that the prosecutor, not the court, does the initial sentencing calculation. The prosecutor sets a base sentence somewhere between the statutory minimum and maximum for the offense, applying the general sentencing considerations of Article 61 of the Turkish Penal Code, adjusts for repeated or continuing offenses where relevant, and then cuts the resulting figure in half. That halved sentence can still be converted into an alternative sanction or suspended, and a deferral of the announcement of the verdict remains available by analogy. Crucially, the prosecutor does not have the final word: the request is submitted to the competent court, which must hear the suspect in the presence of defense counsel before deciding whether to confirm it. If the court is satisfied that the conditions are met and that a conviction is warranted on the existing evidence, it enters a judgment that cannot be harsher than what the prosecutor proposed. If the court is not satisfied, it rejects the request and sends the file back to the chief public prosecutor's office so the investigation can continue under the ordinary rules. A judgment reached this way can still be challenged through an objection, though the reviewing authority looks only at whether the procedural conditions were met, not at the merits of guilt.

## The Simplified Trial Procedure: A Broader, Court-Driven Alternative

The simplified trial procedure under CMK Article 251 operates on entirely different terms. It only becomes available after an indictment has been accepted by the criminal court of first instance (asliye ceza mahkemesi), and it is open to any offense — regardless of its label — for which the applicable penalty is a judicial fine and/or imprisonment with a statutory maximum of two years or less. There is no closed list here; the trigger is the ceiling on the possible sentence, not the type of crime. Whether to use the procedure is left entirely to the court's discretion, and the statute does not spell out criteria for exercising that discretion, though Turkish appellate case law has since required courts to ground a refusal in concrete, case-specific reasons rather than generic formulas. Once a court decides to proceed, and provided the trial date has not already been set, it serves the indictment on the defendant, the victim, and the complainant and gives them two weeks to submit their statements and defense in writing, while also requesting any outstanding documents from relevant institutions. After that period expires, the court can rule — without holding a hearing and without seeking the prosecutor's opinion — on any of the outcomes available under CMK Article 223, applying the same Article 61 sentencing factors. A conviction under this procedure carries an automatic one-quarter reduction in the final sentence, and, as with the expedited procedure, conversion to an alternative sanction, suspension, or deferral of the verdict's announcement can still apply.

## Comparing the Two Procedures

The two mechanisms diverge along four main lines. Stage: the expedited procedure operates during investigation, before any case is filed; the simplified procedure operates during prosecution, after the indictment has already been accepted. Scope: the expedited procedure is confined to a specific list of named offenses, while the simplified procedure covers any offense falling under the two-year sentencing ceiling, without regard to its label. Decision-maker: in the expedited procedure the prosecutor proposes a sentence that the court then confirms or rejects, while in the simplified procedure the court itself hands down the judgment directly, without a prosecutorial recommendation. Discount: the expedited procedure halves the resulting sentence, while the simplified procedure reduces it by only one quarter — a narrower discount that reflects the fact that the simplified procedure sweeps in a much wider range of conduct. The two share several structural features nonetheless: both depend on the suspect or defendant's cooperation in a manner comparable to mediation under CMK Article 253, neither can be applied where one of several co-suspects in the same case declines to accept it, and both are unavailable where the accused is a minor or suffers from a mental condition that affects criminal responsibility.

## Objecting to a Simplified Trial Judgment

A judgment issued under the simplified trial procedure is not subject to appeal in the ordinary sense (temyiz); it can only be challenged through an objection (itiraz), mirroring the same limited review available for expedited-procedure judgments and reinforcing the overall aim of keeping both processes fast. If the objection period passes without action, the judgment becomes final. If an objection is filed, the file is transferred — in localities with more than one criminal court of first instance, according to the applicable case-distribution rules — to a different court, which then holds a full hearing and continues under the ordinary procedural rules. That second court is not bound by the earlier judgment, and it may proceed to decide the case even if the parties fail to appear. In short, the simplified trial procedure offers a genuine shortcut, but a defendant who is dissatisfied with its outcome retains a path back into a fully contested trial by exercising the right to object.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can a suspect be offered the expedited trial procedure without a lawyer present?**
The proposal itself may be communicated by the prosecutor's office or law enforcement, but under CMK Article 250 the suspect's acceptance is only valid if made in the presence of defense counsel. An acceptance given without a lawyer present does not bind the suspect.

**Does the simplified trial procedure apply automatically once the sentence ceiling is met?**
No. Even where the offense carries a maximum sentence within the two-year threshold, applying the simplified trial procedure remains a matter of court discretion, and the court must set the trial date issue before deciding — once a hearing date has been fixed, the procedure can no longer be used.

**Which procedure gives a bigger sentence reduction?**
The expedited trial procedure reduces the calculated sentence by half, while the simplified trial procedure reduces it by one quarter. The narrower reduction under the simplified procedure reflects that it covers a far broader range of offenses than the closed list under the expedited procedure.

**What happens if a defendant disagrees with a judgment issued under either procedure?**
Both judgments can be challenged only through an objection, not a standard appeal. For a simplified trial judgment, a successful objection sends the case to a different court, which then conducts a full hearing under the ordinary rules and is not bound by the earlier decision.
