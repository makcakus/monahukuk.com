TTK Art. 1191-1192 make sure that subcontracting the physical carriage of cargo — extremely common practice among freight forwarders and NVOCCs — never leaves a gap in cargo interests' protection.

## The Contracting Carrier Stays Fully on the Hook

When carriage is handed off, wholly or partly, to an actual carrier, the contracting carrier remains liable for the entire carriage regardless of whether the contract even permitted that delegation. It also answers for the actual carrier's acts and those of the actual carrier's own servants acting within their duties — extending liability transitively across the whole performance chain, not just the contracting carrier's own direct employees.

## The Actual Carrier Gets the Same Regime — and the Same Protections

Every provision governing carrier liability applies equally to the actual carrier for the portion it actually performs — the same exoneration grounds, fault presumptions, notice requirements, SDR caps, and preclusive period. Where a claim targets the actual carrier's own servants, the same reckless-conduct exception (Art. 1187/2) and defense-portability/cumulative-cap rules (Art. 1190/2-3) apply to them too.

## Joint and Several Liability, With a Shared Cap

Where the carrier and actual carrier are both responsible for the same loss, their liability is joint and several — the claimant can pursue either or both for the full amount without first sorting out who was really at fault, leaving the two carriers to settle contribution between themselves afterward. But total recovery from the carrier, actual carrier, and all their servants combined still cannot exceed the statutory cap — the claimant naming more defendants doesn't increase total exposure. Special agreements imposing extra duties on the carrier don't bind the actual carrier without its written consent, though they still bind the carrier who made them; and nothing in these rules affects the carrier's own recourse rights against the actual carrier.

## Exemption Clauses: Conditional and Narrow

A contract may exempt the carrier from liability for loss occurring specifically during the actual carrier's custody — but only if cargo claimants can actually sue that actual carrier in a competent Turkish court, and only if the carrier discloses the actual carrier's name and address (as soon as known, and no later than delivery to the actual carrier). Skip that disclosure, and the carrier's full liability continues regardless of the clause — preventing carriers from offloading risky legs to judgment-proof or inaccessible subcontractors while shielding themselves.
