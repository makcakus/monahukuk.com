The Turkish limited partnership (komandit şirket), governed by TTK Art. 304-328, is the second and last member of the "person company" family. Unlike the general partnership, it splits liability in two: komandite partners bear unlimited personal liability like general partners, while komanditer partners are liable only up to their capital contribution. Only natural persons may serve as komandite partners; legal entities may only participate as komanditer (Art. 304).

## Formation and Interpretation

Formation largely follows the general partnership rules (Art. 305), but the substance of the agreement — not its label — determines whether an entity truly qualifies as a limited partnership; if this cannot be clearly established, it is treated as a general partnership by default (Art. 306), a rule protecting third parties by defaulting to the heavier liability regime whenever the contract is ambiguous.

## The Limited Partner's Restricted Role

Every partner, komandite or komanditer, has one vote (Art. 309), but only komandite partners may manage the business; komanditer partners cannot participate in management or object to acts within a manager's authority. The exception covers extraordinary matters — amending the agreement, mergers, admitting or expelling partners, transferring shares — where limited partners do retain a vote. Turkish courts have required a concrete, provable mismanagement claim before a limited partner's intervention rights carry weight in practice.

To compensate for this exclusion from management, limited partners hold a robust inspection right: reviewing the annual inventory and financial statements, personally or through an expert, with court backing available for cause (Art. 310) — rights that cannot be waived by contract. The general partner non-compete rule does not apply to limited partners, though a limited partner who opens or joins a competing business loses the right to inspect the partnership's records (Art. 311).

## Profit Rights and Succession

A limited partner receives their share of realized annual profit and any agreed interest in cash, but not if their capital contribution has been eroded by losses until the shortfall is restored (Art. 312); profits and interest properly received in good faith generally cannot be clawed back later to cover subsequent losses (Art. 313-314). A limited partner may transfer their interest, though without the other partners' consent the transfer only binds the transferring parties themselves under Code of Obligations rules (Art. 315). Notably, unlike a general partner's death — which typically dissolves the partnership absent a continuation clause — a deceased limited partner's heirs automatically step into their position (Art. 316), reflecting the limited partner's lesser importance to the partnership's continued existence.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is a limited partnership, and how are general and limited partners distinguished?

Under TTK Article 304/1 a limited partnership is one formed to operate a commercial enterprise under a trade name in which <b>the liability of one or more of the partners towards the partnership's creditors is unlimited, while that of the other partner or partners is limited to a specified capital amount</b>. The partner with unlimited liability is the <b>general partner</b>, and the one whose liability is limited to their contribution is the <b>limited partner</b>. General partners must be natural persons; a limited partner may also be a legal person.

### May a limited partner manage the partnership?

As a rule, no. Under TTK Article 309 a limited partner does not have the power to manage the partnership, and under Article 318/1 <b>limited partnerships are as a rule represented by the general partners</b>. The limited partner does, however, have a right of supervision: they may examine the partnership's financial statements and, where just grounds exist, ask the court for leave to examine the books and records.

### How does a limited partner receive their share of the profit?

Under TTK Article 312/1 <b>the limited partner receives in cash the profit share realised at the end of the business year and the interest agreed in the partnership agreement.</b> In addition, under Article 313, where their contribution has been diminished by losses, the limited partner may not claim a profit share until the diminished capital has been made up; they cannot, however, be required to return profit shares and interest received earlier in good faith.
