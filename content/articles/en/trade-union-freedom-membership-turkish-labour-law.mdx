Freedom of association is a constitutional right in Turkey, and Law No. 6356 on Trade Unions and Collective Labour Agreements ("STISK") turns that constitutional promise into a detailed operational framework. For employers running operations in Turkey — including foreign-owned hotels, factories and service companies in the Antalya region — the law is not simply about tolerating a union's existence. It sets out exactly how an employee acquires and loses union membership, what protection a union officer or workplace representative enjoys while carrying out union duties, and what an employee can claim if they are treated worse because of their union choice. Getting these rules wrong exposes an employer to compensation claims that a Turkish court will resolve firmly in the employee's favour, since the burden of proof in this area is deliberately tilted against the employer.

## Who Can Join a Union, and How Membership Is Acquired

Article 17 of STISK sets the eligibility threshold for union membership: an employee must be at least fifteen years old and qualify as an "employee" under the relevant legislation to join a workers' union, while an entity meeting the legal definition of "employer" may join an employers' union. The same article protects both sides of the freedom-of-association coin — joining a union is voluntary, and no one may be compelled either to join or to refrain from joining. As a rule, a person may not hold simultaneous membership in more than one union within the same branch of activity; a second, competing membership taken out in breach of this rule is invalid. There is a practical carve-out, however, for employees who work for different employers within the same branch of activity — such a worker may validly hold separate union memberships tied to each employment relationship.

What is distinctive about the current version of Article 17 is that it moved the entire membership process onto the government's e-Devlet electronic portal. An employee applies for membership through e-Devlet, and membership is acquired once the union's authorised body — as designated in its bylaws — accepts the application. If the union does not reject the application within thirty days, the membership is deemed accepted by operation of law. An applicant who is refused without a justified reason may challenge that refusal in court within thirty days of notification, and if the court rules in the applicant's favour, membership is treated as having existed retroactively from the date of the rejection. This digitised, time-boxed procedure was designed to close the door on the manual, paper-based membership irregularities of the past.

## Losing or Suspending Membership

Article 19 governs how membership ends, and the same voluntariness principle runs through it: an employee or employer cannot be forced to remain a union member or to resign. Resignation is submitted through the e-Devlet portal and takes effect one month after notification — a deliberate cooling-off period that gives the departing member a chance to reconsider a hasty decision. Expulsion, by contrast, requires a resolution of the union's general assembly, and the expelled member has thirty days to challenge that resolution in court; membership continues while the challenge is pending. The law also distinguishes between membership ending outright and membership merely being suspended: a change of branch of activity automatically terminates membership, whereas periods of unemployment not exceeding one year, or military service, do not end the membership — they only put it on hold until the member's situation is regularized.

## Job Security for Union Officers

Article 23 gives special protection to employees who take on union management duties, since these are the individuals most exposed when they step away from day-to-day work to run union affairs. An employee who leaves the workplace to serve as a union officer does not have their employment contract automatically terminated — it is suspended. The officer retains the option to terminate that contract without notice at the moment they take up the post and still qualify for severance pay. When the management role ends — because the union's legal personality is dissolved, the officer is not re-elected, or the officer resigns — the former officer may apply to the employer within one month to return to their old job, and the employer must accommodate that request within a month. If the employer fails to do so, the employment relationship is treated as having been terminated by the employer, triggering the corresponding severance and other statutory payments. In effect, the law freezes the officer's job security for the duration of the union post rather than letting it lapse simply because the person stopped showing up for work.

## Protection for Workplace Union Representatives

Article 24 extends a comparable, though distinct, safeguard to the union representatives who deal directly with the employer at workplace level. The employer may not terminate a representative's employment contract without a valid reason stated clearly and precisely in writing; if it does, the representative — or the union to which they belong — may bring a claim within one month of notification. Where the dismissal is found invalid, the representative is entitled to wages and other entitlements covering the period between the dismissal and the final court decision, capped at the length of the representative term, and if the employer fails to reinstate the representative within six working days of the decision becoming final, the employment relationship is deemed to have continued uninterrupted. The employer is also barred from relocating the representative to another workplace or materially changing their duties without the representative's written consent. This protection matters beyond the individual case: without a representative who can operate free of retaliation, union organising and the collective bargaining authorisation process at the workplace level cannot function as intended.

## The General Guarantee Against Anti-Union Discrimination

Article 25 is the broadest guarantee in the statute, covering the employment relationship from the hiring stage through to its termination. An offer of employment may not be conditioned on joining or not joining a particular union, and once employed, the employer may not treat union members and non-members differently in terms of working conditions or in deciding whom to dismiss. This is the union-specific expression of the broader anti-discrimination duty that Turkish labour law imposes on employers generally. The most consequential part of Article 25 is its compensation rule: where an employer breaches the prohibition on anti-union discrimination, the affected employee is entitled to union compensation ("sendikal tazminat") of no less than one year's wages. Where the discrimination takes the form of a dismissal, the employee may bring a job-security claim under Law No. 4857, and if the dismissal is found to have been motivated by union grounds, union compensation is awarded regardless of whether the employee is ultimately reinstated. The burden of proof also favours the employee: while the employer normally bears the burden of proving the reason for a dismissal, once the employee presents facts that strongly indicate anti-union discrimination, the burden shifts to the employer to show that its conduct rested on some other, legitimate ground.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can an employer refuse to hire someone because they belong to a particular union?**
No. Article 25 of STISK expressly prohibits conditioning employment on union membership or non-membership, and an employer that discriminates on this basis exposes itself to a union compensation claim.

**How is union membership obtained and ended under current Turkish law?**
Both steps run through the government's e-Devlet portal. Membership is acquired once the union's authorised body accepts an e-Devlet application (or fails to reject it within thirty days); resignation is submitted the same way and takes effect one month after notification.

**Does a union manager lose their job automatically when they take up the union role?**
No. Under Article 23, the employment contract is only suspended, not terminated, and the officer has a statutory right to return to their former job within a month of the management duty ending, subject to the employer's obligation to reinstate them.

**What can an employee claim if they are dismissed for union-related reasons?**
The employee may pursue a job-security claim under Law No. 4857 and, if the dismissal is shown to be union-motivated, is entitled to union compensation of at least one year's wages under Article 25 — independent of whether reinstatement is also ordered.
