Once liquidators are appointed under the general rules discussed separately, TTK Art. 286-303 spell out the actual sequence of liquidating a Turkish general partnership — from the opening inventory to final deregistration from the trade registry.

## Initial Inventory and the Ban on New Business

Liquidators must act with the diligence of a prudent businessperson and complete liquidation as quickly as possible (Art. 286). Their first task is preparing an inventory and opening balance sheet, ideally with the outgoing management, after which they take custody of all partnership property, records, and books (Art. 287). A closing balance sheet at the end of liquidation is sent to the partners, who have one month to object before it becomes final (Art. 289). Critically, liquidators may not undertake new business unrelated to winding down the partnership — doing so triggers joint liability toward the partners (Art. 292); continuing the partnership's actual business line is permitted only with unanimous partner consent or, failing that, court approval (Art. 293).

## Converting Assets and Paying Debts

Movable assets may be sold by auction or private sale as circumstances warrant, but real estate can only be sold through the more rigorous public-auction procedure of the Enforcement and Bankruptcy Code unless partners unanimously agree otherwise (Art. 294). Selling significant assets in bulk requires unanimous partner consent or court approval (Art. 295), and cash exceeding a set threshold must be deposited with a court-designated bank (Art. 296). Not-yet-due debts must be paid immediately at a discount, and creditors are obliged to accept this early payment (Art. 297). If partnership assets fall short of covering debts, liquidators — not the creditors directly — approach the partners for the shortfall (Art. 298), reflecting the partners' unlimited liability operating through the liquidation process itself.

## Final Distribution and Deregistration

Liquidators may distribute available cash to partners provisionally, while reserving enough to cover anticipated claims (Art. 299), with final distribution of net assets made in cash absent a different agreement (Art. 300). Partners retain ongoing oversight rights — liquidators must report on the state of affairs on request and account fully at the end (Art. 301-302). Once liquidation concludes, the liquidators notify the trade registry to deregister the partnership's name (Art. 303). Turkish courts have allowed a previously deregistered partnership to be judicially "revived" where an overlooked asset or an unresolved lawsuit later requires it — deregistration is not always the final word.
