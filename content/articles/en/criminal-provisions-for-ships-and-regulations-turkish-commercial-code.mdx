TTK Art. 947-953 back the identity rules covered in the prior article — flag rights, documentation, and marking — with criminal sanctions aimed squarely at the ship's captain.

## The Four Offenses

Art. 947 penalizes a captain who flies the Turkish flag without the right to do so, or who flies a foreign flag when Turkish flag use is required, with up to six months' imprisonment or a judicial fine. Art. 948 splits documentation failures into two tiers: sailing under the Turkish flag without ever obtaining the ship's certificate or flag certificate carries up to four months' imprisonment, while holding the certificate but failing to keep it aboard during the voyage carries a lighter penalty of up to two months — reflecting that the underlying right exists in the second case, only its onboard proof is momentarily missing. Art. 949 penalizes a captain who fails to salute (fly the flag) when passing warships or coastal defenses, or when entering or leaving Turkish ports — a maritime courtesy and identification tradition with real safety functions. Art. 950 penalizes failure to properly mark the ship's name and port of registry on the hull as required under Art. 939.

## Intent Required, With Limited Extraterritorial Reach

Art. 951 makes all four offenses intent-only — negligence or an honest administrative delay does not trigger criminal liability. Art. 952 extends jurisdiction for the two most serious offenses (unlawful flag use and missing documentation) to acts committed abroad or on the high seas, by Turkish or foreign nationals alike, since the real risk and benefit of improper flag use typically arises precisely when the ship is outside Turkish waters. The lesser offenses (failure to salute, failure to mark) remain territorially limited, since they are inherently tied to Turkish ports or waters.

## Practical Takeaway

For captains and owners operating Turkish-flagged vessels, these are modest penalties (six months' imprisonment at most) but real ones, and the extraterritorial reach for flag and documentation offenses means compliance cannot be treated as a Turkish-waters-only concern.
