TTK Art. 713-723 open the recourse (rücu) regime for bills of exchange — the mechanism letting a holder turn to endorsers, the drawer, and other obligated parties when the bill goes unpaid.

## When Recourse Arises

Under Art. 713, a holder gets recourse once the bill is unpaid at maturity. Three situations trigger recourse even before maturity: total or partial refusal to accept; the drawee's bankruptcy, suspension of payments, or a fruitless enforcement action against the drawee (regardless of acceptance); or the drawer's bankruptcy where acceptance was prohibited on the bill. These early-recourse triggers matter most in bankruptcy and restructuring proceedings, letting holders register claims promptly.

## The Protest Requirement

Dishonor must be established by a formal notarial document called a "protest" (Art. 714) — a strict evidentiary requirement reflecting kambiyo law's demand for certainty. A protest for non-acceptance must be made within the acceptance-presentment period; a protest for non-payment must generally follow within two business days after the due date (Art. 714). Once a non-acceptance protest exists, no further presentment or non-payment protest is needed. Turkish notarial protests must follow a prescribed content under Art. 716 — identities of the parties, a statement that the drawee failed to perform or could not be located, the date and place, and the notary's signature.

## Defective Protest Remains Valid

A striking feature of Turkish kambiyo law: under Art. 721, a protest signed by a notary remains legally valid even if drawn up contrary to law or containing incorrect entries — the Court of Cassation Grand General Assembly has confirmed this "defective protest" doctrine exists precisely so that a holder's rights are not lost to a mere formal slip. This is distinct from a "wrongful protest," which gives the wronged party a separate tort claim but does not affect the protest's validity for recourse purposes. The notary's own disciplinary liability for errors survives independently.

## Waiver and Notice Duties

Parties may insert a "no protest" or "without expenses" clause waiving the protest requirement (Art. 722) — but this never excuses the holder from timely presentment or from the separate notice obligations under Art. 723, which require notifying dishonor up the endorsement chain within tight, cascading deadlines. Missing a protest deadline is fatal to recourse rights; missing a notice deadline is not, but exposes the negligent party to liability capped at the face value of the bill.

## Practical Takeaway

For a holder relying on a Turkish bill, note, or check, timely and properly formatted notarial protest is usually a precondition to enforcement — courts must examine on their own motion whether this requirement was satisfied before allowing kambiyo-based enforcement proceedings to continue.
