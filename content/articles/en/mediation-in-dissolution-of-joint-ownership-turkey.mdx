A plot of land inherited from a parent, a flat held in shared ownership between siblings, a field whose co-owners cannot agree — actions for the dissolution of joint ownership (partition) are among the most common types of file before Turkey's civil courts of peace. And since 1 September 2023 it has been compulsory to apply to a mediator before bringing such an action.

At first sight the requirement looks like an obstacle. In fact it offers co-owners something a court cannot: **division in kind instead of sale.**

## Scope and Statutory Basis

Article 18/B/1(b) of Law No. 6325: **disputes concerning the division of movable and immovable property and the dissolution of joint ownership** fall within mandatory mediation.

The scope is broad:

- termination of shared ownership;
- division of joint ownership in undivided shares (the community of heirs);
- division of movable property;
- claims for dissolution of joint ownership by way of sale.

The provision entered into force on 1 September 2023. Under Provisional Article 3, it does not apply to actions pending as at that date before the first-instance courts, the regional courts of justice and the Court of Cassation.

## The Critical Point: Every Co-owner Must Be Included

An action for the dissolution of joint ownership creates a **compulsory joinder of parties**: all co-owners must be parties to the action. The counterpart at the mediation stage is this: **the application must be directed to all co-owners.**

Leaving one co-owner out of the application means that the condition of action is not satisfied as regards that person, and the action is dismissed on procedural grounds.

In practice this is the most frequent mistake. In inherited property in particular the number of co-owners is high, and if the certificate of inheritance is not up to date some of them may be overlooked. Before applying, the list of co-owners must be established in full from **a current land registry record and the certificate of inheritance**.

Likewise, if one of the co-owners has died, all of their heirs must be included in the process.

## Where Mediation Beats the Court: Division in Kind

In a partition action the court first considers division in kind under Article 699 of the Civil Code; but if the nature of the property is not amenable to division in kind, or if division would cause a significant loss of value, it orders dissolution **by way of sale**. In practice the great majority of actions end in a sale, and the sale takes place by public auction — usually at a price below market value.

Mediation changes that picture. At the table the co-owners can agree on solutions a court could not order:

- transfer of the property to a particular co-owner against payment to the others;
- division in kind according to the parts actually in use;
- where there is more than one property, distribution of the properties among the co-owners;
- division into independent units by converting to condominium ownership;
- continuation of the joint ownership for a fixed period, with a sale at the end of it.

This flexibility is usually in the interest of all co-owners, since it avoids the loss of value that occurs at auction.

## How Is the Settlement Registered in the Land Registry?

Before 2023 the answer to this question was problematic: the transfer of ownership of immovable property was subject to official form and to an official deed drawn up before the land registrar (Article 706 of the Civil Code, Article 26 of the Land Registry Law). Even where a settlement was reached, the parties had to attend the land registry together.

Today the process works as follows:

**1. The compulsory enforceability annotation.** Under Article 18/B/3, obtaining the annotation as to the enforceability of the settlement document is compulsory; for documents relating to immovable property the annotation is obtained from the civil court of peace of **the place where the property is located**. The 5th Civil Chamber of the Court of Cassation applies this competence rule strictly and upholds decisions declining competence where the application was made to another court.

**2. Extended judicial review.** The court reviews not only whether the settlement is amenable to mediation and to enforcement, but also whether the **restrictions, procedures and principles** laid down in the laws concerning immovable property have been observed; it may request information and documents from institutions and organisations and, where necessary, hold a hearing.

**3. Direct registration.** Under Article 18/B/5, added by Law No. 7531 of 7 November 2024, a party to a settlement document concerning the transfer of immovable property or the creation of a limited right in rem over it may, once the enforceability annotation has been given, **apply to the land registry directorate for registration**, and the registration is carried out **without an official deed being drawn up**.

The practical consequence is considerable: if a co-owner later fails to attend the land registry, there is no need to bring a separate action for cancellation of title and registration.

## Protecting the Property During the Process

If, while negotiations are under way, a co-owner transfers their share to a third party, the whole process can be rendered pointless. The Law offers two tools against that risk.

**Land registry annotation (Art. 17/B/2).** In disputes concerning the transfer of immovable property or the creation of a limited right in rem over it, where the parties so agree in writing and the mediator records that agreement in the minutes, an **annotation restricting the power of disposal** is entered in the land register at the mediator's request. The annotation is confined to the mediation process and may not exceed three months; it is lifted at the mediator's request if the parties fail to settle or agree that it should be removed, and automatically at the end of the three-month period. This tool requires the other side's consent.

**Interim injunction.** Where there is no consent, the classic route is open. Moreover, following an injunction obtained before the action is brought, the period for bringing the action does not run from the application to the mediation bureau until the date the final minutes are drawn up.

## Structures, Occupation Compensation and Other Claims

Partition files frequently raise other claims as well: determination of the ownership of structures (buildings, trees, installations), occupation compensation (*ecrimisil*) between co-owners, apportionment of expenses incurred.

These claims are the subject of separate actions and are governed by their own regimes. Negotiating them all together in mediation is possible and practical; but each claim must be dealt with **separately** in the settlement document. Otherwise a settlement expressed as a single lump sum gives rise to argument about which claims were closed — and no fresh action can be brought on matters that were agreed.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q: I cannot reach one of the co-owners — can the process still run?

Yes. The applicant provides the bureau with the contact details in their possession; the bureau is also empowered to search the official records, and the institutions concerned are obliged to provide that information. If the parties cannot be reached, the mediator terminates the activity and draws up the final minutes; those minutes satisfy the condition of action.

### Q: I settled with some co-owners but not with others. What happens?

Because the dissolution of joint ownership concerns all co-owners, a partial settlement does not terminate the joint ownership. Arrangements between the settling co-owners may be valid; but an action must be brought for the dissolution and all co-owners must be parties to it. It is important that the final minutes state clearly with whom agreement was and was not reached.

### Q: If we settle, is there no auction?

No. If a settlement is reached, no action is brought and no sale is ordered by a court. The co-owners may divide the property among themselves as they wish; with a settlement document bearing the enforceability annotation, registration may be requested directly from the land registry directorate.

### Q: How do we share the mediation fee?

Unless otherwise agreed, the fee and expenses are borne equally by the parties. In files with many co-owners it may also be agreed that the fee be shared in proportion to the shares; it is advisable to set this out expressly in the settlement document.

### Q: In a community of heirs, must I first obtain a certificate of inheritance?

As a practical matter the certificate of inheritance is indispensable for identifying the co-owners in full. If one co-owner is missing from the application, the condition of action is treated as unsatisfied as regards that person and the action brought will be dismissed on procedural grounds.
