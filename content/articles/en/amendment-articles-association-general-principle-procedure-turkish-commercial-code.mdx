TTK Art. 452 opens the Code's chapter on amending the articles of association with a deceptively simple rule, then Art. 453-455 fill in the procedural machinery that makes any given amendment actually effective and enforceable against third parties.

## The Principle and Its Two Limits

Under Art. 452, the general assembly may amend any provision of the articles, subject to the conditions set by law, unless the articles themselves say otherwise — but two categories of shareholder protection survive any amendment: vested rights (contractual rights granted personally to a specific shareholder, which cannot be removed without that shareholder's consent) and inalienable rights (rights the law grants directly to shareholders that cannot be stripped even by the articles, such as the untouchable core of the information right under Art. 437(6)). Distinguishing these from privileged shares under Art. 478 — rights attached to the share itself, which transfer with it — is where a large share of amendment disputes actually get decided.

## Ministerial Approval, Publication, and the Governing Quorum

Art. 453 requires, before the assembly is even called, that companies needing Ministry approval under Art. 333 obtain it first, and that the board-approved amendment draft be published under the standard call procedure at least two weeks before the meeting — giving shareholders the chance to arrive informed. The quorum itself is governed by Art. 421, discussed separately: the general 50%/one-third tiers, or unanimity or 75% supermajority depending on the amendment's subject matter. Turkish courts have confirmed that a heavier quorum inherited from the pre-2012 Code in a company's existing articles continues to apply even after the new Code took effect, and that such heightened thresholds cannot be circumvented by routing the change through a lighter general amendment clause.

## The Privileged Shareholders' Special Meeting

Where an amendment (or a board decision exercising delegated capital-increase authority) would impair privileged shareholders' rights, Art. 454 requires it to be approved at a separate meeting of those shareholders — convened within a month of the general assembly resolution, with a 60% quorum of the privileged capital. If the board fails to convene it, affected shareholders can ask the court to do so within fifteen days. Turkish case law confirms this mechanism applies not just to explicit articles amendments but also to capital increases that indirectly dilute privileged rights, such as board representation guarantees.

## Registration Determines Third-Party Effect

Art. 455 states plainly that an amendment has no effect against third parties until registered — the internal resolution may be valid the moment it is passed, but outside parties are unaffected until registration (and subsequent publication) occurs.
