Once multiple potential defendants and legal theories enter the picture, a natural question arises: does a liability cap apply once per lawsuit, once per defendant, or once per underlying loss? TTK Art. 1266 answers this across three paragraphs.

## The Cap Measures the Loss, Not the Number of Claims

Art. 1266(1) provides that the Art. 1262 and 1263 caps apply to the aggregate of *all* claims arising from a single death, injury, or baggage loss — regardless of how many separate legal theories the claimant advances (contract, tort, or otherwise). The cap tracks the underlying economic event, not the number of legal labels attached to it — the same principle the series has seen in the cargo context.

## Multiple Defendants: An Aggregate Ceiling and an Individual Floor

When an actual carrier is involved, a claimant may potentially sue the carrier, the actual carrier, and their respective servants together. Art. 1266(2) imposes two complementary constraints. First, the *aggregate* amount recoverable from all of them together cannot exceed the highest amount for which the carrier or actual carrier alone could be held liable — preventing claimants from multiplying recovery by naming more defendants. Second, no individual defendant can be held liable beyond their *own* applicable limit — protecting each defendant from effectively absorbing another's share.

## Servants Sharing the Carrier's Limit

Art. 1266(3) reinforces this for the specific scenario under Art. 1265, where a servant benefits from the same cap as the carrier: total recovery from the carrier, the actual carrier, and their servants together still cannot exceed that shared cap, even if more than one of them is individually liable for it.

## Case Law

The 2026 Istanbul Regional Court of Appeal decision (12th Civil Chamber, 2026/420-2026/757, not yet final) illustrates the underlying precondition for Art. 1266(3): both the carrier and the individually sued captain were held to the *same* 400,000 SDR cap under Art. 1262 and Art. 1265. The court then found both had acted recklessly under Art. 1267, which — as the decision notes — takes the entire capped system, Art. 1266 included, out of the picture once forfeiture is established.

## Practical Takeaway

For claimants weighing whether to sue multiple parties, Art. 1266 means adding defendants does not multiply the maximum recoverable amount. For carriers and their servants, it confirms that shared exposure to the same cap does not create separate, stackable liability.
