TTK Art. 1218 opens a distinct category of contract-ending events: not accidental physical loss, but deliberate government intervention — and Turkish law responds with an elective termination right rather than automatic lapse.

## What Counts as Government Action, and the No-Damages Termination Right

Art. 1218(1) lists illustrative examples — embargo or requisition of the ship, a trade ban with the destination country, blockade of the loading or discharge port, or a ban on exporting, importing, or transiting the cargo — under the umbrella of "government action" blocking performance. The list is open-ended, covering comparable sovereign interventions not expressly named. Either party may then terminate without owing the other any damages. Unlike accidental loss, which ends a contract automatically, government action is often temporary or uncertain in scope, so the law leaves the commercial judgment about whether to wait it out to the parties themselves.

## Waiting Periods Differ Before and After Voyage Start

If the voyage has not yet begun, termination requires it to appear, on the facts available, that the obstacle will not clear soon — a flexible, forward-looking standard. Once the voyage has started, the rule hardens into a fixed one-month waiting period before termination becomes available. The asymmetry tracks the same point-of-no-return logic seen elsewhere in this part of the Code: pre-voyage parties haven't committed irreversibly, so a flexible standard works; mid-voyage parties need predictability, so a fixed clock applies. These periods run from when the captain learns of the obstacle in port, or otherwise from when the ship next reaches a port after being notified at sea.

## Exceptions: Partial Charters, General Cargo Contracts, and War Risk

Partial voyage charters and general cargo (kırkambar) contracts are exempt from any waiting period — their smaller, more easily substitutable commitments don't justify the same patience requirement (Art. 1218(3)). Separately, where war breaks out and the ship or cargo faces seizure or capture risk, termination is likewise immediate and unconditional, reflecting the far greater severity of that danger compared to an ordinary trade restriction (Art. 1218(4)). Where the obstacle arises before the voyage starts, the shipper's separate right to load substitute cargo instead of terminating remains available (Art. 1218(5)).

## Practical Note

Reported Turkish case law specific to Art. 1218's government-action and war-risk termination grounds is scarce, indicating these disputes are typically resolved through contract terms, diplomatic channels, or insurance rather than litigation.
