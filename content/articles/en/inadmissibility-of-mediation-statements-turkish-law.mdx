*Article 5 – (1) Where a civil action is brought or arbitration is resorted to in relation to the dispute, the parties, the mediator or a third person including those who attended the mediation may not advance the following statements or documents as evidence and may not give testimony about them: a) The mediation invitation made by the parties or one party's wish to participate in the mediation activity. b) Views and offers advanced by the parties for terminating the dispute by way of mediation. c) Proposals advanced by the parties during the mediation activity or the admission of any fact or allegation. d) Documents prepared solely on account of the mediation activity.*

*(3) Disclosure of the information referred to in the first paragraph may not be requested by a court, arbitrator or any administrative authority. Even if these statements or documents have been submitted as evidence contrary to the first paragraph, they cannot form the basis of the judgment. However, the information in question may be disclosed to the extent required by a provision of law or necessary for the application and enforcement of the settlement reached at the end of the mediation process.*

*(5) Subject to the limitations set out in the first paragraph, evidence that may be advanced in civil litigation and arbitration does not become inadmissible merely because it was produced in mediation.*

For mediation to work, a party must be able to say "I am willing to pay this amount". That sentence would never be uttered if it could be used as an admission against them in the subsequent action. Precisely for that reason, Article 5 introduces an **evidentiary bar**: what is said at the mediation table is not evidence in later civil litigation or arbitration, and no testimony may be given about it. The provision stands in a complementary relationship with [Article 4](/articles/confidentiality-principle-turkish-mediation-law) on confidentiality: Article 4 prevents the information from leaving the room, Article 5 prevents it from founding the judgment even if it has left.

## The Four Categories Within the Bar

Article 5/1 lists the protected statements and documents in four sub-paragraphs, and that enumeration is exhaustive.

**Invitation and wish to participate (sub-para. a).** The fact that a party was invited to mediation, or expressed a wish to participate, cannot be construed by the other side as "so they knew they were in the wrong". This protection is designed not to make resorting to mediation psychologically risky.

**Views and offers advanced to terminate the dispute (sub-para. b).** This is the core of the bar. An employer who offers to pay TRY 300,000 in mediation does not face that offer being used against them in the action; the amount offered is not treated as an admission of the existence or quantum of the claim.

**Proposals and the admission of a fact or allegation (sub-para. c).** Not only monetary offers but also admissions as to matters of fact are protected. The statement "yes, we did make them work overtime that day" cannot be treated as an admission in the action so long as it was made at the mediation table.

**Documents prepared solely on account of the mediation activity (sub-para. d).** The word "solely" is decisive here and must be read together with Article 5/5. A calculation table produced only for the negotiation, a draft protocol or the text of the mediator's settlement proposal are protected; a contract, invoice or payslip that has an independent existence does not lose its evidentiary character merely because it was produced in mediation.

Under Article 5/2 the bar applies **regardless of the form** of the statement or document: there is no difference between an oral statement, an e-mail, a message, an audio recording or a written draft. Article 5/4 broadens the scope: the bar operates regardless of whether the later action or arbitration relates to the subject matter of the mediation.

## Who Is Bound, and the Court's Position

The bar binds not only the parties but also the mediator and third persons who attended the negotiations. Those persons may neither advance the statements and documents as evidence nor **give testimony** about them. In practice, a request to hear the mediator as a witness must therefore be refused.

Article 5/3 imposes two distinct duties on the court. The first is a prohibition: a court, arbitrator or administrative authority **may not request** disclosure of this information. Obtaining the contents of the mediation file through an expert examination or a judicial letter of request contravenes this provision. The second is a rule of assessment: these statements and documents **cannot form the basis of the judgment** even if they have been placed on the file despite the bar. In other words, even if the court does not remove an improperly submitted document from the file, it cannot rely on it.

## Two Exceptions: Statutory Command and Enforcement of the Settlement

The final sentence of Article 5/3 recognises two exceptions.

**Disclosure to the extent commanded by a provision of law.** The obligation to attach the final minutes recording failure to settle to the statement of claim in mandatory mediation (Art. 18/A/2; for labour disputes Art. 3/2 of Law No. 7036) falls within this exception. The exception is, however, limited to the final minutes; conveying the content of the negotiations along with the minutes breaches the bar.

**Disclosure to the extent necessary for the application and enforcement of the settlement.** Production of the settlement document is required in a request for an annotation of enforceability, in enforcement proceedings or in a dispute concerning performance of the settlement; that disclosure is lawful.

Furthermore, for the non-attendance sanction in Article 18/A/11 to be applied, the final minutes must identify the party who did not attend the meeting; that record is likewise submitted to the court and forms the basis of the award of litigation costs and attorney's fees. The evidentiary bar does not cover this **procedural** function of the minutes.

## The Limit of the Bar: Article 5/5 and Independent Evidence

Article 5/5 is the provision of greatest practical use, and it says this: evidence that may be advanced in civil litigation and arbitration does not become inadmissible merely because it was produced in mediation. The fact that a document has entered the mediation file does not clothe it in immunity.

The practical consequence is this: a lease contract, invoice or ledger entry produced by the other side during mediation, whose existence you learned of at that moment, may be used as evidence in the action — provided the document was not "prepared solely on account of the mediation activity". The distinction is: **does the document have an existence and purpose independent of the mediation?** If so, Article 5/5 applies; if not, Article 5/1(d) does.

## The Consequence of Breaching the Bar

Article 5 does not provide a direct procedural sanction; the evidentiary bar is itself the sanction. A statement or document submitted contrary to it cannot found the judgment, and this may constitute a ground for reversal on appeal or cassation. Where, however, the same act also breaches the confidentiality obligation in Article 4, criminal liability arises separately under Article 33. The protective spheres of the two provisions overlap but are not identical; a breach of Article 5 is not in every case an offence, while a breach of Article 4 may be punished even in situations that do not call for an evidentiary bar.

## Case Law

The following decisions show how the evidentiary bar in Article 5 operates in practice.

**1. Court of Cassation, 9th Civil Chamber, Case No. 2022/3222, Decision No. 2022/3813, dated 21.03.2022**

The Chamber emphasised that the mediation invitation made by the parties or a party's wish to participate in the mediation activity, the proposals advanced during the activity, the admission of any fact or allegation, and documents prepared solely on account of the mediation activity cannot be advanced as evidence where a civil action is brought in relation to the dispute; and stated that the mediation process rests entirely on the principle of confidentiality.

**2. Court of Cassation, 9th Civil Chamber, Case No. 2023/8055, Decision No. 2023/10315, dated 04.07.2023**

The decision confirms, by enumerating the four sub-paragraphs of Article 5/1 one by one, that these statements and documents cannot be advanced as evidence. The Chamber applies the bar consistently in numerous decisions to the same effect.

**3. Council of State, 10th Chamber, Case No. 2018/3449, Decision No. 2021/1714, dated 08.04.2021**

In an annulment action against the Regulation on the Mediation Law, the Chamber addressed confidentiality (Art. 4), inadmissibility of statements and documents (Art. 5) and meeting with the parties (Art. 8) together, and set out the statutory framework of the evidentiary bar.

**4. Court of Cassation, 3rd Civil Chamber, Case No. 2025/5452, Decision No. 2026/129, dated 15.01.2026**

Reasoning from the fact that Article 5/1 frames the bar as applying "where a civil action is brought or arbitration is resorted to", the Chamber concluded that the legislature expressly regards resort to arbitration after mediation as possible. According to the decision, this provision constitutes the statutory basis for arbitral proceedings on matters on which no settlement, in whole or in part, was reached in mediation.

**5. Antalya Regional Court of Justice, 4th Civil Chamber, Case No. 2022/3227, Decision No. 2025/1198, dated 29.12.2025**

The dispute concerned whether a long-term vehicle lease contract produced during the mediation session could be taken into account in the subsequent action. Founded on the full text of Article 5, the decision has practical value in setting out the limits of the evidentiary bar as regards documents which, although produced in mediation, have an independent existence.
