*Article 17/B – (Added: 28/3/2023-7445/34)*

*(1) Disputes concerning the transfer of immovable property or the creation of a limited right in rem over immovable property are amenable to mediation.*

*(2) In disputes within the scope of the first paragraph, where the parties so agree in writing and the mediator records that agreement in the minutes, an annotation that the power of disposal is restricted is entered in the land registry at the mediator's request, limited to the mediation process and not exceeding three months from the date it is entered. That annotation is removed at the mediator's request where the parties fail to settle or agree on its removal, and automatically at the end of the three-month period.*

*(4) Obtaining the annotation as to the enforceability of the settlement document is compulsory, and this annotation is obtained from the civil court of peace of the place where the immovable property is located. (…)*

*(6) (Added: 7/11/2024-7531/24) After the annotation of enforceability has been granted, one of the parties to the settlement document may request registration from the land registry directorate. Following the examination and assessment required by the legislation on immovable property, the land registry directorate gives effect to the registration request without a deed being drawn up.*

In Turkish law, the transfer of ownership of immovable property is as a rule subject to formal requirements and to an official deed drawn up before the land registrar (Art. 706 of the Turkish Civil Code; Art. 26 of the Land Registry Law). Because of that strict formal rule, whether immovable-property disputes could be resolved through mediation was long debated: if the parties could not transfer ownership even after agreeing at the table, what use was mediation?

Article 17/B ended that debate in 2023, and in 2024 the sixth paragraph added by Law No. 7531 completed the solution. The position today is this: immovable-property disputes are amenable to mediation, a protective annotation may be entered in the land registry throughout the process, and registration may be effected **without a deed being drawn up** on the basis of a settlement document bearing an annotation of enforceability.

## Statutory Confirmation of Mediability

Article 17/B/1 expressly provides that disputes concerning the transfer of immovable property or the creation of a limited right in rem over it are amenable to mediation. The provision confirms the application of the general mediability criterion in Article 1/2 in this field and removes any doubt.

Typical disputes falling within the scope are: claims for cancellation of title and registration arising from a promise-to-sell contract; transfer obligations arising from construction-in-return-for-flat contracts; disputes concerning the creation of limited rights in rem such as usufruct, right of way and building rights; and the property-related consequences of claims based on collusive transactions by a deceased person.

Mediability depends on the existence of the power of disposal. A settlement reached in mediation concerning immovable property subject to an interim injunction, attachment or statutory restriction cannot override those restrictions; indeed Article 17/B/3 requires the settlement document to be drawn up **having regard to the restrictions, procedures and principles laid down in the laws concerning the transfer of immovable property or the creation of limited rights in rem**. Restrictions on the acquisition of immovable property by foreigners also fall within this scope.

## The Three-Month Annotation of Restriction of the Power of Disposal

Article 17/B/2 brings one of mediation's most concrete practical gains. In immovable-property disputes, the risk that the other side might transfer the property to a third person while negotiations were ongoing rendered the process meaningless from the outset. The Law addresses that risk through a mechanism that operates without the need for an interim injunction.

The conditions for the annotation are: the parties' **agreement in writing** and the mediator's **recording of that agreement in the minutes**. Once these two conditions are met, the annotation is entered in the land registry at the mediator's request. This is one of the statutorily recognised exceptions to the monopoly of judicial power in Article 15/4 — the mediator can request an annotation in the land registry without a judicial decision.

The limits of the annotation are equally clear: it is confined to the mediation process and cannot exceed **three months** from the date it is entered. There are three ways it is removed: at the mediator's request where the parties fail to settle or agree on its removal; and **automatically** at the end of the three-month period.

Unlike an interim injunction, the annotation requires no security and rests on no court decision; on the other hand, it depends on the other party's consent. Where consent is lacking, the classic route — an interim injunction before an action is brought — remains open, and under Article 18/A/16 the period for bringing an action after an injunction does not run during the mediation.

## The Compulsory Annotation of Enforceability and the Competent Court

Under the general rule, the annotation of enforceability is optional: a settlement document signed jointly by the parties, their lawyers and the mediator is deemed a document having the character of a judgment under Article 18/4 without an annotation. For immovable-property disputes, Article 17/B/4 says the opposite: **obtaining the annotation is compulsory.**

The competent court is also specially designated: the annotation is obtained not from the place where the mediator performed the duty, but from the civil court of peace of **the place where the immovable property is located**. The 5th Civil Chamber of the Court of Cassation applies this rule strictly, upholding decisions of lack of jurisdiction where applications are made to a court other than that of the location of the property.

The court's power of examination has also been broadened. Alongside the narrow review in Article 18/3 (amenability to mediation and to enforcement), Article 17/B/4 adds a review of **whether the restrictions, procedures and principles laid down in the laws concerning the transfer of immovable property or the creation of limited rights in rem have been complied with**. Within that scope, the court may request information or documents from institutions and organisations and may hold a hearing where necessary.

## Registration Without a Deed: The Meaning of the 2024 Amendment

The original version of Article 17/B left a gap: it was unclear how registration at the land registry was to be effected with a settlement document bearing an annotation of enforceability. That gap was closed by the sixth paragraph added by Law No. 7531 of 07.11.2024.

Accordingly, after the annotation of enforceability has been granted, one of the parties to the settlement document may **request registration** directly from the land registry directorate; after carrying out the examination and assessment required by the legislation on immovable property, the directorate gives effect to the request **without a deed being drawn up**.

This is a statutory exception to the formal rule in Article 706 of the Civil Code and Article 26 of the Land Registry Law, and it has two practical consequences. First, where the other side fails to attend the land registry, there is no need to bring a separate action for cancellation of title and registration. Second, the text of the settlement document now functions as a basis for registration; it becomes essential that elements such as the block, parcel, share ratio and transfer price of the immovable property appear in the document without any room for doubt.

The same possibility is recognised, under Article 18/B/5, for immovable-property disputes within the scope of mandatory mediation.

## What Remains Outside: Mediability versus Condition of Action

Article 17/B treats immovable-property disputes as **mediable**; it does not bring them within the scope of mandatory mediation. This distinction is frequently confused. It is not compulsory to apply to a mediator before bringing an action for cancellation of title and registration concerning a transfer of immovable property; the parties go to mediation if they wish.

By contrast, the disputes within the scope of [Article 18/B](/articles/mandatory-mediation-lease-partition-condominium-neighbour-disputes) — lease relationships, division of movable and immovable property and dissolution of joint ownership, and disputes arising from condominium ownership and neighbour rights — are subject to the condition of action. Where a settlement is reached in such a dispute and it produces a result concerning immovable property, the compulsory annotation is again obtained under Article 18/B/3 from the civil court of peace of the place where the property is located.

## Case Law of the Court of Cassation

The following decisions show the supervision of mediation settlements concerning immovable property.

**1. Court of Cassation, 1st Civil Chamber, Case No. 2024/4691, Decision No. 2025/1955, dated 14.04.2025**

Noting that Article 17/B was added to Law No. 6325 by Article 34 of Law No. 7445 and entered into force on 01.09.2023, the Chamber established that under the third paragraph, where the parties settle at the end of the mediation process, the settlement document must be drawn up having regard to the restrictions, procedures and principles laid down in the laws concerning the transfer of immovable property or the creation of limited rights in rem.

**2. Court of Cassation, 5th Civil Chamber, Case No. 2025/3901, Decision No. 2025/6788, dated 05.05.2025**

In the dispute it was established that the mediation request underlying the annotation of enforceability concerned the transfer of an immovable property. The Chamber stated that in disputes concerning the transfer of immovable property or the creation of a limited right in rem, obtaining the annotation as to the enforceability of the settlement document is compulsory and that this annotation is obtained from the civil court of peace of the place where the property is located.

**3. Court of Cassation, 5th Civil Chamber, Case No. 2025/2296, Decision No. 2025/6840, dated 05.05.2025**

The decision addresses a ruling of lack of jurisdiction given because all the immovable properties subject to the annotation of enforceability were located in another district, and confirms that the annotation must be requested from, and given by, the civil court of peace of the place where the property is located.

**4. Court of Cassation, 4th Civil Chamber, Case No. 2021/1607, Decision No. 2023/4580, dated 29.03.2023**

In the dispute it was argued that, in the mediation settlement minutes produced by the debtor defendant, the claimant had waived the right to bring an action to set aside the transaction in return for money received from the company that acquired the immovable property, and had released that company. The decision shows the need to investigate the consequences that mediation settlements linked to a transfer of immovable property produce as regards third parties' rights.

**5. Court of Cassation, 3rd Civil Chamber, Case No. 2023/4626, Decision No. 2024/2576, dated 26.09.2024**

The parties resorted to voluntary mediation in order to establish reciprocal rights in rem and personal rights over immovable properties and businesses, and the annulment of the settlement document drawn up was sought. The Chamber established that, because the request for an annotation of enforceability had been waived, no annotation had been given to the document and that, as such, the document **did not have the character of a judgment**; and it found the dismissal of the annulment claim correct on the ground that the allegation of defective consent had not been proved by the claimant.
