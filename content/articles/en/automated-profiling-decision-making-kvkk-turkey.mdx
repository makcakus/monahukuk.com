Automated profiling has become a feature of daily life in Turkey's digital economy. When a bank assesses your creditworthiness through an algorithm, when an app assigns you a risk score, or when a recruitment tool screens your CV before any human sees it — all of these actions involve processing personal data about you. Turkey's Personal Data Protection Law (KVKK) covers these activities, and for foreign individuals and companies operating in Antalya or anywhere in Turkey, understanding what the law requires is increasingly important.

## What Automated Profiling Means Under KVKK

Profiling is the use of algorithms or software to analyse personal data and draw conclusions about a person — their financial standing, habits, preferences, or behaviour. Automated decision-making takes this further: a system produces a decision that directly affects an individual without meaningful human review at any stage.

Turkey's KVKK does not use the word "profiling" as a standalone legal term, but the law's broad definition of personal data processing covers all such activities. Any collection, storage, analysis, or use of data about an identifiable individual falls within KVKK's scope. When that processing runs through automated systems, additional protections for the individual apply.

## The Legal Basis for Profiling Activities

No company — Turkish or foreign — may process personal data without a recognised lawful basis under KVKK. For most profiling operations, the available bases are explicit consent from the individual, performance of a contract with them, or the data controller's legitimate interest — provided that interest does not override the individual's fundamental rights and freedoms.

When profiling involves special category data — including health records, biometric data, racial or ethnic origin, or religious beliefs — the bar is higher. Processing this type of data requires explicit, informed, and freely given consent, without exception.

Foreign companies that target Turkish residents with personalised offers, credit assessments, or behavioural scoring must identify a valid lawful basis before running their first algorithm. A privacy notice that vaguely mentions data use without specifying the legal basis for profiling will not satisfy KVKK requirements. If you have reviewed your [KVKK compliance obligations](/en/articles/kvkk-compliance-turkey-data-protection) more broadly, the same rigour applies here.

## Your Right to Object: KVKK Article 11

One of the most practical protections in KVKK sits in Article 11, which lists the rights every data subject holds. Among them is the right to object when results are produced against the individual "solely through automated analysis of processed data." In plain terms: if an algorithm alone — without meaningful human involvement — generates an outcome that works against you, you can formally challenge it.

This matters in practice. A person refused a loan because a system flagged them, or a job applicant eliminated by an AI screening tool, can apply to the data controller and ask for the decision to be reviewed with human involvement. The data controller is required to respond within the period set by law.

What this means for companies using [AI tools with employee or customer data in Turkey](/en/articles/ai-tools-employee-data-kvkk-turkey) is clear: a fully automated pipeline that produces adverse outcomes for individuals must also give those individuals a genuine, accessible way to object. Building in a human review mechanism — and telling people it exists — is both a legal requirement and sound practice.

## What Foreign Companies Operating in Turkey Must Do

If your company processes personal data about individuals living in Turkey, KVKK applies regardless of where your company is registered or where your servers sit. The law's reach covers any processing operation targeting Turkish residents.

For [foreign-controlled companies handling cross-border data transfers](/en/articles/cross-border-data-transfer-foreign-companies-turkey), profiling adds a layer of complexity. Not only must the data transfer itself have a lawful basis, but the profiling activity using that data must also meet KVKK's requirements on automated processing.

Practical compliance steps include:

- **Identify and document the lawful basis** for each profiling activity before it begins.
- **Update your privacy notice** to describe what profiling you conduct, on what legal basis, and what rights individuals hold — including the right to object under KVKK Article 11.
- **Build in a human review step** for automated decisions that produce adverse outcomes, and make sure individuals know how to trigger it.
- **Do not use special category data** in profiling unless you have obtained explicit consent from each person concerned.
- **Register with VERBİS** if your organisation meets the threshold for data controller registration in Turkey.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q: Does KVKK apply to my company if we are based outside Turkey?

Yes. If your company processes personal data about individuals living in Turkey — through a website, an app, or any other channel — KVKK applies regardless of your company's registered address or server location.

### Q: Is explicit consent always required for profiling under KVKK?

Not always. Profiling based on contract performance or legitimate interest can proceed without explicit consent, provided you inform individuals and give them a real right to object. However, any profiling involving special category data always requires explicit consent.

### Q: What happens if someone objects to an automated decision?

The data controller must acknowledge the application and respond within the period set by law. If no response is given, or the objection is rejected without a valid reason, the individual can file a complaint with the KVKK Board, which has the power to impose administrative sanctions on the data controller.

### Q: Do we need a local data representative in Turkey?

Foreign data controllers processing data about Turkish residents may be required to appoint a local representative in Turkey under the relevant secondary legislation. Whether this obligation applies to your organisation depends on the volume and nature of your data processing activities — legal advice tailored to your situation is recommended.
