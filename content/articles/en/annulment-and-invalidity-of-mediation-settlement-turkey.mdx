"I signed it, but afterwards I realised I had accepted far less than I was owed." This is the sentence most often heard after mediation. The answer is harsher than most people expect: a signed settlement document closes the road to litigation on the matters agreed, and reopening that door is possible only in exceptional cases.

This article explains which arguments actually succeed and which are not accepted in practice.

## The Rule: No Action on the Matters Agreed

The express provision of Law No. 6325 is this: where a settlement is reached at the end of the mediation activity, the parties may not bring an action on the matters agreed.

This rule draws no distinction between voluntary and mandatory mediation. A settlement reached in a voluntary mediation — whether at the court's encouragement or on the parties' own initiative — produces the same effect.

In judicial practice this bar is characterised as **a particular manifestation of the procedural requirement of legal interest**: since the settlement document counts as an instrument in the nature of a court judgment, the parties no longer need to sue on the matters agreed. The rule is applied by the judge of their own motion, and an action brought despite the settlement is dismissed without the merits being examined.

The only door is the **invalidity** of the document — and that door is expressly recognised in the case law. The 3rd Civil Chamber of the Turkish Court of Cassation accepts that there are exceptions to the bar on suing, and that an action on the same dispute may be brought where the settlement document is **forged**, where the settlement is vitiated by **defects of consent**, or where it is otherwise **invalid**. In another decision of the same Chamber, resolving a conflict between regional courts of appeal, it was stated that annulment of the minutes is possible where the **signature on the settlement minutes is shown to be forged**, where **a party's consent was vitiated**, or where **the mediation session was not conducted in due form**; that once the annulment decision becomes final the bar on suing falls away; and that the matters contained in the annulled minutes may then be made the subject of an action.

## The Settlement Document Is a Contract

The characterisation given by the 9th Civil Chamber opens this door: a mediation settlement document is **a substantive-law contract in the sense of the law of obligations**.

The consequence is that the rules on the invalidity of contracts also apply to the settlement document:

- **Mistake** — Article 30 et seq. of the Turkish Code of Obligations
- **Fraud (deceit)** — Article 36
- **Duress** — Article 37
- **Unconscionable exploitation (lesion)** — Article 28
- Grounds of absolute nullity — illegality or immorality, impossibility, incapacity.

## The One-Year Preclusive Period

People assume the right to annul for defect of consent has no time limit; it does. Under Article 39 of the Turkish Code of Obligations, this right is subject to a **one-year preclusive period** running from the moment the mistake or fraud is discovered or the effect of the duress ceases.

The 9th Civil Chamber accepts that, for a voluntary mediation document, a claim of invalidity must be dismissed once this one-year period has expired; in annulment actions brought years after the minutes were drawn up, the period is treated as having lapsed. Once the period has run, the claim will not be heard however meritorious it may be.

## The Burden of Proof Is Heavy

The second and greater obstacle is proof. The Court of Cassation does not regard an abstract assertion of a defect of consent as sufficient.

The Chamber has consistently held that where a claimant admits signing the information minutes and the settlement document, and does not dispute their signature, a claim of fraud cannot be treated as proven unless supported by **concrete witness testimony or other evidence**. Likewise, decisions dismissing claims of mistake, fraud and duress on the ground that they could not be proved by the evidence in the file are upheld.

The practical conclusion is this: **a signed information record creates a strong presumption against you.** The defence "I did not understand the process" is very hard to sustain if you signed the record.

A high level of education or institutional experience is not decisive on its own either; in one dispute it was argued that the claimant's senior position and experience did not mean they had prior knowledge of the institution of mediation.

## The Easier Route: The Document Not Counting as a "Mediation Settlement" at All

While proving a defect of consent is difficult, there is another route that succeeds far more often in practice: arguing that the document **is not a valid mediation settlement document at all**.

The 9th Civil Chamber has expressly recognised this route. A text that is not treated as valid cannot benefit from the bar on suing; the document is then assessed under Article 420 of the Turkish Code of Obligations or the rules on performance. For an employee the consequence is this: at most, the document serves as a basis for **setting off the amount paid**; it does not extinguish all claims.

The principal situations in which a document is not treated as valid:

**Where the dispute is not capable of mediation.** The Court of Cassation treats mediation minutes "in the nature of a release" — drawn up on the day the employment contract ended or immediately afterwards, without any genuine dispute and without any application by the employee — as incapable of mediation, on the ground that they were drawn up at a time when the parties could not freely dispose. In one case, the fact that the designation of the mediator and the first- and final-session minutes were all drawn up **on the same day** was taken as an indicator of this conclusion.

**Where the mediator breached their impartiality.** The Chamber accepted that a text drawn up in a case where the mediator had acted against one of the parties was not a valid mediation settlement document.

**Where the duties of information and of stating one's capacity were breached.** In a case where there was no document showing that the parties had been invited, where the minutes had the character of a release, and where the mediator had never explained that they were a mediator or what the legal consequences of the process were, this was held to be incompatible with the mediator's independent and impartial position.

**Where no process was conducted at all.** Documents drawn up merely to obtain a signature, without the parties ever being brought together, do not count as mediation in the statutory sense.

## Can Minutes Recording a Failure to Settle Be the Subject of a Separate Action?

The regime for settlement minutes differs from that for minutes recording a **failure to settle**.

Irregularities in minutes recording a failure to settle — not having been invited, a contradiction of dates in the minutes, a missing signature — are as a rule raised as a defence in the substantive action to be brought; no separate annulment action may be brought for that purpose.

The 11th Civil Chamber upheld a decision **dismissing on procedural grounds, for want of legal interest**, an annulment action brought by a company claiming the mediation invitation letter had not been served on it, on the ground that such allegations may be raised in the action to be brought against it and assessed by the court of its own motion. The 9th Civil Chamber likewise endorsed the assessment that an error in the constitution of the parties attributable to the mediator cannot be held against the other side, and that, having regard to the principle of good faith, bringing a separate annulment action was not appropriate.

The position differs for settlement minutes: because the settlement closes the road to litigation, establishing its invalidity does require a separate action.

## Is Partial Invalidity Possible?

If the settlement document contains more than one item, a claim of invalidity may not cover all of them. Setting out the items separately makes an assessment of partial invalidity easier; where the document is drawn up on the basis of a single lump sum, separating them becomes difficult.

This leads once again to the same conclusion about drafting: naming each item separately is in the party's interest both at the moment of signature and afterwards.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q: Do I have a right of withdrawal from the settlement?

No. The Law provides no cooling-off period for a mediation settlement. The document is binding from the moment it is signed, and no action may be brought on the matters agreed.

### Q: Is a very low amount in itself a ground for annulment?

No. In the enforceability annotation review the court does not examine whether the settlement is fair. A low amount can only be relied on together with the elements of unconscionable exploitation — taking advantage of the other party's straitened circumstances, thoughtlessness or inexperience, and a clear disproportion between the performances.

### Q: In which court do I bring the annulment action?

In the court that has jurisdiction over the substance of the dispute. The invalidity of a settlement document concerning employee claims is raised before the labour court; that of a commercial settlement before the commercial court of first instance.

### Q: Do I have to go to mediation again when bringing an annulment action?

It depends on the nature of the claim. If, together with a claim for a declaration that the settlement document is invalid, a claim within the scope of mandatory mediation is also made, whether the requirement has separately been satisfied for that claim will be debated; the Court of Cassation assesses this on a case-by-case basis.

### Q: What happens to the money paid if the settlement is annulled?

Even if the document is held invalid, the payment made is not disregarded. The Court of Cassation accepts that texts which are not valid mediation settlement documents must be assessed under the rules on performance; the amount paid is **set off** against the claim awarded.
