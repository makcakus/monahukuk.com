A foundation is a different legal structure from an association: not a body of persons but a body of assets dedicated to a purpose. That structural difference produces a different regime for foreigners as well. Whereas no prior permission is required to found an association, the founding of a foundation by foreigners is made conditional on **reciprocity**. In addition, the acquisition of assets by a foundation is subject to special rules that vary according to the nationality of its founders. These two points are decisive for foreigners planning to found a foundation in Türkiye.

## The Reciprocity Condition

Article 5 of Law No. 5737 on Foundations governs the formation of new foundations and lays down a single-sentence but decisive rule for foreigners: **foreigners may found new foundations in Türkiye on the basis of legal and factual reciprocity.**

The provision requires two distinct forms of reciprocity, and both must be satisfied.

**Legal reciprocity** looks at whether the legislation of the country of which the foreigner is a national affords Turkish citizens the possibility of founding a foundation in that country. This concerns the regulation on paper.

**Factual reciprocity** looks at practice: even where the legislation affords the possibility, it is required that Turkish citizens are in fact able to found foundations in that country. It is no accident that the law writes both concepts expressly; the point is that a right existing only in the text is not to be regarded as sufficient.

Whether reciprocity exists is a matter that must be researched separately in relation to the country concerned before an application. Formation processes begun without that examination become blocked at a later stage.

## The Formation Regime and Minimum Assets

Article 5 also determines the formation regime for new foundations: **new foundations are formed and carry on activity in accordance with the provisions of the Turkish Civil Code.** The procedure for forming a foundation is thus subject to the general civil law regime; Law No. 5737 adds special rules on top of it.

As to assets, the article provides: **the minimum assets to be dedicated on the formation of new foundations according to their purposes are determined each year by the Assembly.** The "Assembly" here is the Foundations Assembly. The amount is not fixed by statute but designed as a threshold determined annually — so the figure for the current year must be confirmed when planning a formation.

The same article also governs the opening of branches and representative offices: new foundations may open branches and representative offices, **on condition that they make a declaration to the Directorate General**, in order to achieve the purposes set out in their foundation deeds. The procedure and principles for making the declaration are laid down by regulation. What is adopted here is a system of declaration, not permission.

## Acquisition of Immovable Property: The Reference to Article 35 of the Land Registry Law

For foundations with a foreign element, the most important restriction concerns the acquisition of assets. Article 12 of Law No. 5737, having governed the acquisition of assets by foundations in general, lays down a special rule: **Article 35 of Land Registry Law No. 2644 of 22/12/1934 applies to the acquisition of immovable property by foundations the majority of whose founders are foreign nationals.**

The practical significance of this reference is considerable. Article 35 of the Land Registry Law, as re-enacted by Law No. 6302 of 3/5/2012, restricts the acquisition of immovable property by foreigners in several respects at once: acquisition is possible for nationals of **countries determined by the President**; the total area of the immovable property acquired may not exceed **ten per cent of the area of the district subject to private ownership, and thirty hectares per person nationwide**; [restrictions in military forbidden zones and security zones](/en/articles/military-zone-restrictions-foreign-property-turkey) are reserved; and those who purchase unbuilt immovable property must submit the project they intend to develop to the approval of the relevant Ministry **within two years**. A foundation the majority of whose founders are foreign becomes subject to that regime when acquiring immovable property.

One distinction should be underlined here: the **reciprocity requirement** in Article 5 of Law No. 5737 concerns **the formation of the foundation**, whereas the regime established by Article 35 of the Land Registry Law concerns **the acquisition of immovable property** and, since the 2012 amendment, rests not on reciprocity but on the list of countries determined by the President. The two conditions are not interchangeable; a foundation with a foreign majority must satisfy each of them separately.

The criterion is **the majority of the founders** — not the nationality of the board of directors or of the employees. Deliberately determining the founder structure at the formation stage directly affects the foundation's future capacity to acquire immovable property.

The same article also imposes a notification obligation applicable to all foundations: foundation administrators notify the Directorate General of immovable property they acquire or change **within one month from the date of registration in the land registry**.

## Commercial Enterprises and Forming Companies

Article 26 of Law No. 5737 governs the formation of commercial enterprises and companies by foundations and maintains the same logic for foundations with a foreign element: **the provisions governing the acquisition of assets by such foundations apply to the acquisition of assets by companies formed by foundations the majority of whose founders are foreign nationals, or more than half of whose shares are held by such foundations.**

This provision prevents the restriction being circumvented through a corporate structure. A company formed by a foundation with a foreign majority, or more than half of whose shares belong to such a foundation, is subject when acquiring immovable property to the regime applicable to the foundation — that is, to Article 35 of the Land Registry Law.

The article also contains a general limit: income derived from these activities may not be allocated to any purpose other than that of the foundation.

## Foundation or Association?

Foreigners planning to organise in Türkiye should make this choice at the outset, because the regimes differ markedly:

| | Association (Law No. 5253) | Foundation (Law No. 5737) |
|---|---|---|
| Formation by a foreigner | Legal capacity suffices; no prior permission | **Legal and factual reciprocity** required |
| Structure | Body of persons | Body of assets dedicated to a purpose |
| Minimum assets | Not provided for | **Determined annually by the Foundations Assembly** |
| Acquisition of immovable property | General provisions | Where the majority of founders are foreign, **Article 35 of the Land Registry Law** |

[Founding an association as a foreigner](/en/articles/foreigners-establishing-associations-turkey) is subject to a more flexible regime; a foundation, by contrast, offers different advantages in terms of permanent assets and continuity. The choice should be made according to the nature of the purpose and the plan for the assets.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I found a foundation in Türkiye as a foreigner?

Article 5 of Law No. 5737 makes this possible, but subjects it to the condition of **legal and factual reciprocity**. Both the legislation and the practice of your country of nationality must afford Turkish citizens the possibility of founding a foundation.

### How much in assets is required for formation?

The law does not fix the amount. Article 5 provides that the minimum assets to be dedicated on the formation of new foundations according to their purposes are **determined each year by the Foundations Assembly**. The figure for the current year must be confirmed.

### Can the foundation I form acquire immovable property in Türkiye?

If the majority of the founders are foreign nationals, Article 35 of the Land Registry Law applies to the foundation's acquisition of immovable property. That means limits such as the list of countries determined by the President, the ceiling of ten per cent of the district area and thirty hectares per person, and military zone restrictions come into play. It should not be confused with the reciprocity condition applicable on formation; Article 35 does not rest on the principle of reciprocity. Planning the founder structure at the formation stage is therefore important.

### Can I form a company through the foundation and acquire immovable property that way?

The law has closed that route. Under Article 26, the provisions to which the foundation is subject also apply to the acquisition of assets by companies formed by foundations the majority of whose founders are foreign nationals, or more than half of whose shares such foundations hold.
