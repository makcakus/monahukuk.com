*Article 28 – (1) In order to carry out the duties set out in this Law, a Department is established within the General Directorate.*

*(2) In order to carry out the duties set out in this Law concerning mediation services, a Mediation Board is formed within the Ministry.*

*(3) (Added: 12/10/2017-7036/26) In order to inform those applying for mediation, to appoint mediators and to carry out the other duties assigned by law, mediation offices are established in the courthouses deemed appropriate by the Ministry. (…) The mediation offices operate under the supervision and control of a civil judge of peace designated by the Council of Judges and Prosecutors. (…)*

The Turkish mediation system is built not on a professional-body model but **directly on the organisation of the Ministry of Justice**. Mediators have no chamber, union or bar-like organisation; the Ministry keeps the registry, a board within the Ministry lays down the professional rules, the offices in courthouses make the appointments, and those offices work under the supervision of a civil judge of peace. Articles 28-32 establish that structure.

The practical importance of the structure is that all administrative acts relating to mediation are subject to review by the **administrative courts**; indeed, the application of these provisions has been shaped by the case law of the Council of State.

## A Three-Legged Organisation

**The Mediation Department (Art. 28/1, Art. 29).** Established within the General Directorate of Legal Affairs; it consists of a head of department, a sufficient number of judge-rapporteurs and other staff. It is the executive organ of the system.

**The Mediation Board (Art. 28/2, Art. 31).** Formed within the Ministry with broad participation: the General Director of Legal Affairs, the Head of Department, two judges selected by the Council of Judges and Prosecutors from among first-class judges serving in the civil courts, three representatives of the Union of Turkish Bar Associations, one representative of the Turkish Notaries Union, one academic in private law selected by the Council of Higher Education, three mediators selected by the Minister of Justice, one representative of the Union of Chambers and Commodity Exchanges of Türkiye, one representative each from the three workers' union confederations with the most members, one representative of the employers' union confederation with the most employer members, one representative of the Confederation of Turkish Tradesmen and Craftsmen, and the Head of the Training Centre of the Turkish Justice Academy. The Board is chaired by the General Director.

The Board meets **at least twice a year**, in March and September; it may be convened at any time at the request of the Chair or of at least five members. It takes decisions by an absolute majority of the total number of members. A member who fails to attend two consecutive meetings without excuse loses their membership. Members appointed from outside the Ministry serve for three years.

**The mediation offices (Art. 28/3).** Added in 2017, this paragraph sets up the field structure needed to operate mandatory mediation. An office is established in the courthouses deemed appropriate by the Ministry; the judicial commission assigns a registrar and sufficient staff to work exclusively in these offices. The offices work **under the supervision and control of a civil judge of peace designated by the Council of Judges and Prosecutors**. Where no office is established, this duty is performed by the registry of the civil court of peace designated by the judicial commission, under the supervision and control of the relevant judge.

## Division of Tasks: Who Decides What

Reading Articles 30 and 32 together reveals the distribution of powers within the system — a distribution frequently confused in practice.

**Duties of the Department (Art. 30).** To ensure that mediation services are conducted in an orderly and efficient manner; to publish and support scientific work; to carry out the decisions and transactions relating to the Board's work and to cooperate with institutions; to promote the institution; to monitor practice, keep and publish statistics; to submit the applications of training institutions and requests for extension of permits to the Ministry's approval for decision, and to list the training institutions; **to keep the mediator registry, to decide on registration requests and to decide on removal from the registry under Articles 21/1 and 21/3**; to keep records of the final minutes drawn up by mediators and to retain copies of them; to conduct examination and research on the legislation and make proposals; to prepare the annual activity report and plan; **to prepare the annual Mediation Minimum Fee Tariff**.

**Duties of the Board (Art. 32).** To determine the basic principles of mediation services and the **professional rules of mediation**; to determine the basic principles and standards of training and examination; to lay down the rules on the supervision of mediators; to finalise draft regulations; **to revoke the training permits of training institutions**; **to decide on the removal of a mediator from the registry under Article 21/2**; to set the registration and annual fees; **to approve the Mediation Minimum Fee Tariff**; to give recommendations and opinions on the Department's activities.

Two distinctions matter particularly. **Removal from the registry**: where a condition is absent or lost, or at the mediator's own request, the Department decides; where removal is based on a breach of obligations, the deciding authority is the Board. **The fee tariff**: the Department prepares it, and the Board approves it, making changes if necessary.

## The Debate on the Code of Ethics

The Turkish Mediators' Code of Ethics, issued by the Board on the basis of its duty to "determine the professional rules of mediation", was challenged on grounds of competence. The Assembly of Administrative Law Chambers of the Council of State rejected the argument that the Board had not been given that power, noting that Articles 30 and 32 of Law No. 6325 assign to the Department the task of ensuring that mediation services are conducted in an orderly and efficient manner, and to the Board the task of determining the professional rules of mediation.

The 10th Chamber of the Council of State assessed the same regulation on the merits, holding that although both the Law and the Regulation contain general provisions on the basic principles, rights and obligations relating to mediation and mediators, laying down a code of ethics for mediators was necessary in order to meet an important need and was required for the coherence of the mediation legislation.

## The Role of the Offices in Practice

The mediation offices are the first authority a party deals with in mandatory mediation, and their powers are listed one by one in the Law: accepting the application (Art. 18/A/4), appointing the mediator from the list (Art. 18/A/5), researching the parties' contact details in official records (Art. 18/A/6), providing that information to the mediator (Art. 18/A/7), sending the file to the civil court of peace upon an objection to jurisdiction and serving the court's decision on the parties (Art. 18/A/8), and covering necessary expenses from the Ministry's budget (Art. 18/A/14).

These records carry evidential value in practice. The 9th Civil Chamber of the Turkish Court of Cassation, assessing together the letters received from the mediation office and from the Mediation Department, established that the employees had themselves selected the mediator, attended the sessions in person and exercised their power of choice. Where it is disputed how the process actually unfolded, records obtained from these institutions become decisive.

## Decisions of the Council of State

The following decisions illustrate the review of the division of powers and duties within the mediation organisation.

**1. Council of State, Assembly of Administrative Law Chambers, File No. 2023/1830, Decision No. 2024/1165, 23.05.2024**

The Assembly rejected the argument that the Mediation Board had not been empowered to lay down the Turkish Mediators' Code of Ethics, on the ground that Articles 30 and 32 of Law No. 6325 assign to the Department the task of ensuring that mediation services are conducted in an orderly and efficient manner and to the Board the task of determining the professional rules of mediation.

**2. Council of State, 10th Chamber, File No. 2018/62, Decision No. 2022/6437, 22.12.2022**

Considering together the warning and removal procedure of Article 21 and the Department's duty under Article 30/1-a, the Chamber held that laying down a code of ethics for mediators was necessary in order to meet an important need and was required for the coherence of the mediation legislation.

**3. Council of State, 10th Chamber, File No. 2020/2311, Decision No. 2021/6526, 20.12.2021**

By enumerating the Board's duties — determining the basic principles and professional rules, establishing the principles and standards for training and for the qualities of training institutions as well as the basic principles and standards of the examination, and laying down the rules on the supervision of mediators — the decision sets out the limits of the Board's regulatory power.

**4. Council of State, 10th Chamber, File No. 2020/5771, Decision No. 2021/6529, 20.12.2021**

Within the same framework, the Chamber assessed the Board's duty to determine training, examination and supervision standards, and examined the lawfulness of the regulatory acts adopted in the exercise of those duties.

**5. Council of State, 10th Chamber, File No. 2018/3449, Decision No. 2021/1714, 08.04.2021**

Taking the mediation legislation as a whole — together with the provisions on confidentiality, the exclusion of evidence, meeting the parties and mandatory mediation — the decision reviews whether the Ministry's regulatory acts remain within the framework drawn by the Law.
