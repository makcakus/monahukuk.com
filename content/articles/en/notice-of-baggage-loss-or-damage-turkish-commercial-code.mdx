A passenger with a genuine baggage claim can still lose it through a simple procedural failure: not giving notice on time. TTK Art. 1269 sets the deadlines, and mirrors — while notably softening — the parallel notice regime for cargo under Art. 1185.

## Two Timing Tracks

Under Art. 1269(1), if the loss or damage is *externally apparent*, the passenger must notify the carrier at or before disembarkation (for cabin baggage) or at or before delivery (for other baggage) — effectively, on the spot. If the damage is *not externally apparent*, written notice is due within fifteen days of disembarkation, delivery, or the date delivery was due (a phrasing that also covers baggage that never arrives at all).

The fifteen-day window for hidden damage is notably more generous than the equivalent three-day period for cargo under Art. 1185. This is a deliberate, sensible distinction: a commercial cargo recipient typically has dedicated staff and processes for inspecting incoming goods quickly, while an ordinary passenger handling personal baggage realistically needs more time to unpack, notice a hidden defect, and prepare written notice.

## Consequence of No Notice: A Simple Presumption

Under Art. 1269(2), if the passenger fails to give notice, the baggage is presumed — rebuttably — to have been received in good condition. This is notably simpler than the cargo-context presumption under Art. 1185(4), which layers two separate presumptions (goods delivered as described in the bill of lading, and any later-discovered damage not attributable to the carrier). The simplification makes sense: passenger baggage does not travel with an itemized shipping document the way cargo does, so there is no equivalent reference point for a "delivered as documented" presumption.

## Exception: Joint Survey

Under Art. 1269(3), if the condition of the baggage was subject to a joint inspection or survey at the time of delivery, written notice is not required at all — a joint, contemporaneous inspection is inherently more reliable evidence than a later written notice, so it substitutes for it entirely.

## Practical Takeaway

For passengers, the message is simple: report visible damage immediately, and hidden damage within fifteen days in writing, or risk a presumption working against the claim. For carriers, maintaining consistent joint-inspection procedures at delivery can prevent disputes over whether notice was properly given.

No confirmed case law applying Art. 1269's specific notice periods or presumption directly has been identified — a pattern consistent with this being a relatively new and narrow procedural provision.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Within what time must damage to luggage be notified?

TTK Article 1269/1 draws two distinctions. Where the damage is <b>apparent</b>, notice must be given for cabin luggage before or during disembarkation and for other luggage before or at the time of delivery. Where the damage is <b>not apparent</b> (hidden damage), written notice is required within <b>fifteen days</b> of the luggage being disembarked or delivered, or of the date on which it should have been delivered.

### Is the right to compensation lost if notice is not given in time?

No. TTK Article 1269/2 does not lay down a preclusive period; it merely establishes a <b>presumption</b>: if no notice is given, the passenger is deemed, <b>until the contrary is proved</b>, to have received the luggage in good condition. If the passenger proves by other evidence that the loss arose during the carriage, they may claim compensation. Missing the period merely shifts the burden of proof onto the passenger.

### Is there a case in which no notice is required?

Yes. Under TTK Article 1269/3, if the condition of the luggage was the subject of a <b>joint examination or ascertainment</b> at the time of delivery, no separate written notice is required. Where the parties have drawn up a joint record or have had evidence preserved through the court, the record that the notice is meant to create already exists; in that case the presumption in Article 1269/2 does not operate either.
