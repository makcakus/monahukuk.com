*Article 7 – (1) The mediator has the right to claim a fee and expenses in return for the activity carried out. The mediator may also request an advance for the fee and expenses.*

*(2) Unless otherwise agreed, the mediator's fee is determined according to the Mediator Minimum Fee Tariff in force on the date the activity ends, and the fee and expenses are borne equally by the parties.*

*(3) The mediator may not receive a fee in return for acting as an intermediary for, or recommending, particular persons in relation to the mediation process. Transactions contrary to this prohibition are void.*

The mediation fee is the most concrete aspect of the process and the one that raises the most questions. Who pays how much is determined on three separate levels: the general rule in Article 7, the provisions specific to mandatory mediation in Article 18/A/12-14, and the Mediation Minimum Fee Tariff published each year by the Ministry of Justice. These three levels do not replace one another; Article 7 applies in voluntary mediation, while Article 18/A applies predominantly in mandatory mediation.

## The General Rule: The Tariff and Equal Sharing

Article 7/2 contains two separate rules. The first is the basis of calculation: unless otherwise agreed, the fee is determined according to the Tariff **in force on the date the activity ends**. It is not the tariff at the date of application but the one at the date the final minutes are drawn up that applies — a practical difference for processes running close to the turn of the year. The second is the sharing rule: the fee and expenses are borne equally by the parties.

Both rules are default rules; the phrase "unless otherwise agreed" gives the parties freedom of contract. They may set the fee above the tariff, or agree that one party bears it in full. By contrast, a fee **below** the Tariff cannot be agreed; the Tariff is a minimum.

The Tariff has two parts. **Part One** sets the hourly fee applicable where no settlement is reached and varies according to the type of dispute (employment, commercial, consumer, lease and others). **Part Two** sets the fee applicable where a settlement is reached, calculated proportionally on the amount agreed.

## Advances and Expenses

Article 7/1 gives the mediator power to request an advance for the fee and expenses. In practice this power operates in voluntary mediation; in mandatory mediation Article 18/A/14 provides that the necessary expenses that must be incurred by the office are covered from the budget of the Ministry of Justice, to be paid by the parties in accordance with the settlement if one is reached and to be collected from the party who is ultimately unsuccessful if it is not.

The concept of "expenses" is distinct from the fee and covers items such as service of documents, travel, accommodation, room hire and interpreter costs. That these can be claimed independently of the fee is the express consequence of Article 7/1.

## The Fee in Mandatory Mediation: Article 18/A/12-13

In mandatory mediation the fee regime varies according to whether the parties settle and how long the session lasts.

**Where a settlement is reached** (Article 18/A/12): the fee is borne equally by the parties, unless otherwise agreed, according to Part Two of the Mediation Fee Tariff annexed to the Tariff. In that case the fee **cannot be less than the amount of the two-hour fee** determined in Part One.

**Where the parties cannot be reached, where no session can be held because the parties did not attend, or where there is a failure to settle after sessions lasting less than two hours** (Article 18/A/13): the amount of the two-hour fee is paid **from the budget of the Ministry of Justice** according to Part One of the Tariff. This is one of the key design features of mandatory mediation: because the process is compulsory, it is intended that no cost should be imposed on the party.

**Where there is a failure to settle after sessions lasting more than two hours**: the fee relating to the part exceeding two hours is borne equally by the parties, unless otherwise agreed, according to Part One of the Tariff, having regard to the subject matter of the dispute.

In all three cases, the mediation fee — whether paid from the Ministry's budget or borne by the parties — **counts as a litigation cost**. The 10th Chamber of the Council of State has stated the purpose of this rule expressly: to ensure that, in an action brought after the mediation has produced no result, the mediation fee is added to the litigation costs in the calculation.

## No Fee Arises Where the Dispute Is Not Capable of Mediation

A frequent question in practice is whether the mediator is entitled to a fee where they end the process without holding any meeting, having found the dispute not capable of mediation. Relying on the relevant provision of the Mediation Minimum Fee Tariff, the 9th Civil Chamber of the Turkish Court of Cassation has held that no fee is payable to the mediator where the mandatory mediation process is terminated because the dispute is not capable of mediation. The result protects both the party and the Ministry's budget from an unnecessary burden.

As an extension of the same logic, awarding a mediation fee in a file where no application to mediation was ever made is likewise erroneous. By contrast, even where the action is dismissed on procedural grounds for failure to satisfy the mediation condition precedent, if the process actually took place the fee is ruled on as a litigation cost; the 9th Civil Chamber has treated the failure to rule on this point as a ground for reversal, precisely because the mediation fee is a litigation cost.

## The Ban on Fees for Referrals and Recommendations

Article 7/3 lays down a prohibition directed against the commercialisation of mediation: the mediator may not receive a fee in return for acting as an intermediary for, or recommending, particular persons in relation to the mediation process. The prohibition is broad — any benefit received in return for recommending a lawyer, expert, consultant, appraiser or another mediator falls within it.

The sanction is severe: **transactions contrary to this prohibition are void**. What is void is the fee arrangement based on the referral or recommendation; the mediation process itself does not automatically become invalid for that reason. But the conduct also creates a circumstance casting doubt on the mediator's impartiality, giving rise to the duty of disclosure under Article 9/2 and capable of triggering the removal procedure under Article 21/2.

## Legal Aid

A party unable to meet the mediation fee is not without recourse. Under Article 13/3, a party who needs legal aid to meet the mediation fee may benefit from it by decision of the civil court of peace at the place where the mediation office is located; Articles 334 to 340 of the Turkish Code of Civil Procedure apply by analogy.

## Decisions of the Court of Cassation and the Council of State

The following decisions show the practice on who bears the mediation fee and in which situations.

**1. Council of State, 10th Chamber, File No. 2024/6835, Decision No. 2025/2132, 21.04.2025**

The Chamber stated that where the statutory conditions are met the mediation fee is paid from the budget of the Ministry of Justice, and emphasised that the purpose of treating that fee as a litigation cost is to ensure that, in an action brought after the mediation has produced no result, the mediation fee is added to the litigation costs in the calculation.

**2. Court of Cassation, 9th Civil Chamber, File No. 2023/18517, Decision No. 2024/1726, 08.02.2024**

The decision establishes that, under the relevant provision of the Mediation Minimum Fee Tariff, no fee is payable to the mediator where the mandatory mediation process is terminated because the dispute is not capable of mediation.

**3. Court of Cassation, 9th Civil Chamber, File No. 2021/5387, Decision No. 2021/9704, 27.05.2021**

The Chamber found it erroneous to award a mediation fee although mediation had never been resorted to at the litigation stage; but, holding that curing this error did not require a retrial, it corrected the judgment and upheld it as corrected.

**4. Court of Cassation, 9th Civil Chamber, File No. 2022/12116, Decision No. 2022/15180, 23.11.2022**

The decision states that under Law No. 6325 the mediation fee is a litigation cost and that the failure to rule on it was erroneous; it also has regard to the need to decide in a way that does not lead to duplicate payments in an action that may be brought again.

**5. Court of Cassation, 11th Civil Chamber, File No. 2022/6582, Decision No. 2024/2681, 02.04.2024**

The dispute concerned an order to collect a mediation fee from a party in an action dismissed on procedural grounds for failure to satisfy the mediation condition precedent. The decision is instructive for assessing the objection that no mediation fee can arise where mediation was never resorted to at all.
