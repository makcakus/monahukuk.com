When you want to sue over a claim in Türkiye, your first stop is in most cases not the court but the mediation office in the courthouse. In employee claims, commercial receivables, consumer disputes, lease relationships, dissolution of joint ownership, condominium and neighbour-law disputes, an action brought without first applying to a mediator is dismissed without the merits ever being examined.

This article explains what mediation is and how the process works step by step, focusing on the points most often misunderstood in practice.

## What Exactly Is Mediation?

Turkish Law No. 6325 on Mediation in Civil Disputes defines mediation as follows: a method of dispute resolution conducted with the participation of an impartial and independent third person who, applying systematic techniques, brings the parties together, establishes a communication process between them so that they understand each other and produce their own solutions, and who may also put forward a settlement proposal if it emerges that the parties cannot produce a solution.

The most important part of that definition is this: **the mediator does not decide.** They do not render a judgment like a judge, nor a binding award like an arbitrator. They cannot collect evidence, appoint an expert or grant an interim measure. The only thing they can do is build a negotiating ground on which the parties can produce their own solution.

A mediator is a natural person entered in the registry kept by the Department of Mediation of the Ministry of Justice, holding a law degree and with at least five years' seniority in their profession. No one who is not registered may act as a mediator.

## Mandatory or Voluntary?

Both. Two separate mediation regimes operate side by side in Turkish law.

**Mandatory mediation (as a condition precedent to litigation)** requires that a mediator has been applied to before an action can be brought in certain disputes. Its scope has widened progressively since 2018.

**Voluntary mediation** is available in every private law dispute over which the parties may freely dispose. Even if your dispute falls outside the mandatory scope — a tort claim, an occupational accident case, an action for cancellation and registration of title — the parties may go to a mediator if they wish.

A common mistake here is to assume that "because it is voluntary, its consequences are lighter". That is wrong. A settlement reached in voluntary mediation is fully binding as well; no further action may be brought on the matters agreed.

## What Is Mandatory Is Not Settling but Sitting at the Table

This distinction is the most confused aspect of the process. In mandatory mediation the only compulsory things are to **apply** and to **conclude** the process with final minutes. Settling is never compulsory.

You may attend the meeting, say "I do not accept any offer" and leave; the failure to settle is recorded in the final minutes, your road to litigation opens, and you face no sanction.

The sanction attaches only to **not attending at all**: a party who fails to attend the first meeting without a valid excuse is named in the final minutes and, even if entirely successful in the action subsequently brought, is held liable for half of the other party's litigation costs; in addition, half of the attorney's fee determined under the Minimum Attorney Fee Tariff is awarded in favour of the other party.

## The Process Step by Step

**1. Application.** The application is made to the mediation office at the place of the court having jurisdiction over the subject matter of the dispute. In the application you provide contact details for yourself and, if known, for the other party. The office also has power to research contact details from official records.

**2. Appointment.** The mediator is designated by the office from the commission list. However, if the parties agree on a particular mediator appearing on the list, that person is appointed.

**3. Invitation.** The mediator informs the parties using any means of communication and invites them to the first meeting. Under a rule added in 2023, even where a party has a lawyer the mediator must **also inform the party themselves**. These steps must be documented.

**4. First meeting and information.** At the outset the mediator must inform the parties about the essentials, process and consequences of mediation. In practice an "information record" is signed for this purpose.

**5. Negotiation.** The mediator may meet the parties together or separately. Separate meetings — known in practice as private caucuses — are the feature that most clearly distinguishes mediation from adjudication. What you say in a caucus cannot be passed to the other side without your permission.

**6. Conclusion.** The process ends with a settlement, a failure to settle, the withdrawal of one party, the mediator's determination that further effort is unnecessary, or a finding that the dispute is not capable of mediation. In every case **final minutes** are drawn up.

## How Long Does It Take?

In mandatory mediation the mediator must conclude the process within **three weeks** of appointment; that period may be extended by a maximum of one week in cases of necessity. In commercial disputes the period is longer: under Article 5/A of the Turkish Commercial Code it is **six weeks**, extendable by a maximum of two weeks in cases of necessity.

In voluntary mediation there is no statutory maximum; the parties set the pace.

## What Happens If You Settle?

Where a settlement is reached a **settlement document** is drawn up, and this has two serious consequences.

First, no further action may be brought on the matters agreed. It is therefore critical that the document sets out one by one which claims are covered; a general expression such as "all claims" may also cover an item you never had in mind.

Second, in most cases the document can be enforced directly like a court judgment. A settlement document signed jointly by the parties, their lawyers and the mediator counts as an instrument in the nature of a court judgment without any enforceability annotation. In commercial disputes the signatures of the lawyers and the mediator suffice. If one party has no lawyer, an enforceability annotation must be obtained from the civil court of peace. In disputes over immovables and in lease, dissolution of joint ownership, condominium and neighbour-law disputes the annotation is **mandatory**.

## What Happens If You Do Not Settle?

Final minutes recording the failure to settle are drawn up and the road to litigation opens. You must attach **the original of those minutes or a copy certified by the mediator** to the statement of claim. A photocopy is not enough; nor is it sufficient that the document is visible in the national judicial IT system. This defect leads to dismissal of the action on procedural grounds.

Moreover, for the time elapsing from the application to the mediation office until the date the final minutes are drawn up, **the limitation period is suspended and preclusive periods do not run**. For claims close to becoming time-barred, that protection makes the application itself a protective step.

## Can What I Say in the Session Be Used Against Me in Court?

No. The Law lays down a clear exclusion of evidence here. The invitation to mediation, views and proposals put forward for termination, suggestions made in the negotiation, the admission of a fact or claim, and documents prepared solely for the purposes of the mediation cannot be relied on as evidence in a subsequent civil action or arbitration; nor may testimony be given about them.

So saying in mediation "I am willing to pay 300,000 lira" is not treated in court as an admission of the debt.

There is, however, a limit: evidence that could in any event be produced in a civil action does not cease to be evidence merely because it was submitted in mediation. A contract, invoice or payslip with an independent existence can be used in court even if it was shown at the table.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q: Must I attend with a lawyer?

No. You may attend the negotiations in person, through your legal representative or through your lawyer. But because the settlement document signed closes the road to litigation and can be enforced directly, attending without counsel carries serious risk. In addition, a document signed jointly by the parties, their lawyers and the mediator acquires the character of a judgment without any annotation.

### Q: If the other party does not attend, is the process still complete?

Yes. Where the parties cannot be reached, or no session can be held because they did not attend, the mediator likewise ends the activity and draws up the final minutes. Those minutes satisfy the condition precedent; the non-attending party is also named in them and faces the costs sanction.

### Q: Where does the mediation session take place?

Unless the parties agree otherwise, the sessions are conducted within the territorial area of the judicial commission to which the appointing office belongs. In practice they are held at the mediator's office or in meeting rooms in the courthouse; the parties may also agree to conduct them online.

### Q: Can I choose the mediator?

In mandatory mediation the mediator is as a rule designated by the office from the list. However, if the parties agree on any mediator appearing on the list, that mediator is appointed. In voluntary mediation the parties choose the mediator directly.

### Q: Can I change my mind after settling in mediation?

No. The settlement document signed is binding and no action may be brought on the matters agreed. The only route is to seek its annulment on a ground of invalidity such as mistake, fraud, duress or unconscionable exploitation; that claim is subject to a one-year preclusive period and the burden of proof lies on the person asserting it.
