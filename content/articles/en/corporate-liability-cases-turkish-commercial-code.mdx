TTK Art. 549-554 set out six distinct categories of liability protecting the integrity of documents and conduct behind a Turkish joint-stock company's most consequential transactions.

## False Documents at Incorporation and Capital Transactions

Art. 549 makes those who prepare false, fraudulent, or misleading documents, prospectuses, or representations in connection with incorporation, capital increases or decreases, mergers, demergers, conversions, or securities issuance — and, if at fault, those who participate — liable for resulting damages. Art. 550 targets the specific fiction of representing capital as fully subscribed or paid when it was not: those responsible must be treated as having personally taken up the shares and are jointly liable for the shortfall plus interest.

## Valuation Fraud and the Ban on Public Solicitation

Art. 551 covers fraud in valuing in-kind capital or businesses being acquired — overvaluation, misrepresenting the nature of assets, or other manipulation — with liability for resulting loss even where a court-appointed expert approved the valuation. Art. 552 prohibits soliciting funds from the public, by any means, for incorporation or capital increase outside the regulated capital markets framework — a rule reinforced by criminal penalties.

## The General Liability Standard — and a Key 2012 Shift

Art. 553 is the workhorse provision: founders, directors, managers, and liquidators who breach their legal or articles-based duties through fault are liable to the company, shareholders, and creditors alike. A critical point for anyone assessing exposure: before a 2012 amendment, a presumption of fault worked against the defendant, who had to prove innocence. That presumption was removed — the burden of proving fault now rests with the claimant. Delegated authority generally shields the delegating organ from liability for the delegate's acts, absent negligent selection, and no one is liable for violations genuinely outside their control. This provision extends by reference to limited company managers and to anyone exercising de facto management authority, regardless of formal title.

## Auditor Liability

Art. 554 applies a parallel fault-based standard to statutory and special auditors for negligent performance of their duties in reviewing financial statements and reports.

## Practical Takeaway

Investors negotiating share purchases from a Turkish company's founders or directors should factor in that liability claims now require the claimant to prove fault — a materially different starting position than under the pre-2012 regime.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### In which cases are directors liable to the company?

Under Article 553/1 TTK founders, directors, managers and liquidators are liable for the loss they cause to the company, to the shareholders and to the company's creditors where they breach, <b>through their fault</b>, the duties arising from the law and the articles of association. Article 553/3 expressly excludes no-fault liability: no one may be held liable for a breach of law or articles, or for an irregularity, that lay beyond their control; and that exclusion cannot be set aside by invoking duties of supervision and care.

### What is the limitation period in a liability action?

Under Article 560 TTK the right to claim damages from those liable is barred <b>two years after the claimant learns of the loss and of the person liable</b> and in any event <b>five years after the day the act causing the loss occurred</b>. However, where the act also constitutes an offence subject to a longer prosecution limitation period under the Turkish Penal Code, that longer period applies to the damages action as well.

### Can a company creditor sue the board of directors?

Yes, but within limits. Article 553 TTK also confers a right of action on creditors; however, under Article 555/1 compensation for loss suffered by the company may be claimed by the company and the shareholders, and a shareholder may ask only that it be paid to the company. Creditors, under Article 556/1, may as a rule claim payment of the loss to the company where <b>bankruptcy proceedings are opened against the company</b>; if the bankruptcy administration does not sue, any creditor may bring that action.
