TTK Art. 1014-1019 lay out the core rules of the Turkish ship mortgage — the mechanism through which nearly all ship finance in Turkey is secured.

## Exclusivity and Basic Nature

Art. 1014 states, in effect, that the ship mortgage is the *only* contractual security available over a registered Turkish ship — no other pledge technique under the Civil Code may be used instead. This exclusivity keeps the ship-finance market transparent: any lender or buyer checking the registry knows exactly where to look for encumbrances. The mortgage can secure existing, future, conditional, or instrument-linked claims, and — as an accessory right — its scope and survival are always tied to the underlying claim.

## Formation

Under Art. 1015, a ship mortgage requires (i) a notarized written agreement between owner and creditor (or an agreement executed directly before the ship registry office) and (ii) registration. Once the agreement is properly made or the owner has notified consent to registration, neither party may unilaterally back out before registration is completed — an important protection for lenders during the administrative lag between signing and registry entry.

## Registry Content, Ranking, and Interest

Art. 1016 requires the registry entry to specify the creditor, the claim amount (in Turkish lira, with foreign-currency or gold-indexed options available), interest rate, and — critically — the mortgage's rank. Art. 1017 borrows the Civil Code's real-property mortgage ranking rules wholesale: priority runs by registration date, letting multiple lenders (a construction-finance bank, a working-capital bank, a shipyard's statutory lien) coexist in a predictable order. Art. 1018-1019 define what the mortgage secures — including, notably, that a below-market or interest-free claim can be unilaterally topped up to the statutory minimum interest rate without needing consent from equal- or lower-ranking creditors, since that adjustment is standard and foreseeable.

## Practical Takeaway

The exclusivity rule has real teeth: Turkish courts have held that a pledge over a vessel registered only in an administrative "mooring registry" (Bağlama Kütüğü), rather than the actual ship registry, does not qualify as a ship mortgage and cannot support enforcement proceedings via the mortgage-foreclosure route. Lenders financing vessels that lack full ship-registry status should verify registry type before relying on Art. 1014 protections.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How is a ship mortgage established?

Under TTK Article 1015/1, the establishment of a ship mortgage requires two elements together: <b>agreement between the owner of the vessel and the creditor on the establishment of a mortgage over the vessel</b>, and <b>registration of the mortgage in the ship registry</b>. Registration is constitutive; the agreement alone does not create the mortgage. The form of the agreement is additionally regulated in Article 1015/2.

### What right does a ship mortgage give the creditor?

Under TTK Article 1014/1, a ship mortgage gives the creditor <b>the power to obtain satisfaction of their claim from the value of the vessel</b>. That is, the creditor does not take possession of the vessel or operate it; when the debt is not paid, they may demand that the vessel be converted into money and collect from the sale proceeds according to their ranking, with priority. Under Article 1018, the mortgage provides security for the claims listed in TMK Articles 875/1 and 876 (principal, enforcement costs, default interest and necessary expenses).

### Can more than one mortgage be established over a single vessel?

Yes. Under TTK Article 1017/1, the ranking of mortgages over a vessel is determined <b>in accordance with the provisions of the Turkish Civil Code on immovable pledges</b>; the fixed-ranking system applies. In addition, under Article 1021, if more than one vessel or share in a vessel has been mortgaged for a single claim (joint ship mortgage), <b>each of them is liable for the entire debt</b>.
