International protection applications are rarely made by a single person. People arrive with their families and naturally expect everyone to share the same outcome. Law No. 6458 on Foreigners and International Protection largely meets that expectation — but it defines "family" narrowly and in certain cases separates family members from one another. Knowing who falls within the scope, and whom a decision will bind, determines how the application should be framed.

## Whom Does the Law Mean by "Family Member"?

Subparagraph (a) of the first paragraph of Article 3 of Law No. 6458 provides the definition: **family members means the spouse, the minor child, and the dependent adult child of the applicant or of the holder of international protection status.**

The definition consists of three elements, and its boundaries are clear:

- **Spouse.** A formal marriage bond is required.
- **Minor child.** Children who have not completed the age of eighteen are covered automatically.
- **Dependent adult child.** Children who have completed the age of eighteen fall within the scope only if they are *dependent*. Dependency mostly arises in situations such as disability or a need for continuous care.

Who falls outside the definition matters just as much: **mothers, fathers, siblings, and grandparents are not included in this definition.** This is the misconception most frequently encountered in practice. An elderly mother or father, even if actually dependent on the applicant, is not treated as a "family member" under the statutory definition and cannot be included in the application; they must make a separate application in their own name.

## Joint Applications and Consent

The third paragraph of Article 65 of the Law governs how the application is made: **an applicant may apply on behalf of family members who accompany them and whose applications rest on the same ground.** The paragraph then imposes a procedural condition: **in that case, the consent of adult family members to an application being made on their behalf is obtained.**

Two conditions apply together. The first is that the applications **rest on the same ground** — if family members' protection claims rest on different reasons, a joint application may not be appropriate. The second is the **consent** of adult family members; no application may be made on behalf of adults without their will being ascertained.

The consent requirement has considerable practical importance. An application made by one spouse on behalf of the other without consent may later become the subject of a challenge. Particularly in files where domestic violence or the possibility of separation exists, it is legally safer for adult family members to apply separately.

## Registration and the Identity Document

Once registration is completed, family members are documented as well. Under the first paragraph of Article 76 of the Law, an International Protection Applicant Identity Document stating that an international protection claim has been made and **containing the foreigner identification number** is issued to an applicant whose registration has been completed **and to any family members who arrived with them**.

Each family member thereby obtains their own document and their own [foreigner identification number](/en/articles/foreigner-identification-number-turkey). This is decisive for children's school enrolment and access to healthcare.

Where status is granted, Article 83 comes into play: an identity document containing the foreigner identification number is issued to those granted refugee, conditional refugee, and subsidiary protection status; these documents **are subject to no fee whatsoever and take the place of a residence permit.**

## The Unity Principle: The Decision Covers the Whole Family

The second paragraph of Article 78 of the Law lays the cornerstone of the system: save in the cases reserved, **an application made on behalf of a family is assessed as a whole and the decision given covers all family members.**

This principle prevents the family being divided. A single assessment is made in respect of the whole family and the outcome is the same for everyone. Just as a positive decision covers the whole family, so a negative decision binds them all.

The criteria for the assessment are set out in the third paragraph of the same article: in deciding on an application, **the prevailing general conditions in the country of origin or previous residence and the personal circumstances of the applicant** are taken into account.

## The Limit of the Unity Principle: Exclusion

The most important exception to the unity principle arises in cases of exclusion. The sixth paragraph of Article 64 of the Law provides: **the exclusion of an applicant from international protection does not require the exclusion of the applicant's family members, provided that none of the grounds for exclusion arises in respect of them.**

The provision is the migration law counterpart of the principle that criminal responsibility is personal. Even where a person is excluded from protection for reasons such as a war crime or a serious non-political crime, their spouse and children are not automatically left without protection on that account. Unless a separate ground for exclusion exists in respect of the family members, their protection claim is assessed on its own merits.

This is a critical point for defence strategy: where an exclusion assessment is being made in respect of one member of the family, it should be requested that the position of the other members be dealt with independently.

## The Interview and Family Members

The first paragraph of Article 75 of the Law provides that the interview is as a rule conducted individually, affording the person the opportunity to express themselves as fully as possible. However, **where the presence of family members is considered necessary, the interview may be conducted together with family members, with the person's consent.**

Here too consent is essential. Under the same paragraph, **the applicant's lawyer may attend the interview as an observer** at the applicant's request. In files where family dynamics may affect the account given — particularly where violence or pressure is a possibility — it is appropriate to request an individual interview.

## Access to Services

The first paragraph of Article 89 of the Law provides that the applicant or holder of international protection status **and their family members shall benefit from primary and secondary education services**. This provision is a direct basis in relation to [school enrolment for foreign children](/en/articles/foreign-children-school-enrollment-turkey).

Under the second paragraph of the same article, access to social assistance and services may be provided for those in need. In addition, the fifth paragraph of Article 95 provides that applicants or status holders and their family members residing outside reception and accommodation centres may be allowed to benefit from the services at those centres; and the seventh paragraph provides that **the unity of families staying in the centres is preserved as far as possible.**

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can my mother and father be included in my application?

No. Article 3 of Law No. 6458 defines family members as the spouse, the minor child, and the dependent adult child; mothers and fathers are not included in that definition. They must make separate applications in their own names.

### Does my nineteen-year-old child fall within the scope?

Only if they are **dependent**. The law requires dependency in the case of adult children; adult and independent children must apply in their own names.

### Can I apply on behalf of my spouse?

Yes, if the applications rest on the same ground and your spouse consents. Article 65 of the Law requires the consent of adult family members to an application being made on their behalf.

### If a negative decision is given in respect of my spouse, will I be refused too?

As a rule yes — under Article 78 an application made on behalf of a family is assessed as a whole and the decision covers all family members. The position differs in cases of exclusion: under the sixth paragraph of Article 64, the exclusion of one person does not require the exclusion of the others unless the same ground arises in respect of them.
