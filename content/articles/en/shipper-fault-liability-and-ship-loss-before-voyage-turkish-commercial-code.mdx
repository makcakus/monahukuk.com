TTK Art. 1208 flips the usual carrier-liability framework around to cover damage cargo causes to the ship, while Art. 1209 opens a new chapter on what happens when a voyage never gets underway.

## Shipper and Loader Are Liable Only for Their Own Fault

Where cargo — through a dangerous or defective character, for example — damages the vessel itself, Art. 1208 holds the shipper and loader liable only for damage caused by their own fault or that of persons acting on their behalf; "persons acting on their behalf" carries the same broad, functional meaning applied to carriers elsewhere in the Code. Crucially, there is no presumption against the shipper here: unlike the carrier, who bears a reversed burden of proof for cargo damage, the carrier claiming against the shipper must prove fault under ordinary rules. Other statutory provisions imposing stricter liability (for example, on undisclosed dangerous cargo) remain unaffected.

## Ship Lost Before the Voyage Starts: Automatic, No-Fault Termination

Art. 1209 addresses the simplest scenario in this part of the Code: if the ship is lost to an unforeseen event before the voyage even begins, the freight contract lapses automatically by operation of law — no notice, no election by either party, and no damages owed by either side. This "lapse" (as opposed to "termination," a concept covered later in the Code) reflects two ideas: there is no realistic way to salvage a contract once its entire physical infrastructure is gone, and an unforeseen event by definition involves no one's fault, so no one should have to compensate the other. The one carve-out: debts that had already accrued before the loss — such as demurrage already running — remain payable.

## Why This Matters for Foreign Parties

For carriers and shippers structuring cargo contracts, Art. 1208 means a carrier seeking compensation for cargo-caused ship damage must actually prove fault — it cannot rely on a presumption the way it can defend against cargo claims. For anyone financing or insuring a voyage that never starts, Art. 1209 confirms that a lost ship simply ends the freight relationship cleanly, without either side chasing damages, aside from amounts already due.
