Following Art. 1262's bodily-harm cap, TTK Art. 1263 supplies the parallel monetary ceilings for property — cabin baggage, vehicles, and everything else a passenger carries.

## Three Tiered Caps

Art. 1263 sets three separate ceilings, calibrated to each category's typical value and to who controls it. Cabin baggage — items kept under the passenger's own supervision — is capped at 2,250 SDR per passenger per voyage (para. 1). Vehicles, together with everything carried in or on them, are capped at 12,700 SDR *per vehicle* per voyage — the highest figure, reflecting a vehicle's intrinsically higher value (para. 2). Other baggage — checked baggage under the carrier's control, subject to a stronger fault presumption against the carrier under Art. 1256(4) — is capped at 3,375 SDR per passenger per voyage (para. 3). This same 3,375 SDR figure is the limit Art. 1260 refers back to for valuables deposited with the carrier for safekeeping. Notably, the standard of liability (how easily the carrier is found at fault) and the size of the cap (how much is owed once liable) are calibrated independently — the strictest fault standard (other baggage) does not carry the highest cap.

## The Deductible: A Party-Agreed Reduction

Unlike the caps in paragraphs 1–3, which are one-way ratchets that parties may only raise (per Art. 1264), Art. 1263(4) lets carrier and passenger agree on a deductible working in the opposite direction: up to 330 SDR for vehicle damage and up to 149 SDR per passenger for other-baggage damage may be subtracted from the total claim before the remaining loss is compensated (still subject to the relevant cap). This spares carriers the administrative cost of processing trivial claims while leaving passengers' recovery for genuinely significant losses untouched — but it only applies if actually negotiated and agreed, typically through ticket or carriage-contract terms.

## Case Law

A 2018 Court of Cassation decision (11th Civil Chamber, 2016/13791-2018/4553) applied the 3,375 SDR "other baggage" cap directly: a passenger's stolen clothing, stored in a ferry's designated luggage room, was valued at 8,233.45 TL — below the SDR-cap equivalent at the time — so the court awarded the claim in full, without applying the limitation. No confirmed decision applying the cabin-baggage, vehicle, or deductible provisions directly has been identified, consistent with these disputes typically being resolved through insurance rather than litigation.

## Practical Takeaway

Carriers should know precisely which cap applies to a given loss category, and whether a deductible has actually been agreed — this can be the difference between full and capped recovery. Passengers carrying vehicles or valuable cabin items should understand these caps apply per voyage and per passenger/vehicle, not per claim.
