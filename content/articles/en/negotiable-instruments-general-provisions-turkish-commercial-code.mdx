TTK Art. 645-653 open Book Three of the Turkish Commercial Code — Negotiable Instruments (kıymetli evrak) — laying the common foundation for every instrument type that follows: registered, bearer, and negotiable-order instruments including bills of exchange, promissory notes, and checks.

## The Defining Feature: A Right Bound to the Document

Art. 645 states the core principle of negotiable instrument law: the right embodied in the instrument cannot be asserted separately from it, nor transferred separately from it. This binding of the right to the physical document — distinguishing negotiable instruments from ordinary evidentiary documents — is what makes fast, secure circulation of commercial rights possible.

## Payment Only Against Surrender

Under Art. 646, a debtor is only obligated to pay against surrender of the instrument, protecting against double payment if the instrument later resurfaces in a good-faith holder's hands. A debtor who pays, absent fraud or gross negligence, whoever formal indicators identify as the creditor is discharged — the debtor need not investigate the underlying validity of that person's title, only its outward, formal legitimacy.

## Transfer Requirements and Converting Instrument Type

Transfer always requires physical delivery of possession (Art. 647); order instruments additionally require endorsement, and registered instruments require a written assignment declaration — endorsement itself follows the bill-of-exchange rules as the general model for all instrument types (Art. 648-649). Converting an instrument from one type to another (e.g., registered to bearer) generally requires the consent of everyone with rights or obligations under it, noted directly on the instrument; converting a bearer instrument without full consent only binds the parties directly involved (Art. 650).

## Cancellation of Lost Instruments

If an instrument is lost, Art. 651 allows the person who held rights in it at the time of loss to apply to a court for cancellation, after which the holder may assert the right without the instrument or request a replacement (Art. 652). Turkish courts have held that blank, unfilled check forms lost before ever embodying a right do not qualify as negotiable instruments for cancellation purposes, and that in these uncontested (ex parte) proceedings, the applicant need only present convincing — not conclusive — evidence of possession and loss.

## Practical Takeaway

Anyone acquiring or accepting a negotiable instrument in a Turkish transaction should confirm physical possession and proper endorsement or assignment documentation are in order, since a right that is not properly bound to the instrument's transfer chain generally cannot be enforced independently of it.
