TTK Art. 862-865 shift the focus from documenting a Turkish carriage contract to actually preparing the goods for transport, and they place several important — sometimes strict — obligations on the sender rather than the carrier.

## Packaging, Marking, Loading, and Demurrage

Under Art. 862, whenever the goods' nature requires it, the sender must package them to prevent loss or damage and to avoid harming the carrier, and must mark them wherever special handling is required. Art. 863 assigns loading and unloading to the sender by default (absent contrary agreement, circumstances, or trade custom), while the carrier remains separately responsible for ensuring loading does not compromise operational safety. A reasonable loading/unloading window is included in the freight; going beyond it triggers a demurrage fee for the carrier.

## The Sender's No-Fault Liability

Art. 864 is the standout provision: the sender is liable — without any need to prove fault — for carrier losses and costs arising from inadequate packaging or marking, inaccurate information on the consignment note, failure to disclose dangerous goods, or missing accompanying documents. This liability is capped at 8.33 SDR per kilogram of gross weight and reduced proportionally where the carrier's own conduct contributed to the loss. Critically, where the sender is a consumer (acting outside any trade or profession), this no-fault standard drops away entirely — a consumer-sender is only liable where personally at fault.

## Terminating the Contract

Art. 865 lets the sender terminate a carriage contract at any time, for any reason. In exchange, the carrier can claim either its actual loss (freight plus demurrage and costs, minus savings and any income earned from the freed-up capacity) or a flat one-third of the agreed freight without needing to prove anything — except where the termination stems from the carrier's own risk sphere, in which case these claims shrink or disappear.

## Practical Takeaway

A foreign shipper sending cargo into or within Turkey should treat packaging and documentation accuracy as a genuine liability exposure, not a formality — unless the shipper qualifies as a consumer, a packaging failure creates automatic liability regardless of how carefully the shipment was otherwise handled.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Who is responsible for packing the goods?

Under TTK Article 862/1, where the nature of the goods requires packing having regard to the agreed carriage, <b>the consignor</b> must pack them so as to protect them from loss and damage <b>and so as not to cause damage to the carrier</b>. The packing obligation is two-directional: it aims to protect both the goods and the carrier's vehicle and other cargo. The consignor must also mark the goods as required.

### In which cases does the consignor incur strict liability?

Under TTK Article 864/1 the consignor is liable, <b>even without fault</b>, for: (a) inadequate packing and marking; (b) inaccuracies, errors and omissions in the particulars entered in the consignment note; (c) failure to declare the dangerous nature of dangerous goods; (d) the absence, inaccuracy or insufficiency of the official documents referred to in Article 860/1. This is a risk allocation based on the fact that the carrier has no means of checking the information and documents.

### May the consignor terminate the contract of carriage?

Yes. Under TTK Article 865/1 <b>the consignor may terminate the contract of carriage at any time</b>; no just cause is required. Termination is not, however, free of charge: under Article 865/2 the carrier may choose between (a) the agreed carriage charge plus waiting charges and reimbursable expenses, less the expenses saved or the benefits obtained as a result of the termination, or (b) <b>one third of the agreed carriage charge</b>. Where the termination stems from a cause falling within the carrier's sphere of risk, option (b) cannot be relied on.
