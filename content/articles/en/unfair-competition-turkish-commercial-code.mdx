Articles 54 through 63 of the Turkish Commercial Code (TTK) provide the general safeguard for fair competition in Turkish commercial life, complementing the narrower protections for trade names and trademarks. The purpose clause in Art. 54 is deliberately broad: these rules protect not just competing merchants but "the interests of all participants" — suppliers, customers, and the market as a whole.

## The General Clause and Illustrative Categories

Art. 54(2) sets out a general clause: deceptive conduct, or conduct otherwise contrary to good faith, affecting relations between competitors or between suppliers and customers, is unfair and unlawful. Art. 55 then lists illustrative — not exhaustive — categories: misleading advertising and sales practices, inducing breach or termination of contracts, unauthorized exploitation of others' work product, unlawful disclosure of trade secrets, non-compliance with standard trade terms, and unfair standard contract terms. Turkish courts assess whether a statement is "false" against objective truth, and whether it is "misleading" based on the overall impression left on an average recipient; comparative advertising is not unfair per se, but an advertiser who cannot substantiate its comparative claims will be found to have engaged in unfair competition.

## Remedies, Limitation, and Criminal Liability

A party harmed — or threatened with harm — by unfair competition may seek a declaratory judgment, an injunction, correction of the resulting situation, and, where fault is shown, material and moral damages, including damages measured by the infringer's gain (Art. 56). Trade associations, chambers, and consumer organizations may also bring declaratory and injunctive claims on behalf of collective interests. Claims under Art. 56 are subject to a one-year limitation period from discovery and a three-year absolute limitation period from the act itself (Art. 60) — though Turkish courts have clarified that for a continuing unfair-competition practice, the limitation period does not begin to run until the conduct ends. Intentional unfair competition can also trigger criminal liability of up to two years' imprisonment or a judicial fine, prosecuted only upon complaint by a party entitled to bring the civil claim (Art. 62).

Foreign companies expanding into the Turkish market should note that merely hiring a competitor's former employees — even experienced ones with an existing client base — is not, by itself, unfair competition; liability requires concrete evidence of systematic inducement to breach an existing employment contract.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the limitation period for an unfair competition lawsuit?

Under TTK Article 60/1, the actions listed in Article 56 become time-barred upon the lapse of <b>one year from the date the party entitled to sue became aware of the accrual of these rights</b>, and <b>in any event three years from their accrual</b>. If the act of unfair competition also constitutes a criminal offence and is subject to a longer prosecutorial limitation period under the Turkish Penal Code, that longer period also applies to the civil action.

### What lawsuits can be brought against unfair competition?

Under TTK Article 56/1, a person whose customers, credit, professional reputation, commercial activities or other economic interests are harmed by an act of unfair competition, <b>or who may face such a risk</b>, may request <b>a declaratory judgment</b> as to whether the act is unfair, the <b>prohibition</b> of the unfair competition, the <b>removal</b> of the material situation resulting from the unfair competition, <b>compensation for damages</b> if there is fault, and <b>non-pecuniary damages</b> where the conditions are met.

### Is there a criminal sanction for unfair competition?

Yes, subject to a complaint. Under TTK Article 62/1, persons who <b>intentionally</b> commit one of the acts of unfair competition listed in Article 55; who intentionally provide false or misleading information in order to have their own offers or proposals preferred; who deceive employees into obtaining production or trade secrets; and those who, having learned of an act of unfair competition by their employees, fail to prevent it, are, provided the act does not constitute another offence carrying a heavier penalty, punished with <b>imprisonment of up to two years or a judicial fine, upon the complaint of one of the persons entitled to bring a civil action under Article 56</b>.
