TTK Art. 677-680 establish how liability attaches to each signature on a bill of exchange under Turkish law — rules that extend by reference to promissory notes and checks and come up constantly in practice through those instruments.

## Independence of Signatures

Art. 677 states the foundational principle: if a bill carries a signature by someone lacking capacity, a forged signature, a signature of a fictitious person, or any signature that for any reason does not bind its purported signer, the validity of the other signatures is unaffected. This is what allows a holder to rely on each valid signature without having to verify every other signature on the instrument — without it, a single defective signature could undermine everyone else's liability and cripple the bill's usefulness as a credit instrument.

## Unauthorized Signatures

Under Art. 678, someone who signs as another's representative without authority — or who exceeds the authority granted — is personally liable on the bill; if they pay it, they acquire whatever rights the person they purported to represent would have had. Turkish courts require an agent's power of attorney to expressly authorize "entering into bill-of-exchange obligations" specifically — a general authorization to "sign all manner of documents" is not enough, and a signature made without this specific authority binds the signer personally, not the principal. The same principle applies where a company representative acts in breach of their loyalty duty, such as guaranteeing a bill for the benefit of a different company they also control.

## The Drawer's Asymmetric Liability

Art. 679 makes the drawer liable both for non-acceptance and non-payment of the bill, but treats the two asymmetrically: the drawer may validly disclaim liability for non-acceptance, but any clause disclaiming liability for non-payment is deemed unwritten and has no legal effect. The reasoning: acceptance depends on the drawee's independent will, outside the drawer's control, while payment is the very point of the instrument — a drawer immune from non-payment risk would undermine the bill's character as a genuine debt obligation.

## Blank (Incomplete) Bills

Art. 680 permits a bill to be issued deliberately incomplete, with terms filled in later per a separate agreement between the parties; if it is later completed in violation of that agreement, the drawer cannot raise this breach against a good-faith holder — unless the holder acquired the bill in bad faith or with gross negligence. The burden of proving improper completion rests on the drawer.

## Practical Takeaway

A business signing on behalf of a company on a Turkish bill of exchange, promissory note, or check should confirm the power of attorney explicitly authorizes bill-of-exchange (kambiyo) obligations — a general signing authority is routinely held insufficient by Turkish courts, exposing the individual signer to personal liability.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### If one signature on a bill is forged, is the whole instrument void?

No. Under Article 677/1 TTK, <b>where a bill of exchange bears the signatures of persons incapable of binding themselves by a bill, forged signatures, signatures of fictitious persons, or signatures which for any reason do not bind the persons who signed or in whose name it was signed, the validity of the other signatures is not affected</b>. This is the principle of <b>the independence of signatures</b>, which protects the security of circulation of negotiable instruments.

### Is an agent without authority liable on the bill?

Yes, personally. Under Article 678/1 TTK <b>a person who signs a bill as the representative of another without authority to represent them is personally liable on that bill</b>; if they pay the amount, they acquire the rights of the person they purported to represent. Under Article 678/2 the same rule applies to <b>an agent who exceeds their authority</b>; the principal remains liable within the scope of the authority given.

### What if a blank bill is completed contrary to the agreement?

Under Article 680/1 TTK, <b>where a bill that was incomplete when issued is completed contrary to the agreements made, non-compliance with those agreements may not be set up against the holder</b>, unless the holder <b>acquired the bill in bad faith or was grossly negligent in acquiring it</b>. In practice, disputes about the completion of blank instruments given as security are resolved under this provision.
