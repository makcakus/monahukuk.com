In most files a mediation session ends in a single sitting and within a few hours. That brevity is deceptive: the document signed in those few hours closes the road to litigation on the matters agreed and can be enforced directly. What determines the outcome is not performance at the table but how you arrive at it.

This article sets out the preparation, step by step.

## 1. Verify the Scope of the Dispute and the Route

The first question is not substantive but procedural: does this dispute fall within mandatory mediation and, if so, under which statute?

This has three practical consequences:

**Time limits.** The general rule is three weeks plus one; in commercial disputes six weeks plus two; in agricultural production contracts two weeks plus one.

**The competent office.** The application is made to the mediation office at the place of the court having jurisdiction over the subject matter. An application to the wrong office restarts the process if the other party objects at the first meeting.

**Timing.** In lease disputes the Turkish Court of Cassation has held that the application to mediation must be made **after the right to bring an action has arisen**; an application made too early does not satisfy the condition precedent.

## 2. Compile the Documents

In mediation, document preparation is usually what determines the outcome. A minimum list by type of dispute:

**Employee claims:** the employment contract and its annexes, the social security service record and the entry/exit notifications, payslips and bank account movements, timekeeping and workplace entry/exit records, the leave register, the termination notice and proof of its service.

**Commercial receivables:** the contract and its annexes, orders and delivery notes, invoices and correspondence objecting to invoices, the current account statement and reconciliation letters, commercial book entries, payment records, cheques and promissory notes, formal notices.

**Lease disputes:** the lease contract, payment records, formal notices, the undertaking to vacate, the title deed record, photographs and reports on the condition of the property.

**Dissolution of joint ownership:** the current title deed record, the certificate of inheritance, documents on the value of the immovable, records showing actual use.

**Condominium:** the management plan, the minute book of the owners' assembly, dues assessment schedules, proof of notification.

## 3. Calculate the Claim — Do Not Sit Down with a Guess

Going into a session without knowing the answer to "how much am I owed?" means not knowing what proportion of your actual entitlement the offer represents.

The calculation has three components:

**Principal.** Each item must be calculated separately. In employee claims, correctly establishing the **gross wage including benefits** is critical: severance pay is calculated not on the bare wage but on the wage to which continuing payments such as travel, meals and bonuses are added.

**Interest.** The type of interest (statutory, commercial, deposit rate) and the starting date vary from item to item. Calculating accrued interest separately shows the real value of the settlement figure.

**The cost and duration of litigation.** The court fees, expert and witness costs payable if an action is brought, together with the estimated duration of the proceedings, are part of assessing a settlement. An amount received today and the same amount received in three years are not worth the same.

Alongside this you should also set out **the other side's calculation**: who bears the burden of proof? In employee claims a significant part of the burden — as to overtime, annual leave and the reason for termination — lies with the employer, and that is negotiating power for the employee's side.

## 4. Fix Your Negotiation Limit in Advance

Three questions to be answered before the session, not during it:

**Target.** What is the realistic best outcome?

**Limit.** What is the amount below which you will not go, or the condition you will not accept? That limit is set by comparing the expected outcome if an action is brought against the cost and duration of litigation.

**Alternative.** What happens if no settlement is reached? If an action is to be brought, what is its likely duration, cost and evidential position? The stronger that alternative, the stronger your negotiating position.

Fixing the limit in advance prevents decisions taken under pressure at the table. In practice, the most frequent regrets come from offers accepted because "at that moment it seemed reasonable".

## 5. Settle the Question of Authority and Representation

**For legal persons:** the authority of the person attending must be documented by a signature circular or a power of attorney. If a settlement is to be signed, the authority must include the powers to **settle and release**; a general power of attorney may not cover these.

**For parties abroad:** a power of attorney executed before a Turkish consulate is directly valid; one executed before a foreign notary requires an apostille and a sworn Turkish translation.

**For housing estate and apartment managements:** the manager's authority to apply and to settle must rest on a decision of the owners' assembly and on the management plan.

**Where the other party is a public administration:** the administration is represented by a **commission** composed of two members designated by the senior manager together with the head of the legal unit or a lawyer or legal adviser designated by them. Settlements bound by the signature of a single official without a commission decision are later exposed to arguments about authority.

## 6. Plan the Language and the Form of Attendance

Sessions are conducted in Turkish and there is no right to a State-funded interpreter. A party who does not speak Turkish bears the cost of an interpreter or of bilingual counsel.

If you cannot attend, you have three options: attendance through a lawyer, a request for online attendance (the parties may freely agree the procedure of the mediation), or notification of an excuse. Failing to attend without doing any of these means that, even if you win the action subsequently brought, you will be liable for half of the other party's litigation costs and half of the attorney's fee determined under the Minimum Attorney Fee Tariff.

## 7. Decide What to Say and What Not to Say

The Law imposes a strict exclusion of evidence for what is said at the mediation table: the invitation to mediation, views and proposals put forward for termination, suggestions made in the negotiation, the admission of a fact or claim, and documents prepared solely for the purposes of the mediation cannot be relied on as evidence in the subsequent action or arbitration; nor may testimony be given about them.

But there is a limit: evidence that could in any event be produced in a civil action does not cease to be evidence **merely because it was submitted in the mediation**. A contract, invoice, payslip or book entry with an independent existence can be used in the action even if it was shown at the table.

The practical upshot: your offers are protected, your documents are not. Before showing a document at the table, assess whether the other side could use it in court.

## 8. Build a Private Caucus Strategy

The mediator may also meet the parties separately. What you say in a private caucus cannot be passed on to the other side without your permission — a natural consequence of the mediator's duty of confidentiality.

In practice the mediator asks at the end of each caucus: "which part of this may I share with the other party?" You should have thought about your answer in advance: sharing your real bargaining limit may speed up the negotiation, but it may also weaken your position.

## 9. Pre-Signature Checklist

Before the settlement document is signed, the following should be verified:

- **Are the items written out one by one?** General expressions such as "all claims" also close off items not thought of at the time, and no further action may be brought on the matters agreed.
- **Are excluded matters expressly stated?**
- **Is the obligation capable of enforcement?** The Court of Cassation has held that a debtor cannot be compelled to conclude a contract on the basis of a settlement document that merely records a promise and contains no constitutive ruling. Write "such an amount will be paid on such a date" or "the property will be vacated on such a date", not "the parties will conclude the following contract".
- **Are the payment terms clear?** Amount, currency, due date or instalment dates, default interest, the account for payment.
- **If an immovable is involved,** are the block, parcel, share ratio and transfer price stated?
- **Who will bear the mediation fee?** Where a settlement is reached, the proportional tariff applies and the amount may be higher than expected.
- **Are the signatures complete?** A document signed jointly by the parties, their lawyers and the mediator counts as an instrument in the nature of a court judgment without any annotation. In commercial disputes the signatures of the lawyers and the mediator suffice.
- **Is an annotation mandatory?** An enforceability annotation is mandatory in disputes over immovables, in lease, dissolution of joint ownership, condominium and neighbour-law disputes, and in international settlement agreements under the Singapore Convention.

## 10. If You Do Not Settle: Obtain the Final Minutes Correctly

If no settlement is reached, the last step of preparation is the final minutes. Check:

- **Is the date of drawing up correct?** Time limits for bringing an action run from that date.
- **Is the non-attending party recorded?** That entry is essential for the non-attendance sanction to be applied.
- **Did you obtain the original or a copy certified by the mediator?** One of these must be attached to the statement of claim; **a photocopy is not enough**, and the document being visible in the national judicial IT system is not sufficient either.

The mediator is obliged to keep the documents for five years; a certified copy can also be requested later.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q: How many people may I attend with?

The parties may attend the negotiations in person, through their legal representatives or through their lawyers; experts who can contribute to resolving the dispute may also be present. Those experts are likewise bound by confidentiality.

### Q: Which documents should I show the other side?

The exclusion of evidence protects offers, not documents: a document with an independent existence does not cease to be evidence because it was produced in mediation. Rather than showing every document, assess in advance which document will move the negotiation forward.

### Q: Do I have to decide on the spot?

No. The mediator must conclude the process within the statutory period, but that does not require you to decide in a single session. You may ask for a further session to consider.

### Q: Do I lose anything if I refuse the offer?

No. The sanction attaches only to **not attending at all** the first meeting. A party who attends but does not settle faces no adverse consequence; the failure to settle is recorded in the final minutes and the road to litigation opens.

### Q: I went unprepared and signed. Is there a way back?

Only by asserting the invalidity of the document. The right to annul for a defect of consent is subject to a one-year preclusive period and a heavy burden of proof; the information record you signed creates a strong presumption against you. By contrast, the situations in which the document is not treated as a valid mediation settlement at all — non-mediability, breach of impartiality, failure to inform — succeed more often in practice.
