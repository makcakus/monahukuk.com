TTK Art. 973-985 explain what a ship registry entry actually means legally — for whom it counts as true, and how far that truth can be relied upon by third parties.

## Openness and Presumptions

Art. 973 makes the registry open to public inspection, with a narrower "legitimate interest" standard for reviewing underlying files and pending applications. Art. 974 then establishes the core presumptions: the registered owner is presumed to be the owner, the registered mortgage or usufruct holder is presumed to hold that right, and a deregistered right is presumed to no longer exist. These are rebuttable, not conclusive — but they shift the burden of proof onto whoever disputes the registry.

## Correcting and Flagging a Faulty Registry

Where the registry diverges from actual legal reality, Art. 975 lets the injured party demand correction — and, notably, this correction right never becomes time-barred, since a registry error that goes uncorrected keeps misleading third parties indefinitely. Pending correction, Art. 976 allows an "objection" notation to be entered, warning anyone consulting the registry that an entry is disputed; Art. 977-981 add a parallel "notice" (şerh) mechanism that locks in priority for a right not yet perfected — critical for banks securing financing while a mortgage is still being negotiated. Both mechanisms can rest on a preliminary injunction obtained without the usual prima facie evidentiary showing, reflecting how limited their practical intrusion is.

## The Reliance Principle

Art. 983 is the capstone: a person who acquires ownership, usufruct, a mortgage, or a mortgage-related right relying on the registry is protected as if the registry were accurate — unless they knew, or should have known, otherwise. This is the direct maritime counterpart to the land registry reliance principle, and Turkish courts have applied it robustly even in multi-step, cross-border ownership chains, protecting a good-faith final buyer against defects in an earlier link of the chain.

## Practical Takeaway

For banks and buyers, Art. 974's ownership presumption doubles as a procedural tool in enforcement proceedings — Turkish courts treat the registered owner as the "interested party" for standing purposes, meaning registry status decides not just substantive rights but who gets a seat at the table in a foreclosure dispute.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is the ship registry open to the public?

Yes. Under TTK Article 973/1, <b>the ship registry is open to the public. Anyone may examine the registry entries and, upon payment of the cost, obtain certified or uncertified copies of them.</b> Proof of interest is not required. This openness is a precondition for the principle of reliance on the registry (Article 983): the entries can be regarded as accurate only if everyone is able to access them.

### Is a person who buys a vessel in reliance on a ship registry entry protected?

Yes. Under TTK Article 983/1, <b>the content of the ship registry is deemed accurate, to the extent it relates to those rights, in favour of a person who, through a legal transaction, acquires ownership of a vessel, a usufruct right, a ship mortgage, or a right over a mortgage</b>. This protection depends on good faith: an acquirer who knows that the entry does not correspond to the true situation, or in respect of whom an <b>objection</b> (Article 976) to this effect has been entered in the registry, cannot benefit from the principle.

### What can be done if a registry entry is incorrect?

There are two instruments. Under TTK Article 975/1, if the content of the registry does not correspond to the true legal situation as regards ownership, a ship mortgage, a usufruct right, or a restriction on disposal, <b>the person whose right has not been registered, or has been registered incorrectly, may request that it be corrected</b>. Until the correction is completed, under Article 976, <b>an objection may be entered stating that the registry entry is inaccurate</b>; this objection prevents third parties from benefiting from the principle of reliance on the registry.
