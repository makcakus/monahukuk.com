After several articles building toward it, TTK Art. 1262 finally supplies the concrete monetary ceiling on a carrier's liability for a passenger's death or bodily injury.

## Three-Layer SDR Architecture

Art. 1262(1) caps the carrier's liability under Art. 1256 at 400,000 Special Drawing Rights (SDR) per passenger, per incident — but this figure only makes sense read alongside the two other layers the series has already covered. Layer one: Art. 1256(1)'s narrow 250,000 SDR floor applies specifically to death/injury caused by a "shipping incident" (wreck, collision, fire, and similar) where the carrier is not proven at fault. Layer two: Art. 1262's 400,000 SDR cap is the broader outer limit covering the *entire* death/injury regime, including non-shipping-incident, fault-based claims under Art. 1256(2). Layer three, and most important: Art. 1262(1) expressly preserves Art. 1256(1)'s third sentence — where the carrier is at fault (or cannot disprove fault) in a shipping incident, liability becomes genuinely unlimited, and *neither* the 250,000 nor the 400,000 SDR cap applies at all.

## The Annuity Rule

If damages are structured as periodic payments (an annuity) rather than a lump sum — common for long-term disability or ongoing care costs — Art. 1262(1)'s second sentence requires that the aggregate capital value of those payments still not exceed the 400,000 SDR cap. This closes an obvious workaround: a court cannot use the choice of payment format to push a carrier's real exposure above the statutory ceiling.

## Case Law: When the Cap Disappears

A 2026 Istanbul Regional Court of Appeal decision (12th Civil Chamber, 2026/420-2026/757, not yet final) illustrates layer three concretely. An infant passenger fell into the water boarding a passenger boat and suffered catastrophic, permanently disabling injury. The first-instance court initially awarded real damages of roughly 48.7 million TL, then issued a correction order reducing the award to the SDR-cap equivalent (approximately 23.3 million TL). The Regional Court reversed the correction, finding — based on expert and Ministry of Transport accident reports documenting a dangerously narrow boarding area, wave-driven changes in freeboard height, and the captain casting off lines prematurely with only one crew member supervising boarding instead of the required two — that the carrier's conduct was reckless within the meaning of Art. 1267 (forfeiture of the right to limit liability), so the 400,000 SDR cap did not apply at all.

## Practical Takeaway

For carriers, the 400,000 SDR cap only holds if fault (in either its ordinary or reckless form) cannot be established — making documented safety procedures and crew training a direct financial safeguard. For injured passengers and their families, whether a carrier's conduct crosses from ordinary fault into recklessness can be the difference between a capped and an uncapped recovery.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the maximum compensation a passenger injured on board can receive?

Under TTK Article 1262/1 the carrier's liability under Article 1256 may in no case exceed <b>400,000 Special Drawing Rights per passenger per incident</b>. This is the absolute ceiling laid down by the Code. If the compensation is fixed in the form of periodic payments, the total capital value of the payments may likewise not exceed that amount.

### What is the difference between 250,000 SDR and 400,000 SDR?

They serve two different functions. The <b>250,000 SDR</b> in Article 1256/1 is the floor up to which the carrier is liable <b>without proof of fault</b> for death and injury arising from a shipping incident; up to that amount the carrier is strictly liable. If the carrier's fault is proved, liability exceeds that floor. The <b>400,000 SDR</b> in Article 1262 is the <b>absolute upper limit</b> that cannot be exceeded even where fault is proved.

### Is there a case in which the carrier cannot rely on the limit?

Yes. Under TTK Article 1267, if it is proved that the loss arose from the carrier's <b>intent</b> or from <b>a reckless act done with knowledge that damage would probably result</b>, the carrier may not rely on the limits in Articles 1262 and 1263 and is liable for the loss in full. The burden of that proof lies on the party seeking removal of the limit.
