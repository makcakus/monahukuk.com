TTK Art. 691-699 govern acceptance (kabul) — the mechanism by which a bill of exchange's drawee becomes personally liable to pay it. Because a promissory note's issuer is already the primary debtor, these rules apply only to bills of exchange, not promissory notes.

## The Basic Right to Present for Acceptance

Under Art. 691, any holder of a bill may present it to the drawee for acceptance at the drawee's residence, any time before maturity — acceptance is, by default, optional and open to anyone in possession of the bill.

## When Acceptance Can Be Required, Barred, or Delayed

The drawer may require presentment for acceptance, with or without a deadline (Art. 692/1), or conversely prohibit it altogether — except for bills payable through a third party, payable somewhere other than the drawee's residence, or payable at a fixed period after sight, where a prohibition is not permitted because acceptance is functionally necessary to fix the payment location or maturity date. Endorsers may independently require presentment unless the drawer has barred it. For bills payable at a fixed period after sight, presentment must occur within one year of issuance unless the drawer extends or shortens this window (endorsers may only shorten it) (Art. 693). A drawee may also request a second presentment the following day, but can only rely on this request if it was recorded via a formal protest (Art. 694).

## Form and Limits of Acceptance

Acceptance is written on the bill itself — either as "accepted" or an equivalent phrase, or simply the drawee's signature on the face of the bill (Art. 695). Acceptance must be unconditional, though the drawee may limit it to part of the bill's amount (partial acceptance); any other deviation from the bill's terms means the bill is deemed unaccepted, though the drawee remains bound within the terms of their own qualified acceptance (Art. 696).

## Effect of Acceptance and Withdrawal

Acceptance transforms the drawee into a primary bill debtor obligated to pay at maturity (Art. 698/1); if payment fails, the holder — even if that holder is the drawer — can claim the full amount directly from the acceptor. A drawee who crosses out their acceptance before returning the bill is deemed to have refused acceptance, with a rebuttable presumption that the cancellation occurred before return; but if the drawee has already notified the holder or another signatory in writing of the acceptance, that written notice remains binding regardless of the crossed-out signature (Art. 699).

## Practical Takeaway

A party relying on a Turkish bill of exchange as a financing tool should confirm the drawee's acceptance is properly and unconditionally recorded on the instrument, since only a validly accepted bill turns the drawee into a directly and fully liable primary debtor — anything less leaves the holder relying only on the drawer and endorsers.
