Mediation in commercial disputes has been compulsory since 1 January 2019 and is now a standard stage of the collection process for companies. Managed correctly, it allows an unpaid invoice to be resolved without entering months of litigation and without paying a proportional court fee. Managed badly, the action brought is dismissed without the merits ever being reached.

## Which Commercial Actions Are in Scope?

Article 5/A TCC requires two criteria to be met together.

**The dispute must be in the nature of a commercial action.** Article 4 TCC identifies three groups: **absolute commercial actions** arising from matters regulated in the TCC regardless of the parties' capacity; **relative commercial actions** arising from matters relating to the commercial enterprises of both parties where both are merchants; and actions deemed commercial by special laws.

**The claim must fall within one of five types of action:** actions for **payment, damages, removal of objection, negative declaration and restitution** whose subject matter is a sum of money.

Outside the scope: commercial actions whose subject matter is not the payment of money — annulment of a general meeting resolution, dissolution and liquidation of a company, determination and prevention of unfair competition, exclusion of a partner, prevention of trade mark infringement.

## Negative Declaration and Removal of Objection: The 2023 Amendment

In the original version of the provision, only "claims for payment and damages" were in scope. In a 2021 decision, the 11th Civil Chamber of the Court of Cassation stated that including negative declaratory actions within Article 5/A merely because they are commercial actions was contrary to law, and that a purposive reading of the provision led to the conclusion that the legislature had **deliberately** left them outside the scope.

In 2023 the legislature made the opposite choice to that case law and added actions for removal of objection, negative declaration and restitution to the text. The transitional regime is special: under Provisional Article 1 of Law No. 7445, this addition applies to **actions brought on or after 1 September 2023**.

The practical result: in a negative declaratory or removal-of-objection action brought after that date, the mediation condition is required.

## Time Limit: Six Weeks

In commercial disputes the mediator concludes the application within **six weeks** of the date of appointment; in compulsory circumstances this may be extended by a maximum of **two weeks**.

That is markedly longer than the general rule of three weeks plus one. The justification is that commercial disputes require examination of current accounts, comparison of ledger entries and institutional approval mechanisms.

In the period from the application until the date the final minutes are drawn up, limitation is suspended and no forfeiture period runs.

## Preparation: Come to the Table with the Current Account Closed

In commercial mediation the outcome is determined less by negotiating skill than by **documentary preparation**. What should be ready before the session:

- the contract and its annexes, and any general terms of business;
- orders, delivery notes, delivery and acceptance documents;
- invoices and correspondence on objections to invoices (as regards the eight-day objection period in Article 21/2 TCC);
- the current account statement and reconciliation letters;
- commercial ledger entries — under Article 222 of the Code of Civil Procedure, the evidentiary value of commercial books may arise even where one party is not a merchant;
- payment records, cheques and promissory notes;
- notices and default notifications;
- exchange-rate and interest calculations for foreign-currency claims.

That the other side is a merchant means it is under the duty to act as a prudent businessperson under Article 18/2 TCC; this creates ground on which defences such as "I did not read the contract" or "I did not know the terms" will not be heard, and strengthens the creditor's hand in the negotiation.

## The Advantage Specific to Commercial Disputes: Judgment-Like Character Without Annotation

There is a special facility for the settlement document in commercial disputes. Under Article 18/4 of Law No. 6325, save where laws make an annotation compulsory, in commercial disputes a settlement document signed jointly by **the lawyers and the mediator** is deemed a document having the character of a judgment without an annotation of enforceability being required.

While in other types of dispute the parties' personal signatures are also required, in commercial disputes the lawyers' signatures suffice. This also settles, from the outset, arguments about signature circulars and authority.

The 3rd Civil Chamber of the Court of Cassation has confirmed this rule, stating that settlement documents within this scope have the character of a judgment without an annotation, that the annotation is **optional**, and that the document may be put directly to enforcement where the obligations recorded in the minutes are not performed.

## The Court-Fee and Stamp-Duty Advantage

Here lies mediation's most concrete financial advantage in commercial receivables: where an application is made to the court for an annotation of enforceability on the settlement document, and where an appeal is brought against the decisions given on it, a **fixed fee** is charged. If the parties wish to use the settlement document in another official transaction without obtaining an annotation, **stamp duty is likewise charged at a fixed rate**.

In a high-value commercial receivable, the absence of proportional court fees and proportional stamp duty is on its own a significant saving.

## Where There Is an Arbitration Clause, Mediation Is Not Required

Article 18/A/18 of Law No. 6325: where special laws impose an obligation to resort to arbitration or another alternative dispute-resolution method, or where there is an **arbitration agreement**, the provisions on mediation as a condition of action do not apply.

This matters for the arbitration clauses frequently found in commercial contracts. But for an arbitration clause to be valid, the parties' intention must be expressed **clearly and definitively, leaving no room for discussion or confusion**; the 3rd Civil Chamber of the Court of Cassation emphasises this criterion.

The same Chamber has also accepted that stipulating **mediation first and arbitration afterwards** ("med-then-arb") in a contract is lawful, and that the two processes continue to exist as independent routes without merging into one another.

## Process-Management Notes for Companies

**Attendance is compulsory and has a cost.** A company that fails without valid excuse to attend the first meeting is liable, even if successful in the action, for half of the other side's litigation costs and half of the attorney's fee determined under the Minimum Attorney's Fee Tariff.

**Authority must be documented.** If a settlement is to be signed, the attending person must hold authority including the powers to settle and to grant a release. Signature circulars and powers of attorney should be ready before the session.

**The only opportunity for a jurisdiction objection is the first meeting.** If a competent court has been agreed in the contract and the application was made to another bureau, the objection must be raised at the latest at the first meeting, with the documents relating to jurisdiction submitted.

**In groups of companies, identify the correct addressee.** The application must be directed at the legal person that is party to the contract; an application directed at the wrong legal person does not satisfy the condition of action as regards the correct addressee.

**Observe the evidentiary bar.** A payment offer made in the negotiation is not treated as an admission of the claim in the action. By contrast, a document with an independent existence produced at the table — a contract, an invoice, a ledger entry — may be used as evidence in the action.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q: Is mediation required for a claim based on a cheque or promissory note?

No application to mediation is required for attachment proceedings specific to negotiable instruments. But if, following an objection to the proceedings, an action for removal of the objection is to be brought, or a claim is to be brought under the general provisions, the mediation condition applies.

### Q: I am going to bring an action for removal of an objection — is the condition required?

For actions brought on or after 1 September 2023, yes. Actions for removal of objection, negative declaration and restitution were added to Article 5/A TCC by the amendment that entered into force on that date.

### Q: What changes in a dispute with a foreign-owned company?

The Law also brings disputes with a foreign element within scope; the process operates in the same way. Sessions are conducted in Turkish and there is no right to a state-provided interpreter. As for enforcing abroad a settlement reached in a cross-border commercial dispute, the Singapore Convention regime and Article 17/A of Law No. 6325 may come into play.

### Q: If we settle in mediation, what happens to interest and costs?

The parties determine the scope of the settlement. In addition to the principal, accrued interest, default interest, enforcement costs and attorney's fees must be shown **separately** in the settlement document; otherwise those items too may be regarded as closed and can no longer be claimed.

### Q: Can I attach assets directly with the settlement document?

A commercial settlement document signed by the lawyers and the mediator is a document having the character of a judgment without an annotation; judgment-based enforcement proceedings may be commenced on it and attachment applied once the proceedings become final. In judgment-based enforcement, the debtor cannot halt the proceedings by a simple objection.
