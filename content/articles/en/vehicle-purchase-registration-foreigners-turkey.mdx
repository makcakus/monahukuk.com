One of the practical matters foreigners settling in Türkiye face in their first year is acquiring a vehicle. Once the licence question is resolved, attention turns to the vehicle itself, and two distinct legal layers come into play: the validity of the sale and the time limit for registration. Both are governed by Article 20 of Highway Traffic Law No. 2918, and breach of either carries its own sanction.

## Sales Are Made Only Before a Notary

Subparagraph (d) of Article 20 of Law No. 2918 fixes the form of a vehicle transfer definitively. All sales and transfers of registered vehicles are **made by notaries**, on the basis of the registration document issued in the owner's name or the traffic registration records. The sentence that follows leaves no room for argument: **all sales and transfers not made by notaries are void.**

This provision is particularly important for foreign buyers. A handwritten sale agreement, a payment receipt, or the fact that the vehicle has actually been handed over does not transfer ownership. A significant share of the losses seen in practice arise from money being paid without the notarial transaction being carried out.

The same subparagraph also lists the preconditions for the transfer: a sale and transfer may be carried out where **it is established that there is no debt in respect of that vehicle for motor vehicles tax, default interest, late payment surcharge, tax penalty, or traffic administrative fine**, and where there is no measure or annotation on the vehicle restricting its sale and/or transfer. For a buyer this means that the vehicle's freedom from debts and encumbrances will already be checked at the notarial stage.

## What Happens After the Transfer?

Subparagraph (d) also governs the process that follows the notarial transaction. The sale and transfer are notified, for entry in the register, to the relevant traffic registration office and the tax office **within three working days**, and **upon that notification the traffic registration in the buyer's name is deemed to have taken place.**

Tax liability changes hands at the same moment: as of the date of the sale and transfer, the previous owner's motor vehicles tax liability ends and the new owner's begins.

Upon completing the transaction, notaries issue a **provisional document evidencing registration, valid for one month**, in the name of the new owner. The registration document proper is issued by the traffic registration office within one month of the notification and delivered by hand or by post. The article also contains a safeguard: **where the registration document cannot be delivered within one month, no liability may be placed on the new owner.**

## First Registration: The Three-Month Period

Sub-subparagraph 1 of subparagraph (a) of Article 20 of the Law lays down a separate period for vehicles to be registered for the first time. Vehicle owners must apply to the relevant traffic registration office, or to the institutions and organisations to be designated by the General Directorate of Security, for the registration of vehicles subject to compulsory registration and to be registered for the first time **within three months from the date of purchase or of clearance through customs**.

This period runs from the date of clearance through customs where foreigners import a vehicle from abroad. For vehicles that have become scrap, a period of **one month** is provided for cancelling the registration.

The sentence added by Law No. 7574 of 12/2/2026 establishes a different arrangement for vehicles to be registered electronically: the three-month period does not apply to vehicles sold by authorised institutions or organisations, or by natural persons or private law legal persons, whose first registration is to be carried out electronically; registration is instead carried out **within fifteen working days following the sale transaction**.

## The Sanction for Failing to Meet the Period

Subparagraph (e) of the article provides for two separate penalties. An administrative fine of **130 Turkish lira** is imposed on those who purchase a vehicle and fail to comply with sub-subparagraph 1 of subparagraph (a), and on buyers who do not obtain a registration document within one month; and of **1,000 Turkish lira** for each transaction on notaries who fail to comply with the provisions of subparagraph (d).

But the real consequence is not the fine: **vehicles taken onto the road without being registered are barred from traffic until registration is carried out.** Having the vehicle impounded creates a burden far exceeding the administrative fine.

The amounts in the article are the nominal figures in the text of the Law; because traffic administrative fines are updated at the beginning of each calendar year by the revaluation rate, the amount actually applicable should be confirmed against the tariff in force at the date of the transaction.

## Death of the Vehicle Owner: The New Ninety-Day Rule

Subparagraph (f), added to the article by Law No. 7574 of 12/2/2026, introduces a new obligation for heirs. Where a vehicle owner dies, the heirs must apply to the relevant registration office, providing the necessary information and documents, **within ninety days from the date of death**, and obtain a registration document in their names. In registrations carried out on this basis, registration is effected **as of the date of death**.

The sanction is severe: where it is established that such a vehicle has been taken onto the highway after the expiry of the period without being registered in the heirs' names, an administrative fine of **3,000 Turkish lira** is imposed on the driver and the vehicle is barred from traffic until it is registered in the heirs' names.

For foreign heirs ninety days is a short period; [obtaining a certificate of inheritance](/en/articles/certificate-of-inheritance-foreign-heirs) and the [transfer of vehicles and bank accounts](/en/articles/inheriting-vehicles-bank-accounts-turkey) should be planned with that period in mind.

## Registration and the Right to Drive Are Not the Same Thing

Having a vehicle registered in your name does not of itself confer the authority to drive it. The driving licence regime is separate, and [converting a foreign licence into a Turkish one](/en/articles/foreign-driving-licence-turkey-conversion) is subject to its own periods and conditions. In addition, for foreign nationals residing under a residence permit, the restriction on driving vehicles registered in Türkiye for gain or for commercial purposes remains in force.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I buy a vehicle in Türkiye as a foreigner?

Article 20 of Law No. 2918 draws no distinction on the basis of nationality as regards vehicle ownership; the rules on sale and transfer are the same for everyone. Because identity and address details are required during registration, in practice a [foreigner identification number](/en/articles/foreigner-identification-number-turkey) and a valid address registration are needed.

### Is a sale made without a notary valid?

No. The Law expressly provides that all sales and transfers not made by notaries are void. Ownership does not pass even if payment has been made and the vehicle handed over.

### I have bought the vehicle but have not yet registered it — can I drive it?

You should not. Vehicles taken onto the road without being registered are barred from traffic until registration is carried out. The provisional document issued by the notary is valid for one month; the transaction must be completed within that period.

### How long do I have to transfer an inherited vehicle?

Under subparagraph (f) added by Law No. 7574, heirs must apply to the registration office and obtain a registration document in their names within ninety days from the date of death. If the period is exceeded, a fine of 3,000 Turkish lira is imposed on the driver and the vehicle is barred from traffic.
