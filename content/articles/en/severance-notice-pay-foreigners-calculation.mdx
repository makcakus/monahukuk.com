The most-discussed item in labour law is **severance pay** and **notice pay** when an employee leaves work. For foreign employees, these compensations are often important material rights; when correctly calculated, the same protections apply as for a Turkish employee, regardless of the employee's foreign status. This guide explains foreign employees' severance and notice pay rights and calculation principles.

## Turkish Labour Law's Coverage of Foreign Employees

In Turkey, both foreign and Turkish employees:

- Benefit from the same labour law protection,
- Have the same severance and notice rights,
- Can file a lawsuit at the labour court following the same process.

This equality ensures foreignness creates no adverse discrimination in the employment relationship. Every foreigner employed with a work permit begins to have these rights.

## Severance Pay

### Earning Conditions

Main conditions sought legally to earn severance pay:

- Having worked a **specific period** (minimum seniority per prevailing legislation),
- Employment contract ending for a **severance-pay-creating** reason:

  - Termination by the employer in the **absence of a justified reason**,
  - Termination by the employee with a **justified reason** (health reason, ethics and good faith violation, force majeure, etc.),
  - Termination due to military service or **retirement**,
  - A female employee leaving due to marriage (within a specific period),
  - Employee's death.

Severance pay is generally not paid to a resigning employee; exceptions are as stated above.

### Calculation Method

Elements considered in severance pay calculation:

- **Full years worked** and the remaining day count,
- **All-in monthly wage** (giydirilmiş ücret) — not just the bare salary, but including premiums, bonuses, holiday pay, and regular benefits like meal and transport,
- In the calculation, **30 days'** wage is used for each full year worked.

A **ceiling** is set by law for severance pay; this ceiling is typically a high amount, and most employees fall below it. However, the ceiling may be relevant for high-paid foreign managers.

### Intermittent Working

In situations of intermittent work:

- Reasons for breaks matter,
- In some situations, breaks are not counted in the seniority calculation,
- Transitions between company groups are carefully evaluated.

### Tax on Severance Pay

Severance pay is **exempt from income tax** up to a specific amount; however, the portion above the ceiling is subject to tax. Stamp duty also applies.

## Notice Pay

### Earning Conditions

Notice pay:

- Applies when the employment contract is terminated **without observing the notification periods**,
- Is a rule applied to termination by both employer and employee,
- Has no earning period requirement (unlike severance pay).

### Notification Periods

Notification periods determined by law exist **per employee's seniority**. As seniority increases, the notice period also increases. During this period:

- The employee actually continues under the employment contract,
- The employer must give **job-search leave** for the employee to find a new job,
- Termination occurs at the end of the period.

If the employer terminates the contract without complying with this period, a **wage equivalent to the notice period** must be paid to the employee; this is notice pay.

### Calculation

Notice pay:

- Is the portion of the **all-in monthly wage** falling within the notice period,
- Is based on the all-in wage (same as for severance),
- Has a more limited tax exemption, unlike severance pay.

## Practical Issues for Foreign Employees

### 1. All-In Wage Instead of Bare Salary

Foreign managers generally receive a **standard salary + additional benefits** (lodging, vehicle, health insurance, annual premium, education contribution, etc.). In compensation calculation, the **monetary equivalent** of these benefits is included. This significantly increases the compensation amount.

### 2. Foreign Currency Salary

Foreign employees sometimes receive their salary in foreign currency. In compensation calculation:

- It is often converted to TL at the **termination date rate**,
- If a different basis is foreseen in the contract, that applies,
- Courts apply the principle of concrete justice in parity disputes.

### 3. Home-Country Pension Account

If the employee has an SGK-equivalent social security account in their home country, the period worked in Turkey can be transferred to the home-country pension account under social security agreements.

### 4. Off-Payroll Payments

Some employers pay employees **part of their salary off-payroll** to evade tax. This practice:

- Works against the employee, since compensation calculation is based on the payroll record,
- Can, when taken to litigation, still allow the real wage to be determined through witness statements and bank records,
- Creates tax evasion risk for both employer and employee.

Foreign employees should be careful on this; receiving salary through a **fully-declared bank transfer** where possible is recommended.

## Legal Routes if Compensation Is Not Paid

When the employer does not pay compensation, the employee can pursue the following routes:

### 1. Mediation

In labour cases, **mediation** is a mandatory pre-litigation stage. The process:

- Application to a mediator,
- Meeting within a specific period,
- The parties' settlement effort,
- If settlement is reached, a document is signed which has the nature of a court decision.

If settlement is not reached, a lawsuit is filed.

### 2. Labour Court Lawsuit

At the labour court:

- Expert reports (particularly for wage and benefits calculation),
- Witness statements,
- Documents

are evaluated. An additional translator may be needed for a foreign employee.

### 3. Enforcement Proceeding

If a favourable court ruling or mediation document exists, the employee can collect the compensation by initiating an **enforcement proceeding**. A lien can be applied to the employer's salary, bank accounts, or real estate.

## Risk Management from the Employer's Perspective

Practical recommendations for employers:

1. **Payroll must be net and correct** — the real wage must be in the records,
2. **Document the termination process** — disciplinary and performance evaluation reports,
3. **Provision for severance pay** — for predictability in financial planning,
4. **Social security agreement notification for the foreign employee**,
5. **Settlement clauses in the employment contract** — covering the monetary equivalent of variable benefits.

## Important Documents from the Employee's Perspective

Documents the employee must carefully preserve at the start of and during the employment relationship:

- Employment contract,
- Pay slips,
- Bank transfer receipts,
- Social security notifications,
- Performance evaluation reports,
- Disciplinary and warning correspondence,
- Annual leave usage records.

When a dispute arises, these documents form the foundation of the defence.
