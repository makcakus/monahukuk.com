[In the previous article](/articles/salvage-special-compensation-turkish-commercial-code) we examined Art. 1312 and so completed both sub-headings of "V — Rights of the salvor": "1. Salvage award" (Arts. 1304-1311) and "2. Special compensation" (Art. 1312). Those two sub-headings governed the conditions under which, and the amounts in which, a salvor becomes entitled to a claim. Articles 1313 and 1314 move on to the mechanics of collecting a claim now taken to exist — whether a salvage award or special compensation: when interest begins to run on it (Art. 1313), and when and how the debtors must pay or secure it (Art. 1314). These two short articles mark the chapter's shift from "how much" to "how is it collected".

## When Interest Begins to Run (Art. 1313)

*Article 1313 – (1) Interest shall run on the salvor's claims governed by this Chapter from the date on which the salved property should have been taken over pursuant to Article 1303(2)(c), and, where the property cannot be delivered, from the date on which the salvage operation was concluded as regards special compensation. General provisions apply to all other matters concerning interest.*

Art. 1313 fixes the starting point for interest on "the salvor's claims governed by this Chapter" — that is, on both the ordinary salvage award calculated under Arts. 1304-1311 and the special compensation earned under Art. 1312. The rule packs two scenarios into a single sentence. In the first and principal scenario, interest runs from the date on which the salved property should have been taken over [under Art. 1303(2)(c)](/articles/salvage-parties-obligations-and-salvage-award-principles-turkish-commercial-code). In the second, exceptional scenario — where the property cannot be delivered for any reason — interest on special compensation runs from the date on which the salvage operation was concluded.

The logic behind this dual structure reflects the practical reality of salvage. In most cases the salved vessel or property is brought to a port or place of safety and taken over by the owner; on that normal course, choosing the take-over date as the starting point is natural, because it is precisely at that moment that the salvor's claim crystallises as a concrete, ascertainable amount. But in some situations — where the salved property has sunk or been lost, or where, as under the special compensation regime of Art. 1312, the operation aimed at preventing environmental damage and no moment of "delivery of property" ever arose — taking the delivery date as the reference becomes impossible. Art. 1313 fills that gap by substituting, for special compensation, the date on which the salvage operation was concluded, that is, the moment the operation was actually completed.

### Why "should have been taken over" Matters

The phrase "should have been taken over" repays careful reading: the statute ties the start of interest not to the date the property was *actually* taken over, but to the date on which it *ought* to have been taken over [under Art. 1303(2)(c)](/articles/salvage-parties-obligations-and-salvage-award-principles-turkish-commercial-code). The practical consequence is significant. If the owner delays taking delivery despite the salvor's reasonable request, that delay does not push the start of interest back; interest continues to run from the date the owner's take-over obligation arose — that is, from the salvor's reasonable request. The rule turns the take-over duty imposed on the owner by Art. 1303(2)(c) from a merely abstract standard of conduct into a concrete legal consequence backed by an interest sanction: the longer the owner delays, the longer interest accrues on the salvor's claim. This adds an indirect but effective economic sanction to the Art. 1303(2)(c) duty — much as [Art. 1311](/articles/salvage-award-forfeiture-turkish-commercial-code) attaches forfeiture of the award to the salvor's duty of care.

## Anchoring to the Take-Over Rule in Art. 1303(2)(c)

As examined in [the article on Arts. 1303-1304](/articles/salvage-parties-obligations-and-salvage-award-principles-turkish-commercial-code), Art. 1303(2)(c) is the last of three duties owed by the owner or master to the salvor: to take over the vessel or other property once secured, when the salvor makes a reasonable request. There we explained the function of that duty as ensuring that the salvage relationship ends within a reasonable time and that the salvor's resources are not tied up unnecessarily. Art. 1313 brings that same moment of take-over back into play, this time with a different function: in Art. 1303(2)(c) the moment is the object of a duty of conduct owed by the owner, whereas in Art. 1313 it becomes the reference point from which interest begins to run on the salvor's monetary claim. This reveals an internal consistency in the statute's scheme: Art. 1303(2)(c) lays down a rule of conduct, and Art. 1313 attaches the monetary consequence of breaching it — or simply of time passing — to that same rule's own reference point. Two provisions the series treated separately are thus locked together around a single point in time.

## Reference to General Provisions on Other Interest Matters

The second sentence of the paragraph — "general provisions apply to all other matters concerning interest" — makes clear that Art. 1313 itself governs only the moment interest begins, and that the rate of interest, the type of interest (statutory interest, default interest, foreign-currency interest on foreign-currency claims) and other technical questions are referred to the general interest regime of the Turkish Code of Obligations (and, where relevant, Law No. 3095 on Statutory Interest and Default Interest). This referencing technique is a legislative method the series has met before: the chapter regulates matters peculiar to salvage law (here, when interest starts) while leaving matters that are not peculiar to salvage law, and that general obligations law already resolves (here, the rate and mechanics of interest), to the general provisions rather than restating them. It shows the statute adopting an economical drafting technique that avoids needless repetition — just as we saw in the reference [in Art. 1312(3)](/articles/salvage-special-compensation-turkish-commercial-code) to particular sub-paragraphs of Art. 1305.

## Payment or Security on Take-Over (Art. 1314)

*Article 1314 – (1) When the salved property is taken over pursuant to Article 1303(2)(c), the debtors shall pay the sums falling to their respective shares of the salvor's claims governed by this Chapter, or, upon the salvor's request, provide security for those sums so as to cover interest and litigation costs as well.*

Art. 1314 attaches a duty of action on the part of the debtors to the very reference point Art. 1313 identified — the moment of take-over under Art. 1303(2)(c). The rule imposes a two-option obligation: at the moment of take-over the debtors must either actually pay the sums falling to their respective shares of the salvor's claim, or — upon the salvor's request — provide security for those sums covering not merely the principal but interest and litigation costs as well. The use of the plural "debtors" is directly consistent with the fact, [examined under Art. 1306](/articles/salvage-award-debtors-turkish-commercial-code), that a salvage award may have more than one debtor: different interests such as the shipowner, the cargo owner and the freight creditor are each liable in proportion to their own shares, and each debtor bears this payment or security obligation only for the portion falling to its own share.

### The Logic of the Two-Option Structure: Immediate Security of Collection

The pay-or-secure option in Art. 1314 solves a practical problem of salvage law. When a salvage operation is completed, the full and definitive amount of the salvor's claim is often not yet settled — the assessment under the ten criteria of Art. 1305 may be in dispute between the parties, or the special compensation calculation under Art. 1312 may not yet be complete. Had the statute obliged the debtors only to make actual payment at the moment of take-over, that uncertainty could have made collection practically impossible: a debtor could postpone payment indefinitely by saying that the amount was not yet fixed. Art. 1314 removes that risk through the security option: even where the definitive amount is not yet settled, the salvor may require that its claim — including interest and litigation costs — be secured before the salved property passes out of reach, that is, at the moment when the debtor is economically most accessible. This forms part of the multi-layered mechanism of collection security, alongside the lien and right of retention regulated in Art. 1315.

The express requirement that the security cover not only the principal but interest and litigation costs is also important: it guarantees that even if the salvor later has to bring an action, the additional cost generated by the proceedings themselves is covered, so that the salvor's ultimate recovery is not eroded by litigation costs.

## How Arts. 1313 and 1314 Fit Together

Arts. 1313 and 1314 are complementary provisions working the same reference point — the moment of take-over under Art. 1303(2)(c) — from different angles. Art. 1313 says that this moment is the date from which interest begins to run on the salvor's claim; Art. 1314 says that the same moment is the date on which the debtors' duty either to pay or to provide security arises. Read together, they show the statute positioning the moment of take-over as a turning point in the salvage relationship: before take-over, the salvage operation is still under way or not yet concluded; at the moment of take-over — or, where delivery is impossible, at the moment the operation is concluded — the salvor's claim becomes a fully due claim that both starts to bear interest and triggers a duty of payment or security. This structure turns the owner's take-over duty examined in [the article on Arts. 1303-1304](/articles/salvage-parties-obligations-and-salvage-award-principles-turkish-commercial-code) from a mere rule of conduct into the axis around which all the monetary consequences of the salvage relationship turn.

## Court of Cassation Track Record

A comprehensive search using terms such as "start of interest on salvage award", "TTK 1313 salvage interest", "security for salvage award" and "TTK 1314 time of payment for salvage" found no Court of Cassation decision that cites Art. 1313 or Art. 1314 by article number and decides on the merits; as noted in earlier articles in this series, that should be stated honestly. Even so, the questions of when interest starts to run on salvage claims and when such a claim falls due have in fact been litigated in Turkish practice — argued not on Art. 1313 itself but on general principles of obligations law, which shows the practical counterpart of the direction in the second sentence of Art. 1313 that "general provisions apply to all other matters concerning interest".

An instructive example is the judgment of the Istanbul 17th Commercial Court of First Instance (sitting as a specialised maritime court), **E.2015/62, K.2019/100, T.04.03.2019**, together with the decision setting it aside, that of the 13th Civil Chamber of the Istanbul Regional Court of Justice, **E.2019/1155, K.2021/209, T.18.02.2021**. In that case the parties had fixed the salvage award at a lump sum (TRY 33,000) by a 2012 salvage contract, but made payment conditional on the defendant paying "as soon as it received payment [from the cargo interest]". The first-instance court characterised that condition as an indefinite term, held that the claim fell due on the date the debtor collected from the third party, and ruled that interest should run from that date. The Regional Court of Justice, relying on the freedom of contract (Art. 90 of the Turkish Code of Obligations) and the suspensive condition (Art. 170), dismissed the action on the ground that the contractual payment condition had not yet materialised at the date of the action and that the claim was therefore not yet due. Although this chain of decisions does not cite Art. 1313, it is instructive precisely because it demonstrates in a concrete dispute the mechanism to which the second sentence of Art. 1313 points — that questions of interest and maturity on a salvage claim are resolved by general principles of obligations law to the extent salvage law contains no special rule. It also indirectly confirms that the parties may fix the time of payment of a salvage award by contract, differently from the moment of take-over contemplated by Art. 1313, since Arts. 1313 and 1314 are as a rule default rules under the principle [examined under Arts. 1301-1302](/articles/salvage-contract-adaptation-annulment-and-mandatory-rules-turkish-commercial-code) and are not among the provisions listed as mandatory.

## Bridge to the Rest of the Chapter

With Arts. 1313 and 1314, the first two mechanisms for collecting a salvage claim — the start of interest and the payment or security obligation at take-over — are in place. The chapter goes on to two provisions that complete this security of collection: Art. 1315 regulates the salvor's maritime lien over the salved vessel and its right of retention over other salved property, adding a layer of security in rem to the security obligation of Art. 1314; and Art. 1316 allows an interim payment to be made to the salvor by interlocutory order while proceedings are pending, supporting the salvor economically through a long trial. Those two articles are the subject of the next article in this series.

## Practical Takeaway

Art. 1313 provides that interest runs on the salvor's claims from the date on which the salved property should have been taken over pursuant to Art. 1303(2)(c) — or, where the property cannot be delivered, from the date the salvage operation was concluded as regards special compensation — and that general provisions apply to all other matters.
