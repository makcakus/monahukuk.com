TTK Art. 965-972 govern how a ship exits the registry and how the ship's certificate — the registry's portable proof — is issued, renewed, and withdrawn.

## Deregistration on Request

Art. 965(1) allows deregistration when a ship sinks beyond recovery, becomes unrepairable, or loses Turkish flag rights for any reason; a voluntarily-registered ship (under the 18-gross-ton threshold) can be deregistered on the owner's bare request alone. Where "not worth repairing" is the stated ground, Art. 965(2) requires the registrar to notify mortgage creditors and give them a chance to object before deregistration proceeds.

## The Mortgage Creditor's Veto

The chapter's most distinctive mechanism sits in Art. 965(3): if a ship loses Turkish flag rights, its registration can be cancelled only with the consent of mortgage creditors and other rights-holders shown in the registry. Without that consent, the loss of flag rights is simply recorded rather than cancelling the registration outright — and if a mortgage exists, that recording does *not* amount to deregistration; the ship stays "on the books" for the mortgage creditor's benefit until consent is given or the debt is otherwise satisfied. Turkish courts have applied this protection even against a foreign state's seizure of a vessel — a mortgage creditor's consent requirement survives extraordinary events and is treated as a matter of Turkish public policy.

## Ex Officio Deregistration

Art. 966-967 give the registrar power to deregister without an owner request in two situations: a ship registered despite failing an essential eligibility condition, or twenty years of total inactivity combined with evidence the ship no longer exists — but only if no mortgage or usufruct is recorded against it.

## The Ship's Certificate

Art. 968-971 govern the certificate that mirrors the registry entry: it must reflect registry contents exactly, a new certificate is issued only against surrender of the old one (or credible proof of loss), and every registry change must be reflected in the certificate without delay. The certificate is withdrawn and cancelled automatically when a ship is deregistered under Art. 965.

## Practical Takeaway

For lenders financing Turkish-flagged vessels, the mortgage-consent requirement in Art. 965(3) is one of the strongest creditor protections in Turkish maritime law — Turkish courts have consistently refused to let deregistration proceed around it, even where an owner's flag rights were lost through extraordinary circumstances abroad.
