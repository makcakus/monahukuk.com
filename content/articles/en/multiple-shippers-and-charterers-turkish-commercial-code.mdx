TTK Art. 1161-1162 address a complication the earlier loading-time provisions assume away: what happens when more than one party is involved on either side of a voyage charter.

## Multiple Shippers Under One Charterer

Art. 1161 covers the common scenario where a single contracting shipper (say, a freight forwarder) loads cargo belonging to several different underlying owners at the same port. The rule is unambiguous: even then, notice of readiness goes only to the shipper, and the entire loading-time and demurrage machinery (Art. 1152-1160) applies without regard to how many underlying cargo owners exist — a single clock, tied to the single contract, rather than one clock per cargo owner. Underlying cargo owners may still request their own cargo's loading location be changed, but the cost of any such relocation falls on the shipper, and loading/demurrage time keeps running during the move.

## Multiple Independent Charterers on One Ship

Art. 1162 covers the reverse situation: separate, independent voyage charter agreements covering different parts of the same ship, made with different charterers. Here the loading-time and demurrage provisions (Art. 1152-1157) apply *separately* to each contract — one charterer's late notice or demurrage has no bearing on another's contract. But there is an important limit: if one charterer's termination under Art. 1158 would require unloading cargo already aboard in a way that delays the voyage or forces transshipment, that charterer cannot demand the unloading without the consent of every other charterer sharing the ship — a cross-consent mechanism balancing individual contract freedom against the shared, physical reality of one ship's schedule.

## Practical Takeaway

For freight forwarders and consolidators acting as a single contracting shipper for multiple cargo owners, Art. 1161 means the loading-time exposure is consolidated under the forwarder's own contract — a material point when negotiating who bears demurrage risk further down the chain to individual cargo owners.
