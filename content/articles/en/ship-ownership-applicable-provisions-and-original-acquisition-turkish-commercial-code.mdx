TTK Art. 996-1000 open the ownership chapter of Turkish maritime law with a threshold question — which legal regime applies — before turning to the ways ownership can be acquired outright, without a transfer from a prior owner.

## Registered Versus Unregistered Ships

Art. 996 confines this chapter's special ownership rules to ships actually registered on the Turkish Ship Registry. Art. 997 sends everything else — Turkish ships never registered, such as small vessels only recorded on a local mooring register — back to the Civil Code's ordinary movable-property rules. This is not a technicality: Turkish courts have reversed lower courts for applying the registered-ship transfer formalities (discussed below) to a vessel that was, in fact, unregistered, where ordinary possession-based transfer rules should have governed instead.

## Occupation of an Ownerless Ship

Art. 998 reserves the right to occupy an ownerless ship exclusively to the State — a deliberate departure from the ordinary "finders keepers" rule for movables, justified by the navigational-safety, environmental, and wreck-removal dimensions unique to ships. The State perfects this right simply by registering itself as owner; Turkish courts require that any such registration proceeding formally name the last known owner as a party, even where that owner's identity is otherwise unclear.

## Acquisitive Prescription

Art. 999 lets someone wrongly registered as owner acquire real ownership after five years of undisputed, uninterrupted possession as the apparent owner — the maritime counterpart to land-registry prescription, and a practical safety valve for registry errors. A recorded objection to the registry entry pauses this clock for as long as it remains recorded. Art. 1000 provides a tougher, ten-year route for ships that were never registered at all, or whose registered owner has been dead or missing for at least ten years — but this route requires a court judgment following public notice, not automatic accrual, giving any real owner a chance to intervene before the claimant's registration becomes final.

## Practical Takeaway

Whether a vessel is registered or not decides everything downstream — which formalities govern its sale, and which prescription period (five years automatic, or ten years through court proceedings) can cure a defective chain of title.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How is an unregistered vessel transferred?

Under TTK Article 997/1, <b>the provisions of the Turkish Civil Code relating to movables</b> apply to ownership and limited rights in rem over Turkish vessels not registered in the Turkish Ship Registry. That is, agreement and transfer of possession suffice for the transfer; registration is not required. For registered vessels, however, under Article 1001 the transfer of ownership requires agreement between the owner and the acquirer <b>and transfer of possession of the vessel</b>.

### Can a vessel be acquired through the passage of time?

Yes, in two ways. Under <b>ordinary prescription</b> (Article 999), a person registered as owner in the ship registry without actually being the owner acquires ownership provided that <b>the registration has lasted at least five years</b> and, during that period, they have possessed the vessel as an uncontested and uninterrupted original possessor. Under <b>extraordinary prescription</b> (Article 1000), a person who has possessed, as an uncontested and uninterrupted original possessor for <b>at least ten years</b>, a vessel that should have been but was not registered, may request registration of the vessel as their own property.

### Does a person who finds an ownerless vessel become its owner?

No. Under TTK Article 998/1, <b>only the State has the right to appropriate an ownerless vessel</b>. An ownerless vessel is one whose owner cannot be determined from the registry records, or whose ownership has been duly abandoned. This rule departs from the general freedom of appropriation applicable to movables, and serves to protect maritime safety and the integrity of the registry system.
