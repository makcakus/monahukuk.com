Losing track of a visa, visa exemption, or residence permit expiry date is among the most common legal problems foreigners face in Türkiye. The consequences are not uniform: a one-day lapse and a three-month overstay produce different outcomes under Law No. 6458 on Foreigners and International Protection (YUKK). This article sets out the statutory thresholds, how the fine is determined, when an entry ban is not imposed, and the correct sequence of steps once an overstay is discovered.

## When Does the Overstay Begin?

Article 19 of YUKK requires a residence permit for any foreigner staying in Türkiye beyond the period granted by a visa or visa exemption, or beyond ninety days. The overstay therefore begins the day after the permit or visa period ends. For permit holders, the critical provision is Article 24: extension applications are filed with the governorate from sixty days before expiry and **in every case before the permit expires**. Applicants who file in time receive a document exempt from any charge, and may remain in Türkiye on that document until a decision is issued, even after their permit period has ended. In other words, a timely [extension application](/en/articles/renewing-turkish-residence-permit-guide) prevents the overstay from arising at all; an application made after expiry does not.

## The Ten-Day Threshold for a Deportation Order

The Law sets two tiers according to the length of the overstay. Article 54(1)(e) provides that a deportation order may be issued against those who **exceed the visa or visa exemption period by more than ten days**; subparagraph (g) extends the same outcome to residence permit holders who, **without an acceptable justification, exceed their permit period by more than ten days** after it ends.

The ten-day figure does not mean shorter overstays go unpunished; it marks the ordinary threshold for a deportation order. Fines and fee liabilities arise for overstays under ten days as well. The concept of "acceptable justification" in subparagraph (g) leaves the administration a margin of appreciation: a documented medical condition or a genuine impossibility of travel may be assessed within it. Evidencing the justification in the file has a direct bearing on the outcome.

## How the Administrative Fine Is Calculated

Article 102 of YUKK lists the administrative fines item by item. The figures in the statutory text are 2013 nominal amounts; under Law No. 5326 on Misdemeanours they are **revalued at the start of each calendar year**. The current amount must therefore be taken from the official tariff in force at the time of the application.

The subparagraphs most often encountered in practice concern foreigners who enter or leave Türkiye unlawfully or attempt to do so, those who manage to enter despite an entry ban, and those who fail to leave within the period granted under Article 56. Paragraph two further imposes a fine on anyone who enables travel, shelter, or accommodation within Türkiye for foreigners lacking a valid travel document, residence permit, or travel authorisation, and on those who lease property to them — calculated **per foreigner identified**. The sanction thus targets not only the foreigner but also the landlord and the accommodation provider.

Two procedural rules matter: fines are payable **within thirty days** of notification, and where the misdemeanour is repeated within a calendar year, the fine is **increased by half**. An unpaid fine does not simply remain a debt; under Article 15(ğ), a visa is refused to a foreigner who declines to pay sums arising from a visa violation or a previous residence permit.

## The Entry Ban and the Provision That Prevents It

The most lasting consequence of an overstay is not the fine but the [ban on entry to Türkiye](/en/articles/entry-ban-turkey-foreigners). Under Article 9, an entry ban lasts a maximum of five years as a rule; where a serious threat to public order or public security exists, the Presidency of Migration Management may extend it by up to a further ten years.

Paragraph four of the same article, however, leaves a decisive way out. Foreigners whose visa or residence permit has expired and who apply to the governorate to leave Türkiye **before the situation is detected by the competent authorities**, and against whom a deportation order is then issued, **may be spared an entry ban, provided they have paid their administrative fines and have not exceeded the overstay periods determined by the Ministry**. Paragraph five likewise provides that no entry ban need be imposed on those invited to leave Türkiye under Article 56 who do leave within the time granted.

These two provisions dictate the practical strategy: on discovering an overstay, the step to take is not to wait but to approach the Provincial Directorate of Migration Management voluntarily, before being caught in an inspection. That difference in timing is the difference between a five-year ban and a clean departure.

## Invitation to Leave and the Exit Permit Document

Article 56 governs the granting of time to leave Türkiye to a foreigner subject to a deportation order. Those leaving within that period are issued an **Exit Permit Document**, which carries no charge. The grant of time is not automatic, however: it is withheld from those presenting a risk of absconding, those who have breached lawful entry or exit rules, those who have used forged documents, those who have sought or obtained a residence permit on false documents, and those posing a threat to public order, public security, or public health. For them, [administrative detention](/en/articles/administrative-detention-removal-centers-turkey) and removal proceedings follow directly.

## What to Do Once an Overstay Is Discovered

The steps, in order, are:

- Calculate the exact number of overstay days from the residence permit document or the passport entry stamp,
- Gather documents supporting any acceptable justification (illness, hospital report, force majeure),
- Approach the Provincial Directorate of Migration Management without waiting to be identified in an inspection — this is the precondition for the protection under Article 9(4),
- Pay the assessed administrative fine and residence fee in full and retain the receipts,
- Obtain the Exit Permit Document if you will be leaving Türkiye,
- If you intend to stay, assess whether a fresh residence permit application remains possible given the nature of the overstay,
- Where a deportation order has been issued, [challenge it before the courts](/en/articles/residence-permit-refusal-appeal-turkey) within the applicable time limit.

One point deserves emphasis: time spent in removal centres under administrative detention does not count as a breach of the residence permit period, under Article 26 of YUKK.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### My visa expired a week ago — will I be deported?

For overstays not exceeding ten days a deportation order does not ordinarily arise, but an administrative fine and fee liability do. These must be settled on departure; otherwise your future visa applications may be refused under Article 15.

### If I pay the fine, is the entry ban automatically lifted?

No. Payment alone is not enough. To avoid an entry ban being imposed you must also have initiated the process by your own application before the overstay was detected by the authorities, and must not have exceeded the overstay periods set by the Ministry.

### My permit expired while my extension application was still pending — am I in breach?

No. If you filed within time, remaining in Türkiye on the document issued to you until a decision is made is lawful. A breach arises only where the extension application was made after the permit had already expired.

### Am I liable if I host a foreigner with irregular status at my home?

You may be. Article 102(2) of YUKK imposes an administrative fine, calculated per foreigner identified, on those who provide shelter or accommodation to foreigners without valid documents and on those who lease property to them.
