TTK Art. 601-607 form the financial core of limited company membership: capital protection, limited liability, and the two narrow tools — additional payment and ancillary performance obligations — that can expand a partner's exposure beyond the capital contribution.

## Capital Protection and Limited Liability

Art. 601 bars any repayment of a capital contribution to partners (except through a formal capital reduction) and forbids releasing partners from that payment obligation — any disguised profit distribution or below-market transaction with a partner is void. Art. 602 confirms the core feature distinguishing a limited company from a partnership: the company alone answers for its debts with its own assets, and a partner's exposure to company creditors is, absent a statutory exception, limited to the capital committed. Turkish courts have repeatedly held that a partner cannot be made personally liable for company debts, including employee claims, even by contract.

## Additional Payment and Ancillary Performance Obligations

Under Art. 603, the articles of association may require partners to make additional cash payments beyond their capital, but only when the company's losses exceed capital plus statutory reserves, the company cannot otherwise continue operating, or a similarly defined equity need arises; the cap is twice the nominal value of the share, and the obligation becomes due automatically upon bankruptcy. A partner who exits remains exposed for two years if the company goes bankrupt within that window (Art. 604), and any repayment of amounts already contributed is only possible from freely available reserves (Art. 605). Ancillary performance obligations (Art. 606) cover non-monetary contributions serving the company's business purpose — supply of goods, capacity, or services — but if such an obligation lacks a proper consideration and effectively meets an equity need, it is reclassified and subjected to the additional-payment rules, preventing parties from using the "ancillary performance" label to evade the cap.

## Unanimous Consent for New or Increased Obligations

Art. 607 sets an especially demanding threshold: a resolution that imposes new or increases existing additional payment or ancillary obligations requires the unanimous consent of all affected partners — not the standard supermajority for articles amendments. Turkish courts treat a resolution passed only by majority under these circumstances as legally non-existent, not merely voidable.

## Practical Takeaway

Foreign investors negotiating a Turkish limited company shareholding should scrutinize the articles of association for existing additional payment or ancillary performance clauses, since these can materially exceed the nominal capital commitment — and confirm that any future increase requires the consent of every affected partner, not just a majority vote.
