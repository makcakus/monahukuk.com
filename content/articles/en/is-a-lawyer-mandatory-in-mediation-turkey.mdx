The short answer: no, it is not mandatory. The long answer is this: the presence of a lawyer changes the legal force of the document signed, and attending without one means assessing on your own a document that produces irreversible consequences.

## What Does the Law Say?

Law No. 6325 ties participation in the negotiations to three options: the parties may take part **in person**, through their **legal representatives**, or **through their lawyers**. In addition, experts who can contribute to resolving the dispute may be present at the negotiations.

The phrase "through" shows that the party's personal presence is not compulsory; the lawyer's attendance discharges the obligation. The converse is also true: attending without a lawyer is fully valid in law.

A rule added in 2023 places a safeguard on that flexibility: the mediator must inform **the party themselves, even where they have a lawyer**. This addition aims to prevent processes conducted through a representative without the party's knowledge.

## A Lawyer's Signature Changes the Force of the Document

Although not compulsory, the presence of a lawyer directly affects the character of the settlement document.

**With lawyers:** a settlement document signed jointly by the parties **and their lawyers** together with the mediator counts as an **instrument in the nature of a court judgment** without any enforceability annotation. In commercial disputes the parties' personal signatures are not required; the signatures of the lawyers and the mediator suffice.

**Without a lawyer:** if one party has no lawyer, that route is closed. To acquire the character of a judgment, the document must obtain an **enforceability annotation** from the civil court of peace. That means an extra application, an extra wait and an extra fee, even if only a fixed one.

A lawyer therefore makes a difference not only in the negotiation but in the collectability of the outcome.

## The Real Risk of Attending Without a Lawyer

A mediation session is not an information meeting but a negotiation stage whose consequences cannot be undone. The document signed has two serious effects: no action may be brought on the matters agreed, and in most cases the document can be enforced directly.

The risk is not "failing to settle" but **signing a document whose scope is misunderstood**. The three most common mistakes in practice are:

**Not itemising the claims.** If the document uses a general expression such as "all employee claims", an item you never had in mind is also closed off. Naming the items one by one determines on which matters you keep the road to litigation open.

**Not having done the calculation.** Sitting down at the table without a document-based calculation of severance pay, overtime and annual leave claims means not knowing what proportion of your actual entitlement the offer represents.

**Drafting the obligation in an unenforceable way.** The 12th Civil Chamber has held that a debtor cannot be compelled to conclude a lease contract on the basis of a settlement document that merely records a promise and contains no constitutive ruling. A vaguely drafted obligation may leave you with nothing at the enforcement stage.

## Why "I Will Have It Annulled Later" Is Not Realistic

Parties who attend without a lawyer often think "if it turns out wrong, I will have it annulled". Two obstacles make this difficult.

**Time.** The right to annul for a defect of consent is subject to a one-year preclusive period under Article 39 of the Turkish Code of Obligations.

**Proof.** The Court of Cassation accepts that where a party admits signing the information record and the settlement document and does not dispute their signature, a claim of fraud cannot be treated as proven unless supported by concrete witness testimony or other evidence. The information record you signed creates a strong presumption against the defence "I did not understand the process".

## The Mediator Is Not Your Lawyer

This distinction is the one most often confused by unrepresented parties.

The mediator is obliged to **observe equality** between the parties and must be impartial. They do not give you legal advice, do not tell you how much you are owed, and do not assess whether an offer is in your favour. The only duty of information the Law imposes on them is to inform the parties about the essentials, process and consequences of mediation.

Moreover, a mediator **may not later act as counsel** for either party in an action brought in relation to the dispute in which they served. The mediator is not your representative at any stage.

## For Companies: Who Should Attend?

For legal persons, an authorised representative or a lawyer holding a power of attorney may take part in the negotiations. If a settlement is to be signed, the person attending must hold authority that includes the **powers to settle and to release**; a general power of attorney may not cover them.

If the other party is a **public administration**, a special regime applies: the administration is represented by a **commission** composed of two members designated by the senior manager together with the head of the legal unit or a lawyer or legal adviser designated by them. At the end of the negotiations the commission draws up a reasoned report and keeps it for five years. Settlements bound by the signature of a single official without a commission decision are later exposed to arguments about authority.

## For Foreign Parties

Sessions are conducted in Turkish, and the Law provides no right to a State-funded interpreter; the cost of an interpreter or bilingual counsel falls on the party.

For foreign parties this makes a lawyer effectively indispensable. A signature given without fully understanding the content of the document is extremely difficult to prove as a "defect of consent" afterwards.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q: Can I attend mediation without hiring a lawyer?

Yes. Attending the negotiations in person is legally valid. But because the document to be signed closes the road to litigation and can be enforced directly, signing without a calculation and a review of scope carries serious risk.

### Q: Can my lawyer attend and sign on my behalf?

Yes. Participation through a lawyer is possible and the lawyer's signature makes the settlement document valid. For documents containing a settlement and release, the power of attorney must include the powers to settle and release. In addition, the mediator must inform you as well, even if you have a lawyer.

### Q: The other side has a lawyer and I do not — am I at a disadvantage?

In law the parties have equal rights and the mediator must observe equality. But an information asymmetry arises in fact: the other side comes with a calculated offer while you decide without any tool of assessment. Moreover, the route by which the settlement document acquires the character of a judgment differs depending on whether both sides have lawyers.

### Q: Can the mediator tell me which offer is good?

No. The mediator is bound by impartiality and cannot give legal advice. If it emerges that the parties cannot produce a solution, they may put forward a **settlement proposal**; that proposal is not binding and is not an opinion assessing your interests.

### Q: Is the attorney's fee separate from the mediation fee?

Yes, entirely. The mediation fee is paid to the mediator according to the Mediation Minimum Fee Tariff; the attorney's fee is governed by the retainer agreement and the Minimum Attorney Fee Tariff.
