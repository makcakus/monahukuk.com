It is natural for foreigners living in Turkey or having business in Turkey to occasionally enter into dispute with administrative authorities. Many matters such as visa denial, residence permit cancellation, deportation order, restriction code, tax penalty, municipal penalty, tender decision, or construction permit denial can become subjects of lawsuit before Turkish **administrative courts**. This guide covers foreigners' processes for applying to administrative courts, competent courts, and lawsuit procedure.

## Structure of Administrative Judiciary

In Turkey, administrative judiciary has three-tiered structure:

### 1. Courts of First Instance

- **Administrative Court**: General administrative disputes (visa denial, residence permit cancellation, permit and license transactions, etc.),
- **Tax Court**: Disputes regarding tax, fee, and financial obligations.

### 2. Appeal

- **Regional Administrative Court**: Authority for objection against first instance decisions.

### 3. Cassation / Final Decision

- **Council of State**: Highest administrative judicial authority.

For foreign applicant, this three-tiered structure requires careful conduct of lawsuit.

## What Matters Are Heard at Administrative Court?

Typical situations that can be subject of lawsuit at administrative court:

### 1. Transactions Related to Foreigners Legislation

- Visa denial,
- Residence permit denial or cancellation,
- Deportation order,
- Restriction code applications,
- Work permit denial or cancellation.

### 2. Permit, License, and Authorisation

- Municipal building permits,
- Workplace opening and operating permits,
- Sectoral licenses.

### 3. Tender and Public Contracts

- Tender decisions,
- Public contract processes.

### 4. Administrative Sanctions

- Traffic penalties (administrative fines),
- Environmental penalties,
- KVKK penalties,
- Municipal sanctions.

### 5. Civil Service and Discipline

- Civil servants' disciplinary and assignment transactions.

### 6. Compensation Lawsuits

- Compensation for damage suffered due to administration's wrong act or negligence.

## Competent Court

For foreigner, determining **competent court** to file lawsuit is important:

- General rule: court of place where headquarters of administration making transaction is located,
- For foreigners legislation transactions, court of province where migration administration making transaction is located,
- For central administration transactions, **Ankara Administrative Court**,
- For transactions made in Antalya, **Antalya Administrative Court**.

Lawsuit filed at wrong court isn't rejected but **referred to competent court**; this leads to time loss.

## Lawsuit Filing Period

In administrative judicial cases, **statute of limitations is very critical**:

- General rule for administrative court lawsuits: period prescribed by administrative procedure rules,
- Shorter period for tax court lawsuits,
- Period runs from **notification date** of administrative act.

When period is missed, lawsuit is rejected on substance; therefore, getting legal support immediately after notification is essential.

## Foreigner Filing Lawsuit Without Being in Turkey

Foreign applicant can file lawsuit at administrative court without coming to Turkey:

- Authorises Turkish lawyer with **apostilled notarised power of attorney**,
- Sworn Turkish translation of power of attorney is done,
- Power of attorney signed at Turkish consulate is also valid.

This way, foreigner can run all trial processes through their counsel.

## Stay of Execution

One of strongest measures in administrative judiciary. When filing lawsuit, stay of execution is requested; court:

- Possibility of winning case on merits,
- **Irreparable or hardly reparable damage** that may arise if act is enforced

evaluates and decides. When stay of execution decision is given, consequences of administrative act are suspended until case concludes.

## Petition Preparation

Administrative judicial petition should include following elements:

### 1. Parties' Information

Identity and address information of plaintiff and defendant.

### 2. Identification of Subject Act

Which administrative act's cancellation is requested, when notified.

### 3. Legal Reasoning

**Reasons of unlawfulness** of act:

- In terms of authority,
- In terms of form,
- In terms of cause,
- In terms of subject,
- In terms of purpose.

### 4. Request

In cancellation lawsuit: cancellation of act and stay of execution request.
In compensation lawsuit: compensation amount and basis.

### 5. Documents

All documents attached to lawsuit petition.

## Trial Process

Administrative trial process:

1. **Filing petition**: Petition and attachments are submitted to court.
2. **Court's notification**: Petition is notified to defendant administration, response is requested.
3. **Response petition**: Administration's response is received.
4. **Reply to response, second response**: Parties' additional statements.
5. **Expert review**: Expert report is obtained on technical matters when necessary.
6. **Hearing**: Hearing isn't held in every file in administrative judiciary; can be optional.
7. **Decision**: Court's reasoned decision.

In administrative judiciary, written trial is essential; oral trial rate is relatively low.

## Expert and Documents

Expert reports and documents are foundation of administrative trial:

- Court can request report from court-appointed expert,
- Expert can also be appointed at party's request,
- Original or certified copy of documents is submitted,
- Apostille and translation mandatory for foreign documents.

## Decision and Consequences

Decisions court can give:

### Cancellation Decision

Administrative act is **cancelled**. In this case:

- Act is deemed never to have been done,
- Resulting consequences are reversed,
- If administration needs to make new act, does so.

### Rejection Decision

Court decides administration's act is found lawful. In this case:

- **Appeal** to Regional Administrative Court,
- Then **cassation** to Council of State,
- ECHR application (in international dimension).

## Routes of Appeal

Against first instance court decision:

- **Appeal to Regional Administrative Court**,
- **Cassation to Council of State**

are possible. Each phase has specific period limits.

## Compensation Lawsuits

If foreigner has suffered damage due to administration's wrong act or negligence:

- Compensation can be claimed within scope of **administrative liability**,
- Material damages (lost money, expenses) and moral damages (suffering, distress) are claimed,
- Claimed amount must be supported by concrete evidence.

## Common Mistakes

1. **Missing period** — most common and hardest-to-correct mistake.
2. **Not applying to competent court** — time loss.
3. **Not requesting stay of execution** — damage grows during trial.
4. **Missing apostille on documents** — foreign documents not processed.
5. **Filing lawsuit alone without lawyer** — losing case due to procedural errors.
