TTK Art. 912-916 close out the passenger-transport chapter of Turkish commercial law, covering what happens to a passenger's belongings and, far more consequentially, to the passenger's own body and life during the journey.

## Baggage and the Carrier's Lien

Art. 912 folds baggage loss or damage directly into the general cargo-carrier liability regime (Art. 875-886) — the same exemption grounds, burden of proof, and value calculations apply, not by analogy but by direct reference. Personal items kept with the passenger (a wallet, a phone, jewelry) are covered separately under Art. 912(2), without any requirement that the item was ever handed over to the carrier. Art. 913 gives the carrier a lien over baggage as security for the fare, mirroring the cargo lien but attaching to unpaid ticket price rather than freight.

## The Core Duty: Safe Carriage of Passengers

Art. 914 is the chapter's center of gravity. The carrier must transport passengers safely and compensate accident-related injury or death — and the only escape is proving the accident stemmed from a cause that even the utmost care by the carrier or its staff could not have avoided or overcome. Turkish courts treat this standard as close to unavoidable, particularly in rail and bus accidents, effectively producing near-strict liability. A separate and unusual mechanism sits in Art. 914(3): four specific carrier failures — bumping a ticketed seat, substituting a lower-grade vehicle, missing a passenger by departing early, or failing to carry required first-aid supplies — trigger automatic damages of three times the fare, with no need to prove any loss at all.

## Death During the Journey

If a passenger dies en route, Art. 915 obliges the carrier to safeguard the deceased's baggage for the heirs, and any relative present may demand a written confirmation of what the carrier is holding — a practical evidentiary safeguard in later disputes.

## Practical Takeaway

For a foreign claimant, the litigation almost never turns on whether the carrier is liable — it nearly always is — but on whether the carrier can meet the exceptionally high "utmost care" threshold to escape it. Turkish Court of Cassation case law shows carriers rarely succeed, and contractual claims under these articles run on their own limitation period rather than general tort rules, which can matter significantly for claim timing.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is a separate charge payable for luggage?

As a rule, no. Under TTK Article 912/1 <b>the passenger pays no separate charge for their luggage and hand luggage, unless otherwise agreed.</b> The provision is of a default character; the carrier may provide for a luggage charge in the contract or its tariff. The carrier is liable for the loss of and damage to luggage; as regards <b>hand luggage</b>, however, since it remains in the passenger's own custody, the carrier's liability depends on <b>its own fault</b>.

### May the carrier retain the luggage?

Yes. Under TTK Article 913/1 <b>the carrier has a lien over the luggage, as security for the fare, in accordance with Articles 950 to 953 of the Turkish Civil Code.</b> The right is granted only for the <b>fare</b>; the lien cannot be exercised over the luggage for the carrier's other claims against the passenger. In the carriage of passengers by sea, Article 1254 provides for a broader lien.

### What are the carrier's duties if a passenger dies during the voyage?

Under TTK Article 915/1 <b>if the passenger dies during the journey, the carrier takes the measures necessary to keep the passenger's luggage and effects in good condition, in order to protect the interests of the heirs, until they are handed over to those entitled.</b> The same provision applies to the carriage of passengers by sea through the reference in Article 1255. Claims for loss of support arising from the death are subject to the <b>ten-year</b> limitation period under Article 855/1.
