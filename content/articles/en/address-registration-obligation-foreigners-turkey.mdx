One of the obligations most often neglected by foreigners residing in Türkiye is address registration. People assume the matter is closed once a residence permit is issued; in fact address registration is a separate obligation, resting on a separate statute, and breach of it carries a separate sanction. Failing to notify a move within the period can produce consequences far more serious than an administrative fine: service made to the old registered address is treated as valid, so deadlines are missed without the person ever learning of the decision.

## Two Separate Statutes, Two Separate Obligations

Two sets of provisions must be read together on address registration.

The first is the second paragraph of Article 26 of Law No. 6458 on Foreigners and International Protection: **foreigners who come to Türkiye having obtained a residence and work permit from a consulate are required to register in the address registration system within twenty working days at the latest from the date of entry.** This provision is specific to a foreigner who obtains the permit abroad and then travels to Türkiye, and it starts the period running **from the date of entry into the country**.

The second is Article 50 of Law No. 5490 on Population Services, which establishes the general address notification regime for everyone, drawing no distinction between citizens and foreigners. The third paragraph of that article lays down the same period: **address notification must be made within twenty working days.**

The two provisions complement one another: Article 26(2) of Law No. 6458 governs the initial registration, and Article 50 of Law No. 5490 governs every subsequent change.

## Where and How Is Notification Made?

Article 50 of Law No. 5490 sets out the procedure in detail.

**Declaration is the basis.** Under the first paragraph, the written declaration of the person concerned is taken as the basis for address notification, and notification is made in person or by secure electronic signature. As a rule the administration does not investigate the declaration — but the seventh paragraph introduces a safeguard: **where there is doubt as to the declaration, information and documents confirming it may be requested.** In practice a lease agreement, title deed, or utility bill is sought on this basis.

**The competent authorities** are listed in the second paragraph: address notification is made to civil registry offices or to missions abroad. In addition, a declaration of a change of address may be made, when applying for a service, to the organisations providing address-based services designated by the Ministry.

**Notification on behalf of another** is governed by the fourth and fifth paragraphs. Adult members of a family sharing the same place of residence and other address may make an address declaration on one another's behalf; **persons producing a power of attorney** may likewise make notifications concerning a person's address. This opens the route of [representation under a power of attorney](/en/articles/power-of-attorney-turkey-foreigners) for foreigners outside Türkiye. The addresses of children and of persons under legal incapacity are notified by the parent, guardian, or trustee; failing them, by the child's grandmother, grandfather, adult siblings, or the persons with whom the child lives.

**Institutional accommodation** is addressed in the sixth paragraph: for those staying in places such as residential care homes, children's homes, prisons, and student dormitories, address notifications are made by the officials of the institution concerned.

## The Sanction for Failing to Notify in Time

Subparagraph (c) of the first paragraph of Article 68 of Law No. 5490 — as amended by Law No. 6661 of 14/1/2016 — provides for two separate penalties: **an administrative fine of 50 Turkish lira on those who fail to perform the obligation to notify a change of address within the period, and of 1,000 Turkish lira on those who make a false declaration**, imposed within the country by the civil administrative authorities and abroad by the missions.

These are the figures written into the article in 2016 and, because administrative fines are updated at the beginning of each calendar year by the revaluation rate, the amount actually applicable should be confirmed against the tariff in force at the date of the transaction.

The twentyfold difference between the two penalties is no accident: the legislature treated late notification as a minor matter and **false notification as a serious one**. Declaring an address at which the person does not in fact live — for instance giving the address of an acquaintance merely to complete a residence permit application — falls within the scope of false declaration.

## The Real Risk: Service of Documents

The true importance of address registration does not lie in the fine. The address held in the address registration system is the address at which official notices are served. If the registered address is not current, service on the foreigner may be legally valid yet never actually reach them.

The concrete consequences are severe: a decision cancelling a residence permit, a [deportation order](/en/articles/deportation-order-appeal-turkey), or an administrative fine is deemed to have been served, and the time limits for bringing proceedings begin to run. By the time the foreigner learns of the matter, the period may long since have expired. Keeping the address current is therefore not a procedural formality but a measure that prevents the loss of rights.

## The Address Standard and the National Address Database

The infrastructure of the system is established by Articles 48 and 49 of Law No. 5490. Under Article 48, institutions and natural and legal persons **must comply with the address standard** in their address-related work and transactions; the Ministry is empowered to request any information relating to addresses from institutions, and institutions are obliged to respond within twenty days.

Article 49 places on special provincial administrations and municipalities the obligation to define the address components within their areas of responsibility in accordance with the address standard and to assign them an **unchangeable fixed identification number**. The "your address could not be found in the system" problem that foreigners encounter in practice usually originates here: if the building numbering process has not been completed, no registration can be made to that address.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### When do the twenty working days begin?

For foreigners arriving with a residence or work permit obtained from a consulate, Article 26 of Law No. 6458 starts the period **from the date of entry into Türkiye**. For subsequent changes of address, the twenty working days under Article 50 of Law No. 5490 run from the date of the move.

### I am not in Türkiye — can someone make the address notification for me?

Yes. The fourth paragraph of Article 50 of Law No. 5490 expressly provides that persons producing a power of attorney may make notifications concerning a person's address. Adult family members living at the same address may also declare on one another's behalf.

### I am staying in a hotel or somewhere temporary — what should I declare?

The law defines the place of residence as the place occupied with the intention of remaining permanently. Temporary accommodation is not a place of residence. Once a person settles at an address with the intention of remaining, notification must be made within twenty working days; until that point the existing record must be kept accurate.

### What is the penalty for declaring a false address?

Article 68 of Law No. 5490 provides for an administrative fine of 1,000 Turkish lira — the 2016 figure in the article — on those who make a false declaration; that is twenty times the penalty for late notification. The amount is updated each year by the revaluation rate.
