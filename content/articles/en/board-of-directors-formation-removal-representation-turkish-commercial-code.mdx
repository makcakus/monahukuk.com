TTK Art. 359-373 govern the board of directors of a Turkish joint-stock company — how it is formed, who may serve, how directors are removed, how management may be delegated, and the scope of representation authority that binds the company toward third parties.

## Formation and Qualifications

A single director is now sufficient, appointed either in the bylaws or by shareholder vote (Art. 359) — another simplification from the old five-member minimum. A legal entity may serve as director, but only through one designated natural person registered to attend meetings and vote on its behalf. Directors serve for a maximum three-year term and may be re-elected unless the bylaws say otherwise (Art. 362).

## Removal and Compensation

Directors — even those appointed directly in the bylaws — can always be removed by shareholder vote, either via a relevant agenda item or for just cause even without one (Art. 364). Turkish courts have confirmed that a director removed before their term expires without just cause can claim compensation for lost fees and benefits, though a removal without stated reasons does not by itself amount to a personality-rights violation warranting moral damages.

## Delegation of Management

While the board collectively manages and represents the company by default (Art. 365), it may delegate management wholly or partly to one or more directors or to a third party (such as a general manager) through bylaw authorization and a written internal directive specifying reporting lines and responsibilities (Art. 367). Turkish courts have treated the existence of documented delegation as a decisive factual question in determining who bears legal responsibility for a company's obligations.

## Duty of Care and Loyalty

Directors and anyone entrusted with management must act with the diligence of a prudent manager and safeguard the company's interests in good faith (Art. 369). Turkish courts have found this duty breached where outgoing directors assigned a company receivable to themselves the day before a shareholders' meeting replacing them, and separately where a company representative signed a promissory note in a dual capacity that conflicted with the company's interests — such acts falling outside the representative's authority absent explicit consent.

## Representation Authority

By default, representation requires joint signature by two board members unless the bylaws provide otherwise or the board has only one member (Art. 370). Turkey abolished the ultra vires doctrine for representation too: acts outside the company's stated business purpose still bind the company unless the third party knew or should have known otherwise — mere publication of the bylaws is not, by itself, enough to prove that knowledge (Art. 371). Representation authority and its holders must be registered and published (Art. 373), after which any defect in a representative's appointment can only be raised against third parties if they knew of it.
