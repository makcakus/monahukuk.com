The general assembly is where Turkish joint-stock company shareholders exercise their rights collectively — individual shareholders cannot act on company matters outside this forum. TTK Art. 407-416 set the assembly's powers and the procedural gateway (the call to meet) that must precede any valid meeting.

## Powers That Cannot Be Delegated

Under Art. 408, six areas belong exclusively to the general assembly and cannot be handed to the board or any other body even by unanimous resolution: amending the articles of association, electing and dismissing board members and setting their pay, electing and dismissing the auditor, approving financial statements and profit distribution, dissolving the company, and selling substantially all company assets. A resolution attempting to delegate any of these — even partially, such as letting the board fix a bonus amount within a genel kurul-set range — is void under Art. 447. A sole shareholder holds all assembly powers but must record decisions in writing (Art. 408(3)).

## Ordinary and Extraordinary Meetings, and Who May Call Them

Art. 409 requires an ordinary meeting within three months of each fiscal year-end to handle organ elections, financial statements, and board discharge; extraordinary meetings can be called whenever needed. The board is the default convening authority and retains that power even after its term has technically expired (Art. 410). Only where the board cannot function at all — a persistent, not merely temporary, inability to convene — may a single shareholder ask a court for permission to call the meeting.

## Minority Call Rights and the Agenda Rule

Shareholders holding at least 10% of capital (5% in listed companies) can demand, via notary, that the board call a meeting or add items to the agenda (Art. 411). If the board refuses or stays silent for seven business days, the same shareholders may apply to the commercial court, which can appoint a trustee to run the process (Art. 412). Separately, Art. 413's "agenda-binding" rule — nothing not on the published agenda may be voted — is one of the most frequently litigated points in Turkish company law; Turkish courts have consistently limited its exceptions to a short, closed list, meaning matters like retroactively revoking a prior year's discharge cannot be smuggled in via a floor motion.

## Meeting Without a Formal Call

Art. 416 allows all shareholders, if none objects, to hold a valid meeting without following the formal call procedure — but this requires every shareholder's presence and unbroken non-objection throughout; a shareholder walking out partway renders the meeting invalid.
