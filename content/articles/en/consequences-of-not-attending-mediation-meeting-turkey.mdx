If an invitation to mediation has arrived from the courthouse and you are thinking "I will not settle anyway, so there is no point going", this article is for you. In Turkish law, refusing to settle is free; **failing to attend is not**, and the price appears as an amount you pay even in a case you win.

## You May Refuse to Settle, but Not to Attend

In mandatory mediation the only compulsory things are to apply to the mediation office and to conclude the process with final minutes. Settling is never compulsory.

You may attend the first meeting, say "I do not wish to settle on these terms" and withdraw from the process. That produces no financial sanction; the failure to settle is recorded in the final minutes and the road to litigation opens.

The sanction attaches only to **not attending the first meeting at all**. This distinction is the point most often overlooked in practice.

## What Exactly Is the Sanction?

A party who fails to attend the first meeting without showing a valid excuse is **named** in the final minutes and faces two consequences:

**1. Half of the litigation costs.** That party is held liable for half of the litigation costs the other party would otherwise bear, **even if they are wholly or partly successful** in the action subsequently brought. In other words, even if you win the case outright, you bear half of the other party's court fees, expert costs and service expenses.

**2. Half of the attorney's fee.** Under the provision amended by Law No. 7531 of 7 November 2024, **half of the attorney's fee determined under the Minimum Attorney Fee Tariff** is awarded against the non-attending party in favour of the other party.

**If neither party attends**, the sanction does not operate: in that case each party bears its own litigation costs in the action subsequently brought.

## The Three Conditions of the Sanction

For the court to apply this sanction, three conditions must be satisfied together.

**The non-attendance must relate to the first meeting.** Attending the first meeting and then missing later sessions falls outside the rule; attendance at the first meeting is enough to discharge the obligation.

**There must be no valid excuse.** The Law does not enumerate what an excuse covers; illness, being abroad, natural disaster and similar objective impediments are accepted in practice. The excuse should be notified to the mediator and, if possible, documented.

**The non-attending party must be named in the final minutes.** This is critical for proof. If the minutes do not state who failed to attend, the court cannot apply the sanction.

## No Sanction If the Invitation Was Not Made in Due Form

The Law places the duty to inform the parties and invite them to the first meeting on **the mediator**. Using the contact details supplied by the office or researched by the mediator, they inform the parties by any means of communication, invite them to the first meeting and **document these steps**. Under a rule added in 2023, even where a party has a lawyer, the mediator must also inform the party themselves.

This has consequences in two directions.

If there is no document in the file showing that you were invited, the non-attendance sanction cannot be applied to you. On the other hand, the 9th Civil Chamber of the Turkish Court of Cassation has stated that even where it is accepted that the other party was not duly invited, no fault can be attributed to the party who made the application in conformity with the Law. In other words, a defect in the mediator's invitation does not cause the applicant's action to be dismissed on procedural grounds.

## Two Important Exceptions

**Consumers.** Article 73/A/2 of the Turkish Law on the Protection of Consumers is explicit: the non-attendance sanction in Article 18/A/11 of Law No. 6325 **is not applied against a consumer**. For the seller or supplier it operates in full. This asymmetry is deliberate; the legislator did not want to put the economically weaker party under pressure through the risk of non-attendance.

**Voluntary mediation.** The sanction applies only in mandatory mediation. There is no sanction for failing to take part in a voluntary mediation started at the court's encouragement or by agreement of the parties.

## Attending Does Not Mean Settling

The most common reservation in practice is: "if I go to the meeting, I will be in a weak position because I received an offer." That concern has no basis in law.

The Law imposes a strict **exclusion of evidence** for what is said at the mediation table. The invitation to mediation, views and proposals put forward for termination, suggestions made in the negotiation, the admission of a fact or claim, and documents prepared solely for the purposes of the mediation cannot be relied on as evidence in a subsequent civil action or arbitration; nor may testimony be given about them.

So a payment offer you make in mediation is not treated in court as an admission of the debt. Equally, having been invited to mediation creates no presumption against you.

## What Should You Do If You Cannot Attend?

There are three routes in practice, and all three protect against the sanction.

**Attend through a lawyer.** You may take part in the negotiations through your lawyer rather than in person. The lawyer's attendance discharges the obligation.

**Notify an excuse.** If you are unable to attend, notify the mediator of your excuse in advance and document it. The mediator may move the meeting to another date within the three-week period.

**Request online attendance.** The parties may freely agree the procedure of the mediation, provided they do not contravene mandatory rules. Conducting the session online falls within that freedom and is a practical solution, especially for parties abroad or living in another city.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q: If I attend but accept no offer, will the sanction apply?

No. The sanction attaches only to **not attending at all** the first meeting. A party who attends but does not settle faces no adverse consequence; the failure to settle is recorded in the final minutes and the road to litigation opens.

### Q: Whom should I send as a company?

A party may take part in the negotiations in person, through its legal representative or through its lawyer. For legal persons, attendance by an authorised representative or by a lawyer holding a power of attorney is sufficient. If a settlement is to be signed, the representative must hold authority that includes the power to settle.

### Q: If the other party does not attend, is the process wasted?

No. Where the parties cannot be reached or no session can be held because they did not attend, the mediator also ends the activity and draws up the final minutes. Those minutes satisfy the condition precedent; the non-attending party is named in them and faces the sanction.

### Q: How should I notify my excuse?

The excuse should be notified to the mediator, in writing if possible and together with supporting documents. Since the mediator is obliged to document their steps on information and invitation, having the excuse recorded in the file prevents later argument.

### Q: How is the sanction applied in the action — do I have to request it separately?

Because the provision concerns litigation costs and the attorney's fee, the court takes it into account in the judgment of its own motion. But the non-attending party must be named in the final minutes and those minutes must be filed; it is therefore advisable to draw express attention to this point in the statement of claim.
