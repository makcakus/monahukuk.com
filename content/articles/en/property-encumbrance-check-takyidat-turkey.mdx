Buying an apartment or villa in Antalya feels straightforward until you discover — sometimes after signing — that the property carries a debt, a court freeze, or a right belonging to someone else entirely. These burdens attach to the property itself, not just to the seller, which means they become your problem the moment the title transfers. Running a thorough encumbrance check before you commit is the single most important step a foreign buyer can take in Turkey.

## The Turkish Land Registry and Why It Matters

Every right, restriction, and claim over a piece of real estate in Turkey is recorded in the **tapu sicili**, the official land registry maintained by the Directorate General of Land Registry and Cadastre (TKGM) at local Land Registry offices (Tapu Sicil Müdürlüğü). The registry is public — anyone may inspect it.

This publicity has a direct legal consequence for buyers. Under the Turkish Civil Code (Türk Medeni Kanunu), Article 1023, a person who acquires property by relying in good faith on the land registry is protected by law. However, that same article cuts the other way: any encumbrance already entered in the registry is presumed to be known to the buyer. If a mortgage or a court injunction was on the record before you signed, you cannot later claim ignorance. The registry is both your protection and your responsibility.

## Types of Encumbrances That Can Appear on a Turkish Title

The encumbrance report — called a **takyidat belgesi** — lists every registered burden on a property. As a buyer, you need to understand what each item means:

- **İpotek (mortgage)**: the seller's lender holds a security interest over the property. If the underlying loan is not cleared before transfer, the mortgage passes to you as new owner.
- **Haciz (attachment or seizure)**: a creditor has obtained a court order to lock the asset. A property under haciz cannot normally be transferred until the debt is resolved.
- **İhtiyati tedbir şerhi (interim court injunction)**: a court has ordered that the property may not be sold or encumbered while litigation is ongoing. Any sale completed in breach of this order is at serious legal risk.
- **İntifa hakkı (usufruct)**: a third party has the right to use and enjoy the property, often for their lifetime. You could become the paper owner of a home you cannot occupy.
- **Kira şerhi (registered lease)**: a long-term lease that has been annotated in the registry binds any new owner for its remaining duration.
- **Önalım şerhi (pre-emption right)**: a right entitling a third party to step into your place as buyer if they exercise it within the prescribed period.
- **Beyanlar sütunu (annotations column)**: this section records matters such as building-share agreements, site management obligations, and notes about zoning or construction restrictions. Foreign buyers frequently overlook this column, yet it often contains the most practically significant information.

## How to Obtain a Takyidat Report

Any person with a legitimate interest — including a prospective buyer or their Turkish lawyer — can request a takyidat report from the local Land Registry office. The TKGM also operates an online system called WebTAKBİS, and Turkey's e-government portal (e-Devlet) allows users to query basic ownership and encumbrance information.

Online access gives you a useful first look, but interpreting older annotations in the beyanlar column or cross-checking information against court records genuinely requires legal expertise. Relying on a screenshot from e-Devlet as your sole due diligence is the kind of shortcut that leads to expensive surprises.

## The Habitation Certificate: A Separate but Critical Check

A clean takyidat report is necessary but not sufficient. An equally important document is the **yapı kullanma izin belgesi**, commonly called the **iskan** (habitation or occupancy certificate). This certificate confirms that a building has been inspected and officially declared fit for human habitation.

Under İmar Kanunu Article 30, every completed building must obtain this certificate from the relevant authority. Article 31 of the same law is even more direct: electricity, water, and sewage connections may not be provided to buildings that lack a valid iskan. Buying a property without checking this document can leave you unable to connect utilities legally — or facing significant costs to bring an illegal extension into compliance.

Many foreigners buying in coastal Antalya encounter properties where the main structure has an iskan but an added floor, a rooftop terrace, or a garden annexe does not. Ask the seller for the full iskan document, and have a lawyer verify that it covers every part of the structure you are purchasing.

## Municipal and Zoning Checks

The local municipality (belediye) holds records that the land registry does not. Before completing any purchase, it is worth checking:

- **İmar durumu belgesi (zoning certificate)**: this document confirms what the land is permitted to be used for and what construction is allowed. Zoning changes, protected area designations, or coastal setback rules in Antalya can significantly restrict your plans for the property.
- **Outstanding municipal debts**: unpaid property tax (emlak vergisi) or municipal service charges can, under certain circumstances, be pursued against the new owner. Confirm that the seller's accounts with the municipality are settled.
- **Pending enforcement orders**: if the municipality has issued a notice about an illegal structure, that obligation does not disappear when the property changes hands.

These checks require a visit or formal request to the belediye, which is another reason having a local lawyer handle due diligence is far more efficient than trying to navigate Turkish administrative offices as a foreign national.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q: Can I check a Turkish property's encumbrances online before hiring a lawyer?

Yes, partly. Turkey's e-Devlet portal and the TKGM WebTAKBİS system allow basic ownership and encumbrance queries. Use them for an initial screening, but never treat a portal printout as a complete legal opinion — older entries, court cross-references, and the beyanlar column require professional review.

### Q: If a new mortgage appears after I sign the preliminary sale contract, what are my options?

You have the right to refuse to complete the purchase or to demand that the new encumbrance is cleared before the title is transferred. Your preliminary sale contract (gayrimenkul satış vaadi sözleşmesi) should be drafted to include this protection explicitly — another reason to involve a lawyer before, not after, you sign.

### Q: Who bears the cost of clearing an existing mortgage or lien?

Clearing existing encumbrances is the seller's obligation unless your contract states otherwise. The title transfer at the Land Registry should only proceed once all agreed encumbrances have been formally released (fek).

### Q: Is there any encumbrance that would not show up in the takyidat report?

Any right that has not yet been registered will not appear. Unwritten agreements, informal usage rights, or encumbrances that a party has failed to register are not visible in the registry — which is precisely why checking the physical condition of the property and making direct municipal enquiries is equally important.
