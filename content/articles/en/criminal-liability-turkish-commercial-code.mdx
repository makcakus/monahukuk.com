TTK Art. 562-563 close out the Code's joint-stock company provisions with a graduated catalog of sanctions — administrative fines, judicial fines, and prison terms — for breaches ranging from bookkeeping failures to fraud.

## Administrative Fines for Bookkeeping and Audit Failures

Failing to keep, certify, or produce commercial books and records as required draws an administrative fine (Art. 562/1-2) — set at a periodically updated amount. Refusing to provide books or records to an authorized auditor, or obstructing an audit, is treated more seriously with a judicial fine (Art. 562/4). A key procedural point: administrative fines under this article are imposed by the local administrative authority, not a court — a distinction Turkish courts have repeatedly had to correct when cases were mistakenly resolved judicially.

## A Major 2012 Reclassification

When the Code was first adopted, failing to keep or certify commercial books was a criminal offense. Before it even took effect, a 2012 amendment (Law No. 6335) reclassified most of these bookkeeping failures from crimes into administrative infractions. This shift still generates litigation today: courts must apply the more lenient regime retroactively, which in practice often means converting a criminal fine into an administrative one, or — if the administrative limitation period has since expired — dismissing the matter with no penalty at all.

## More Serious Conduct: Fraud, Prohibited Loans, and Unlawful Solicitation

Where books are entirely missing, contain no entries, or weren't retained as required, a judicial fine applies (Art. 562/6) — a heavier consequence than merely defective bookkeeping. Forging the documents referenced in Art. 549 (incorporation, capital transactions) or knowingly falsifying commercial book entries draws one to three years' imprisonment (Art. 562/8) — among the harshest penalties in the catalog. Violating the ban on lending to shareholders or breaching board self-dealing restrictions draws a judicial fine (Art. 562/5); false capital statements draw three months to two years' imprisonment or a judicial fine (Art. 562/9); valuation fraud draws a judicial fine (Art. 562/10); and unlawfully soliciting funds from the public draws six months to two years' imprisonment (Art. 562/11).

## Practical Takeaway

Anyone assessing legacy compliance issues at a Turkish company should check the conduct date against the 2012 reform — pre-2012 bookkeeping failures may now qualify for the more lenient administrative regime, and limitation periods may have already run.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does failing to keep commercial books carry a prison sentence?

No. Conduct that was an offence in the original text of Law No. 6102 was <b>converted into a misdemeanour</b> by Law No. 6335 of 26/6/2012. Today, under Article 562/1(d), failing to keep the books properly attracts an administrative fine. Article 562/6 is the exception: where the commercial books <b>do not exist at all</b>, contain no entries, or are not kept in accordance with the Code, those responsible are punished with a <b>judicial</b> fine of not less than three hundred days.

### How much are the administrative fines in the TTK and who imposes them?

The figure written into Articles 562/1 and 562/2 is TRY 4,000, but under Article 17/7 of Misdemeanours Law No. 5326 it is increased each year, with effect from the start of the calendar year, <b>by the revaluation rate</b>; the current amount is TRY 88,997. For breach of the notification duty in Article 562/13(a) the amount is TRY 173,801. Unless otherwise provided, the fines are imposed by the <b>senior civil administrator of the locality</b> (Article 562/14) — not by a court.

### Is it an offence for the company to lend money to a shareholder or a director?

Yes, in certain circumstances. Article 358 TTK prohibits shareholders from borrowing from the company unless they have performed their due obligations arising from their capital undertaking and unless the company's profit, together with its free reserves, covers the losses of previous years. Article 395/2 prohibits a director and their relatives from borrowing cash from the company. Breach of either prohibition carries, under Article 562/5, <b>a judicial fine of not less than three hundred days</b>.
