TTK Art. 866-869 address the practical friction points that arise once a Turkish carriage contract moves from paperwork into execution: what happens when loading is incomplete, who controls the shipment mid-route, and what a carrier must do when delivery hits an obstacle.

## Incomplete Loading

Under Art. 866, a sender can force the carrier to depart even with less than the full agreed cargo loaded, but must still pay the full freight, demurrage, related costs, and post additional security if the carrier's claims become under-secured — unless the shortfall stems from the carrier's own risk sphere, in which case the carrier's claims shrink proportionally. Art. 867 mirrors this from the carrier's side: if the sender fails to load or make goods ready on time, the carrier must first warn with a reasonable grace period before terminating and claiming the Art. 865 compensation.

## The Right of Disposal

Art. 868 gives the sender broad mid-route control — ordering the carriage stopped, the goods returned, redirected, or delivered to a different consignee — subject to the carrier's right to refuse instructions that endanger its operations or other shipments, and subject to being paid for compliance (including an advance). This control passes automatically to the consignee once the goods reach the delivery point, and where a two-party-signed consignment note exists, exercising the right can require presenting it.

## Delivery Obstacles

Art. 869 requires the carrier to seek instructions from whoever holds the right of disposal when delivery becomes obstructed, falling back to the sender if the consignee cannot be found. If no instructions arrive within a reasonable time, the carrier must act in the best interest of the rights-holder — unloading and storing the goods, depositing them with a third party, returning them, or, for perishables, selling or even destroying them where storage costs would be disproportionate.

## Practical Takeaway

A foreign sender relying on the right of disposal under Art. 868 should keep the two-party-signed consignment note accessible throughout transit — without it, a mid-route instruction can be refused or delayed exactly when speed matters most.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Must the carrier set off before all the goods are loaded?

Yes. Under TTK Article 866/1 the carrier <b>must set off at the consignor's request</b> even if the whole of the goods agreed to be carried has not been loaded. This is not free of charge, however: the consignor must pay <b>the full</b> carriage charge and compensate the carrier for the loss caused by the unused space. The waiting time and additional expenses arising from the partial carriage are likewise borne by the consignor.

### May the consignor give instructions during the carriage?

Yes. Under TTK Article 868/1 the consignor may give the carrier orders and instructions for performing the carriage and may also dispose of the goods by <b>stopping the carriage, having the goods brought back, having them taken to another destination or place of delivery, or having them delivered to another consignee</b>. The carrier must comply with such instructions but may claim the resulting additional expenses and any loss. Once the goods have arrived at the place of delivery, the right of disposal passes to the consignee.

### What must the carrier do if an obstacle to delivery arises?

Under TTK Article 869/1, if before the goods reach the place where they are to be delivered it becomes clear that the carriage cannot be performed in accordance with the contract, or obstacles arise at the place of delivery, the carrier <b>must obtain instructions from the person holding the right of disposal under Article 868</b>. If instructions cannot be obtained or do not arrive in time, the carrier must take the measures that appear <b>most consistent with the interests of the person entitled</b>; it may unload the goods and deposit them with a third party and, where the conditions are met, have them sold.
