Articles 134 through 158 of the Turkish Commercial Code (TTK) regulate mergers — the process by which two or more companies combine their assets without liquidation, into a single legal entity. Because a merger directly affects the interests of shareholders and creditors of all participating companies, the Code subjects it to a detailed, largely mandatory procedure.

## Two Forms and Universal Succession

A merger can occur through absorption (one company takes over another) or through combination into a newly formed company (Art. 136). Shareholders of the absorbed company automatically receive shares of the surviving company based on an agreed exchange ratio, and — this is the critical practical point — the surviving company acquires the absorbed company's entire estate as a single legal act, with no need for separate transfer formalities, creditor consent, or debt-assignment agreements. The absorbed company then ceases to exist and is struck from the commercial registry.

## Which Companies Can Merge, and Shareholder Protections

Capital companies may generally merge with other capital companies, cooperatives, or — as the surviving entity only — with person companies; person companies may merge with capital companies or cooperatives only as the absorbed party (Art. 137). A company that is insolvent or over-indebted may only merge as the absorbed party into a financially sound company capable of covering the shortfall (Art. 139). Shareholders of the absorbed company are entitled to shares of equivalent value in the surviving company, with only a limited cash top-up (capped at 10% of the real value of the shares issued) permitted (Art. 140). Merger agreements can also offer a cash "exit payment" instead of shares — but Turkish courts have confirmed this compulsory buy-out mechanism is available only against shareholders of the absorbed company, not the surviving one.

## Approval, Registration, and Creditor Protection

Merger agreements require a qualified majority in capital companies (generally three-quarters of votes present) and unanimity in person companies (Art. 151). The merger only takes legal effect upon registration with the commercial registry — not upon signing — at which point all assets and liabilities pass automatically (Art. 153). Creditors of the merging companies who make a claim within three months of the merger's registration are entitled to have their claims secured by the surviving company (Art. 157); Turkish courts require concrete proof that performance of the claim is actually endangered before this security obligation applies. Partners of an absorbed person company remain liable for pre-merger debts for a special three-year limitation period after the merger's publication (Art. 158).

Foreign investors involved in a Turkish group restructuring should note that a simplified procedure — dispensing with a merger report, inspection rights, and shareholder approval — is available where the surviving company already owns all or substantially all shares of the absorbed company (Art. 155-156).

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How are creditors protected in a merger?

Under TTK Article 157/1 <b>if the creditors of the companies participating in the merger so request within three months of the merger taking legal effect, the acquiring company secures their claims.</b> Under Article 157/2 the companies notify their creditors of their rights by announcements made <b>three times at intervals of seven days</b> in the Turkish Trade Registry Gazette and additionally by an announcement placed on their websites. Under Article 157/4, where other creditors will not be prejudiced, the company may pay the debt instead of providing security.

### Can shareholders be paid in cash in a merger?

Yes. Under TTK Article 141/1 the companies participating in the merger may confer on the shareholders, in the merger agreement, a right to choose between acquiring shares and membership rights in the acquiring company and <b>a departure payment corresponding to the true value of the shares to be acquired</b>. Under Article 141/2 the merger agreement may also provide that only a departure payment will be given; that decision is taken by the affirmative vote of ninety per cent of the shareholders holding voting rights in the transferring company.

### Which types of company may merge with one another?

Under TTK Article 137/1 <b>capital companies may merge with capital companies, with cooperatives and — provided they are the acquiring company — with general and limited partnerships.</b> Under Article 137/2, <b>partnerships</b> may merge with partnerships, with capital companies provided they are the transferring company, and with cooperatives provided they are the transferring company. Under Article 137/3, <b>cooperatives</b> may merge with cooperatives, with capital companies and — provided they are the acquiring company — with partnerships.
