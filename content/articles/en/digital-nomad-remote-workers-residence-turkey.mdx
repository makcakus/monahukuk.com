The number of foreigners who want to live in Türkiye while working for an employer or client abroad is rising rapidly. Because Turkish legislation contains no separate residence permit category called "digital nomad", their status has to be built within the existing categories of Law No. 6458. The legal weight of the question lies less in the residence permit than on the work permit and tax side. This article addresses three questions separately: which residence permit applies, whether a work permit is required, and what follows for tax and social security.

## There Is No "Digital Nomad Residence Permit" in Türkiye

Article 31 of Law No. 6458 lists the foreigners who may be granted a short-term residence permit: those coming for scientific research, **those owning immovable property in Türkiye**, **those who will establish commercial connections or a business**, those attending in-service training programmes, those coming under student exchange programmes, **those staying for tourism purposes**, those receiving medical treatment, those required to remain by a judicial or administrative decision, those transferring from a family residence permit, those attending Turkish language courses, those attending education and internships through public institutions, those who have completed higher education in Türkiye, those making investments of a scope and amount to be determined by the President, and citizens of the Turkish Republic of Northern Cyprus.

No subparagraph in that list corresponds to remote work. In practice, remote workers use one of three routes:

- **Stay for tourism purposes (subparagraph e)** — the most common route. Short-term residence permits may be granted for periods of up to two years at a time, other than under subparagraphs (j) and (k).
- **Owners of immovable property (subparagraph b)** — for those owning a home in Türkiye. Under paragraph six, added by Law No. 7533 in 2024, **the nature and value of the property qualifying for a short-term residence permit are determined by the Ministry**; not every property therefore generates a permit automatically, and the current thresholds must be confirmed at the time of application.
- **Establishing commercial connections or a business (subparagraph c)** — for those who decide to conduct their activity through a company in Türkiye; this route is assessed together with the [entrepreneur residence permit](/en/articles/entrepreneur-residence-permit-turkey) and company formation.

In addition, the Ministry of Culture and Tourism operates a **digital nomad identification certificate** programme intended to facilitate visa and residence applications by remote workers. That certificate is not a new statutory residence permit category but an administrative facilitation instrument; because the eligibility criteria — nationality, age, education, and minimum income — are set administratively, the current conditions should be verified before applying.

## Is a Work Permit Required?

This is the most debated point in these files. International Labour Force Law No. 6735 and its Implementing Regulation cover foreigners working, undergoing vocational training, interning, or **providing cross-border services** in Türkiye, along with the natural and legal persons employing them. A work permit is, as a rule, issued for dependent work for an employer in Türkiye or for independent work.

For a foreigner working remotely for an employer abroad, with no employer, client, or workplace in Türkiye, there is no employer in whose name a work permit could be issued; in practice, therefore, no Turkish work permit is required of such persons. But two critical limits apply:

**First, the picture changes the moment services are supplied to the Turkish market.** Providing services to clients resident in Türkiye, advising a Turkish company, or invoicing from Türkiye takes the activity outside the scope of "remote work for abroad". At that point either a work permit or tax registration through a Turkish company arises.

**Second, the purpose of the residence permit must match the actual position.** Being present in Türkiye on a tourism-purpose permit while in fact carrying on continuous income-generating activity can raise a purpose-mismatch issue at renewal. As the activity becomes settled, moving to subparagraph (c) or to a company structure is the route that reduces legal risk.

## The Tax Side: The Six-Month Threshold

Tax is the issue remote workers most often overlook. Article 4 of Income Tax Law No. 193 treats as resident in Türkiye **those whose domicile is in Türkiye** and **those who reside continuously in Türkiye for more than six months within a calendar year**, and expressly states that temporary absences do not interrupt that period. A person deemed resident becomes a full taxpayer; as a rule, all income earned inside and outside Türkiye is taxed in Türkiye.

The exception in Article 5 must be read carefully here: businesspeople, scientists, and experts coming for a specific and **temporary** assignment are not deemed resident even if they stay longer than six months. But the typical digital nomad's position is not a "specific and temporary assignment" — it is an open-ended way of living, so assuming the exception applies automatically is risky. In each case the duration of the assignment, the nature of the contract, and whether a centre of living has formed in Türkiye must be assessed together.

The **double taxation treaties** to which Türkiye is a party also contain tie-breaker rules operating in sequence — permanent home, centre of vital interests, habitual abode, and nationality — where a person may be resident in both states. A stay exceeding six months therefore does not automatically mean worldwide income is taxed in Türkiye; the applicable treaty must be applied. The question of [tax numbers and tax liability](/en/articles/tax-number-bank-account-foreigners-turkey) should be kept separate from this assessment.

## Social Security

Social security follows a regime separate from tax and is generally resolved through **social security agreements**. A foreigner whose country has such an agreement with Türkiye can, by documenting insurance cover at home, be protected from a double contribution burden in Türkiye. Where no agreement exists, no compulsory affiliation to the Turkish social security system arises unless an employment relationship is actually established in Türkiye; the **valid health insurance** condition required for residence permit applications must nevertheless be satisfied separately. The [health insurance requirement](/en/articles/health-insurance-requirement-residence-permit-turkey) is a residence permit condition independent of social security affiliation.

## A Special Procedural Rule for Refusals and Cancellations

Paragraph seven, added to Article 31 by Law No. 7533 in 2024, introduces a procedure specific to short-term residence permit disputes. Under it, **in court applications against the refusal of a short-term residence permit application or the cancellation of such a permit, the file is deemed complete once the defence is filed or the time for filing it has passed**. Where notification cannot be effected at the address given by the claimant, the period in Article 26(3) of Law No. 2577 is applied as **two months**, and **whether a hearing is held is at the court's discretion**.

For remote-working foreigners, this carries a practical warning: failing to keep the Turkish notification address current during periods spent abroad exposes the case to a shortened period that can lead to it being struck out. Maintaining a Turkish address on file, and preferably a lawyer, removes that risk.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I work remotely on a tourism-purpose residence permit?

If you work for an employer abroad with no client or workplace in Türkiye, no Turkish work permit is required in practice. As the activity becomes more continuous, however, the alignment between the permit's purpose and the actual position may be questioned at renewal; for a settled arrangement, a move to subparagraph (c) or company formation should be considered.

### If I exceed 183 days in Türkiye, do I automatically become a taxpayer?

For the purposes of Article 4 of the Income Tax Law, residing continuously for more than six months within a calendar year constitutes residence, and temporary absences do not interrupt that period. But the outcome is determined together with the applicable double taxation treaty and the exceptions in Article 5; the day count alone is not decisive.

### Does the digital nomad identification certificate replace a residence permit?

No. That certificate is the product of an administrative programme designed to facilitate applications; for stays exceeding ninety days or the visa period, a residence permit must still be obtained under Article 19 of Law No. 6458.

### What changes if I provide services to a company in Türkiye?

Once the recipient of the activity moves to Türkiye, its character as "remote work for abroad" ends. Either a work permit tied to a Turkish employer, or tax registration and invoicing through a company established in Türkiye, then comes into play.
