Once a voyage has started, losing the ship is no longer a clean, zero-sum event — the carrier has already performed part of the carriage. TTK Art. 1210-1213 work out what the carrier is still owed and who protects the cargo.

## Distance Freight: An Equity-Based, Capped Calculation

Under Art. 1210, if the ship is lost after the voyage begins, the carrier is entitled to accrued claims plus "distance freight" — payable even if rescued cargo ends up landed at a different port than planned. Rather than a simple mileage ratio, the law directs a holistic, equity-based calculation weighing the quantity rescued, distance covered, costs, duration, and risks and difficulty encountered — because equal distances are rarely equally burdensome. This flexibility has a hard ceiling: distance freight can never exceed the value of the rescued cargo at the place and time it was secured.

## The Captain's Emergency Duties and the Carrier's Lien

Even where the contract lapses, Art. 1211 keeps the captain's duty to protect cargo interests alive — acting, if necessary, without prior consultation, to forward cargo by another ship, warehouse it, or sell it at a fair price, even pledging or selling part of it to cover the costs. In exchange, the captain need not release cargo until distance freight, other accrued claims, general average, and salvage amounts are paid or secured — a lien-like leverage point. Both the carrier and the shipowner are liable for damage arising from how the captain exercises these powers.

## Continuing Carriage on Another Ship, and Constructive Total Loss

Where the contract already authorizes loading or transshipment onto a different vessel, Art. 1212 lets the carrier complete carriage that way even after losing the named ship, provided the shipper is notified without delay. And under Art. 1213, a ship that becomes unseaworthy — without physically sinking — is deemed lost only by a court's formal determination, extending the same consequences as an actual loss while guarding against a carrier unilaterally declaring a merely damaged, repairable ship a total loss.

## Practical Note

Turkish courts that have touched distance freight in practice have generally resolved the underlying dispute on statute-of-limitations grounds without reaching the calculation method itself — so the exact weighting of Art. 1210's five factors remains largely untested in reported case law.
