No mandatory regime is absolute. TTK Art. 1244 carves out three narrow exceptions to the mandatory carriage rules just established, and Art. 1245 — the closing provision of the entire Freight Contract chapter — draws the line between voyage charters and the bills of lading issued under them.

## Three Exceptions to the Mandatory Floor

**Live animals and deck cargo** (Art. 1244(1)(a)): where the shipping document records that deck-carried cargo is actually carried on deck, or the shipment is livestock, the mandatory floor does not apply — both carry materially different, foreseeable risk profiles that justify letting the parties allocate risk on their own terms.

**Genuinely special, non-negotiable shipments** (Art. 1244(1)(b)): unusual cargo or unusual shipping conditions can justify a bespoke agreement outside the mandatory floor, but only if the document both records the special terms *and* is marked "not negotiable" — a double safeguard ensuring transparency and, critically, preventing an unsuspecting downstream holder from ever being bound by terms they never agreed to.

**Pre-loading and post-discharge duties** (Art. 1244(1)(c)): the mandatory regime is calibrated to the period the carrier has the cargo under its control, so obligations outside that window fall outside the mandatory floor too.

General average clauses remain unaffected regardless (Art. 1244(2)), and the Turkish Code of Obligations' own mandatory rules on exclusion clauses remain fully in force alongside this regime (Art. 1244(3)).

## Voyage Charters vs. Bills of Lading Issued Under Them

Art. 1245(1) exempts voyage charter parties from the mandatory regime entirely — sophisticated commercial parties who negotiated their own bargain do not need the protection designed for downstream strangers. But if a bill of lading is issued under that charter and passed on, the mandatory regime springs back to life for the relationship between the carrier and any holder who was *not* the original charterer. The exemption tracks precisely who actually needs protection: charter parties, not later holders who never negotiated anything.

## Why This Matters

For a buyer who ends up holding a bill referencing a voyage charter, whether an arbitration or jurisdiction clause in that charter binds them often turns on whether they were the original charterer (bound directly) or a later, non-charterer holder (protected, and requiring actual production of the charter under Art. 1237(3)). Turkish courts apply this distinction precisely — enforcing charter arbitration clauses against parties who were themselves the charterer, while requiring proof of production against everyone else.
