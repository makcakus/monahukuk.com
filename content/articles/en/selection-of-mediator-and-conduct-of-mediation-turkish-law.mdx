*Article 14 – (1) Unless another procedure is agreed, the mediator or mediators are selected by the parties.*

*Article 15 – (1) After being selected, the mediator invites the parties to the first meeting as soon as possible.*

*(2) The parties may freely agree the mediation procedure, provided it does not conflict with mandatory rules of law.*

*(4) Acts which, by their nature, may be performed only by a judge as the exercise of a judicial power cannot be performed by the mediator.*

*(5) Where, after an action has been brought, the parties declare that they will jointly apply to a mediator, the proceedings are stayed by the court for a period not exceeding three months. That period may be extended by up to three months upon the parties' joint application.*

*(6) (Amended: 12/10/2017-7036/22) The parties may attend the mediation negotiations in person, through their statutory representatives or through their lawyers. Expert persons who may contribute to resolving the dispute may also be kept present at the negotiations.*

*(8) (Added: 12/10/2017-7036/22) In mediation negotiations, a public administration is represented by a commission composed of two members determined by the senior manager and the head of the legal unit or a lawyer or legal adviser designated by them.*

Together, these two articles answer the questions of who conducts the mediation process and how. The Law's preference is plainly for flexibility: the parties select the mediator, the parties determine the procedure and, failing that, the mediator exercises discretion. The only limit on that flexibility is the monopoly of judicial power in Article 15/4.

## Selection of the Mediator: Two Different Regimes

Article 14 lays down a short and clear rule for voluntary mediation: unless another procedure is agreed, the mediator or mediators are selected by the parties. The parties may appoint more than one mediator, leave the selection to an institution, or determine the selection procedure in advance by contract.

In mediation as a condition of action, the selection regime is reversed. Under Article 18/A/5, the mediator is designated **by the bureau** from the list notified to the commission presidencies; but where the parties agree on any mediator appearing on the list, that mediator is appointed. The parties' will therefore prevails here too, but it cannot go outside the list.

In practice this distinction has become a test for review. The 9th Civil Chamber of the Court of Cassation, assessing together the letters received from the mediation bureau and from the Mediation Department, found that the employees had themselves selected the mediator, had attended the sessions in person and had exercised their will as to the mediator's selection. The ability to prove the exercise of that will through official records is decisive, particularly in reviewing mediation processes organised on a collective basis.

## The Invitation to the First Meeting and Time Pressure

Article 15/1 imposes on the mediator the duty to invite the parties to the first meeting **as soon as possible** after being selected. In voluntary mediation this phrase contains no time limit; in mediation as a condition of action, however, Article 18/A/9 sets a strict calendar: the mediator concludes the application within **three weeks** of the date of appointment, and this period may be extended by a maximum of **one week** in compulsory circumstances.

This four-week ceiling is the essential basis of the compatibility of mandatory mediation with the freedom to seek justice. Exceeding the period does not invalidate the outcome; the period is directory, and even where it is exceeded the final minutes drawn up satisfy the condition of action.

## Free Determination of the Procedure

Article 15/2 allows the parties to agree the mediation procedure freely, provided this does not conflict with mandatory rules of law. The parties may determine the number of meetings, the venue, whether the process will be conducted online, whether private sessions will be held and who will be present.

The mandatory rules that form the limit of that freedom are: confidentiality (Art. 4), the evidentiary bar (Art. 5), impartiality and performance in person (Art. 9), the duty to inform (Art. 11), the right to withdraw from the process (Art. 3/1) and the provisions of Article 18/A on mediation as a condition of action. The parties cannot set these rules aside by agreement.

Where the parties have not agreed a procedure, Article 15/3 applies: the mediator conducts the activity having regard to the nature of the dispute, the parties' wishes and the procedure and principles required for a swift resolution of the dispute.

## The Limit of Judicial Power

Article 15/4 draws the outer boundary of the mediator's powers: acts which, by their nature, may be performed only by a judge as the exercise of a judicial power cannot be performed by the mediator.

The concrete manifestations of this limit are as follows. The mediator cannot collect evidence, or evaluate evidence and establish facts; cannot appoint a court expert (an expert person may under Article 15/6 only be kept present at the negotiations and does not draw up a report in the capacity of court expert); cannot order an interim injunction or interim attachment; cannot compel a party into the process or alter a party's capacity. Indeed, in one dispute it was held that there is no compulsory joinder among employers in employee-claim disputes, that the employee may go to mediation against whomever they choose, and that the mediator is not obliged to accept a request to add a party.

The exception to this limit consists of the powers expressly granted to the mediator by statute. Under Article 17/B/2, in disputes concerning the transfer of immovable property or the creation of a limited right in rem over it, where the parties so agree in writing and the mediator records this in the minutes, an annotation that the power of disposal is restricted is entered in the land registry at the mediator's request — an exceptional power conferred by law.

## Mediation During Proceedings: The Three-Month Stay

Article 15/5 provides that, where in a pending action the parties declare that they will jointly apply to a mediator, the proceedings are **stayed by the court for a period not exceeding three months**; the period may be extended by up to three months upon the parties' joint application. A stay exceeding six months in total is not possible.

The stay has two conditions: the declaration must be made **jointly** (one party's request is insufficient) and it must be addressed to the court. Under Article 16/1, in mediation after an action has been brought, the process begins to run from the date on which the court's invitation is accepted by the parties, or the parties declare in writing to the court outside a hearing that they have agreed to apply to a mediator, or those declarations are recorded in the minutes at a hearing.

## Who May Attend the Negotiations

Article 15/6 took its present form with the 2017 amendment: the parties may attend the negotiations **in person**, through their statutory representatives or through their lawyers; expert persons who may contribute to resolving the dispute may also be kept present.

The word "through" in the provision shows that the party's personal attendance is not compulsory; the lawyer's participation suffices. However, a sentence added to Article 18/A/7 in 2023 introduced a safeguard: the mediator **also informs the party themselves, even where they have a lawyer**. The participation of expert persons does not make them parties to the negotiation; they too are bound by confidentiality under Article 4/2.

Article 15/7 provides that, where it emerges that the parties cannot produce a solution, the mediator may make a proposal for settlement. The proposal is not binding and its rejection carries no sanction.

## Representation of Public Administrations and Protection of Commission Members

Where the other side is a public administration within the meaning of Article 2/e, the negotiations are conducted under Article 15/8 by a special **commission**: two members determined by the senior manager and the head of the legal unit or a lawyer or legal adviser designated by them. At the end of the negotiations the commission draws up a reasoned report and keeps it for five years.

Article 15/9 protects the commission members: actions for damages to be brought on account of the acts they perform and the decisions they take within the scope of the mediation activity may be brought **only against the State**; the State has recourse, within one year of the date of payment, against members who abuse their office by acting contrary to the requirements of their duties. The purpose of the rule is to prevent public officials from refusing a reasonable settlement out of concern for personal liability.

## Case Law of the Court of Cassation

The following judgments show the practice concerning the selection of the mediator and the conduct of the activity.

**1. Court of Cassation, 9th Civil Chamber, Case No. 2024/7293, Decision No. 2024/9150, dated 27.05.2024**

Assessing together the letters received from the mediation bureau and the Mediation Department, the Chamber found that the claimant and the comparator employees had themselves selected the mediator, had attended the sessions in person, and that the claimant had exercised their will as to the mediator's selection.

**2. Court of Cassation, 9th Civil Chamber, Case No. 2022/7271, Decision No. 2022/8056, dated 20.06.2022**

In the dispute, the invalidity of the final minutes was asserted after the mediator refused a request to include a third company in the process. The action was dismissed on the grounds that there is no compulsory joinder among employers in employee-claim disputes, that the employee may resort to mediation against whomever they choose, and that the mediator is under no obligation to accept a request to add a party.

**3. Court of Cassation, 3rd Civil Chamber, Case No. 2025/5452, Decision No. 2026/129, dated 15.01.2026**

Emphasising that in mediation it is the parties who produce the solution and that the mediator gives no judicial decision, the Chamber set out the fundamental distinction between mediation and arbitration, and accepted that the parties may agree the two methods consecutively in a dispute-resolution agreement.

**4. Court of Cassation, 9th Civil Chamber, Case No. 2022/5527, Decision No. 2022/6035, dated 12.05.2022**

The dispute concerns liability for the mediation fee, attorney's fees and litigation costs in a file to which a public administration was a party. The decision is illustrative of the financial consequences of the administration's position in the mediation process.

**5. Court of Cassation, 9th Civil Chamber, Case No. 2022/3398, Decision No. 2022/5294, dated 26.04.2022**

The Chamber stated that the obligation to inform the parties of the appointment and to invite them to the first meeting after the mediator's selection lies with the mediator, and that no fault can be attributed, on account of a defect in the invitation procedure, to a party who has made a proper application.
