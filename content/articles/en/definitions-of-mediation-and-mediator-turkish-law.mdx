*Article 2 – (1) In the application of this Law:*

*a) Mediator means the natural person who conducts the mediation activity and is entered in the mediator registry kept by the Ministry;*

*b) Mediation means the dispute resolution method conducted voluntarily and with the participation of an impartial and independent third person who has received specialist training, who applies systematic techniques, brings the parties together for the purpose of meeting and negotiating, establishes a communication process between them so that they understand each other and thereby produce their own solutions, and who may also put forward a settlement proposal if it emerges that the parties cannot produce a solution;*

*e) (Added: 12/10/2017-7036/17) Administration means the administrations and institutions listed in schedules (I), (II), (III) and (IV) annexed to Law No. 5018 on Public Financial Management and Control, the local administrations defined in Law No. 5018 and the enterprises established by them, other public institutions, boards, supreme boards and organisations established by special statute, state economic enterprises together with their subsidiaries, establishments and enterprises, and other companies more than fifty per cent of whose capital belongs to the public.*

Definition provisions are often read as a technical formality; Article 2 of Law No. 6325 is not like that. The three definitions here — mediator, mediation and administration — produce direct consequences for validity. A process conducted by a person not entered in the registry is not "mediation"; a document drawn up at the end of a meeting lacking the elements of Article 2(b) cannot benefit from the character of a court judgment conferred by Article 18, however it is labelled; and if the other party is an administration within the meaning of Article 2(e), who sits at the negotiating table and how the decision is taken is subject to a special regime.

## The Mediator: Only a Registered Natural Person

Article 2(a) ties the mediator to two criteria: actually conducting the activity, and being entered in the mediator registry kept by the Ministry of Justice. The words "natural person" in the definition are deliberate — a law firm, company or association cannot be a mediator; mediation is performed personally (Article 9/1). The conditions of registration are listed in Article 20: Turkish citizenship, a law degree with at least five years' seniority in the profession, full legal capacity, no conviction for the listed offences, no connection or association with terrorist organisations, and completion of mediation training with success in the written examination. Those with twenty years' seniority in their profession are exempt from the examination. A mediator cannot begin practising before the date of registration (Article 20/3).

Supervision of this registration requirement has also been the subject of case law of the Council of State. The 10th Chamber has accepted that whether a mediation examination is held and how many mediators are to be admitted lies within the discretion of the Ministry of Justice, having regard to criteria such as the number of mediators currently serving, the volume of applications and the widening scope of mandatory mediation. In this respect the registry is not a register of entry into a liberal profession but a list for a public service whose size is planned by the administration.

## The Elements of Mediation: Five Criteria Inside the Definition

The long definition in Article 2(b) in fact brings together five distinct elements, each of which corresponds to a question of validity in practice.

**The application of systematic techniques and the bringing together of the parties.** Mediation is a structured activity in which the parties are seated at the table and a communication process is established. Documents drawn up merely to obtain a signature, without the parties ever being brought together, do not satisfy this element.

**The solution is produced by the parties.** The mediator does not decide and cannot compel a settlement. In this respect mediation differs from arbitration, where the arbitrator renders a binding award, and from litigation, where the judge gives judgment.

**The power to put forward a settlement proposal.** The definition allows the mediator to make a proposal "if it emerges that the parties cannot produce a solution"; Article 15/7 regulates this separately. The proposal is not binding and its rejection produces no adverse consequence.

**An impartial and independent third person with specialist training.** Impartiality and independence turn into concrete obligations in Article 9: the mediator must disclose circumstances that may cast doubt on their impartiality and may not later act as counsel for one of the parties in the same dispute.

**Voluntariness.** The phrase "conducted voluntarily" in the definition is a trace of the original 2012 design. Today it must be read together with the sentence added to Article 3/1 in 2018: "the provision of Article 18/A on mediation as a condition precedent is reserved". **Applying** has become compulsory in many disputes, but **settling** is never compulsory. Voluntariness survives as regards the outcome of the process.

## The Mediator's Disclosure of Capacity and the Review of the Definition

The abstract elements of the definition are reviewed in practice through the mediator's concrete conduct. The 9th Civil Chamber of the Turkish Court of Cassation held, in a case where there was no document at all showing that the parties had been invited to the session, where the minutes had the character of a release, and where the mediator had never explained to the other party that they were a mediator or what the legal consequences of mediation were, that this was incompatible with the mediator's independent and impartial position. Article 6/2, imposing on the mediator the duty to state their title while acting, and Article 11, providing for the duty to inform the parties about the essentials, process and consequences, form the normative basis of that review.

## The Definition of "Administration" and Representation of the Public Side

The definition of administration in Article 2(e) was added in 2017 by Law No. 7036 and is broad: general and special budget administrations, regulatory and supervisory institutions, social security institutions, local administrations and the enterprises they establish, institutions and supreme boards established by special statute, state economic enterprises together with their subsidiaries, and companies more than fifty per cent of whose capital belongs to the public all fall within it.

That definition has no meaning on its own; the operative provision is Article 15/8. Under it, in mediation negotiations the administration is represented by a **commission** composed of two members designated by the senior manager together with the head of the legal unit or a lawyer or legal adviser designated by them. At the end of the negotiations the commission draws up a reasoned report and keeps it for five years. Article 15/9 provides a critical safeguard for public officials: actions for damages brought in respect of acts done and decisions taken by commission members within the scope of the mediation activity may be brought only against the State; the State has recourse against a member who abuses their office within one year of the date of payment. The purpose of this rule is clear — to prevent a public official from rejecting a reasonable settlement offer out of fear of personal liability.

In a mediation conducted with a municipal subsidiary or a state economic enterprise, whether the other side comes to the table with a properly constituted commission is therefore a matter to be monitored; settlements bound by the signature of a single official without a commission decision are later exposed to arguments about authority.

## Decisions of the Court of Cassation and the Council of State

The following decisions show how the definitions in Article 2 operate in practice.

**1. Court of Cassation, 3rd Civil Chamber, File No. 2025/5452, Decision No. 2026/129, 15.01.2026**

Placing mediation among the alternative dispute resolution methods, the Chamber set out the criterion distinguishing it from arbitration: in mediation the parties produce the solution, whereas in arbitration the award is binding like a court judgment. The decision stated that providing in the same contract first for mediation and then for arbitration ("med-then-arb") is possible, and that the two processes continue to exist as independent routes without merging into one another.

**2. Court of Cassation, 9th Civil Chamber, File No. 2024/8846, Decision No. 2024/11005, 09.09.2024**

In the dispute, no document at all was produced showing that the parties had been invited to the session, the minutes had the character of a release, and witness statements showed that the mediator had not introduced themselves as a mediator or explained the legal consequences of the process. The Chamber held that this conduct was incompatible with the mediator's independent and impartial position.

**3. Court of Cassation, 9th Civil Chamber, File No. 2022/7271, Decision No. 2022/8056, 20.06.2022**

The decision concerns a dispute in which the invalidity of the final minutes of a mandatory mediation was asserted on the ground that the mediator had exercised their duties and powers contrary to the legislation. It establishes that the mediator's powers are limited by the Law and by the regulations of the Department of Mediation, and that exceeding those limits may affect the fate of the minutes.

**4. Council of State, Assembly of Administrative Law Chambers, File No. 2023/1830, Decision No. 2024/1165, 23.05.2024**

The Assembly rejected, on the basis of Articles 30 and 32 of Law No. 6325, the argument that the Mediation Board had not been empowered to lay down the Turkish Mediators' Code of Ethics. According to the decision, the Department is charged with ensuring that mediation services are conducted in an orderly and efficient manner and the Board with determining the professional rules of mediation; the code of ethics is an exercise of that power.

**5. Council of State, 10th Chamber, File No. 2020/2366, Decision No. 2021/6689, 22.12.2021**

The Chamber stated that completion of mediation training and success in the written examination are statutory conditions of registration in the mediator registry, that determining the basic principles and standards of training and examination is the responsibility of the Mediation Board, and that determining the number of mediators required lies within the administration's discretion.
