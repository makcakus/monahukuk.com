TTK Art. 335-352 set out how a Turkish joint-stock company actually comes into being — the founding act, required documents, who counts as a founder, the mandatory bylaw content, in-kind capital rules, and payment of share capital. The 2012 Code significantly simplified the old multi-step incorporation process used under the prior 1956 Code.

## The Founding Act and Founders

A company is formed when founders unconditionally commit, in bylaws meeting statutory form (notarized signatures or execution before the trade registry), to pay in the full share capital (Art. 335). "Founder" simply means anyone who subscribes shares and signs the bylaws — including a person acting for an undisclosed third party's account, who then shares founder liability alongside that principal (Art. 337). Notably, a single founder is now sufficient — the old five-founder minimum was abolished (Art. 338).

## Mandatory Bylaw Content and the Mandatory-Provisions Principle

Art. 339 lists eleven mandatory elements bylaws must address, from the trade name and registered office to share structure, in-kind contributions, founder benefits, board composition, and financial year. A distinctive rule under Art. 340 — absent from the old Code — is that bylaws may only deviate from the Code's joint-stock provisions where the law expressly permits it; this "mandatory provisions" principle significantly narrows contractual freedom compared to the pre-2012 regime.

## In-Kind Capital and Payment of Cash Contributions

Assets contributed as in-kind capital must be free of liens and attachments, cash-valuable, and transferable; personal services, labor, goodwill, and not-yet-due receivables cannot be contributed as capital (Art. 342). A court-appointed expert must value any in-kind contribution or business being taken over, in a report open to challenge by founders and interested parties (Art. 343). For cash contributions, at least 25% of a share's nominal value must be paid before registration, with the balance due within 24 months of registration; share premiums must be paid in full upfront (Art. 344). These funds sit in a dedicated bank account usable only by the company once it acquires legal personality (Art. 345).

## Founder Benefits

Founders cannot be given cash or free shares as a reward for their efforts — anything that would reduce company capital — though up to one-tenth of distributable profit, after statutory reserves and a 5% shareholder dividend, may be paid to founders via "founder profit certificates" (Art. 348). Turkish courts treat this founder-certificate relationship as contractual rather than a shareholding, meaning it cannot be unilaterally stripped away by a later shareholders' resolution.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much of the capital is paid up on incorporation of a joint stock company?

Under TTK Article 344/1, <b>at least twenty-five per cent</b> of the nominal value of shares subscribed for in cash is paid <b>before registration</b> and the remainder <b>within twenty-four months</b> following the company's registration. No such instalment facility exists for contributions in kind; a contribution in kind passes to the company upon registration. The time and conditions for payment of share issue premiums and of the price of shares issued at a premium are fixed in the articles of association or determined by the board of directors.

### Can a joint stock company be formed by a single person?

Yes. Under TTK Article 338/1 the formation of a joint stock company requires <b>one or more</b> founders who are shareholders. A single-shareholder joint stock company is possible; however, where the company is formed with a single shareholder or later becomes such, that fact, together with its cause, must be notified in writing to the board of directors within seven days, and the board must have it registered and announced within seven days of receiving the notification (Article 338/2).

### What may be contributed as a contribution in kind?

Under TTK Article 342/1, assets that are free of any limited right in rem, attachment or injunction and that <b>can be valued in money and transferred</b> — including intellectual property rights and virtual environments — may be contributed in kind. By contrast, service performances, personal labour, commercial reputation and claims that are not yet due cannot be contributed as capital. Contributions in kind are valued by experts appointed by the commercial court of first instance at the place where the company is to have its seat (Article 343).
