TTK Art. 766-775 close out the chapter on bills of exchange with Turkey's private-international-law regime for cross-border bills — parties, signatures, and payment can each sit in different countries, so the Code assigns each legal question its own governing law. These rules apply only to the bill itself, not to the underlying commercial relationship, which is governed by Turkey's general conflict-of-laws statute (MÖHUK).

## Capacity

Art. 766 ties a person's capacity to incur bill obligations to their home-country law (with renvoi to a third country's law where the home law so directs). A key protective exception: someone lacking capacity under their home law is nonetheless validly bound if they had capacity under the law of the place where they signed — a lex loci actus rule sparing a holder from investigating a signer's foreign capacity rules.

## Form and Time Limits

Each bill-related undertaking (drawing, acceptance, endorsement, aval) is governed by the law of the country where it was signed (Art. 767) — echoing the "independence of signatures" principle in conflict-of-laws terms. A defect in one signature's form under its own law does not taint a later, validly formed signature under a different law. A Turkish citizen signing abroad is bound as against another Turk in Turkey if the signature met Turkish formal requirements, regardless of local rules. Protest form and timing follow the law of the place where the protest is made (Art. 768), while the time limits for exercising recourse rights are unified across all obligors under the law of the place of issuance (Art. 769) — avoiding a fragmented set of recourse deadlines for the same instrument.

## Effects of Obligations

The consequences of the acceptor's undertaking follow the law of the place of payment (Art. 770/1); every other obligor's undertaking follows the law of wherever they signed (Art. 770/2). Whether acceptance can be partial, and whether the holder must accept partial payment, is likewise governed by the place-of-payment law (Art. 771), as are maturity calculations and foreign-currency payment (Art. 772).

## Unjust Enrichment, Underlying Claim, and Cancellation

Unjust-enrichment claims against the drawee, a third-party payer, or the party for whose account the bill was drawn follow the law of that party's domicile (Art. 773). Whether the holder acquires the underlying claim that gave rise to the bill follows the law of the place of issuance (Art. 774). Measures for a lost or stolen bill follow the law of the place of payment (Art. 775).

## Practical Takeaway

Foreign lawyers and businesses handling a Turkish-connected bill should identify the governing law issue-by-issue rather than assuming one law applies throughout — Turkish courts have required expert reports on foreign law before ruling on a bill's validity in cross-border disputes, most often involving notes drafted in a foreign language.
