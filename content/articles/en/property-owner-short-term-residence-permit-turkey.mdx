For many foreign nationals who have bought an apartment in Antalya, a villa along the Aegean coast, or a flat in Istanbul, the next natural step is converting that ownership into the right to live in Turkey on a lawful basis. The **property-based short-term residence permit** is the legal mechanism that makes this possible — and since late 2024, the rules governing it have changed in important ways.

## Your Right to a Residence Permit as a Property Owner

Under the Foreigners and International Protection Law (YUKK), foreign nationals who own real estate in Turkey are explicitly entitled to apply for a [short-term residence permit](/en/articles/short-term-residence-permit-turkey-guide). This right is established under Article 31 of the YUKK alongside other grounds such as scientific research, tourism, and business connections. A permit granted on this basis can be renewed for up to two years at a time, making it a practical solution for those who want to split their time between Turkey and their home country.

The permit does not automatically confer work authorisation. It simply allows the holder to reside lawfully in Turkey for the duration of the permit. Foreign nationals wishing to work in Turkey need a separate work permit, regardless of property ownership.

## What Changed in November 2024

An important amendment to Article 31 of the YUKK came into force in November 2024. The new provision — Article 31, paragraph 6 — gives the Ministry of Interior the authority to set minimum standards for the **type and value** of the property that can serve as the basis for a short-term residence permit. In practice, this means that not every property purchase will qualify: properties that fall below the standards set by the Ministry may no longer support a residence permit application.

This change was introduced to address situations where very low-value or legally problematic properties were being used primarily as a procedural step to obtain residence status, rather than as genuine investments. The Ministry's implementing criteria can also be updated over time.

Before [purchasing property in Turkey](/en/articles/buying-property-in-turkey-foreigner-guide) with the specific intention of using it as a basis for a residence permit, consult an immigration lawyer to confirm that the property meets the current requirements.

## General Conditions for the Application

Owning qualifying property is a necessary but not sufficient condition. Article 32 of the YUKK also requires applicants to:

- State a legitimate reason for the permit corresponding to a listed ground (property ownership in this case) and submit supporting documentation
- Not fall within the prohibited categories under Article 7 of the YUKK — for example, those who pose a threat to public order or security
- Have accommodation that meets general health and safety standards
- Provide accurate address information in Turkey for the duration of the stay

If criminal records are requested, applicants may also need to submit an official police clearance from their country of nationality or habitual residence.

## Common Reasons for Rejection

Even with a valid title deed (tapu), property-based residence permit applications can be refused. Practical experience in Antalya and other areas popular with foreign buyers points to several recurring issues:

- **Property value below current Ministry thresholds**: Since the November 2024 amendment, properties that do not meet the minimum standards set by the Ministry may disqualify the applicant.
- **High foreign population in the requested district**: Migration authorities retain the power to manage overall foreign population concentration in specific districts. In neighbourhoods where the foreign population is already high, applications may be subject to additional scrutiny or administrative restrictions.
- **Documentation deficiencies**: Missing or expired documents — outdated tapu extracts, absent health insurance, or incomplete address records — are a frequent cause of refusals that can be corrected on appeal.
- **Article 7 exclusions**: A prior removal order, entry ban, or a record flagged by public security authorities will result in denial regardless of the property's value.

## Appealing a Rejected Application

The same 2024 amendment that introduced property value requirements also clarified the appeal procedure under Article 31, paragraph 7 of the YUKK. When a property-based short-term residence permit is refused or cancelled, the applicant or their lawyer may challenge the decision before an administrative court. The court file is considered complete once the administration files its defence or the defence deadline lapses — which keeps the process moving without unnecessary delays. If the applicant cannot be reached at the address on file, a two-month extension applies under the relevant procedural rules.

In Antalya, rejection challenges are typically filed with the Antalya Administrative Court. Acting promptly matters because administrative deadlines are short. An experienced immigration lawyer can assess the grounds for the refusal, file the necessary documents, request a stay of execution if needed, and see the case through to judgment. For a general overview of the challenge process, see our guide on [residence permit refusal appeals in Turkey](/en/articles/residence-permit-refusal-appeal-turkey).

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q: How long does a property-based residence permit last in Turkey?
Permits granted on the basis of property ownership are issued for up to two years at a time under Article 31 of the YUKK. You can renew before the permit expires, provided the property still qualifies and other conditions are met.

### Q: Do I need to actually live in the property to keep the permit?
You must register the property as your address in Turkey and meet general accommodation standards. Spending extended periods outside Turkey can affect your renewal, though short absences are generally accepted in practice.

### Q: Can my family members obtain permits based on my property?
Children and spouses of property owners do not automatically receive property-based permits. They would typically need to apply for a family residence permit or qualify independently under another ground.

### Q: What happens to my residence permit if I sell the property?
Selling the property removes the underlying basis for your permit. You should either transition your permit to another qualifying ground before selling, or be prepared for the permit to be reviewed upon the sale.

### Q: Can the permit be denied even if I have a valid tapu?
Yes. A valid tapu (title deed) establishes your ownership but does not guarantee a residence permit. The property must also meet the Ministry's value and quality standards, and you must not fall within any of the exclusions discussed above.
