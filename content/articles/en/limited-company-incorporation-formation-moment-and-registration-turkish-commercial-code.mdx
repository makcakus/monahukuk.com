TTK Art. 585-588 govern how a Turkish limited company comes into existence — and the critical gap between signing the articles and acquiring legal personality.

## No 25% Upfront Payment Required

Art. 585 marks formation at the moment founders sign the articles of association before authorized trade registry personnel, unconditionally committing to pay the full capital. Joint-stock company rules on payment, timing, and default apply by analogy — with one important 2018 carve-out: unlike joint-stock companies, limited companies are not required to pay 25% of cash capital commitments before registration. This removed a significant upfront cash burden and streamlined incorporation.

## Filing for Registration

After signing the articles per Art. 575, the application for registration goes to the trade registry at the company's registered office, signed by all managers, with the articles and proof of the representative/auditor selection attached (Art. 586). The application must state each partner's identity, their committed capital share and amount paid, the managers' identities, and how the company will be represented.

## The 30-Day Registration Window

Art. 587 gives founders thirty days from signing to register and publish the articles in the Trade Registry Gazette — double the general fifteen-day filing period, reflecting that limited company incorporation documents can take longer to assemble.

## Legal Personality and Pre-Registration Liability

Art. 588 is one of the most heavily litigated provisions here: the company only acquires legal personality upon registration. Turkish courts apply this symmetrically at the other end of a company's life too — a deregistered company loses its capacity to sue or be sued, and litigation involving it must be paused pending its formal revival.

Anyone acting on the company's behalf before registration is personally and jointly liable for those transactions (Art. 588/3) — unless the transaction explicitly stated it was made "on behalf of a company to be formed" and the company formally ratifies it within three months of registration (Art. 588/4). Turkish courts treat both conditions — the explicit statement and timely ratification — as indispensable; without them, personal liability persists.

## Practical Takeaway

Entrepreneurs signing contracts before a Turkish limited company is registered should explicitly flag the transaction as being for a company "to be formed" and ensure the company formally adopts it within three months — otherwise the individual signer remains personally on the hook.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Must twenty-five per cent of the capital be paid up in advance in a limited liability company?

No. Under the sentence added to TTK Article 585/1 by Law No. 7099 of 15/2/2018, <b>the requirement that at least twenty-five per cent of the nominal value of shares subscribed for in cash be paid before registration does not apply to limited liability companies.</b> That requirement applies to joint stock companies under Article 344/1; in a limited liability company the whole of the capital may be paid within twenty-four months following registration.

### When is a limited liability company deemed formed?

There are two stages. Under TTK Article 585/1 the company <b>is formed</b> when the founders unconditionally undertake to pay the whole of the capital and declare their intention to form a limited liability company in the articles of association that they <b>sign before authorised staff at the trade registry directorate</b>. However, under Article 588/1 the company <b>acquires legal personality upon registration with the trade registry</b>. The moment of formation and the birth of legal personality are different moments.

### Is there a time limit for registration?

Yes. Under TTK Article 587/1 <b>the whole of the articles of association is registered with the trade registry of the place of the company's seat and announced in the Turkish Trade Registry Gazette within thirty days following the date on which the founders' signatures were affixed before authorised staff at the trade registry directorate.</b> Under Article 588/2, if they are not accepted by the company, <b>the incorporation expenses are borne by the founders</b>.
