TTK Art. 1163-1165 close out the loading chapter of Turkish maritime law by covering the "general cargo" (kırkambar) contract — where a carrier commits to carry a specific, individually identified cargo rather than chartering out ship capacity — and a document rule that applies to every type of freight contract.

## Loading on Call, Not on a Schedule

Unlike voyage charters, which run on notice-of-readiness and laytime clocks, a general cargo contract under Art. 1163 simply obliges the shipper to load without delay once the carrier calls for it. If the shipper is late, the carrier owes no duty to wait: the voyage may begin without the cargo ever being received, and the shipper still owes full freight (offset only by whatever freight the carrier earned from substitute cargo). Critically, the carrier keeps this full-freight claim only if it gave the shipper written notice — fax, email, or similar — before sailing; missing that notice forfeits the claim entirely.

## Cancelling After Loading, and the Cross-Consent Rule

Once cargo is aboard, Art. 1164 lets the shipper cancel by paying full freight plus any secured claims (or posting security) — mirroring the exit right voyage charterers have under Art. 1158. But if discharging that cargo would delay the voyage or force a port call, the shipper needs every other shipper's consent first, and the carrier is never obliged to divert or call at another port to accommodate the cancellation.

## Documents Owed in Every Freight Contract

Art. 1165 closes the loading section with a rule that applies regardless of contract type: the shipper and loader must hand the carrier every document the cargo needs (customs declarations, certificates of origin, phytosanitary papers, hazardous-goods documentation, export licenses, and the like) by the time the cargo is due for collection. Liability for defects in these documents — particularly false statements — follows the same regime as inaccurate cargo declarations under Art. 1145: direct liability toward the carrier, fault-based liability toward other affected parties.

## Practical Takeaway

For carriers taking on general cargo, the written pre-sailing notice under Art. 1163/3 is not optional paperwork — skip it and the full-freight claim against a no-show shipper disappears. For shippers and loaders, incomplete or inaccurate accompanying documents carry the same liability exposure as misdescribing the cargo itself, and can mean customs holds, fines, or a cargo that never clears the destination port.
