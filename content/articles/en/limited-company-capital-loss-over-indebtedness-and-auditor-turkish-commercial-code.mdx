TTK Art. 633-635 close the "Company Organs" chapter by addressing what happens when a Turkish limited company faces financial distress, and how it is audited.

## Capital Loss and Over-Indebtedness

Rather than create a separate regime, Art. 633 imports the joint-stock company early-warning framework wholesale (Art. 376). Under a three-tier system: if half of capital plus statutory reserves has been eroded by losses, managers must call the general assembly to propose remedial measures; if two-thirds is eroded and the assembly does not vote to reduce capital to the remaining third or replenish it, the company dissolves automatically; and if over-indebtedness is suspected, managers must prepare an interim balance sheet at liquidation values and, if assets fall short of liabilities, notify the court and request bankruptcy. This notification duty is also listed among managers' non-delegable duties under Art. 625. Importantly, the additional-payment mechanism under Art. 603 remains available before bankruptcy is pursued — a safeguard specific to limited companies. Turkish criminal courts have applied this same framework directly when assessing the crime of failing to file for bankruptcy under Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law Art. 345/a.

## Bankruptcy Notification and Concordat

Following a 2018 reform that abolished the "postponement of bankruptcy" institution, Art. 634 now applies the joint-stock company rules to both bankruptcy notification and concordat requests for limited companies — meaning an over-indebted limited company can pursue a court-supervised restructuring (concordat) rather than immediate bankruptcy, provided it can show a credible recovery plan.

## Auditing

Art. 635 applies the general and special audit provisions for joint-stock companies (with narrow exceptions for publicly-held company rules) to limited companies as well — including the special-auditor mechanism a partner can invoke when information rights are being obstructed. Turkish courts remain divided on whether a court's rejection of a special-auditor request is final and unappealable, though the majority view currently treats it as such.

## Practical Takeaway

Directors and managers of a Turkish limited company subsidiary should treat the early-warning capital-loss thresholds as mandatory trigger points requiring immediate board action — failure to notify the court of over-indebtedness in time exposes managers to both civil liability and, in serious cases, criminal exposure under bankruptcy law.
