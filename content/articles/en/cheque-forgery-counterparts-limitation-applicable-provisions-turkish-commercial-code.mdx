TTK Art. 812-818 close the domestic cheque regime with rules on forged instruments, multi-jurisdiction counterparts, limitation, and — in Art. 818 — a detailed map of which bill-of-exchange provisions carry over to cheques.

## Forged or Altered Cheques: Strict Bank Liability

Art. 812 places the loss from paying a forged or altered cheque on the drawee bank as a rule — and Turkish courts treat this as strict liability, regardless of how convincing the forgery was, with contractual disclaimers of this liability void. The only escape is proof that the drawer was themselves at fault, such as failing to safeguard their chequebook; even then, ordinary comparative-fault principles apply rather than a full shift of liability. A bank cannot avoid responsibility merely because the forged cheque was processed at another branch or through a clearing house — the improperly paid amount can be reclaimed from whoever wrongfully collected it.

## Multi-Jurisdiction Counterparts

Art. 813 allows counterpart cheques only in narrow cross-border scenarios — where drawing and payment occur across overseas locations — unlike the broader counterpart right for bills of exchange. Bearer cheques are excluded entirely, since multiple bearer counterparts in circulation would create unmanageable duplicate-payment risk.

## Limitation: Three Years, Simplified

Because a cheque has no acceptance stage, its limitation regime is simpler than a bill's tiered structure: the holder's recourse rights against endorsers, the drawer, and other obligors expire three years after the presentment period ends (Art. 814/1), with a separate three-year period for each obligor's own recourse against earlier parties, running from their payment or being sued (Art. 814/2). This three-year period is relatively recent — cheques carried only a six-month limitation before a 2012 law change, with courts applying whichever period was in force when the presentment period expired.

## Bank Defined, and Holiday Computation

"Bank" means an entity subject to Turkey's Banking Law, with foreign cheques' "bank" concept determined by the payment-place law instead (Art. 815). Presentment and protest can only occur on a business day, with deadlines falling on holidays extended to the next business day (Art. 816), and the first day of any period never counted (Art. 817).

## The Selective Cross-Reference (Art. 818)

Rather than rewriting the entire regime, Art. 818 imports a long but selective list of bill-of-exchange provisions — signature liability, endorsement, defenses, aval, protest, joint liability, limitation-interruption, and cancellation — while conspicuously omitting anything tied to acceptance or maturity mechanics, since cheques have neither. One notable omission: the pledge-endorsement provision (Art. 689) is not imported, meaning — as Turkish courts have confirmed — a cheque cannot be pledged by endorsement; only collection or outright transfer endorsements are available.

## Practical Takeaway

A bank facing a forged cheque bears the loss absent clear drawer fault, while a holder relying on a cheque should track the three-year window from the end of the presentment period and understand that pledge-style endorsements simply do not work on cheques.
