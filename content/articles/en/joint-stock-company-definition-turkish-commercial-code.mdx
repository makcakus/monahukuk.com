The Turkish joint-stock company (anonim şirket, or A.Ş.) is the Commercial Code's most heavily used and most extensively regulated company type, spanning TTK Art. 329-563. This first article in the series covers the opening provisions — the company's definition, its purpose freedom, its minimum capital, and the limited circumstances under which the state supervises its formation.

## A Pure Capital Company

Under Art. 329, a joint-stock company has capital fixed in amount and divided into shares, and it is liable for its debts only with its own assets; shareholders are liable to the company solely for their subscribed capital contributions — there is no personal liability toward company creditors. This marks the fundamental line separating the A.Ş. (and the limited liability company) from the person companies discussed elsewhere in the Code.

## Purpose Freedom

A significant modernization under the 2012 Code was abolishing the old "ultra vires" doctrine that confined a company's legal capacity to its stated business purpose. Art. 331 now allows joint-stock companies to be formed for any lawful economic purpose — meaning acts outside the stated business purpose generally still bind the company toward third parties. Turkish courts have made clear, however, that "economic purpose" still requires a genuine profit orientation: a company that in substance operates like a non-profit foundation to sidestep foundation-level oversight can be judicially dissolved.

## Minimum Capital and Registered Capital System

The minimum capital stated in the text of Art. 332 is 50,000 Turkish Lira, or 100,000 Lira for non-public companies opting into the "registered capital" system, which lets the board raise capital up to a pre-approved ceiling without convening a full shareholders' meeting each time. **These figures are no longer the ones applied:** the power to increase them has been exercised by **Presidential Decision No. 7887** (Official Gazette 25/11/2023, No. 32380; in force 1 January 2024), which raised the minimum capital to **250,000 Turkish Lira** and the minimum initial capital under the registered capital system to **500,000 Turkish Lira** (the limited company minimum under Art. 580 became 50,000 Lira). Under **provisional Article 15**, added by Law No. 7511 of 23 May 2024, companies below these thresholds must raise their capital **by 31 December 2026** or be **deemed dissolved**.

## Limited State Oversight

Company formation generally requires no governmental permission — Art. 333 makes clear that, apart from a narrow category of sectors (banks, insurers, and similar regulated industries) designated by the Ministry, no authority may condition a company's formation or bylaw amendments on its approval, and even then review is limited to checking compliance with mandatory statutory provisions. Separately, Art. 334 lets public entities (the State, municipalities, and similar bodies) hold board representation in companies performing a public service, even without owning shares — with the public entity itself liable for its representative's acts on the board.

For founders, the practical takeaway is that Turkish company law now imposes very few formation barriers outside regulated sectors, but the minimum capital and purpose requirements remain hard floors.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the minimum capital of a joint stock company?

By Presidential Decree No. 7887 of 24/11/2023 (Official Gazette of 25/11/2023, No. 32380; in force from 1/1/2024) the minimum basic capital was raised to <b>TRY 250,000</b> and, for non-public joint stock companies that have adopted the registered capital system, the minimum initial capital to <b>TRY 500,000</b>. The words 'fifty thousand' and 'one hundred thousand' in the text of TTK Article 332 are no longer applied; the power to increase these figures that the article confers on the President has been exercised.

### What must companies whose capital is below TRY 250,000 do?

Under <b>TTK Provisional Article 15</b>, added by Law No. 7511 of 23/5/2024, joint stock and limited liability companies whose capital falls below the new minimum amounts must raise it <b>by 31/12/2026</b>. If the increase is not made within that period, the companies are <b>deemed dissolved</b>. Compliance requires a general assembly resolution and registration of the capital increase with the trade registry.

### Is a shareholder liable for the company's debts in a joint stock company?

As a rule, no. Under TTK Articles 329/1 and 480/1, a joint stock company is liable for its debts with its own assets alone, and the shareholder's sole obligation is to pay the capital share they have subscribed. The most important exception in practice concerns public claims: under repeated Article 35 of Law No. 6183, liability falls not on the shareholder but on the <b>members of the board of directors</b> in their capacity as legal representatives.
