Rarely used but conceptually distinctive, the capital-divided partnership ("sermayesi paylara bölünmüş komandit şirket") under TTK Art. 564-572 blends unlimited-liability partners with limited-liability, share-holding investors — most often chosen by family businesses seeking to grow institutionally without losing control.

## The Defining Structure

Art. 564 defines it as a company whose capital is divided into shares, where one or more partners (the "commandite") are liable to company creditors like general partnership partners — fully and personally — while the rest (the "commanditer") are liable only like joint-stock shareholders, limited to their capital commitment. If the capital is merely divided into notional portions to track commanditer contribution ratios rather than genuine transferable shares, ordinary commandite partnership rules apply instead.

## Which Rules Apply, and When

Art. 565 sets the governing framework: relationships between commandite partners, and between commanditers and third parties — especially management, representation, and withdrawal — follow commandite (and by extension, general partnership) rules. Everything else — incorporation, capital, shares, general assembly, audit, financial statements, dissolution — follows joint-stock company rules unless the Code specifically provides otherwise.

## Incorporation Requirements Specific to This Structure

Beyond the joint-stock incorporation framework it otherwise follows, this structure imposes two mandatory conditions: there must be at least five founders, and at least one founder must be a commandite partner (Art. 568) — without a commandite partner, the entity is simply a joint-stock company. Notably, the Ministry authorization otherwise required for joint-stock incorporation does not apply here (Art. 566).

## Management Standard and Removal

Managing commandite partners are held to the same duty-of-care and liability standard as joint-stock company directors (Art. 570) — a materially higher accountability bar than ordinary partnership management. Yet removal from management follows commandite/partnership rules requiring just cause, because removal directly affects the partner's ongoing personal liability exposure (Art. 571); once registered, the removed partner's personal liability for future debts ends.

## Practical Takeaway

Foreign investors or advisors encountering this structure should not assume standard joint-stock company protections apply uniformly — management, representation, and exit are governed by partnership-law logic even though most other aspects mirror a joint-stock company.
