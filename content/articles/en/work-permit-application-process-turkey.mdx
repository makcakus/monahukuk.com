For an employer looking to hire a foreign national in Türkiye, the first task is choosing the right application channel and the right type of permit. Law No. 6735 on International Labour Force builds the work permit process around a shared effort between employer and employee, fixed statutory deadlines, and a largely digital procedure. This article walks through how the process runs from the outset — which documents to prepare and which permit type fits the situation. We cover the appeal and litigation routes available after a refusal in a separate article.

## Where to Apply: From Abroad or From Within Türkiye

Article 7 of Law No. 6735 sets out two distinct application channels. Foreign nationals located abroad apply through the Turkish embassy or consulate general in the country of which they are a citizen or where they are lawfully present; that mission then forwards the application to the Ministry of Labour and Social Security. Foreign nationals already in Türkiye who hold **a valid residence permit with at least six months of validity** apply directly to the Ministry, with the employer submitting the case through the **e-Permit System** (ecalismaizni.csgb.gov.tr) using an e-Government password or e-signature. Under the second paragraph of Article 7, the application may also be filed through an authorised intermediary institution.

Who files the application matters too: in a dependent employment relationship, the employer is the applicant, and the foreign national contributes the supporting documents and information. For an independent work permit, the foreign national applies directly.

## Types of Work Permits

Article 10 of the Law sets out several categories:

- **Fixed-term work permit** — capped at one year on first application, not exceeding the term of the employment contract; up to two years on the first renewal with the same employer, and up to three years on subsequent renewals. A renewal filed for a different employer is assessed as a fresh first application.
- **Indefinite-term work permit** — available to foreign nationals holding a long-term residence permit or at least eight years of lawful work permit history. Meeting the conditions does not create an automatic entitlement; the Ministry still exercises discretion in its assessment.
- **Independent work permit** — granted to foreign professionals who meet the specific conditions set out in relevant legislation, issued for a fixed term but without the duration caps that apply to dependent permits. The assessment weighs the applicant's education, professional experience, and the contribution of their investment to the Turkish economy.
- **Exceptional work permits** — Articles 16 through 20 of the Law provide tailored conditions for specific groups, including holders of international protection status, employees in free zones, foreign students, and foreign engineers and architects.
- **Turquoise Card** — issued to foreign nationals recognised as highly qualified in academic, scientific, industrial, or investment terms. It carries a three-year transition period, after which it may become permanent on application, granting the holder the rights of an indefinite-term work permit. This route has its own separate evaluation criteria.

## Required Documents

A complete file is decisive for meeting the thirty-day statutory review period. A standard dependent-employment application typically requires:

- An employment or service contract, or an assignment letter, signed between the foreign national and the employer,
- The foreign national's diploma or professional qualification certificate, with a sworn Turkish translation and apostille or consular certification where required; certain professions additionally require a **YÖK equivalence certificate**,
- The employer's recent social security (SGK) declarations and proof of no outstanding tax debt,
- A copy of the foreign national's passport and, for applications filed from within Türkiye, proof of a valid residence permit,
- For roles in healthcare or education, the **prior approval** required under Article 8 of the Law (from the Ministry of Health or the Ministry of National Education).

A missing document does not by itself cause a refusal. Under Article 7(7), the review is suspended until the gap is cured — but that suspension may not exceed thirty days unless force majeure is certified by an official authority, and a file left incomplete past that window is refused.

## Review Period and Outcome

Article 7(8) is explicit: a properly filed application with complete information and documents is decided **within thirty days**. The Ministry assesses the application against Turkey's international labour force policy and may consult relevant public bodies and professional organisations where necessary. Renewals follow a separate deadline: under Article 7(3), a renewal must be filed before the existing permit expires and no earlier than sixty days before that expiry; a request filed after expiry is refused outright.

If the application succeeds, the work permit is issued digitally. If it is refused, Article 21 of the Law opens both an administrative appeal and an administrative lawsuit route; we cover the common grounds for refusal and how to challenge them in [Work Permit Denial in Turkey: Appeal Procedures](/en/articles/work-permit-denial-appeal-turkey).

## Shared Obligations of Employer and Employee

The work permit process is not a one-sided undertaking. Article 6 of the Law prohibits working or employing someone without a permit, and a breach exposes both the employer and the foreign national to administrative sanctions. Article 22 further requires employers of foreign nationals, and holders of indefinite or independent work permits, to notify the Ministry within **fifteen days** of the start or end of employment or of any circumstance requiring the permit's cancellation, and to meet social security obligations on time under Law No. 5510. Neglecting this notification duty can count against the employer in later applications and renewals. We cover an employer's broader duties in [Employer Obligations When Hiring Foreign Nationals in Turkey](/en/articles/employer-obligations-hiring-foreign-nationals-turkey).

## How a Work Permit Connects to a Residence Permit

Once granted, a work permit also serves in place of a residence permit, and a separate residence permit application is not required. However, applications filed from within Türkiye require an existing residence permit (with at least six months of validity) as a precondition. Foreign nationals without a residence permit who plan to start working in Türkiye should therefore settle their residence status first; those planning to run their own business rather than take up employment may instead consider the [entrepreneur residence permit](/en/articles/entrepreneur-residence-permit-turkey) route.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

The most frequent error is a mismatch between the job title and duties stated in the employment contract and what the diploma or qualification certificate actually shows. A close second is forgetting the apostille or consular certification, or filing before the sworn translation is ready. A third is filing a renewal after the sixty-day window has passed — the Law requires an outright refusal, interrupting the foreign national's work status. Finally, lapsed SGK declarations or tax clearance documents can stall an otherwise complete file.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How long does a work permit application take to process?

Under Article 7 of the Law, a complete, properly filed application is decided within thirty days. If documents are missing, that period can extend while the gap is cured, capped at an additional thirty days.

### Can I apply for a work permit without a residence permit in Türkiye?

An application filed from within Türkiye requires a valid residence permit with at least six months remaining. Without one, you must apply from abroad through the Turkish embassy or consulate general where you reside.

### Does the employer or the employee file the application?

In a dependent employment relationship, the employer files through the e-Permit System while the foreign national supplies the required documents. For an independent work permit, the foreign national applies directly.

### Is a Turquoise Card different from a standard work permit?

Yes. The Turquoise Card is reserved for foreign nationals recognised as highly qualified in academic, scientific, industrial, or investment terms. It starts with a three-year transition period and can become permanent on application, carrying the rights of an indefinite-term work permit — a separate track from standard fixed-term or independent permits, with its own evaluation criteria.
