TTK Art. 1059-1060 close out the Code's chapter on ships with the least common of the limited rights available over a vessel: usufruct.

## Creating the Right

Only registered ships can carry a usufruct (Art. 1059(1)) — unregistered vessels, like with mortgages, are simply outside this framework. Unless the parties agree otherwise, a usufruct holder gets the *full* benefit of the ship — operating it, chartering it, and keeping the resulting economic return — while the owner retains bare title (Art. 1059(2)). This is a default rule, not a mandatory one, so the scope can be contractually narrowed. For contractual (as opposed to statutory) usufruct, creation follows the exact same formula already familiar from the ship mortgage: a notarized written agreement (or one executed at the registry office) plus registration (Art. 1059(3)).

## Governing Law and Ranking Against Mortgages

Art. 1060 makes explicit something that runs through the entire chapter on ships: although a ship is legally a movable asset, it is treated like real property for purposes of limited rights. A ship usufruct is governed by the Civil Code's real-property usufruct rules — not the simpler, possession-based rules for movables. Where a usufruct and one or more mortgages coexist on the same ship, priority is governed by the Civil Code's general ranking provision (Art. 869): rights registered on the same date rank equally, and the rules on modifying mortgage rank and on limitation of an owner's obligations to a creditor apply here too.

## Practical Takeaway

Ship usufruct is rare in Turkish maritime practice — financing overwhelmingly runs through the mortgage, and usufruct tends to appear only in family or partnership arrangements that rarely reach litigation. For any transaction combining a usufruct with existing or planned mortgage financing, the registration date is what determines relative priority, so timing the registry entry correctly is essential to avoid an unintended subordination.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can a usufruct right be established over a vessel?

Yes, but only over registered vessels. Under TTK Article 1059/1, <b>a usufruct right may be established over vessels registered in the registry</b>. Under Article 1059/2, unless otherwise agreed, the usufruct right grants its holder <b>the power to derive full benefit from the vessel over which it is established</b>. As with a ship mortgage, it is established through agreement of the parties and registration of the right in the ship registry.

### Which provisions apply to a usufruct over a vessel?

Under TTK Article 1060/1, a usufruct right over a registered vessel is subject to <b>the provisions of the Turkish Civil Code on usufruct over immovables</b>. Although a vessel is deemed movable property under Article 936, it is linked to the immovable-property regime as regards usufruct; the reason is that the ship registry establishes a system of publicity and reliance similar to that of the land registry. Under TMK Article 794 et seq., the holder of the usufruct right acquires the powers of possession, management, use and enjoyment.

### Is the usufructuary liable for the expenses of the vessel?

Yes. Under the reference made by Article 1060 to the TMK, the usufructuary is subject to the obligations set out in TMK Article 803 et seq.: it is their debt to carry out the repairs necessary for the preservation and ordinary maintenance of the property, to meet the ordinary expenses and taxes, and, where necessary, to <b>have the property insured</b>. Extraordinary repairs and renewals, however, are as a rule the responsibility of the owner; the usufructuary is obliged to notify the owner of such a situation.
