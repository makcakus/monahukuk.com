Dissolution does not instantly end a Turkish general partnership's legal existence — it opens a liquidation period governed by TTK Art. 267-285, which covers who winds down the business and with what authority, before the actual liquidation steps addressed in a following article.

## The Partnership Survives, for a Purpose

Under Art. 269, a dissolved partnership retains its legal personality throughout liquidation, limited to that purpose, and must add "in liquidation" to its trade name. Liquidation does not bar a subsequent bankruptcy filing (Art. 270), and partnership creditors retain priority over the partners' personal creditors with respect to partnership assets even after dissolution (Art. 271) — the asset-separation principle that applied during the partnership's active life continues into liquidation.

## Appointing and Removing Liquidators

Liquidation is handled exclusively by liquidators, except where bankruptcy is the cause of dissolution (Art. 272). Liquidators are typically chosen by unanimous partner vote — in the founding agreement or afterward — or, absent such a choice, all partners are deemed liquidators by default, with a court able to appoint one on a partner's request (Art. 273). Turkish courts have treated this liquidator authority as exclusive: in one case, the former management's post-dissolution transfer of partnership assets to a third company — with an overlapping shareholder and timed right after certain partners' departure — was voided as an unauthorized, collusive act outside the liquidators' sole competence, with the assets ordered returned. Removal follows a tiered process: generally by unanimous partner vote, or by court order for just cause if unanimity cannot be reached (Art. 274-277).

## Powers, Representation, and Liability of Liquidators

Liquidators represent the partnership in and out of court, may settle, waive claims, and arbitrate within ordinary business, and every liquidation document must bear the "in liquidation" designation (Art. 280). Their statutory powers can be narrowed or broadened by unanimous partner vote or court order, but any narrowing only binds good-faith third parties once registered and published (Art. 282). Liquidators acting as partners are unpaid by default unless otherwise agreed; non-partner liquidators are entitled to reasonable compensation (Art. 284). Critically, liquidators who breach the law, the agreement, or their duties are jointly liable for resulting damage unless they prove they were not at fault — a reversed burden of proof — with claims prescribing two years from when the harm and the liable party became known, and five years in any event (Art. 285).

For creditors and partners alike, the practical takeaway is that only the appointed liquidators — not the former management — may validly act for a dissolved partnership; any other actor's dealings with partnership assets during this period carry real invalidity risk.
