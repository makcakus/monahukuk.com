TTK Art. 1257 extends the liability regime just built for the contracting carrier to the "actual carrier" — the party who physically performs the carriage. Its five subsections track the equivalent cargo-carriage provision almost word for word, simply substituting passengers for cargo.

## Full Liability Survives Delegation

Art. 1257(1) provides that even where all or part of the carriage is delegated to an actual carrier, the contracting carrier remains fully liable for the whole carriage under this chapter. The actual carrier is separately liable, under the same chapter, for the portion it actually performs. A passenger's claim against the party they contracted with is not diminished by delegation — delegation adds a parallel liable party, it does not substitute one for the other.

## Fault of the Actual Carrier (and Its Own Servants) Imputed to the Carrier

Art. 1257(2) extends the vicarious-fault principle one link further: where the actual carrier performs the carriage, the contracting carrier is liable not only for its own servants' fault but also for the actual carrier's fault and that of the actual carrier's own servants. A claimant does not need to pinpoint precisely which layer of the delegation chain caused the harm — liability traces back to the contracting carrier regardless.

## Side Agreements Do Not Bind the Actual Carrier

Art. 1257(3) protects the actual carrier's own contractual autonomy: agreements by which the carrier assumes obligations beyond the statute, or waives rights the statute grants it, do not bind the actual carrier unless expressly and in writing accepted by the actual carrier itself. And where both are liable, their liability is joint and several to the extent of their shared responsibility (Art. 1257(4)) — a claimant can pursue either or both for the full loss, leaving contribution between them to be settled separately.

## Recourse Between Carrier and Actual Carrier Is Unaffected

Art. 1257(5) preserves the internal recourse relationship between carrier and actual carrier — this chapter governs the external relationship toward the passenger only; how the two ultimately share the loss between themselves is a separate matter this article does not touch.

Notably, unlike its cargo-carriage counterpart, Art. 1257 has no separate subsection imposing a cumulative cap on combined carrier/actual-carrier compensation — a structural simplification worth noting without further speculation as to its cause.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### If another company operates the vessel, is the company that sold the ticket still liable?

Yes. Under Article 1257/1 TTK, even where carriage is wholly or partly entrusted to an <b>actual carrier</b>, <b>the contracting carrier remains liable for the carriage as a whole</b> under this Chapter. Article 1257/2 adds that the carrier is also answerable for <b>the fault of the actual carrier and of its servants acting in the course of their duties</b>. The passenger may therefore proceed against the carrier with whom the ticket contract was made.

### Whom should the passenger sue?

Either or both. Under Article 1257/4 TTK the carrier and the actual carrier are liable <b>jointly and severally</b> to the extent of their respective liability, so the passenger may claim against one or both. In practice suing the contracting carrier is simpler, because the contractual relationship is easy to prove; a claim against the actual carrier requires proof of the period during which it actually had the carriage in its charge.

### Do exclusion clauses in the ticket conditions bind the actual carrier?

No. Under Article 1257/3 TTK, special agreements by which the carrier assumes an obligation not imposed on it by law, or waives a right conferred on it, <b>have no effect as against the actual carrier unless it has expressly consented in writing</b>. By the same logic, extensions made in the carrier's favour do not pass automatically to the actual carrier; Article 1257/5 preserves their right of recourse against one another.
