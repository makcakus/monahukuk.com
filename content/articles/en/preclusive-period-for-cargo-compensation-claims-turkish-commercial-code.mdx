TTK Art. 1188 puts a hard one-year clock on cargo claims — and however strong the underlying claim, missing this deadline ends the case before the merits are ever reached.

## One Year, and Why It Isn't Ordinary Limitation

Every claim against a carrier for cargo loss, damage, or delay is extinguished ("düşer") unless legal proceedings are commenced within one year. The critical distinction is that this is a preclusive period, not a limitation defense: unlike ordinary statutes of limitation, it is not subject to the Turkish Code of Obligations' tolling or interruption rules, and — per settled Turkish case law — courts apply it on their own motion even if the carrier never raises it. A claimant cannot count on the carrier simply forgetting to plead it.

## When the Clock Starts

The year runs from actual (partial) delivery, or — where cargo is never delivered at all — from the date delivery *should* have occurred. This second alternative matters for total loss cases, where there's no real delivery event to anchor the clock; courts calculate the hypothetical date by reference to the contractual or Art. 1178/4 reasonable delivery timeframe.

## Filing Through Enforcement Proceedings Counts

Turkish courts have confirmed that starting enforcement (icra) proceedings within the year satisfies the "commencing legal proceedings" requirement just as filing a lawsuit would — a useful procedural nuance for claimants working against the deadline.

## A Separate Ninety-Day Window for Recourse Claims

A party held liable can still bring a recourse claim after the one-year period has expired — but only within ninety days of paying the compensation or being served with the claim against it, whichever comes first. This protects parties lower in a carriage chain (an actual carrier a contracting carrier seeks recourse against, for instance) who may not yet have a concrete claim to act on while the main one-year period is running.

## Extension by Agreement

The parties can agree to extend the period, but only *after* the cause of action has already arisen — a pre-dispute waiver in a standard-form contract carries no weight under this provision.
