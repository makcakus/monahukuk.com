TTK Art. 986-992 solve a practical financing problem: a hull under construction is not yet a "ship" and cannot enter the main Turkish Ship Registry, yet shipbuilding finance often requires a mortgage on that very asset. This separate registry fills the gap.

## When and Where Registration Happens

Under Art. 986, a structure under construction may be registered on the owner's request, or must be when a mortgage, attachment, or the shipyard's statutory mortgage right needs to be secured by notation. Jurisdiction follows the build location, not a port of registry, and stays with the original registry office even if the hull is later moved to another yard.

## Application and Required Proof

Art. 987-988 allow the owner, a shipyard seeking to register its statutory mortgage, or an attaching creditor (through the enforcement office) to apply, with a short set of identifying details plus — where a mortgage is intended — a certificate from a qualified ship-measurement body confirming the structure meets the minimum requirements for a mortgage under Art. 1054(2). Once construction is reported complete, Art. 990(2) closes the door on registering any further mortgage on this special registry — financing needs shift to the main registry from that point.

## A Stricter Access Standard

Art. 991 largely imports the main registry's rules (court supervision, the reliance principle, the notice mechanism) — but deliberately tightens public access: reviewing the underlying files requires proving an actual interest, not merely a "credible" one, reflecting the commercially sensitive nature of financing details for a project still under negotiation.

## Deregistration and the Move to the Main Registry

Under Art. 992, the entry is cancelled when the shipyard reports delivery abroad, when both the owner and shipyard jointly request cancellation, or when the structure is destroyed — and, consistent with the main registry, any mortgage creditor's consent is required first. Once construction finishes, the vessel transitions into the ordinary Ship Registry, carrying its mortgage priority with it automatically.

## Practical Takeaway

For lenders financing newbuilds at Turkish yards, this registry is what makes it possible to secure a mortgage against a hull that legally isn't a ship yet — but the tighter access standard means due diligence on an in-progress project typically requires demonstrating a concrete stake, not just curiosity.
