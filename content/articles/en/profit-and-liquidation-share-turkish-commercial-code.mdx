TTK Art. 507-513 govern the economic core of shareholding: the right to a share of profit and, on winding up, of what remains after liquidation.

## No Distribution Without a General Assembly Decision

Art. 507 gives each shareholder a right to net profit "resolved to be distributed" — language the Court of Cassation reads literally. Profit distribution sits among the general assembly's non-delegable powers under Art. 408; absent an actual distribution resolution, a shareholder cannot sue directly for payment, and a court cannot substitute its own distribution decision for the assembly's. Article 508 sets the default calculation method: profit and liquidation shares are proportional to paid-in capital unless the articles say otherwise.

## Interest on Capital Is Prohibited, With One Narrow Exception

Art. 509 bans paying interest on capital itself — a shareholder earns from company profit, not from having paid in capital — and confines profit distribution to net profit and free reserves. Art. 510 permits a narrow exception: during a pre-operational "preparation period" for major investment projects, the articles may authorize a limited interest payment charged to the investment cost rather than treated as profit.

## Director Compensation Is Subordinated to Shareholders' First Dividend

Art. 511 conditions any profit share paid to board members on two things happening first: the legal reserve allocation, and a minimum 5% dividend (or higher, if the articles specify) to shareholders. Recent Court of Cassation rulings confirm this priority, while also striking down assembly decisions to withhold dividends when the articles mandate distribution and no concrete justification (a pandemic reference alone was found insufficient) is given.

## Clawback for Bad Faith and Company Insolvency

Art. 512 requires shareholders and directors who received dividends or preparation-period interest in bad faith to return them, subject to a five-year limitation period. Art. 513 imposes a separate, stricter rule for insolvency: within the three years before bankruptcy, directors must repay compensation exceeding what a prudent management would have budgeted as reasonable — an objective standard independent of bad faith, applied at the court's discretion.

## Practical Takeaway

Before relying on an expected dividend, foreign investors and counterparties should confirm that an actual, properly authorized distribution resolution exists — board promises or informal arrangements carry no legal weight under Turkish law until the general assembly acts.
