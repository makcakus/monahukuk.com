A person under temporary protection may stay in Türkiye for years, work, and send children to school. The natural expectation is that at some point this time will convert into a residence permit and ultimately into citizenship. The legislation does not meet that expectation: temporary protection is by definition a transitional regime, and the legislature has carefully separated it from other statuses. Not knowing this distinction leads to a refusal at the end of years of waiting.

## What the Temporary Protection Identity Document Does and Does Not Provide

Article 25 of the Temporary Protection Regulation draws four limits in a single paragraph. Under that article, **the temporary protection identity document confers a right to remain in Türkiye.** But the sentence that follows is decisive: this document

- **is not treated as equivalent to a residence permit or to the documents that take the place of a residence permit under the Law**,
- **confers no right of transition to a long-term residence permit**,
- **its duration is not taken into account in the aggregate of residence permit periods**,
- and it **does not give its holder a right to apply for Turkish citizenship**.

Read together, the four points make the picture clear: time spent under temporary protection is added neither to the residence permit period nor to the residence period required for citizenship. The stay is lawful, but it accumulates no status.

## The Equivalent Provision at the Level of the Law

This limit in the Regulation is mirrored in the Law. The second paragraph of Article 42 of Law No. 6458 on Foreigners and International Protection provides that **holders of refugee, conditional refugee, and subsidiary protection status, holders of a humanitarian residence permit, and persons granted temporary protection are not accorded a right of transition to a long-term residence permit.**

This provision is an exception to the general rule in the first paragraph of the same article. Under that first paragraph, foreigners who have resided in Türkiye continuously for at least eight years under a residence permit, or who satisfy the conditions determined by the Ministry, are granted an indefinite residence permit by the governorates with the approval of the Ministry. The eight-year door is closed to protection statuses.

The logic of the distinction lies in the nature of the statuses: a [long-term residence permit](/en/articles/long-term-residence-permit-turkey-requirements) is a status that rewards permanent settlement, whereas protection regimes provide temporary and conditional protection. When the need for protection ends, so does the status.

## So Which Routes Remain Open?

The door that temporary protection closes is not the only door. If a person's circumstances change, it may be possible to establish a separate basis of stay under the general provisions.

What matters here is the logic established by provisional Article 1 of the Temporary Protection Regulation. That article provides that persons who, from 28/4/2011 onwards, **obtained a residence permit that was not extended or whose residence permit was cancelled**, together with those who seek protection at the end of a visa period or exemption, are — without prejudice to the provisions of Article 8 — to be taken into the scope of temporary protection; and that **the general provisions apply to those among them who do not seek protection**.

The final point matters: temporary protection and the general provisions are alternatives to one another. Where the conditions are met, a person may apply under the general provisions for a [type of residence permit](/en/articles/short-term-residence-permit-turkey-guide) — for instance if they acquire immovable property in Türkiye, obtain a [work permit](/en/articles/work-permit-application-process-turkey), or satisfy the conditions for a [family residence permit](/en/articles/family-residence-permit-spouse-turkish-citizen) by marrying a Turkish citizen. This is not a matter of the temporary protection period "counting"; it is a separate status resting on a new legal basis, starting from zero.

## The Effect of the Difference Between Statuses on Citizenship

Article 11 of Turkish Citizenship Law No. 5901 requires a foreigner seeking to acquire Turkish citizenship **to have resided in Türkiye continuously for five years counting back from the date of application**. Because Article 25 of the Temporary Protection Regulation expressly prevents that period from being counted, a person who has spent ten years under temporary protection is not treated as satisfying the condition.

The misconception encountered in practice is that "physical presence in Türkiye" is enough. What the law requires is a legally recognised residence that accumulates time; mere physical presence does not suffice. For a person aiming at citizenship, therefore, the right question is not "how many years have I been here" but **"under what status am I here"**.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do the ten years I spent under temporary protection count towards citizenship?

No. Article 25 of the Temporary Protection Regulation expressly provides that the duration of the temporary protection identity document is not taken into account in the aggregate of residence permit periods and that it does not give its holder a right to apply for Turkish citizenship.

### Does my temporary protection identity document take the place of a residence permit?

It does not. The same article provides that the document is not treated as equivalent to a residence permit or to documents that take the place of one under the Law. The document confers only a right to remain in Türkiye.

### Can I never move to a long-term residence permit?

You cannot do so on the basis of temporary protection status; Article 42 of Law No. 6458 expressly prohibits it. But if circumstances change and a separate residence permit is obtained under the general provisions, the duration of that permit begins to run within its own regime.

### I have a work permit — does that change anything?

If the work permit was issued within the scope of temporary protection, it does not change the status. A work permit obtained under the general provisions may constitute a separate basis of stay. The distinction turns on the regime under which the permit was issued.
