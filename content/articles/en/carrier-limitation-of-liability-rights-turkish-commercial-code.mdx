TTK Art. 1186 sets the monetary ceiling on carrier liability for cargo loss or damage — an eight-paragraph provision closely paralleled with the Hague-Visby Rules and probably the single most litigated, highest-value provision in Turkish maritime cargo law.

## The Base Cap: Whichever Is Higher

The carrier's liability is capped at whichever is higher: 666.67 Special Drawing Rights (SDR, the IMF's stable multi-currency unit) per package or unit, or 2 SDR per kilogram of the damaged cargo's gross weight — unless the shipper declared the cargo's nature and value before loading and it was written into the bill of lading, in which case that declared value governs instead. SDR converts to Turkish lira at the Central Bank's rate on the payment date. Actual damages, calculated by reference to value at the discharge port and date, still form a floor: if real loss falls below the SDR cap, the carrier pays the lower, actual figure — the cap is a ceiling, not an entitlement.

## Containers: Where the Real Money Is

If a bill of lading itemizes each package or unit inside a container, the per-package limit applies separately to each one; if it only describes the container generically ("1 container general cargo"), the entire container counts as a single package. The practical gap is enormous — potentially a five-hundredfold difference between an itemized and a non-itemized bill for the same 500-carton shipment — making accurate container-content description a critical commercial safeguard, not a formality.

## Fraud Kills the Cap Entirely

A shipper's knowingly false declaration of cargo type or value strips the carrier of *all* liability for that cargo's loss or damage — a much harsher consequence than the ordinary evidentiary treatment of declared-value entries, which create only a rebuttable presumption not binding on the carrier.

## Delay Gets Its Own Separate Limit

Liability for exceeding the transit time is capped at two-and-a-half times the freight payable for the delayed cargo, never exceeding total freight under the contract. When both the physical-damage and delay caps apply to the same shipment, combined liability still cannot exceed what total loss under the base cap would cost. Parties may agree to higher limits, but never lower than the statutory floor.
