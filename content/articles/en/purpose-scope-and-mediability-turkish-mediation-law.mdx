*Article 1 – (1) The purpose of this Law is to regulate the procedure and principles applicable to the resolution of civil disputes through mediation.*

*(2) This Law applies, including where a foreign element is present, only to the resolution of private law disputes arising from matters or transactions over which the parties may freely dispose. However, disputes involving an allegation of domestic violence are not capable of mediation.*

Mediation entered Turkish law in 2012 with Law No. 6325 on Mediation in Civil Disputes. It was initially designed as an entirely voluntary route and, from 2018 onward, successive amendments turned it into a compulsory preliminary stage that opens the courthouse door in many types of dispute. The first article draws the institution's boundaries in two sentences: mediation law is not a procedural code but a special statute regulating the procedure and principles of a dispute resolution method; and not every dispute can be taken to mediation. Whether a dispute is **capable of mediation** is the question to be answered at the very outset — because a settlement document signed in a dispute that is not capable of mediation is void, and the rights the parties believe they have gained under it cannot be enforced.

## The Purpose of the Law: Not an Alternative to the Courts but a Parallel Route

Article 1/1 defines the statute as a regulation of "procedure and principles". Mediation is not an adjudicative activity in which a third person decides the dispute; it is a communication process established between the parties by an impartial third person applying systematic techniques, so that the parties can generate their own solution. Article 15/4 reinforces this distinction with an express limit: acts which, by their nature, may be performed only by a judge as an exercise of judicial power cannot be performed by a mediator. A mediator cannot assess evidence, commission an expert report or grant an interim measure; where it emerges that the parties cannot produce a solution, the mediator may only put forward a settlement proposal (Article 15/7).

In a 2026 decision, the 3rd Civil Chamber of the Turkish Court of Cassation placed mediation, following the settled scholarly definition, within the family of methods grouped under "alternative dispute resolution", and stressed that the common aim of those methods is that "the parties reach a solution by their own will in matters that fall within private law, do not concern public policy, and may be freely disposed of". That formulation also summarises how Article 1/2 works in practice.

## The Mediability Test: Three Conditions Required Together

The Court of Cassation requires three conditions to be satisfied **together** for a dispute to be capable of mediation:

1. **The dispute must concern private law.** Administrative disputes, tax disputes and relationships forming the subject matter of criminal proceedings fall outside this Law. The institution of [reconciliation](/articles/reconciliation-turkish-criminal-procedure-code) in criminal procedure, despite the similarity of names, is a different restorative-justice mechanism governed by a different statute (Article 253 of the Turkish Code of Criminal Procedure).
2. **It must arise from a matter or transaction over which the parties may freely dispose.** The criterion is the parties' power to settle, waive and admit in respect of the right in question. Matters that concern public policy, that the judge must investigate of their own motion, or that affect the rights of third persons do not meet this test.
3. **It must not involve an allegation of domestic violence.** The last sentence of Article 1/2 lays down an absolute prohibition; the parties cannot agree otherwise. This exception is intended to exclude situations in which the imbalance of power between the parties renders negotiation meaningless from the outset, and it is consistent with the approach of the Istanbul Convention to mandatory alternative dispute resolution.

## The Foreign Element: A Deliberate Choice by the Legislator

The phrase "including where a foreign element is present" in Article 1/2 is not accidental. The legislator removed at the outset any doubt that mediation might fall outside the scope where one party is a foreign national, is domiciled abroad, or where the contract is governed by foreign law. In a judicial district such as Antalya, with its concentration of foreign investors and residents, the practical consequence is this: the mediation stage applies in exactly the same way to a lease dispute between a foreign tenant and a Turkish landlord as to a commercial receivable of a company with foreign shareholders.

Two practical warnings are in order here. First, mediation sessions are conducted in Turkish and the Law provides no right to a State-funded interpreter; the party bears the cost of an interpreter or of bilingual counsel. Second, enforcement in Türkiye of a settlement agreement produced in a foreign mediation is subject to a separate regime, that of [Article 17/A on the enforcement of international settlement agreements](/articles/enforcement-of-international-settlement-agreements-singapore-convention).

## Disputes Not Regarded as Capable of Mediation

In practice the debate on mediability concentrates on three groups.

**Actions concerning public policy and affecting third persons.** The 6th Civil Chamber of the Court of Cassation expressly held that bankruptcy proceedings concern public policy, that disputes in bankruptcy are not matters over which the parties may freely dispose, and that this field is therefore not capable of mediation either as a condition precedent or under the general mediation rules — so that a mediation settlement record cannot form the basis of a bankruptcy decision. The same logic applies to actions for the determination of service arising from social security law, in which the Social Security Institution is a party.

**Claims concerning personal status.** The parties have no power of free disposition over matters of personal status such as marriage, divorce, parentage and custody itself. By contrast, the phrase "family law disputes that are capable of mediation" in Article 18/3 shows that family law is not entirely closed; mediability is accepted for ancillary property claims such as claims arising from the liquidation of the matrimonial property regime.

**A dispute that has not yet arisen, and a period in which there is no power of disposition.** According to the settled case law of the 9th Civil Chamber, mediation minutes "in the nature of a release" — drawn up while the employment contract is still in force or immediately after termination, without any genuine dispute and without any application by the employee — are not capable of mediation, because they were drawn up at a time when the parties could not freely dispose. This case law matters because it shows that the mediability review extends not only to the subject matter of the dispute but also to the timing of the process.

## The Consequences of Non-Mediability

Non-mediability is one of the grounds for termination of the mediation activity (Article 17/1-d). In mandatory mediation, if the mediator finds that the dispute is not capable of mediation, they terminate the process; under the Mediation Minimum Fee Tariff no fee is payable to the mediator in that case. If a settlement document has been drawn up despite non-mediability, the civil court of peace refuses the request for an enforceability annotation; for under Article 18/3 the court's power of review is limited precisely to "whether the content of the settlement is capable of mediation and of enforcement". Where a document has been enforced without an annotation, non-mediability is raised through enforcement law remedies.

## Decisions of the Court of Cassation

The following decisions show how the mediability criterion in Article 1/2 takes concrete shape in practice.

**1. Court of Cassation, 3rd Civil Chamber, File No. 2025/5452, Decision No. 2026/129, 15.01.2026**

The Chamber held that private law disputes remain within the scope of mediation regardless of whether they carry a foreign element, provided the parties are able to dispose of them freely, thereby requiring together the conditions of being a matter of private law, free disposition and the absence of an allegation of domestic violence. The decision also established that the "med-arb" model — where the parties agree to go first to mediation and, failing a result, to arbitration — is lawful, and that in this procedure the dispute must be capable of both mediation and arbitration.

**2. Court of Cassation, 6th Civil Chamber, File No. 2023/142, Decision No. 2023/855, 06.03.2023**

The dispute concerned whether a mediation settlement record could form the basis of a bankruptcy decision. The Chamber ruled that, because bankruptcy law concerns public policy and disputes in bankruptcy are not matters over which the parties may freely dispose, this field is capable of mediation neither as a condition precedent nor under the general rules, and that the settlement record cannot be relied on as the basis of a bankruptcy decision.

**3. Court of Cassation, 6th Civil Chamber, File No. 2022/3015, Decision No. 2023/1098, 20.03.2023**

The decision confirmed that bankruptcy proceedings concern public policy and affect the rights of third persons, and are therefore not capable of mandatory mediation. It also addressed the error of ordering the claimant to pay the mediation fee in a dispute not capable of mediation.

**4. Court of Cassation, 9th Civil Chamber, File No. 2022/436, Decision No. 2022/1380, 07.02.2022**

Assessing together the date on which the mediation minutes were drawn up and the content of the release declaration, the Chamber concluded that minutes drawn up in the nature of a release — at a time when the parties could not freely dispose, with no subject matter of dispute and without any application by the employee — were not capable of mediation. The decision demonstrates the temporal dimension of the mediability review.

**5. Court of Cassation, 9th Civil Chamber, File No. 2025/4865, Decision No. 2025/5551, 30.06.2025**

It was established that the designation of the mediator and both the first-session and final-session minutes were all dated the same day on which the employment contract ended. The Chamber held that this picture indicated that documents had been produced at a time when the parties could not freely dispose and without any genuine dispute, and reached a finding of non-mediability.
