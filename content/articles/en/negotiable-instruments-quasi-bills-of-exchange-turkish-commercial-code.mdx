TTK Art. 824-829 open a section of the Commercial Code dealing with instruments that function like bills of exchange, notes, and cheques but were never drafted using those exact statutory labels. Understanding this category matters for anyone holding a Turkish payment instrument that looks like a bill but does not say "bill of exchange" on its face.

## Two Origins of "Negotiable"

Under Art. 824, an instrument can be negotiable either by law (bills, notes, and cheques are automatically negotiable as members of the kambiyo family) or by choice (an instrument the law does not treat as inherently negotiable becomes so only because the drafter added a "to order" clause). Art. 825 then applies a single, unified defense regime to both origins, closely mirroring the bill-of-exchange defense rules: defenses going to the instrument's validity or apparent from its text are good against any holder, while personal defenses arising from a direct relationship are cut off against a good-faith transferee — unless that transferee knowingly acquired the instrument to the debtor's detriment.

## The Negotiable Order-to-Pay

Art. 826 creates the clearest illustration of the "form versus substance" tension running through this part of the Code: a payment order that carries every mandatory element of a bill of exchange and is expressly drawn "to order," but simply omits the word "bill," is treated as a bill for legal purposes. Yet Art. 827 immediately marks a real difference — this instrument cannot be presented for acceptance, and a holder who tries anyway and is refused acquires no recourse right from that refusal. Art. 828 allows voluntary acceptance by the drawee with bill-like effect, but narrows the pre-maturity recourse triggers (drawee insolvency, suspension of payments, failed enforcement) compared to an ordinary bill.

## Excluded From Kambiyo Enforcement

The most consequential rule is Art. 829: none of the Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law provisions on the special summary enforcement track for cheques, bills, and notes apply to a negotiable order-to-pay. Being "equivalent to a bill" in substantive terms does not make the instrument a bill for procedural purposes — a creditor must fall back on ordinary attachment proceedings, with the debtor's longer objection period and the creditor's heavier procedural burden.

## Practical Takeaway

A foreign creditor should never assume that an instrument's functional resemblance to a bill of exchange unlocks Turkey's fast kambiyo enforcement track; only the word itself, plus full formal compliance, does.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is an order instrument?

Under TTK Article 824/1, <b>negotiable instruments that are made to order or are deemed such by law</b> are order instruments. Being made to order allows the instrument to be transferred by endorsement and delivery. Bills of exchange, promissory notes and cheques are instruments deemed to be to order by law; for any other document to be an order instrument, an express order clause must appear in its text.

### Which defences may the debtor raise against the holder?

Under TTK Article 825/1 the debtor may raise against a claim arising from an order instrument only defences <b>relating to the invalidity of the instrument</b>, defences <b>apparent from the text of the instrument</b>, and defences <b>they personally have against whoever the creditor is</b>. In other words, the debtor cannot raise against a new and bona fide holder the defences arising from the underlying relationship with previous holders (for example that the goods were never delivered). This is the foundation of the security of circulation of negotiable instruments.

### Is an order transfer instruction a bill of exchange?

It has the effect of one. Under TTK Article 826/1, although not described in the text as a bill of exchange, <b>transfer instructions expressly drawn to order and containing in other respects the elements required of a bill of exchange have the effect of a bill of exchange</b>. Under Article 828, the optional acceptance by the person instructed has the effect of acceptance of a bill of exchange. However, under Article 829, <b>the provisions of the Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law on the enforcement of bills of exchange cannot be applied to an order transfer instruction.</b>
