Family law is the most hesitant corner of the mediation debate. On the one hand, the relationship between the parties is long-term and this is precisely the field that needs negotiation most; on the other, family law is full of matters in which the State intervenes heavily and over which the parties cannot freely dispose.

The answer given by Turkish law is twofold: **some family law disputes are capable of mediation and some are not** — and in none of them is mediation a condition precedent to litigation.

## First, a Clarification: There Is No Mandatory Mediation in Family Law

The scope of mandatory (condition-precedent) mediation in Turkey is fixed by five statutory provisions: employment disputes, commercial disputes, consumer disputes, the four groups listed in Article 18/B of the Turkish Law No. 6325 on Mediation in Civil Disputes (lease, dissolution of joint ownership, condominium, neighbour law), and agricultural production contracts.

Family law does not appear on that list. You are **not** required to apply to a mediator before filing for divorce.

The law does, however, treat part of family law as **capable of mediation** — meaning the parties may use mediation if they wish, and any settlement they reach will be binding.

## The Clearest Statutory Signal: Article 18/3

The most explicit proof that family law is not entirely closed to mediation is the wording of Article 18/3 of Law No. 6325:

> "The granting of the enforceability annotation is a matter of non-contentious jurisdiction and the examination is conducted on the file. However, **in family law disputes that are capable of mediation the examination is conducted with a hearing.**"

By using the phrase "family law disputes that are capable of mediation", the legislator acknowledged that such a category exists — while separating it from all other fields by requiring an **oral hearing** for the enforceability examination.

The rationale for the mandatory hearing is plain: in family law the need to protect the parties is regarded as higher, and the judge is expected to hear the parties in person.

## An Absolute Bar: Domestic Violence

The final sentence of Article 1/2 of Law No. 6325 sets out an absolute prohibition: **disputes involving an allegation of domestic violence are not capable of mediation.**

This bar cannot be overcome even with the consent of the parties. Its rationale is that the imbalance of power inherent in a violent relationship makes genuine negotiation impossible from the outset; an agreement that merely looks like a settlement would in fact mean the party in need of protection waiving their rights.

In practice this must be read broadly, covering not only allegations of physical violence but every situation in which a protection order under Turkish Law No. 6284 on the Protection of the Family and the Prevention of Violence Against Women is in place.

## Matters That Are Not Capable of Mediation: Claims Concerning Personal Status

For a dispute to be capable of mediation, the parties must be able to **dispose of it freely**. At the core of family law that condition is not met:

**Divorce itself.** Only a court can dissolve a marriage. The parties cannot agree in mediation that they are "divorced"; even an uncontested divorce requires, under Article 166/3 of the Turkish Civil Code, that the judge hear the parties in person and find the protocol appropriate.

**Custody.** The best interests of the child are a matter of public policy and the judge is not bound by the parties' agreement. Arrangements concerning custody are subject to court approval.

**Parentage.** Claims such as paternity, denial of parentage and acknowledgement lie entirely outside the parties' disposition.

**Nullity and annulment of marriage.** These concern public policy.

The Assembly of Civil Chambers of the Turkish Court of Cassation has emphasised that although relationships arising from family law are essentially matters of private law, the protection of the family and its healthy continuation require **greater State intervention** than in other branches of private law, and has held that Turkish Law No. 4787 on the Establishment and Procedure of Family Courts serves that purpose.

## The Field That Is Accepted as Capable of Mediation: Property Claims

The property-related part of family law, over which the parties may dispose freely, is accepted as capable of mediation:

- **Claims arising from the liquidation of the matrimonial property regime:** the participation claim, the added-value share and the contribution claim,
- Disputes over the spouses' **personal assets** and loan relationships between them,
- Claims for **pecuniary and non-pecuniary damages** as ancillary consequences of divorce (Article 174 of the Turkish Civil Code),
- Property arrangements concerning the family residence,
- Distribution disputes between heirs (which are in any event also subject to mandatory mediation under Article 18/B/1-b of Law No. 6325).

Liquidation of the matrimonial property regime is where mediation is most productively used in family law: the dispute is largely one of calculation and valuation, it can take years with court-appointed expert reports, and the parties' interests usually converge on a quick resolution.

## The Contested Field: Maintenance

Whether maintenance is capable of mediation is disputed in both scholarship and practice, and a distinction must be drawn according to the type of claim.

**Child maintenance** concerns the best interests of the child; the judge decides of their own motion and is not bound by the parties' agreement. The prevailing view is therefore that child maintenance is not capable of mediation.

Similar doubts arise for **poverty maintenance** and **interim maintenance**; because maintenance is forward-looking and adaptable to changing circumstances, binding it by a final settlement is regarded as problematic.

By contrast, **accrued maintenance arrears** — a monetary claim that has already fallen due for a past period — may be accepted as capable of mediation; this is no longer a maintenance arrangement but the collection of an existing debt.

The practical consequence: a request for an enforceability annotation on a mediation settlement concerning maintenance will be reviewed by the court for mediability during the oral examination under Article 18/3, and may be refused.

## What Happens If a Settlement Is Reached?

Where a settlement is reached in a family law dispute that is capable of mediation:

**1.** The settlement document is signed by the parties and the mediator. If it is signed jointly by the parties, their lawyers and the mediator, the document qualifies as an instrument in the nature of a court judgment without any annotation being required.

**2.** If an annotation is requested, the examination is conducted **with a hearing** under Article 18/3. This is the only exception specific to family law.

**3.** The scope of the examination remains narrow: whether the content of the settlement is **capable of mediation and of enforcement**. The court does not review whether the settlement is fair.

**4.** If mediation is used while a divorce action is pending, the annotation is requested from the court hearing that action, and the proceedings may be adjourned for up to three months under Article 15/5 of Law No. 6325.

## How Is Mediation Used Within a Divorce Action?

Even though divorce itself is not capable of mediation, mediation may be conducted over the **ancillary consequences** of the action — and in practice this brings substantial benefits.

The typical use is this: while the divorce action is pending, the parties settle in mediation the liquidation of the matrimonial property regime, damages and the division of assets; the divorce decree itself is granted by the court. The settlement document may be submitted to the case file and taken as the basis of the judgment, or enforced independently.

This separation removes from the litigation the matrimonial property dispute — the single most common reason divorce cases run for years.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q: Do I have to go to a mediator in order to divorce?

No. There is no condition-precedent mediation in family law; you may file the divorce action directly with the family court.

### Q: Can we agree on the divorce itself in mediation?

Only a court can dissolve a marriage; "divorce" cannot be agreed in mediation. Even in an uncontested divorce the judge must hear the parties in person and find the protocol appropriate. Mediation is, however, available for the property-related ancillary consequences of divorce.

### Q: Can custody be mediated?

Because custody concerns the best interests of the child, it is not a matter over which the parties may dispose freely, and the court is not bound by their agreement. The parties may reach an understanding on the arrangement of personal contact, but its legal effect depends on court approval.

### Q: My spouse was violent — can we still go to mediation?

No. Under Article 1/2 of Law No. 6325 disputes involving an allegation of domestic violence are not capable of mediation, and this bar cannot be overcome by the parties' consent. In that situation the protective and preventive measures under Turkish Law No. 6284 should be sought.

### Q: Does mediation really work for the division of matrimonial property?

Liquidation of the matrimonial property regime is largely a valuation and accounting dispute; in court it can run for years through expert reports. In mediation the parties can settle directly on how immovables are divided, what is paid and on what timetable; the settlement document can be enforced, and where a transfer of immovable property is required, registration can be completed at the land registry after the enforceability annotation without a formal deed being drawn up.
