TTK Art. 1166-1173 govern discharge and largely mirror the loading rules examined previously — same berthing logic, same notice-of-readiness mechanism, same discharge-time/demurrage pairing — but flip one important cost allocation and preserve a consequential asymmetry on who pays.

## Berthing and Cost Allocation

Art. 1166 mirrors the loading-berth rule: the master berths where the contract specifies, or awaits instructions in the designated waiting area if only a port or zone was agreed. Art. 1167 flips the loading-side cost split: while the cost of physically discharging cargo off the ship still falls on the carrier, everything beyond that point now falls on the consignee — not the shipper as at loading. The carrier's obligation stays fixed at the ship's boundary in both directions; only the counterparty on the other side of that boundary changes.

## Discharge Time and Demurrage

Art. 1168-1170 replicate the loading regime step for step: notice of readiness triggers discharge time the following calendar day, and — absent a contractual figure — discharge time defaults to what continuous 24-hour work would require. Demurrage, if agreed, defaults to ten days and starts automatically once discharge time expires. One textual addition matters: Art. 1168/4 lets notice go to whoever the bill of lading names, reflecting that by discharge, the bill has often changed hands by endorsement and the consignee may be a stranger to the original contract.

## The Key Asymmetry: Demurrage Debtor at Discharge

Art. 1171/4 is this batch's most important divergence: demurrage arising at the discharge port is owed by the shipper — not the consignee who is actually failing to unload. This reflects demurrage's contractual (not tortious) nature: the shipper, not the consignee, is the carrier's actual counterparty, and the consignee only becomes liable if the contract or bill of lading expressly says so. Unlike at the loading port, the carrier has no right here to withhold the ship pending payment — the voyage is already complete, so there is nothing left to withhold.

## Risk Allocation and Dispatch Money

Art. 1172 applies the same three-tier fortuitous-event allocation as loading (consignee's sphere counts against discharge time, carrier's sphere doesn't, shared events like storms extend discharge time but still accrue demurrage), and Art. 1173 lets carriers pay shippers dispatch money for early completion — void if used to dodge mandatory freight regulations.
