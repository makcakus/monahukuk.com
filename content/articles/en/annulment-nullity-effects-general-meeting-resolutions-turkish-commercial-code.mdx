Not every defective general assembly resolution is invalid in the same way or to the same degree. TTK Art. 445-451 build a graduated system — annulment (voidable), nullity (void), and, recognized by case law though not codified, non-existence — each with different plaintiffs, deadlines, and consequences.

## Who Can Sue for Annulment, and When

Under Art. 445, resolutions violating law, the articles, or — notably — the principle of good faith may be challenged within three months of the resolution date. Art. 446 lists who may sue: shareholders who attended and voted against, recording their dissent in the minutes; shareholders (present or not) alleging defective calls, improper agenda publication, unauthorized participants, or wrongful exclusion, provided the defect affected the outcome; the board; and board members individually where implementing the resolution would trigger personal liability. Turkish courts treat the "voted against and recorded dissent" requirement as a strict, cumulative procedural precondition, though timing of the dissent (before or after the vote) has divided the courts.

## Nullity: A Different, Harder Category

Art. 447 lists — non-exhaustively — resolutions that are void from the outset: those stripping a shareholder's inalienable rights (assembly participation, minimum voting rights, litigation rights), those improperly restricting information, inspection, and audit rights, and those undermining the company's fundamental structure or capital-protection rules. Unlike annulment, nullity is not subject to the three-month deadline or the limited plaintiff list — any interested party can seek a nullity finding at any time. Turkish courts have stressed that mixing up annulment grounds with nullity grounds is a real risk, and getting the categorization wrong is itself grounds for reversal.

## Procedure, Interim Relief, and Consequences

Art. 448 requires the board to publicize any annulment or nullity suit and its hearing date, bars hearings from starting before the three-month deadline expires, and lets the court require plaintiffs to post security against the company's potential losses. Art. 449's stay-of-execution mechanism — allowing the court to suspend a challenged resolution after hearing the board — is a specialized substitute for general interim injunction rules, not an addition to them. Once a judgment on annulment or nullity becomes final, it binds all shareholders, not just the litigants (Art. 450), and the board must register and publish it immediately. Art. 451 discourages abuse: shareholders who bring a bad-faith annulment or nullity action are jointly liable for the resulting company losses.
