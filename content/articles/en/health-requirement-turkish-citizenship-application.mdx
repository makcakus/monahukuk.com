The health condition is among the least discussed requirements in a Turkish citizenship application, yet it appears frequently in refused files. Applicants focus on the residence period, Turkish language proficiency, and the income condition, and assume that the health requirement the law imposes is a formality. In fact it is one of the enumerated conditions for an application, and failure to meet it is on its own a ground for refusal. Understanding the scope of the condition correctly prevents both needless anxiety and needless risk-taking.

## The Condition the Law Requires

Article 11 of Turkish Citizenship Law No. 5901 lists the conditions required of foreigners seeking to acquire Turkish citizenship by application. Subparagraph (ç) among those conditions reads: **not to have an illness constituting a danger from the point of view of public health.**

Taken as a whole, the article sets out the conditions as follows: to be of full age and capable of discernment according to one's own national law; to have resided in Türkiye continuously for five years counting back from the date of application; to have confirmed by one's conduct a decision to settle in Türkiye; **not to have an illness constituting a danger from the point of view of public health**; to be of good moral character; to be able to speak Turkish sufficiently; to have income or an occupation sufficient to provide for oneself and one's dependants in Türkiye; and not to be in a situation constituting an obstacle from the point of view of national security and public order.

The conditions are required cumulatively. Satisfying all the others does not remedy a failure to meet the health condition.

## What Does "A Danger from the Point of View of Public Health" Mean?

The wording used by the law must be read carefully. What is required is not that the applicant **be healthy**. What is required is that the applicant **not have an illness constituting a danger from the point of view of public health.**

The difference between the two formulations is decisive. The criterion is not the effect of the illness on the person's own health but **the danger it poses to public health.** A chronic condition, a disability, a past operation, or continuous medication — although these make a person unwell, they are not an obstacle in the sense the law intends so long as they pose no danger to others.

What the legislature had in view here are contagious illnesses that threaten public health. The same approach appears in subparagraph (f) of the first paragraph of Article 31 of Law No. 6458 on Foreigners and International Protection: a short-term residence permit for treatment purposes is granted **provided the applicant does not carry one of the diseases classified as a threat to public health**. The legislation systematically distinguishes between "being ill" and "constituting a threat to public health".

## The Condition's Relationship with the Other Conditions

The health condition is assessed independently of the other conditions in Article 11, but it intersects indirectly with some of them.

**Its relationship with the income and subsistence condition.** The law requires the applicant to have income or an occupation sufficient to provide for themselves and their dependants. A serious health situation that prevents work may create a problem not directly through the health condition but through the income condition. Being aware of this distinction determines which shortcoming the file should be strengthened against.

**Its relationship with the residence condition.** As regards the five-year continuous residence condition, long periods of treatment abroad may create a risk of interruption. The [effect of time spent abroad on a residence permit](/en/articles/absence-rules-turkish-residence-permit) should therefore be planned separately for applicants with a health condition.

## The Medical Report and Documentation

The second paragraph of Article 11 was repealed by Law No. 7039 of 19/10/2017; the article today consists of the single paragraph listing the application conditions. How the health condition is to be documented is not set out in detail in the law and has been left to practice and secondary regulation.

In practice a medical report obtained from an authorised health institution is added to the application file. Which institution issues the report and in what format is determined by the administration and may change over time; current practice should be confirmed before applying. The [documents required for a citizenship application](/en/articles/citizenship-application-documents-turkey) should be considered as a whole in this respect.

As to the content of the report, the practical point is this: the report must establish that the applicant **does not have** an illness constituting a danger from the point of view of public health. The appearance of an existing chronic condition in the report does not of itself produce a negative outcome; what is decisive is whether that condition poses a danger to public health.

## Refusal and Appeal

A refusal on the ground that the health condition is not satisfied is an administrative act and is subject to review by the administrative courts. In files of this kind the case should be built on two axes.

The first is **legal characterisation**: whether the health situation relied on by the administration genuinely meets the statutory criterion of "a danger from the point of view of public health". Characterising a personal ailment as a danger to public health may not be reconcilable with the wording of the law.

The second is **the facts**: fresh reports establishing the current state of health, and documents showing that treatment has been completed or that the condition is no longer contagious, may be produced. Because a state of health can change over time, the picture at the date on which the refusal was based may differ from the present one.

In an [appeal against the refusal of a citizenship application](/en/articles/turkish-citizenship-application-rejection-appeal), it is critical not to miss the time limit for bringing proceedings; the period begins to run upon service of the decision.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### I have a chronic illness — can I not obtain citizenship?

The law does not require you to be healthy but requires that you not have an illness constituting a danger from the point of view of public health. The criterion is danger to public health. A chronic condition is not an obstacle in the sense the law intends so long as it poses no danger to others.

### Is disability a bar to a citizenship application?

Disability does not of itself breach the health condition in Article 11 of Law No. 5901. However, if it prevents work, the income and subsistence condition in the same article will be assessed separately.

### Which illnesses are treated as a bar?

The law does not enumerate illnesses; it sets the criterion in the abstract as "constituting a danger from the point of view of public health". In practice contagious illnesses that threaten public health are assessed within this scope. The specific list should be confirmed from administrative regulation and current practice.

### What can I do if I am refused on health grounds?

The refusal is an administrative act and may be made the subject of proceedings before the administrative courts. The case may address both the lawfulness of the administration's characterisation and fresh reports establishing the current state of health. The time limit for bringing proceedings must be tracked from the date of service.
