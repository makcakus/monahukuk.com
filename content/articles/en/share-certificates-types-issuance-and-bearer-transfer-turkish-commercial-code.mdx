TTK Art. 484-490 define what a share certificate actually is, how it is produced, and how ownership of a bearer certificate changes hands — an area reshaped significantly by a 2020 amendment aimed at closing anonymity gaps in the financial system.

## Bearer or Registered, With One Key Restriction

Art. 484 allows share certificates to be issued as bearer or registered — but bans bearer certificates entirely for shares not yet fully paid; any issued in violation are void, though good-faith holders retain compensation claims. This protects the company's ability to pursue an unpaid shareholder, which becomes practically impossible once a bearer certificate has changed hands anonymously. Under Art. 485, converting registered shares to bearer form requires full payment first, closing the obvious workaround.

## Printing Certificates and Interim Certificates

Art. 486 voids shares issued before the company's (or a capital increase's) registration, though subscription obligations survive; anyone issuing shares before registration bears liability for resulting harm. For bearer shares, the board must print and distribute certificates within three months of full payment, and — since a 2020 amendment — bearer shareholders and their holdings must be reported to the Central Registry Agency before distribution. Interim certificates (ilmühaber) may be issued pending printing and are treated, by analogy, as registered share certificates — meaning Turkish courts require their transfer to follow the same endorsement-plus-delivery formalities, not a simple separate assignment document.

## Formal Requirements and the Bearer Transfer Regime

Art. 487 lists a certificate's mandatory contents (company name, capital, issue tranche, type, nominal value, number of shares represented) and requires signatures from at least two authorized signatories, with extra anti-forgery measures for closely held companies. Art. 488 lets a shareholder request a replacement for a worn or damaged certificate at their own cost.

The most consequential change sits in Art. 489: since Law No. 7262 (2020), a bearer share transfer takes effect against the company and third parties only once the transferee notifies the Central Registry Agency — mere possession transfer is no longer sufficient. Until notification, the holder cannot exercise statutory shareholder rights, and priority among competing claims is determined by notification date, not possession date. This shift, aimed at making beneficial ownership traceable for anti-money-laundering purposes, sits alongside the older, still-relevant principle that whoever lawfully possesses a bearer certificate is presumed its owner unless that possession is shown to have been acquired unlawfully.

## Registered Shares: Freely Transferable by Default

Art. 490 confirms registered shares are freely transferable unless law or the articles say otherwise — the topic of transfer restrictions covered separately — and that transfer by legal transaction requires both endorsement and actual delivery of possession; endorsement alone, without delivery, does not complete a transfer.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How is a bearer share certificate transferred?

Under TTK Article 489/1, <b>the transfer of bearer share certificates takes effect against the company and third parties only upon notification to the Central Registry Agency (MKK) by the transferee, made in addition to delivery of possession.</b> This MKK notification requirement, introduced by Law No. 7262 of 27 December 2020, has limited the anonymity of bearer shares and made the transfer traceable. Until notification is made, the transferee cannot exercise the rights arising from share ownership.

### Is it mandatory to print share certificates?

Yes, for bearer shares. Under TTK Article 486/2, <b>bearer share certificates must be printed and distributed to shareholders within three months from the date the share price is paid in full</b>, and the board of directors' resolution to this effect is registered, announced, and posted on the company's website; notification to the MKK is also made. For registered shares, the obligation to print arises upon the request of the minority. Under Article 486/1, <b>shares issued before the registration of the company or the capital increase are invalid</b>.

### Can a registered share be converted into a bearer share?

Yes. Under TTK Article 485/1, <b>unless otherwise provided in the articles of association, the type of the share may be changed by way of conversion.</b> Conversion is carried out and registered through an amendment to the articles of association. However, the limitation in Article 484/2 remains reserved: <b>bearer share certificates may not be issued for shares whose price has not been paid in full</b>; accordingly, a registered share whose price has not been paid cannot be converted into a bearer share.
