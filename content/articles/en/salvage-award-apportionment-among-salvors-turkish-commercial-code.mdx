Art. 1308 established that all salvage activity in one operation is covered by a single total award, drawn from one pool rather than separate claims. That leaves an obvious follow-up question: when more than one salvor took part, how is that single total actually split between them? Art. 1309 answers it directly.

## The Apportionment Rule

Under Art. 1309, where multiple salvors are involved, the award is divided among them in proportion to their participation in the salvage operation, using the same ten criteria set out in Art. 1305(1) — salved value, effort to prevent environmental harm, degree of success, nature and extent of the danger, skill and effort shown, time and expense incurred, risk assumed, promptness, and the readiness and effectiveness of equipment used.

This reuse of the Art. 1305 criteria serves two distinct functions in salvage law. The first — its original role — is fixing how much the *owner* owes in total. The second, under Art. 1309, is dividing that same total among the salvors themselves. The logic carries over cleanly: a salvor that took on greater danger or acted with more skill and speed drives up both the size of the award the owner pays and that salvor's own share of it.

## Why Multi-Salvor Operations Are the Norm

This provision matters in practice because Turkish salvage law actively encourages multiple salvors to take part in a single operation — a salvor may call in additional help on its own initiative, and is separately obligated to accept further salvors when the owner or master reasonably requests it. Having created that expectation, the law needed a companion rule ensuring each participant is rewarded in proportion to what it actually contributed; otherwise salvors would be pushed into joint operations without a fair mechanism for splitting the reward.

## Practical Takeaway

For salvors operating alongside others — separate tugboat operators, crews, or companies — a share of the award is not fixed by who arrived first or who negotiated the contract, but by weighing each participant's contribution against the same ten Art. 1305 factors. Documenting time on scene, resources deployed, and risk taken is therefore directly relevant to the eventual split, not just to the overall award size.

No Court of Cassation ruling has been found applying Art. 1309 by its current article number. An Istanbul Commercial Court decision (a multi-tugboat shipyard fire response) illustrates the underlying method well, however: after allocating an overall contribution ratio between the responding units, the court calculated one tugboat operator's individual share based on the actual hours it spent on scene relative to the other vessels involved — a concrete example of "participation" being measured in hard, comparable terms rather than by rough impression.
