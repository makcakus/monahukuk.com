Among the questions foreign families living in Türkiye ask most often is the legal position of a child born there. The question has three distinct layers, and they should not be confused: notification of the birth to the civil registry, establishing the child's residence status, and citizenship. This article treats each separately and sets out the steps to be taken in the first months.

## Birth Notification: The Thirty-Day Period

Article 15 of Civil Registration Services Law No. 5490 governs birth notification without limiting it to citizenship: **every child born alive must be notified to the civil registry office within thirty days of birth if in Türkiye, and to a mission abroad within sixty days.** The same article provides that birth notifications **may also be made to the health institution where the birth took place**.

A further provision eases the process in practice: births occurring in health institutions or under the supervision of health personnel are notified by the relevant health unit to the local civil registry office **within five working days**. Where the birth takes place in hospital, the first notification is usually made by the institution; that does not, however, discharge the family's own notification duty. Obtaining the birth report and completing the process at the civil registry office remains for the family to do.

## The Baby's Residence Status: An Interim Safeguard

For a foreign baby to remain in Türkiye, a travel document (passport) is needed first, and then a residence permit. The passport is obtained from the consulate in Türkiye of the state of the family's nationality, which can take several weeks.

The legislation resolves that gap. Under the article of the Regulation on the Implementation of the Law on Foreigners and International Protection governing residence permit applications, **foreign children born in Türkiye may remain in Türkiye on their birth certificate, tied to the validity period of the mother's or father's residence permit, until a travel document is issued and a residence permit application is made.** This is an interim safeguard that prevents the baby falling into irregular status while consular formalities are pending.

The limit of the safeguard is equally clear: the protection is bounded by **the validity period of the mother's or father's residence permit**. If the parent's permit expires, so does the basis for the baby's stay. The parent's permit expiry date and the baby's application timetable must therefore be planned together in the period following the birth.

The baby's residence permit is, as a rule, structured according to the parent's permit type. Where a parent holds a work permit or a short-term residence permit in Türkiye, a [family residence permit](/en/articles/family-residence-permit-dependent-children-turkey) or another suitable permit type is assessed for the child. The application generally requires the birth certificate, the child's passport, the parents' identity and permit documents, address registration, and valid health insurance; the [health insurance requirement](/en/articles/health-insurance-requirement-residence-permit-turkey) must be satisfied separately for infants as well.

## Citizenship: Being Born in Türkiye Is Not Enough on Its Own

Turkish nationality law rests on the **principle of descent**; the place-of-birth principle is admitted only as a narrow exception. Article 7 of Turkish Citizenship Law No. 5901 addresses three situations:

- A child born, in or outside Türkiye, **to a Turkish mother or father within marriage** is a Turkish citizen,
- A child born **to a Turkish mother and a foreign father outside marriage** is a Turkish citizen,
- A child born **to a Turkish father and a foreign mother outside marriage** acquires Turkish citizenship upon completion of the procedures establishing descent.

The conclusion is clear: where both parents are foreign, birth in Türkiye does not confer Turkish citizenship on the child. There is no general place-of-birth citizenship rule in Türkiye.

The sole exception appears in Article 8: **a child born in Türkiye who cannot acquire the citizenship of any country at birth by reason of their foreign mother and father is a Turkish citizen from birth.** Paragraph two of the same article provides that a child found in Türkiye is deemed to have been born there unless the contrary is established. This provision is directed at preventing statelessness, and its application requires examination of the nationality law of the parents' states; for detail, see [stateless children born in Türkiye](/en/articles/citizenship-stateless-persons-born-turkey).

If a parent later acquires Turkish citizenship, the child's position is assessed separately; that outcome turns on the circumstances at the time of acquisition, not at birth.

## Consular Registration and Dual Citizenship

Notifying the birth to the Turkish civil registry does not enter the child in the registers of their own country. The family must also apply to the consulate in Türkiye of their state of nationality to register the birth. Consulates usually require an **apostilled** and translated copy of the birth certificate issued by the Turkish authorities, so building the document chain correctly from the outset matters. The [apostille and translation rules](/en/articles/apostille-translation-document-legalization-turkey) are decisive at this stage.

Where one parent is a Turkish citizen, the child may acquire both Turkish citizenship and the other parent's nationality. Turkish law permits dual citizenship as a rule; but if the other state's law restricts it, the outcome is determined by that state's legislation.

## Steps for the First Three Months

The sequence after the birth is:

- Obtain the birth report or certificate from the hospital,
- Notify the birth to the civil registry office within the thirty-day period and obtain the birth certificate,
- Complete the apostille and sworn translation of the certificate,
- Register the birth at the consulate of the state of nationality and obtain a passport for the baby,
- Make the residence permit application to the Provincial Directorate of Migration Management as soon as the passport is issued — do not wait, as the interim safeguard is limited to the parent's permit period,
- Register the baby's address and obtain valid health insurance,
- Where one parent is a Turkish citizen, initiate the descent-based procedures for entry in the Turkish civil register.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### My child was born in Türkiye — will they be a Turkish citizen?

Not if both parents are foreign. Turkish nationality law rests on descent. The sole exception is where the child cannot acquire the citizenship of any country at birth by reason of their foreign mother and father; in that case the child is a Turkish citizen from birth.

### Is the baby in irregular status until the passport is issued?

No. Foreign children born in Türkiye may remain on their birth certificate, tied to the validity period of the mother's or father's residence permit, until a travel document is issued and a residence permit application is made. That protection is, however, bounded by the parent's permit period.

### What happens if we do not notify the birth within thirty days?

The statutory period is thirty days, and missing it creates both a risk of administrative sanction and delay in obtaining documents. The Civil Registration Services Law provides that no document charge is taken for identity cards issued where the birth notification is made within the statutory period; missing the deadline therefore has a concrete cost.

### The mother is a Turkish citizen, the father foreign, and we are not married. What is the child's position?

A child born to a Turkish mother and a foreign father outside marriage is directly a Turkish citizen under Article 7 of Law No. 5901. In the converse case — Turkish father and foreign mother, outside marriage — citizenship depends on completion of the procedures establishing descent.
