With the shipowner's contribution debts and security mechanisms covered, the series turns to how the general average adjustment itself — the technical report that quantifies each party's contribution — gets commissioned in the first place (TTK Art. 1278).

## The Shipowner's Duty to Commission the Adjustment

Under Art. 1278(1), the shipowner must have the adjustment prepared without delay after declaring general average. This duty falls on the shipowner because it is typically the shipowner (or the master acting on the shipowner's behalf) who declares the general average act in the first place, and who is closest to the facts. Failure to act promptly makes the shipowner liable to each interested party individually — a deliberately broad exposure meant to discourage foot-dragging, since the adjustment process can otherwise take months and delay every creditor's recovery.

## A Backup Right for Other Interested Parties

If the adjustment is not commissioned in time, Art. 1278(2) lets any interested party — expressly including insurers — request and arrange for it themselves. Insurers are named specifically because they are often the ones who will actually pay out the contribution share and therefore have a direct financial stake in getting the calculation done quickly.

## When the Adjuster Refuses

Art. 1278(3) addresses what happens if the adjuster refuses to prepare the report on the ground that the event does not qualify as general average at all. The adjuster is a technical expert, not a judge, so this refusal is not final: any interested party (insurers included) may apply to the court at the place designated under Art. 1279 (essentially the port where the voyage ends), which decides the question — on the file or after hearing the parties — under simplified (summary) procedure.

## Practical Takeaway

For foreign counterparties, the key point is that inaction by the shipowner does not stall the process indefinitely — any interested party, including the cargo insurer, can step in to get the adjustment moving, and disputes over whether the event even qualifies get resolved quickly through summary proceedings rather than a full trial.
