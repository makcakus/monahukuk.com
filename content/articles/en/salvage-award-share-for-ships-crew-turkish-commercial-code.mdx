Art. 1309 covers how the total award is split *between* multiple salvors. Art. 1310 turns to a different split: *within* a single salvor, between the shipowner who collects the award and the master and crew who actually carried out the operation.

## Owner Deducts First, Then Shares With the Crew

Under Art. 1310(1), where property is salvaged by a ship, the owner of that salvaging vessel first deducts any damage the ship suffered and expenses incurred in the operation from the award collected, then distributes a share of what remains to the master and crew, again applying the Art. 1305(1) criteria. The award is legally paid to the owner — the salvage contract is typically made on the owner's behalf by the master — but the people who actually took the risk and did the work are the crew, so the law guarantees them a cut of the proceeds.

## Distribution Schedule, Objection Window, and Finality

As soon as the salvage operation ends, the owner must prepare a schedule showing each crew member's share and notify it to them in writing. Any objection must be filed within 15 days of arrival at the first Turkish port reached after notification; the court then hears the parties and approves the schedule, as drafted or amended, and that decision is final — not subject to further appeal. This tight sequence mirrors a pattern seen elsewhere in this chapter: because a salvage operation has a clear beginning and end, the law wants entitlements settled quickly rather than left open for years of litigation.

## Exception: Dedicated Salvage Vessels and Tugboats

Where the salvage was carried out by a vessel or tugboat specifically dedicated to that purpose, none of the above applies — and crew serving on such a vessel cannot claim any separate award or share, whether from the owner or directly from the owners of the salvaged property. The reasoning mirrors a rule seen earlier in this series exempting a distressed vessel's own crew from claiming salvage pay for routine efforts to save their own ship: professional salvage crew are already paid by their employer to perform exactly this work, so no additional reward is due. A chain of Antalya court rulings — trial court, regional court of appeal, a Court of Cassation reversal, and a final regional appellate decision on remand — consistently applied this exception under the old, pre-2012 Commercial Code, whose relevant provision was structurally near-identical to today's Art. 1310(5); none of these rulings cites the current article number directly, which should be noted honestly.

## Non-Vessel Salvage: Contract Comes First

Where the rescuing party is not a ship at all — a land vehicle, a helicopter, or manual rescuers, for example — the award is split between the salvor and its personnel according to their contract, and only by analogy to the Art. 1305 criteria if there is no contract. The detailed schedule-and-objection procedure is reserved for the more standardized owner-crew relationship found on ships.

## Practical Takeaway

For shipowners and salvage operators, this means crew compensation planning should track the distinction sharply: ordinary crew on a general-purpose vessel that happens to perform a salvage are entitled to a statutory share through the formal schedule process, while personnel on a vessel dedicated to salvage work are not — their compensation is purely a matter of their employment terms.
