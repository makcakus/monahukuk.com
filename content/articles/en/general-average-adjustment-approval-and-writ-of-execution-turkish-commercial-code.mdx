Once objections have been raised (or not), the court must resolve them and decide whether — and how — to approve the general average adjustment (TTK Art. 1282-1284).

## Approval, With or Without Objections

Under Art. 1282(1), if no objection was raised at or before the hearing, the adjustment is approved outright. If an objection was raised, the interested parties are heard; if the objection is found well-founded, or the parties otherwise reach agreement, the adjustment is corrected accordingly and then approved. Art. 1282(2) allows partial approval: if an objection cannot be resolved immediately, the parts of the report outside its scope are approved by a separate decision, while the hearing continues on the disputed part — so unobjected portions do not have to wait for a full resolution.

## Procedure and Its Hierarchy

Art. 1283 applies simplified (summary) procedure to the approval and objection process, but expressly subject to the special rules of Art. 1281 and 1282 (notice periods, the first-hearing objection deadline, default approval, partial approval) — those special rules take priority, and the general simplified procedure fills in only what they do not cover.

## The Approval Decision as a Writ of Execution

The most consequential rule is in Art. 1284(1): once the approval decision becomes final, it has the force of a writ of execution for the claims listed in the report — meaning the creditor can go straight to enforcement proceedings without filing a separate lawsuit to collect. Importantly, if the report was never objected to, the approval decision has this force even before it becomes final — there is no need to wait out the appeal window on an uncontested report. Turkish courts have treated this directly as a matter of legal interest: a party who could have sought approval to obtain a writ of execution, but instead filed a separate collection lawsuit, risks having that lawsuit dismissed for lack of legal interest.

## A Limit: No Effect Against Improperly Summoned Parties

Art. 1284(2) provides an important safeguard: the approval writ has no effect against any interested party who was not properly summoned to the hearing — reinforcing how seriously the notice requirements in Art. 1281 are taken.
