Having closed out sea passenger carriage, the series now turns to an entirely new topic within maritime law: general average, governed by TTK Art. 1272–1285. This article covers the foundational definition.

## Ancient Roots, Modern Application

General average is one of the oldest institutions in maritime law, tracing back to the Rhodian Sea Law (*lex Rhodia de iactu*). Its core intuition: if cargo is jettisoned in a storm to save the ship, that sacrifice should not fall solely on the cargo owner whose goods were thrown overboard — because the act was taken to save the ship, the remaining cargo, and the freight together. TTK Art. 1272 is the modern Turkish expression of this centuries-old principle: extraordinary sacrifice or expenditure incurred to save a ship, its cargo, and freight from a common peril is shared proportionally among all the interests that benefited.

## The Four Elements of a General Average Act

Art. 1272(1) defines a "general average act" as requiring four cumulative elements. First, a *common maritime adventure* — the peril must threaten the ship, cargo, other property, and freight together, not just one isolated cargo interest. Second, an *extraordinary* sacrifice or expenditure — distinguishing it from routine operating costs like pilotage or port dues. Third, the act must be undertaken *intentionally* — this is what separates general average from an accident: jettisoning cargo to prevent capsizing is a deliberate decision, unlike the capsizing itself. Fourth, the act must be carried out in a *reasonable manner* — an objective standard requiring the measure to be proportionate to the danger, not excessive or reckless. Only losses and expenses that are the *direct* consequence of such an act qualify as general average.

## Avoided Expenses Also Count

Art. 1272(2) extends the principle: if an alternative, typically cheaper measure is taken specifically to *avoid* an expense that would otherwise qualify as general average, that alternative expense also counts toward general average — up to the amount that would have been spent otherwise — even if other parties incidentally benefit from it too. This keeps the captain's incentives aligned toward the most economical solution to a common danger, rather than pushing them toward a more expensive "classic" general average act out of concern that a cheaper alternative would not be shared.

## The Sharing Principle

Art. 1272(3) confirms that losses and expenses falling within general average are shared among ship, cargo, freight, and other property according to the Chapter's rules — with the mechanics of that sharing (contribution amounts, valuation, and personal debtors) left to the articles that follow.

## Case Law

Turkish courts have repeatedly cited this four-element definition verbatim. A 2018 Court of Cassation decision (11th Civil Chamber, 2016/13815-2018/4816), later revisited on a correction-of-decision motion in 2020 (2018/4685-2020/3211), confirmed a carrier's declaration of general average following an engine breakdown mid-voyage, applying the same four-element test and addressing the standing question for direct recovery of a contribution share based on an adjuster's average statement.
