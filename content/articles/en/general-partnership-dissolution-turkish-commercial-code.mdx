TTK Art. 243-266 address two related but distinct questions: when does a Turkish general partnership dissolve entirely, and when can a single partner leave — by choice, expulsion, death, or bankruptcy — without ending the partnership itself. The Code generally favors keeping the business alive over dissolving it outright.

## Grounds for Dissolution

Art. 243 lists the triggers for dissolution, referencing the Code of Obligations' general partnership-dissolution grounds plus partnership-specific ones: bankruptcy, loss of two-thirds of capital without a remedial resolution, merger, failure to register within the statutory window, or a partner's bankruptcy. "Just cause" for dissolution under Art. 245 includes a partner's breach of trust, neglect of core duties, misuse of the trade name or assets, or a permanent incapacity — though a partner who caused the just cause personally cannot invoke it to seek dissolution.

## Keeping the Partnership Alive: Exit Instead of Dissolution

Rather than dissolve the whole entity over one partner's situation, the Code offers alternatives. On a partner's death, if the agreement contains no continuation clause, the partnership may continue with the heirs by unanimous consent, or the heirs may be bought out (Art. 253); if the agreement does provide for continuation, heirs have three months to elect between joining as full partners or as limited partners in a related structure. A partner's bankruptcy permits their expulsion, with their share paid into the bankruptcy estate (Art. 254). Where just cause exists, the other partners may expel the offending partner by unanimous (or contractually reduced) vote instead of dissolving the partnership, subject to a three-month period for the expelled partner to challenge the decision in court (Art. 255). In a two-partner partnership, the court can award the entire business to one partner and expel the other without a full liquidation (Art. 257).

## Valuing and Paying the Departing Partner's Share

An exiting or expelled partner's share is valued as of the exit date (or the closest prior date in a dispute) and must be paid in cash, typically at the next balance-sheet date (Art. 260-262). Creditors' claims against a departed partner for partnership debts prescribe three years after the exit is published in the Turkish Trade Registry Gazette (Art. 264) — publication is what fixes the departing partner's ongoing exposure. Turkish courts have required close scrutiny of a company's books when calculating a departing or deceased partner's share, particularly regarding claimed loans to the partnership.

Investors structuring an exit from a Turkish general partnership should treat timely registration and publication of the departure as the single most important step for limiting future liability exposure.
