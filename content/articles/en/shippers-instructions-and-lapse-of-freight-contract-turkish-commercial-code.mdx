TTK Art. 1232-1233 close out the delivery rules by addressing two remaining questions: how far a shipper's instructions can redirect delivery, and what happens to those rules if the freight contract itself ends early.

## Order Bills: All Originals Must Be Returned Before Instructions Are Followed

Because an "order" bill defaults to the shipper's own order, the shipper retains a degree of control over delivery even after loading. But Art. 1232(1) limits that control tightly: the captain may follow the shipper's instructions to redirect or redeliver cargo only if every original of the bill has been returned. The same restriction applies if any holder requests early delivery before the ship reaches the discharge port (Art. 1232(2)) — because an outstanding original could be in the hands of a bank or downstream buyer with its own claim. A captain who ignores this leaves the carrier liable to the true legitimate holder (Art. 1232(3)); Turkish courts have also confirmed a party lacking shipper status on the bill has no authority to redirect delivery at all, regardless of instructions given.

## Straight Bills: Consent Can Substitute for Surrender

Where the bill is not an order bill, Art. 1232(4) allows delivery without any original being presented at all, provided both the shipper and the named consignee consent — since no negotiable third-party holder can exist under a straight bill. The carrier may still demand security against potential loss if not all originals are accounted for. Turkish courts have found this provision breached — alongside Art. 1230(1) — where cargo was released without any documented consent from both shipper and consignee. Separately, courts have also held that releasing cargo without the bill does not automatically create liability where no real causal loss follows, such as in cash-against-documents sales where the seller already accepted payment risk.

## Same Rules Apply if the Contract Lapses Early

Art. 1233 confirms that if the freight contract lapses — automatically or by termination — before the ship reaches its destination, the same Art. 1232 delivery rules still govern who gets the surviving cargo. This shows the bill of lading operates independently of the underlying contract's fate: even after the contract ends, someone still needs a documented, legitimate basis to claim the goods.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can the shipper demand return of the cargo?

Only by returning all copies of the bill of lading. Under TTK Article 1232/1, <b>if an order bill of lading has been issued, the master complies with the shipper's instruction concerning the return or delivery of the goods only if all copies of the bill of lading are returned to him</b>. This requirement arises from the bill of lading's function of representing the goods: while the document is in circulation, the rights of third parties who rely on it must be protected.

### Why are all copies required?

Because, under Article 1230/2, each copy is independently sufficient to demand delivery. If one remaining copy has been transferred to a third party, that person may demand the goods as a holder in good faith. Recovering all copies ensures that the document is fully withdrawn from circulation and frees the carrier from the <b>risk of double delivery</b>.

### Does this rule disappear if the contract comes to an end?

No. Under TTK Article 1233/1, <b>the provision of Article 1232 also applies where the contract of carriage lapses, automatically or as a result of termination under Articles 1209 to 1227, due to an unforeseen event before the vessel reaches the port of destination</b>. That is, termination of the contract does not affect the bill of lading's character as a negotiable instrument or the rights of its holder; the master must still demand all copies.
