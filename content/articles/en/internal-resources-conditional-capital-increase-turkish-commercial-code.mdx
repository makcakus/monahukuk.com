Beyond the subscription-based increase covered under TTK Art. 456-461, the Code recognizes two more mechanisms: converting existing company resources into capital, and a self-executing increase tied to bondholders' or employees' rights. TTK Art. 462-472 cover both.

## Internal Resources: No New Money, Just Reclassification

Under Art. 462, unrestricted reserves and legally eligible funds can be converted into capital. Unlike a subscription increase — where genuinely new capital (cash, in-kind, receivables) enters the company — this simply reclassifies existing balance-sheet items, verified by an approved annual balance sheet and the board's written confirmation (refreshed if older than six months). The article's most litigated rule sits at the end of Art. 462(3): any legally eligible fund must be converted into capital before — or simultaneously and proportionally with — any subscription-based increase; upon registration, existing shareholders automatically receive bonus shares proportional to their holdings, a right that cannot be removed, limited, or waived. Turkish courts have held that a single, one-off denial of a shareholder's bonus shares is grounds for annulment (subject to the standard three-month deadline), while a permanent contractual waiver or restriction of the right itself is void as against this mandatory rule. Once registered, however, the resolution's nullity or non-existence generally cannot be challenged.

## Conditional Capital Increase: Self-Executing by Design

Art. 463 lets the general assembly authorize, via the articles, a capital increase that occurs automatically as bondholders or employees exercise conversion or purchase rights tied to bonds or similar debt instruments — the assembly sets the ceiling and conditions in advance, but the actual increase happens without further assembly action each time. Two hard caps protect against abuse: the conditionally increased capital cannot exceed half of existing capital, and payment must be at least equal to nominal value (Art. 464).

## Advance Disclosure and Protecting Both Sides

Because this mechanism bypasses per-instance assembly approval, Art. 465 requires the articles to spell out the increase amount, share details, eligible groups, any preemptive-right limitation, privileges, and transfer restrictions in advance — rights granted before this articles provision is registered are void. Art. 466 gives existing shareholders first refusal on new convertible instruments (limitable only for justified reasons), and Art. 467 protects rights-holders from having their conversion or purchase rights blocked by transfer restrictions or diluted without compensation. Under Art. 468, shareholder rights actually arise only once the conversion right is exercised and payment made. At year-end, the board consolidates the period's actual conversions into the articles within three months (Art. 470-471), and once conversion/purchase rights expire entirely, the board removes the enabling clause from the articles (Art. 472).
