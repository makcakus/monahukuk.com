Unpaid dues, annulment of a management board resolution, an enclosed balcony, an encroachment on a common area — because the neighbourly relationship continues, apartment and residential-complex disputes are often not truly resolved even when they are "won" by a court judgment. Since 1 September 2023, applying to a mediator before bringing an action in these disputes has been compulsory, and this requirement is perhaps the arrangement best suited to the nature of the field.

## Scope: Every Dispute Arising from Law No. 634

Article 18/B/1(c) of Law No. 6325: **disputes arising from Condominium Law No. 634 of 23/6/1965** fall within mandatory mediation.

Because no types of action are enumerated, the scope is broad:

- dues and common-expense claims, together with default compensation;
- annulment of resolutions of the assembly of condominium owners;
- liability, dismissal and accounting obligations of the manager;
- prevention of interference with common areas and restoration to the former state;
- remedying breaches of the project and of the management plan;
- disputes arising from the manner of use of an independent unit (conversion between dwelling and workplace, restrictions on letting);
- intolerable conduct of an owner or a tenant (Article 33 of Law No. 634);
- determination of the shares of participation in common expenses.

The 5th Civil Chamber of the Court of Cassation construes the scope broadly; it has accepted that Article 18/B also applies to a **negative declaratory action** brought against enforcement proceedings based on non-payment of dues and common expenses arising from Law No. 634.

## Two Routes for Dues Claims

In dues claims the management has two options, and the mediation condition affects them differently.

**Enforcement proceedings without judgment.** The management may commence enforcement proceedings directly for unpaid dues; no application to mediation is required for this. In addition, default compensation under Article 20 and the statutory lien under Article 22 of Law No. 634 come into play.

**The judicial route.** If the proceedings are opposed and an action for annulment of the opposition is to be brought, or if a debt claim is to be brought directly, the mediation condition applies.

In practice the fastest route for most managements is proceedings without judgment; but the mediation stage must be planned for in case an opposition is filed.

## Time Pressure in the Annulment of Board Resolutions

Article 33 of Law No. 634 lays down narrow periods for the annulment of a resolution of the assembly of owners: an owner who dissents from the resolution or did not attend the meeting may apply to the court within **one month** of learning of the resolution and in any event within **six months** of the date of the resolution.

These are forfeiture periods and the mediation stage must be completed within them. The good news: from the application to the mediation bureau until the date the final minutes are drawn up, **no forfeiture period runs**. Since the mediator must conclude the process within three weeks (four in compulsory circumstances), staying within the period is feasible — but the application must not be delayed.

## The Question of Who the Party Is

The most common procedural error in apartment and complex disputes is directing the application to **the wrong addressee**.

The assembly of condominium owners is not a legal person; under Article 38 of Law No. 634 the manager is liable to the owners **exactly as an agent**. The power to sue and to pursue enforcement is governed by Article 35: among the manager's duties are bringing actions and enforcement proceedings against owners who fail to perform their condominium debts and obligations, and having the statutory lien registered in the condominium register.

Accordingly:

- where the management claims dues from an owner, the application is made by the manager;
- where an owner has claims against the management, the addressee is the manager or the other owners, depending on the type of action;
- in the annulment of a board resolution, the action may have to be directed against all owners, and the mediation application should be made accordingly.

In a decision of the 9th Civil Chamber of the Court of Cassation, bringing an action against a complex management where the mandatory mediation meetings had not taken place with that management was held to warrant dismissal on procedural grounds for failure of the condition of action. Correctly identifying the addressee is therefore a technical necessity.

## Arbitration Clauses and the Management Plan

Some management plans contain provisions that disputes are to be resolved by arbitration. Under Article 18/A/18 of Law No. 6325, the provisions on mediation as a condition of action do not apply where there is an arbitration agreement.

But this does not mean that every management-plan provision displaces the mediation condition. In a dispute concerning judicial intervention and the annulment of a resolution of the assembly of owners, the 5th Civil Chamber upheld a regional court decision holding that **an arbitration clause in the management plan cannot apply to a dispute requiring judicial intervention under Article 33 of Law No. 634** — because a breach of the mandatory provisions of the statute is not among the matters left to the will of the two parties. The same decision also established that, the mediation meetings having ended in non-settlement before the date of the action, the mandatory mediation condition had been satisfied.

## If You Settle: The Annotation Is Compulsory

Under Article 18/B/3, obtaining the annotation as to the enforceability of the settlement document **is compulsory**. Competence is divided in two: for settlement documents relating to immovable property, the civil court of peace of **the place where the property is located**; for other settlement documents, that of **the place where the mediator performed the duty**.

A settlement document concerning a dues claim falls as a rule into the "other" category and the annotation is obtained from the court of the place where the mediator acted. By contrast, in settlements concerning the removal of interference with a common area or the creation of a right over an independent unit, the court of the place where the property is located is competent.

In drafting the settlement document the obligation must be **amenable to enforcement**. A directly enforceable obligation such as "the owner shall remove the structure in the common area within 30 days" should be written; expressions requiring a constitutive result, such as "the parties shall amend the management plan", should be avoided. The 12th Civil Chamber of the Court of Cassation has stated that a debtor cannot be compelled to conclude a contract on the basis of a settlement document that merely evidences a promise and contains no constitutive pronouncement.

## Practical Notes for Complex Managements

**A collective application is possible but must be individualised.** Where dues are claimed from several owners, there is a separate dispute in respect of each; the application must be made in due form for each of them.

**Attendance is compulsory.** A party — including the management — that fails to attend the first meeting without a valid excuse is liable, even if it wins the action, for half of the other side's litigation costs and half of the attorney's fee determined under the Minimum Attorney's Fee Tariff.

**Document the authority.** The manager's authority to apply and to sign a settlement must rest on a resolution of the assembly of owners and on the management plan. If the settlement amount exceeds the manager's authority, a resolution of the assembly must be obtained.

**Have the records ready.** Dues accrual schedules, the resolution book, the management plan and service documents are decisive both in the negotiation and in any subsequent action.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q: I am going to commence enforcement proceedings for unpaid dues — must I go to mediation?

Not for enforcement proceedings without judgment. But if the proceedings are opposed and an action for annulment of the opposition is to be brought, or if a debt claim is brought directly, the mediation condition applies.

### Q: I want a resolution of the assembly annulled — are the periods running?

The one-month and six-month periods in Article 33 of Law No. 634 are forfeiture periods; but they do not run from the application to the mediation bureau until the date the final minutes are drawn up. Not delaying the application remains critical.

### Q: My neighbour has enclosed their balcony — which scope am I in?

An alteration to an independent unit contrary to the project is a dispute arising from Law No. 634 and falls within Article 18/B/1(c). If the dispute also concerns neighbour law, Article 18/B/1(ç) comes into play as well; in either case the result is the same — mediation is compulsory.

### Q: I am going to sue the complex management — to whom should I direct the application?

The addressee varies with the type of action. For claims concerning the acts of the management the addressee is the manager; in the annulment of a board resolution it is as a rule the other owners. An application directed to the wrong addressee may result in the condition of action not being satisfied.

### Q: Our management plan contains an arbitration clause — must I still go to mediation?

The 5th Civil Chamber has accepted that an arbitration clause in the management plan cannot apply to disputes requiring judicial intervention under Article 33 of Law No. 634; the mediation condition operates in such disputes. In other disputes that are amenable to arbitration, the provisions on mediation as a condition of action do not apply under Article 18/A/18 of Law No. 6325.

### Q: Can a tenant also be a party to the mediation process?

Law No. 634 imposes certain obligations on tenants too (for example liability for common expenses and intolerable conduct within Article 33). If the dispute arises from the tenant's conduct, the application may also have to be directed to the tenant; this is determined by the nature of the file.
