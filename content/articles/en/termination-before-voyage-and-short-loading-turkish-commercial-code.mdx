TTK Art. 1158-1160 turn the loading-time mechanics into concrete legal consequences: what happens when the shipper walks away, or loading never happens, or happens only partly.

## The Shipper's Late-Stage Termination Right

Art. 1158 gives the shipper a strikingly broad termination right: the voyage charter can be terminated any time up until the ship actually departs — even after loading is complete. The price is set by Art. 1158(2): the carrier can claim lost profit and accrued claims, with a default presumption of 30% of the agreed total freight as lost profit if the amount is in doubt — a practical shortcut sparing the carrier from proving lost earnings line by line. But this is offset by a mitigation rule: any income the carrier earns from new freight contracts during what would have been the voyage period is deducted. If cargo was already loaded, the carrier must wait for it to be unloaded (a period not counted toward laytime/demurrage) and can claim all resulting costs, with a floor set at no less than the demurrage rate. Turkish courts have confirmed that the fact a ship's route or schedule was unaffected does not by itself defeat this indemnity claim.

## When Loading Never Starts

Art. 1159 gives the carrier, once laytime and any demurrage period expire with no loading started, a choice: treat the contract as terminated (triggering Art. 1158 rights, but requiring written notice once the waiting period ends) or keep waiting — in which case the carrier can claim its full resulting loss from the shipper.

## Short Loading

Art. 1160 handles partial loading in three tiers: if the carrier waits at the shipper's request beyond laytime, it can claim resulting costs and loss; if the shipper insists on departure with cargo still incomplete, the carrier must comply but recovers full freight as if fully loaded, accrued demurrage, resulting costs, and any needed extra security — minus freight earned from replacement cargo taking the unused space; if neither instruction is given, the carrier can demand instructions by written notice within a set deadline, defaulting to Art. 1158 termination rights if none arrives.

## Practical Takeaway

Turkish courts have confirmed both halves of the Art. 1158(2) formula apply strictly: the 30% presumption stands even absent proven lost time, but the carrier's own subsequent earnings during the would-be voyage period are deducted from the award.
