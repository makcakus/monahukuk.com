Articles 124 through 133 of the Turkish Commercial Code (TTK) lay the common groundwork for the five commercial company types the Code goes on to regulate individually: general partnership (kollektif), limited partnership (komandit), joint stock company, limited liability company, and cooperative.

## The Person-Company vs. Capital-Company Distinction

Art. 124(2) classifies general and limited partnerships as "person companies," and joint stock companies, limited liability companies, and share-capital limited partnerships as "capital companies." This is not an abstract label: in person companies, partners bear personal, unlimited liability for company debts, while in capital companies, liability is generally limited to the capital contributed or committed. This distinction shapes many later rules on mergers, demergers, and conversions covered elsewhere in the Code.

## Legal Personality and What Can Be Contributed as Capital

All commercial companies have legal personality and, under the Civil Code's general rule for legal entities, may hold nearly any right or obligation a natural person can (Art. 125). Where a specific rule is missing for a given company type, the general rules on legal entities apply first, then the Code of Obligations' rules on ordinary (adi) partnership (Art. 126). Art. 127 lists a broad range of assets that can be contributed as capital — cash, receivables, intellectual property, movables, real estate, personal labor, commercial goodwill, and more — though for capital companies specifically, narrower rules exclude personal labor and goodwill from qualifying as capital, protecting creditors who rely on the company's stated capital as real value.

## Performing the Contribution Obligation

Each partner is bound by the company agreement to contribute the capital it committed to (Art. 128). In-kind contributions become effective on different terms depending on the asset type: real estate requires registration in the land registry for the company to exercise ownership rights, while movable and intangible assets pass to the company automatically once it acquires legal personality. Where a founding partner who committed real estate as an in-kind contribution instead transfers it to a related third party in breach of that commitment, Turkish courts have annulled the transfer as made in bad faith and ordered registration in the company's name.

## Personal Creditors of a Partner

Art. 133 limits how far a partner's personal (non-company) creditor can reach into company assets: in person companies, the creditor may only attach the partner's share of profit or liquidation proceeds; in capital companies, the creditor may additionally seize and liquidate the partner's shares directly. Notably, the Turkish Supreme Court (Court of Cassation, sitting en banc) has confirmed that Art. 133 does not give a limited liability company partner's personal creditor a right to demand dissolution of the company — a remedy the prior 1957 Code allowed but the current Code deliberately dropped.

Foreign investors structuring a Turkish joint venture should note this liability distinction carefully when choosing between a partnership and a capital company, as it directly determines personal exposure to company debts.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How many types of commercial company are there under Turkish law?

Under TTK Article 124/1, there are five types: <b>collective (general partnership), limited partnership (komandit), joint stock, limited liability, and cooperative</b> companies. Under Article 124/2, of these, the <b>collective and limited partnership companies are companies of persons</b>; the <b>joint stock, limited liability, and share-capital limited partnership companies are companies of capital</b>. This distinction is not abstract: the liability of the partners, the decision-making procedure, and the capital regime all differ according to this classification.

### What can be contributed to a company as capital?

Under TTK Article 127/1, unless otherwise provided by law, the following may be contributed as capital: <b>money, claims, negotiable instruments and shares belonging to capital companies, intellectual property rights, movables and immovables of every kind, rights to use and enjoy them, personal labour, commercial goodwill, commercial enterprises, lawfully used transferable electronic environments and domains, mining licences</b>, and other transferable rights. However, <b>personal labour and commercial goodwill may serve as capital only in companies of persons</b>.

### Can a partner's personal creditor make a claim against the company's assets?

Not in a company of persons. Under TTK Article 133/1, <b>for as long as a company of persons continues, the personal creditor of one of the partners may satisfy their claim only from the share of profit accruing to that partner under the company's balance sheet, and, if the company has been dissolved, from the liquidation share</b>. Under Article 133/2, however, <b>in companies of capital</b>, the creditor may, in addition to satisfying their claim from the profit or liquidation share accruing to that partner, also <b>have the debtor's shares attached</b> and converted into money.
