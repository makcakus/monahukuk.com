Having covered the personal debtors and creditor safeguards for general average contribution, the series turns to how those safeguards are actually put into effect before the ship can leave port and while cargo remains in the carrier's hands (TTK Art. 1276-1277).

## Security Before the Ship Leaves Port

Under Art. 1276, before a ship may leave the port where the loss is to be assessed and apportioned (the port determined under Art. 1279), security must be given to cargo interests for the contribution share attributable to the ship. This rule targets a real timing problem: preparing the average adjustment can take months, and once the ship has sailed on, collecting the shipowner's share becomes far harder. Requiring security while the ship is still detainable — precisely when the owner has the strongest incentive to cooperate — converts an abstract creditor protection into a concrete, front-loaded guarantee.

## The Master's Lien Over Cargo

Art. 1277(1) gives the master a lien-based tool: cargo subject to general average contribution may not be delivered until contribution shares are paid or security is given under Art. 1201 (the carrier's general lien for freight claims). If the master delivers anyway, he becomes personally liable for those shares. Art. 1277(2) then applies the general master-shipowner liability allocation rule (Art. 1089) to this context — following the shipowner's instructions does not excuse the master, but a shipowner who knowingly ordered the improper delivery becomes separately liable alongside him.

## Who Actually Exercises the Lien

Art. 1277(3) clarifies that the lien belongs to the contribution creditors but is exercised on their behalf by the carrier, again per Art. 1201 — because the creditors (shipowner, other cargo interests, insurers) typically do not have physical possession of the cargo, while the carrier does.

## Practical Takeaway

In practice, this framework is rarely litigated directly: shipowners, cargo interests, and insurers typically resolve it commercially through letters of guarantee or "average bonds" posted in exchange for release of cargo, before any court becomes involved.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### May the ship leave port after a general average?

Not without security being provided. Under TTK Article 1276/1, <b>for the ship to be able to leave the port where the loss is to be determined and apportioned under Article 1279, security must be provided to the cargo interests for the contributions falling on the ship.</b> The rule brings the port of adjustment and the port of security together at a single point and prevents the ship from leaving the cargo interests unsecured.

### May the master deliver the cargo before the contribution is paid?

No. Under TTK Article 1277/1 <b>the master may not deliver contributing property unless the contributions have been paid or security has been provided for them under Article 1201.</b> Delivery in breach of this prohibition engages the master's liability. Under Article 1277/2, where the shipowner ordered the master to act in this way, the second and third paragraphs of Article 1089 apply and liability falls on the shipowner.

### Do the cargo creditors exercise the lien themselves?

No, they do so through the carrier. Under TTK Article 1277/3 <b>the creditors' right of retention over the contributing property is exercised on their behalf by the carrier in accordance with Article 1201.</b> This agency-like arrangement is practical: the property is in fact under the carrier's control, and separate exercise of the lien by each cargo interest would be both impossible and apt to paralyse discharge.
