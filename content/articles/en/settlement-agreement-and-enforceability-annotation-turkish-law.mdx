*Article 18 – (1) The scope of the settlement reached at the end of the mediation activity is determined by the parties; where a settlement document is drawn up, it is signed by the parties and the mediator.*

*(2) If the parties reach a settlement at the end of the mediation activity, they may request an annotation as to the enforceability of that settlement document. Where mediation was resorted to before an action was brought, the annotation of enforceability may be requested from the civil court of peace of the place where the mediator performed the duty. Where mediation is resorted to during the hearing of the case, the annotation may be requested from the court hearing the case. A settlement bearing this annotation is deemed a document having the character of a judgment.*

*(3) The granting of the annotation of enforceability is a matter of non-contentious jurisdiction and the examination is made on the file. However, in family-law disputes amenable to mediation the examination is made with a hearing. The scope of this examination is limited to whether the content of the settlement is amenable to mediation and to enforcement. (…)*

*(4) (Added: 12/10/2017-7036/35) Save where laws make obtaining an annotation of enforceability compulsory, a settlement document signed jointly by the parties and their lawyers together with the mediator — and, in commercial disputes, by the lawyers together with the mediator — is deemed a document having the character of a judgment without any annotation of enforceability being required.*

*(5) (Added: 12/10/2017-7036/24) Where a settlement is reached at the end of the mediation activity, the parties may not bring an action concerning the matters agreed upon.*

The entire weight of mediation is gathered in Article 18. This provision does two things: it gives the signed document the same enforcement force as a court judgment, and it closes the judicial route on the matters agreed. This is also the point that parties most often come to regret in practice — the settlement document produces irreversible consequences the moment it is signed, and every item whose scope is left uncertain is either a lost right or a new dispute lasting years.

## The Parties Determine the Scope of the Settlement

Article 18/1 states that the scope of the settlement is determined by the parties. The mediator records in the document the matters on which the parties have agreed; the mediator cannot impose content.

Read together with Article 18/5, that freedom creates a critical responsibility. If the document uses a general expression such as "all the claimant's employee claims", no action can later be brought for a separate claim item. By contrast, if the items are enumerated one by one, the judicial route remains open for those not listed. For this reason, writing out the name of each claim separately in the settlement document — severance pay, notice pay, annual leave pay, overtime pay — and stating clearly which items are left outside its scope, is the most technical part of the negotiation.

## Two Separate Routes to Judgment-Like Character

The Law provides two independent routes for the settlement document to acquire the character of a judgment.

**First route — the annotation of enforceability (Art. 18/2).** The parties request the annotation from the court; a settlement bearing the annotation is deemed a document having the character of a judgment. The competent court is the **civil court of peace of the place where the mediator performed the duty** where mediation was resorted to before an action was brought, and **the court hearing the case** where it was resorted to during proceedings.

**Second route — qualified signature (Art. 18/4).** Under this paragraph, added in 2017, a settlement document signed jointly by the parties and their lawyers together with the mediator is deemed a document having the character of a judgment without an annotation being required. In commercial disputes the parties' personal signature is not required; the signatures of the lawyers and the mediator suffice. The 3rd Civil Chamber of the Court of Cassation has expressly confirmed this rule, stating that settlement documents falling within Article 18/4 have the character of a judgment without an annotation of enforceability being required, that the annotation is in that case **optional**, and that the document may be put directly to enforcement where the obligations recorded in the minutes are not performed.

The precondition of the second route is that **both parties are represented by lawyers**. If one party has no lawyer, Article 18/4 does not apply and an annotation must be obtained. The proviso at the beginning of the paragraph — "save where laws make obtaining an annotation of enforceability compulsory" — is also important: in disputes over immovable property (Art. 17/B/4), in disputes within the scope of Article 18/B (Art. 18/B/3) and in international settlement agreements within the scope of the Singapore Convention (Art. 17/A/1), the annotation is **compulsory**.

## The Scope of the Annotation Examination: Narrow and Limited

Article 18/3 contains three rules.

**Non-contentious jurisdiction and examination on the file.** The 9th Civil Chamber emphasises this character in settled case law. There is no defendant in a request for an annotation; it is an application without an opposing party.

**Compulsory hearing in family law.** In family-law disputes amenable to mediation, the examination is made with a hearing. That exception stems from the higher need for protection of the parties in family law.

**The scope of review.** The examination is limited to whether the content of the settlement is **amenable to mediation and to enforcement**. The court does not review whether the settlement is fair, whether the consideration is appropriate, or whether a party was deceived.

The criterion of "amenability to enforcement" is decisive in practice. In a case where the mediation settlement document merely recorded a forward-looking promise by the parties and did not contain a constitutive judicial pronouncement, the 12th Civil Chamber held that enforcement proceedings could not be commenced on the basis of that document so as to compel the debtor to conclude a lease contract. According to the decision, an enforcement office has no power to produce a constitutive effect such as the formation of a contract.

The same criterion also prevents an annotation being given to an unsuitable document. In respect of minutes drawn up the day after the termination of an employment contract and containing a blanket release declaration by which the employee waived all the enumerated claims, the 9th Civil Chamber (Case No. 2016/25300, Decision No. 2016/21744, dated 08.12.2016), after emphasising that the examination is limited to amenability, and having regard to the mandatory rule in Article 420/2 of the Turkish Code of Obligations, concluded that the document was not amenable to mediation by reason of its date and content, nor to enforcement by reason of its nature, and that an annotation could therefore not be granted.

## Service of the Annotation Request and Litigation Costs

Even where the examination is made on the file, service of the request on the other party is mandatory. Assessing Article 18 together with Article 27 of the Code of Civil Procedure on the right to be heard, the 9th Civil Chamber has stated that, whether the decision is given on the file or after a hearing, service of the request on the other party is essential, and that giving a decision without service and without constituting the parties is a ground for reversal.

As to costs, the application remains on the shoulders of the requesting party. According to the 3rd Civil Chamber's decision resolving a conflict between regional courts of justice, because the annotation request is a matter of non-contentious jurisdiction there are no two parties in contention; accordingly **no attorney's fee is awarded in favour of the requesting party and the litigation costs are left on that party**.

Where an appeal is brought against the annotation request and against the decisions given on it, a **fixed fee** is charged; if the parties wish to use the settlement document in another official transaction without obtaining an annotation, stamp duty is likewise charged at a fixed rate (Art. 18/3, final sentences).

## The Bar on Bringing an Action: Article 18/5

Article 18/5 is the gravest consequence of mediation: where a settlement is reached, the parties **may not bring an action** concerning the matters agreed upon. The provision draws no distinction between voluntary and mandatory mediation; it applies in both.

This bar has the character of a condition of action and is taken into account by the judge of their own motion. An action brought despite the settlement is dismissed without the merits being entered into.

The only way to overcome the bar is to assert the **invalidity** of the settlement document. Stating that a mediation settlement document is a substantive-law contract in the sense of the law of obligations, the 9th Civil Chamber has opened that route: the document may be annulled on grounds of invalidity such as mistake, fraud, duress and unconscionable exploitation. However, the right to annul for defective consent is subject to a one-year forfeiture period under Article 39 of the Turkish Code of Obligations, and the burden of proof lies on the party asserting it.

Where the document is not regarded as a valid mediation settlement document, a different result follows: the Chamber holds that such texts must be assessed under the conditions in Article 420 of the Turkish Code of Obligations or the provisions on performance. In other words, the document cannot benefit from the protection of Article 18/5; at most it founds a set-off of the amount paid.

## Case Law of the Court of Cassation

The following decisions show the practice concerning the enforceability of the settlement document and the bar on bringing an action.

**1. Court of Cassation, 3rd Civil Chamber, Case No. 2024/971, Decision No. 2024/4021, dated 02.12.2024 (Resolution of Conflict)**

In this decision resolving a conflict between regional courts of justice, the Chamber stated that, under Article 18/4, settlement documents signed jointly by the parties and their lawyers together with the mediator have the character of a judgment without an annotation of enforceability being required, that the annotation is optional in that case, and that the document may be put directly to enforcement where the obligations recorded in the minutes are not performed. Because the annotation request is a matter of non-contentious jurisdiction and there are no two parties in contention, it was held that **no attorney's fee should be awarded in favour of the requesting party and that the litigation costs should be left on that party**. Referring to the rationale of Article 18/5, the decision also emphasised the necessity of setting out the agreed matters clearly and item by item in the minutes.

**2. Court of Cassation, 9th Civil Chamber, Case No. 2019/5438, Decision No. 2019/15284, dated 05.09.2019**

The decision establishes that Article 18/3 treats the granting of an annotation of enforceability as a matter of non-contentious jurisdiction with an examination on the file, but that in family-law disputes amenable to mediation the examination is made with a hearing.

**3. Court of Cassation, 9th Civil Chamber, Case No. 2016/31376, Decision No. 2016/21368, dated 01.12.2016**

Assessing Article 18 together with Article 27 of the Code of Civil Procedure on the right to be heard, the Chamber stated that, in a request for an annotation of enforceability, **service of the request on the other party is essential**, whether the decision is given on the file or after a hearing. In the case at hand, dismissing the request on presumptive reasoning although service on the other party could not be effected amounted to deciding without constituting the parties, and was therefore held to be a ground for reversal.

**4. Court of Cassation, 12th Civil Chamber, Case No. 2025/6239, Decision No. 2025/7199, dated 06.11.2025**

The Chamber stated that the mediation minutes merely evidenced a promise by the parties and did not contain a constitutive judicial pronouncement; holding that an enforcement office has no constitutive power such as the formation of a contract, it ruled that the debtor could not be compelled to conclude a lease contract through enforcement proceedings based on a settlement document containing such a provision.

**5. Court of Cassation, 12th Civil Chamber, Case No. 2022/9143, Decision No. 2022/9318, dated 26.09.2022 (Resolution of Conflict)**

In this decision resolving a conflict between regional courts of justice, the Chamber stated that a settlement document bearing the annotation being deemed to have the character of a judgment **does not mean that it can be made the subject of enforcement based on a judgment in all circumstances**. For a judgment to be amenable to enforcement it must contain an order to give, to do or to refrain from doing. In the case at hand, having found that the claim in the settlement document was made conditional and that the document contained no operative order, the Chamber ruled that it could not be made the subject of judgment-based enforcement despite bearing an annotation of enforceability.
