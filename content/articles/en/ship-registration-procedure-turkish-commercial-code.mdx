TTK Art. 959-964 set out how a ship is actually registered — the application, its required content, supporting documentation, and how later changes must be reported.

## Application by Owner Request Only

Under Art. 959, the registry acts only on the request of the owner or one of several co-owners (any one is enough), submitted in writing. Turkish courts have confirmed that only the owner can request registration, correction, or deregistration — a third party cannot compel the registry to act directly, though anyone can challenge a registrar's decision.

## What the Application Must Contain

Art. 960 requires nine categories of disclosure: the ship's name, type and construction material, port of registry, build location and year (where ascertainable), measurement results, and — in considerable detail — the owner's identity, broken down separately for individuals, trading companies, other legal entities, and joint ownership arrangements (donatma iştiraki). Art. 961 requires most of this to be shown as "credibly probable" rather than conclusively proven, except flag rights and measurement results, which need firmer documentation.

## Ownership Disputes and Prior-Construction Mortgages

If a third party disputes the applicant's ownership before registration completes, Art. 963(2) does not block registration — the ship is registered regardless, but a notation is entered in favor of the objecting party, putting later buyers on notice. Where a ship was previously registered on the separate under-construction registry, Art. 963(3) automatically transfers any recorded mortgages to the main registry, preserving their priority ranking without needing to be re-established — a critical protection for shipyard lenders.

## Keeping the Registry Current

Art. 964 requires any later change to registered particulars — including a ship sinking, becoming unrepairable, or losing flag rights — to be reported without delay; failure to do so can trigger the registry's ex officio deregistration powers covered in a companion article.

## Practical Takeaway

Turkish courts have confirmed that registered ownership can transfer outside the registry through an informal agreement between the parties, with the registry entry merely reflecting (not creating) that transfer — meaning disputes often turn on proving the underlying transaction rather than the registry entry itself.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Who may request registration of a vessel?

Under TTK Article 959/1, <b>a vessel is registered in the ship registry only upon the request of the owner or one of the owners</b>. The registry office does not carry out registration on its own initiative. In cases of co-ownership, a request from one of the owners suffices; the participation of the others is not required. The registration request is completed by notifying the matters listed in Article 960 and by submitting the documents listed in Article 961.

### How is a vessel registered in a foreign registry transferred to the Turkish registry?

Under TTK Article 962/1, in order for a Turkish vessel previously registered in a foreign ship registry to be registered in the Turkish Ship Registry, <b>documents strongly indicating that it is no longer registered in the foreign ship registry</b> must be submitted to the registry office. The purpose is to prevent <b>dual registration</b>: a vessel cannot appear simultaneously in the registries of two states; this is a fundamental principle of flag-state control.

### How are changes occurring after registration notified?

Under TTK Article 964/1, <b>changes occurring in matters registered in the ship registry must be notified to the registry office by petition so that they can be entered in the registry</b>. Under Article 972, the persons subject to this obligation must act <b>within fifteen days</b> of learning of the change. Furthermore, under Article 971, when a change is requested, submission of the ship's certificate and, if any, its certified summary to the registry office is mandatory.
