The 2012 Commercial Code replaced the old internal "murakıp" (statutory supervisor) model with independent, external audit — one of the Code's most significant structural changes. TTK Art. 397-401 set out the general framework: which companies must be audited, what the audit covers, how auditors are chosen and removed, who may serve as auditor, and the company's duty to give the auditor access.

## Scope and Consequence of Non-Compliance

Companies designated by presidential decree must have their financial statements audited under Turkish Auditing Standards, and the board's annual activity report is checked for consistency with those audited statements (Art. 397). Financial statements must state on their face whether they were audited; if a company subject to mandatory audit fails to have this done, its financial statements and activity report are legally treated as if never prepared at all (Art. 397/2) — a consequence Turkish courts have tied directly to the invalidity of shareholder resolutions approving unaudited accounts. The substantive audit standard is whether the company's financial position is presented with a "true and fair view" (Art. 398).

## Selecting, Removing, and Replacing the Auditor

Shareholders select the auditor for each financial year before that year ends, and the appointment must be registered and published (Art. 399). Only for cause — particularly a well-founded doubt about impartiality — may the commercial court appoint a different auditor, on application by the board or shareholders holding the statutory minimum stake, filed within a strict three-week deadline from the appointment's publication. Turkish courts have repeatedly re-characterized shareholder suits filed nominally as "resolution annulment" claims as actually being auditor-removal claims under this three-week bar, dismissing several as time-barred. An auditor may only resign for just cause or once a removal suit is pending against them — disagreement over the content of an audit opinion does not qualify.

## Independence Requirements

Only licensed financial professionals authorized by the Public Oversight Authority may serve as auditor, and Art. 400 lists eight independence-defeating scenarios — being a shareholder or employee of the audited company, having a close relative on its management, working for a related entity, having contributed to preparing its accounts, or deriving more than 30% of professional income from that company. Turkish courts have read this "connection" test broadly, voiding an auditor's appointment where the audit firm merely rented office space from a shareholder-affiliated business. A rotation rule also bars an auditor who has served seven of the last ten years from re-appointment for three years (Art. 400/2).

## The Company's Disclosure Duty

The board must promptly hand over financial statements and the activity report and give the auditor full access to books, records, and assets (Art. 401) — failure to do so can itself taint the resulting shareholder resolutions, as Turkish courts have found.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### When and by whom is the auditor appointed?

Under Article 399/1 TTK the auditor is appointed by the <b>general assembly of the company</b>, and the group auditor by the general assembly of the parent. The appointment must be made <b>before the end</b> of the financial year the auditor is to audit. The board must then register the appointment with the trade registry without delay and announce it in the Trade Registry Gazette and on the company's website. If no appointment is made by the fourth month of the financial year, the commercial court of first instance appoints the auditor (Article 399/6).

### Is auditor rotation compulsory?

Yes. Under Article 400/2 TTK an auditor who has been appointed for the same company for a total of <b>seven years within ten years</b> may not be reappointed <b>until three years have elapsed</b>. The Public Oversight, Accounting and Auditing Standards Authority is empowered to set the rules for applying this paragraph and to shorten the periods. The rule aims to prevent the familiarity that can develop over time between auditor and audited company from eroding independence.

### May the auditor provide consultancy to the company it audits?

No. Under Article 400/3 TTK the auditor may not provide consultancy or services to the company it audits <b>other than tax consultancy and tax audit</b>, nor may it do so through a subsidiary. In addition, under Article 400/1(e), a person who has contributed to keeping the company's books or preparing its financial statements, otherwise than through the audit, cannot be its auditor — no one may audit their own work.
