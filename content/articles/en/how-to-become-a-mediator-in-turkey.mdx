Mediation is not an activity that may be practised freely in Turkey. To become a mediator you must hold a law degree, acquire professional seniority, complete training at an authorised institution, pass the examination held by the Ministry of Justice and, finally, **be entered in the registry**. And that last step does not follow automatically from having completed the others.

## The Six Statutory Conditions

Article 20/2 of Turkish Law No. 6325 on Mediation in Civil Disputes lists six conditions for registration in the mediator registry, all of which must be satisfied together.

**1. Turkish citizenship.** Foreign nationals cannot be entered in the mediator registry.

**2. A law degree with at least five years' professional seniority.** Two elements are required together: graduation from a faculty of law and professional seniority. This is the condition that generates most disputes in practice; "seniority in the profession" means time spent in the legal professions — practice at the bar, service as a judge or public prosecutor, and an academic career in law. Whether time served by law graduates who do not practise a legal profession counts is contested and is regularly reviewed by the Council of State.

**3. Full legal capacity.**

**4. No conviction for certain offences.** A sentence of more than one year's imprisonment for an intentional offence is a bar even if the periods under Article 53 of the Turkish Criminal Code have elapsed. In addition, conviction for the following offences bars registration even if pardoned: offences against the security of the State, offences against the constitutional order and its functioning, embezzlement, extortion in office, bribery, theft, fraud, forgery, breach of trust, fraudulent bankruptcy, rigging of tenders, rigging of performance of an obligation, laundering of assets derived from crime, smuggling, giving a false expert report, false testimony and perjury.

**5. No connection or association with terrorist organisations.** Introduced by decree-law in 2017 and enacted in 2018, this condition is in practice the most frequent ground for removal from the registry. The 10th Chamber of the Council of State held that there was no unlawfulness in a decision striking a mediator from the registry after concluding, on the basis of a pending criminal case before the assize court, that the mediator did not satisfy this condition.

**6. Completion of mediation training and success in the written examination.** Those with **twenty years' seniority** in their profession are exempt from the examination.

## Training: Who Provides It and What Does It Cover?

Mediation training is a specialist course taken **after completion of a law degree**. The statutory subject headings are: basic knowledge relating to the conduct of mediation, **communication techniques**, **negotiation and dispute resolution methods** and **behavioural psychology**. Other theoretical and practical subjects set out in the regulation are also included.

The institutions permitted to deliver the training are **limited in number**: the law faculties of universities that have a faculty of law, the Union of Turkish Bar Associations and the Turkish Justice Academy. Private training companies, associations and foundations may not provide mediation training.

Even these institutions have no automatic right to deliver training; they must **obtain permission from the Ministry**. Permission is granted for a maximum of **three years** and may be extended for three years at a time. The list of authorised training institutions is published electronically — before enrolling, always check that the institution appears on that list.

Those who successfully complete the course receive from the training institution **a certificate confirming completion of mediation training**.

## The Examination: When Is It Held and Who Passes?

Here lies the fact most often overlooked: **completing the training does not tie the right to sit the examination to any timetable.**

According to the settled case law of the 10th Chamber of the Council of State, deciding whether a mediation examination will be held at all lies **within the Ministry's discretion**. Indeed, the decisions record that the examination was scheduled because new mediators were needed once employment disputes were brought within mandatory mediation in 2018 and commercial actions in 2019. The training certificate does not create a time-bound right.

**The pass criterion changed in 2021.** Article 45 of the Regulation on the Law on Mediation in Civil Disputes, headed "Condition of success in the examination", took its present form with the amendment of 17 November 2021: as a rule, **candidates scoring at least seventy out of one hundred are deemed successful**. However, the Ministry may set the number of candidates to be deemed successful according to need; in that case the number of mediators required is announced in the examination notice, and candidates are deemed successful starting from the highest score until that number is reached. Candidates with the same score as the lowest-scoring successful candidate are also deemed successful.

Before the amendment the quota rule applied directly. In an action brought after the 2019 examination — where a requirement of 5,000 mediators was announced and the pass threshold was 70, yet the effective pass mark rose to 91 — the Council of State concluded that the administration had exercised its discretion having regard to the public interest and the requirements of the service, and found the act lawful.

Determining the basic principles and standards of training and examination is the task of the Mediation Board; the duration, content and standards of the training and the principles and rules of the examination are set by regulation issued by the Ministry.

## Registration and Starting to Practise

Registration is effected upon **written application to the Department of Mediation**. A mediator may begin practising from the date of registration; a session conducted before registration does not count as mediation in the statutory sense.

Registration gives rise to two financial obligations: an **admission fee** on entry in the registry and an **annual fee** for each year. The amounts are set by the Mediation Board and the fees are recorded as revenue of the general budget. Mediators have no professional body, chamber or union.

## Choosing Where to Practise

The Department of Mediation lists registered mediators **according to the judicial commissions of the first-instance courts in which they wish to serve** and sends the lists to the presidencies of those commissions. A mediator may be entered on **no more than three commission lists**.

Appointments in mandatory mediation are made from these lists: the mediator is designated by the mediation office, but if the parties agree on any mediator appearing on the list, that person is appointed.

## Fields of Specialisation

A provision added in 2017 empowers the Department of Mediation to determine mediators' **fields of specialisation** and the procedures and principles governing specialisation. The Department notifies the commissions of those wishing to conduct mandatory mediation, **stating their fields of specialisation where applicable**.

Specialisation is not a binding criterion for appointment; it is an organisational tool aimed at service quality. In some areas, however, the statute specifically requires it: in disputes arising from agricultural production contracts, mediators **who have received specialist training in contract farming** are appointed, and other mediators only if none are available.

## The Mediator's Obligations

The principal obligations arising on registration are:

- **Personal performance:** the assignment cannot be delegated,
- **Impartiality and independence:** disclosing circumstances that may cast doubt on impartiality and observing equality between the parties,
- **Information:** informing the parties at the outset about the essentials, process and consequences of mediation,
- **Statement of title:** disclosing the capacity of mediator while acting,
- **Confidentiality:** its breach is an offence punishable under Article 33 by up to six months' imprisonment,
- **Advertising ban:** any attempt that may be regarded as advertising in order to obtain work is prohibited,
- **Retention and notification:** keeping documents for five years and sending a copy of the final minutes to the General Directorate within one month,
- **Bar on acting as counsel:** a mediator may not act as counsel for either party in an action brought in relation to a dispute in which they served as mediator.

Further, a mediator may not receive a fee in return for acting as an intermediary for, or recommending, particular persons in relation to the mediation process; transactions contrary to this prohibition are **void**.

The detail of professional rules is set out in the **Turkish Mediators' Code of Ethics** adopted by the Mediation Board. The Assembly of Administrative Law Chambers of the Council of State found the Board's power to make those rules lawful, basing it on Articles 30 and 32 of Law No. 6325.

## Removal from the Registry

There are three separate routes, and the deciding authority differs in each:

**Absence or loss of a condition.** The Department of Mediation itself **strikes out** the registration of a mediator who was entered in the registry without meeting the required conditions or who subsequently loses them.

**Breach of obligations.** The Department first issues a **written warning**; if the warning is not heeded, it **takes the mediator's defence** and, where necessary, **asks the Board** to strike the mediator's name from the registry.

**At the mediator's own request.** A mediator may ask at any time to be removed from the registry, without giving reasons.

Refusal of an application for registration and removal from the registry are administrative acts and may be challenged by an action for annulment.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q: I am not a law graduate — can I become a mediator?

No. The statute expressly requires graduation from a faculty of law and at least five years' seniority in the profession as a condition of registration.

### Q: How is the five years' seniority calculated?

Seniority means time spent in the legal professions (practice at the bar, service as a judge or prosecutor, academic posts in law). Whether periods during which a law graduate did not practise a legal profession count is contested and is subject to review by the administrative courts.

### Q: Can I be exempted from the examination?

Those with **twenty years' seniority** in their profession are exempt from the written examination held by the Ministry. For all other candidates, passing the examination is a condition of registration.

### Q: I completed the training but no examination is being held — what can I do?

Whether an examination is held and — where a required number is set — how many candidates are deemed successful lies, according to the case law of the Council of State, within the administration's discretion. The training certificate creates no time-bound right. An action for annulment may be brought against the examination notice or against the act setting the number of successful candidates; but the Council of State upholds such acts where the discretion was exercised having regard to the public interest and the requirements of the service.

### Q: If I score 70 in the examination, do I become a mediator?

Under Article 45 of the Regulation the rule is that candidates scoring seventy or above are deemed successful. However, the Ministry may set the number of successful candidates according to need; if such a quota has been announced, exceeding seventy is not enough on its own — you must be among the highest scorers up to that number.

### Q: Can I practise as a lawyer and a mediator at the same time?

Yes. Under Article 12 of the Turkish Attorneyship Law, legal practice and mediation may be carried on together. However, a mediator may not act as counsel for either party in an action relating to a dispute in which they served as mediator, and must clearly state in which capacity they are acting.
