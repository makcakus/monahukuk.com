TTK Art. 724-732 close out the recourse regime, defining who is liable, for how much, through what instruments, and until when.

## Joint and Several Liability

Art. 724 makes everyone who draws, accepts, endorses, or gives aval on a bill jointly and severally liable to the holder — described by the Court of Cassation Grand General Assembly as the "chain liability principle" underpinning bills and notes. The holder may sue any one obligor, several, or all, in any order, without losing rights against the others (Art. 724/2, 724/4); anyone who pays the bill acquires the same selective recourse against earlier parties in the chain (Art. 724/3).

## What Can Be Claimed

A holder exercising recourse may claim the unpaid principal plus any agreed interest, statutory interest from maturity, protest and notice expenses, and a commission capped at 0.3% of the bill's value (Art. 725) — with a discount applied if recourse is exercised before maturity. A party who pays and then seeks reimbursement from earlier obligors may claim the amount paid, interest from the payment date, expenses, and a commission capped at 0.2% (Art. 726) — a slightly lower ceiling than the holder's, preventing costs from compounding down the recourse chain. Payers may demand the bill and protest document plus a receipt (Art. 727), and may cancel their own and later endorsements to simplify further recourse.

## Redraft (Retret)

Art. 729 allows anyone with recourse rights to draw a new bill — a "redraft" — on an earlier obligor, payable at sight at that obligor's domicile, covering the original claim plus an additional commission. This mechanism is largely a historical curiosity today: no recent Turkish case law addresses it, reflecting the limited modern use of bills of exchange generally.

## Loss of Recourse and Force Majeure

Missing the deadlines for presentment, protest, or (where "no-expenses" applies) payment presentment causes the holder to lose recourse against endorsers, the drawer, and other obligors — though not against the acceptor (Art. 730). Turkish courts treat these as strict, forfeiture-triggering deadlines. Force majeure extends these periods, but only if the holder promptly notifies and endorses the delay onto the bill; if force majeure persists more than thirty days past maturity, recourse may be exercised without presentment or protest at all (Art. 731).

## Unjust Enrichment After Limitation

Even after kambiyo claims against the drawer or acceptor lapse through limitation or procedural neglect, Art. 732 lets the holder recover to the extent those parties were unjustly enriched at the holder's expense — a one-year window running from the date the bill's limitation period expired, with the burden on the obligor to disprove enrichment. Turkish courts describe the combined kambiyo and unjust-enrichment windows as "3+1 years."

## Practical Takeaway

Selecting the right obligor for recourse, calculating the correct commission cap, and — where the ordinary limitation period has run — pursuing the one-year unjust-enrichment claim can be the difference between recovery and a lost claim.
