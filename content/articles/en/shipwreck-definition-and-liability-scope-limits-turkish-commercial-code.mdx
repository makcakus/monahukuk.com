Having covered the liability triggers in Art. 1256(1)-(4), the remaining six subsections supply the technical definitions and boundaries that make the regime workable.

## Four Key Definitions

Art. 1256(5) defines the terms the whole article rests on. A **"shipping incident"** means wreck, capsizing, stranding, collision, onboard explosion, fire, or defect — a closed, catastrophic list, not "anything bad that happens aboard." **"Carrier's fault"** expressly includes fault by the carrier's servants acting in the course of their duties — an ordinary vicarious-liability extension. **"Shipboard defect"** is defined with unusual precision: failure or non-compliance with safety standards of any ship part or equipment used in embarkation/disembarkation, navigation, steering, mooring, anchoring, arriving at or leaving berth, flood control, or launching life-saving equipment — broad enough to capture essentially any safety-critical system. **"Damage"** expressly excludes punitive or exemplary damages, consistent with Turkey's civil-law tradition of purely compensatory awards.

## Baggage Loss Includes Unreasonable Delay in Return

Art. 1256(6) extends "loss or damage to baggage" to cover the financial loss from baggage not being returned within a reasonable time after the ship's arrival — a familiar modern grievance — while excluding delays caused specifically by labor disputes.

## Limited to the Carriage Period

Art. 1256(7) confines the entire liability regime to accidents occurring "during carriage," with the claimant bearing the burden of proving both that timing and the extent of the loss. (What "during carriage" precisely covers — including the pre-boarding and post-disembarkation periods — is defined separately.)

## Preserved Rights and Fair Evidence Treatment

Nothing in this chapter cuts off the carrier's recourse against third parties, its right to plead contributory fault, or its limitation-of-liability rights (Art. 1256(8)). And a party facing a fault presumption or a shifted burden of proof is still entitled to have favorable evidence considered — a presumption affects who must prove what, not what a court may look at (Art. 1256(9)). Finally, the overall liability caps in this article remain subject to separate provisions on maximum limits (Art. 1256(10)).

Turkish courts have applied the "carrier's fault includes servants' fault" definition to hold operators liable for a captain's negligent maneuver during a rescue attempt, confirming the definition's practical reach.
