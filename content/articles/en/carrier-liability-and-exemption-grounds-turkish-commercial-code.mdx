TTK Art. 875-879 open the most heavily litigated part of Turkish carriage law: once goods are damaged, lost, or delivered late, is the carrier actually liable, or can it point to a recognized exemption?

## The Liability Window and General Exemption

Under Art. 875, a carrier is responsible for loss, damage, or delay occurring between taking the goods and delivering them — and Turkish courts treat this as close to strict liability, since the carrier must affirmatively establish an exemption rather than the claimant proving fault. Art. 876 sets that exemption at a genuinely high bar: the carrier must show the loss stemmed from a cause it could not avoid or overcome despite exercising the utmost care — a technical breakdown alone rarely qualifies unless its unforeseeability and unavoidability are separately proven.

## What Cannot Excuse a Carrier

Art. 877 bars the carrier from citing a vehicle defect or the fault of whoever it hired the vehicle from — that is the carrier's own commercial choice, not something the shipper had any say in, so its consequences stay with the carrier.

## Presumptive Special Exemptions

Art. 878 lists seven specific circumstances — open or deck loading by agreement, inadequate packaging by the sender, handling or loading done by the sender, goods naturally prone to breakage or spoilage, inadequate labeling, live-animal transport, and customs-driven causes — where a plausible link between the loss and the listed cause creates a rebuttable presumption in the carrier's favor, shifting the burden to the claimant. That presumption does not apply to extraordinary losses tied to open-vehicle transport, and it never rescues a carrier who ignored the sender's specific instructions or who failed to take the precautions its own duty of care demanded for climate-sensitive goods or live animals.

## Liability for Auxiliaries

Art. 879 makes the carrier answer for its own employees and for any third party it uses to actually perform the carriage — including subcontractors and actual carriers — exactly as if the carrier itself had acted.

## Practical Takeaway

A foreign shipper pursuing a Turkish carrier for cargo damage benefits from a liability structure that starts against the carrier — the practical battle is almost always over which of the narrow Art. 876-878 exemptions the carrier can actually prove, not whether liability exists in principle.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### When is the carrier relieved of liability?

Under Article 876/1 TTK the carrier is relieved where the loss, damage or delay arises from causes <b>which it could not avoid, and the consequences of which it could not prevent, even exercising the utmost care</b>. That test is wider than force majeure but far stricter than ordinary care. Article 877 expressly excludes two cases: the carrier cannot escape liability for loss arising from <b>a defect in the vehicle</b> or from <b>the fault of the person from whom it hired the vehicle</b>.

### Who bears the burden of proof?

As a rule the carrier: faced with the presumption of liability in Article 875, it is for the carrier to prove a ground of exoneration. Article 878 TTK, however, lists special cases that reverse the burden — carriage in an open vehicle, insufficient packing, loading or unloading performed by the sender, goods liable to deterioration, inadequate marking and the carriage of live animals. In those cases it is <b>presumed</b> that the loss arose from the risk in question, and it falls to the claimant to prove otherwise.

### Is the carrier liable for the fault of its staff?

Yes, directly. Under Article 879/1 TTK the carrier is liable <b>as for its own acts and omissions</b> for the acts and omissions of <b>its own servants</b> and of <b>the persons it uses to perform the carriage</b> in the course of their duties. There is no possibility of exculpatory proof: the diligence defence available to an employer under Article 66 of the Code of Obligations does not operate here.
