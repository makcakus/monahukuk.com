TTK Art. 808-811 close the payment chapter by addressing what happens when a cheque, timely presented, goes unpaid.

## Three Ways to Establish Dishonor

Unlike a bill of exchange, which relies solely on notarial protest, Art. 808 gives a cheque holder three equally valid ways to establish dishonor: (a) a formal notarial protest; (b) a dated statement written on the cheque by the drawee bank itself, showing the presentment date — in practice, the bank's "insufficient funds" endorsement; or (c) a dated statement from a clearing house confirming the cheque was delivered on time but not paid. Option (b) is by far the most common in Turkish banking practice, but it must be formally adequate — the endorsement must clearly show both the presentment date and the fact of non-payment. Turkish courts have split on how strictly to read a bank's endorsement wording, with one 2025 Court of Cassation panel decision (with dissent) illustrating how much turns on whether a bank's shorthand notation on the back of a cheque satisfies these formal requirements.

## Timing of the Protest

Art. 809 requires the protest or equivalent to be made before the presentment period expires, with a narrow grace period allowing next-business-day protest if presentment itself occurred on the last permissible day.

## What Recourse Covers

The holder may claim the unpaid face value, interest from the presentment date, protest and notice expenses, and a commission capped at 0.3% of the cheque's value (Art. 810) — mirroring the bill-of-exchange recourse structure. Turkish courts distinguish sharply between who bears which cost: the 10% cheque indemnity for insufficient funds under Art. 783/3 falls only on the drawer, never on endorsers, while the Art. 810 commission may be claimed from endorsers as well. Where the drawer was blameless — for example, funds were available but an unrelated party wrongly obtained an injunction blocking payment — courts have limited the drawer's liability to the principal amount, shifting interest, indemnity, and commission onto whoever caused the wrongful block.

## Force Majeure

Art. 811 extends presentment and protest deadlines where an insurmountable obstacle — government action or genuine force majeure — prevents timely action, provided the holder promptly notifies the prior party and endorses the delay onto the cheque. If the obstacle persists more than fifteen days past notification (and before the presentment deadline), the holder may exercise recourse without further presentment or protest at all. Purely personal obstacles affecting only the holder or their agent do not qualify.

## Practical Takeaway

Because Turkish banks routinely use their own dated endorsement rather than notarial protest, holders and their counsel should scrutinize the exact wording a bank uses when dishonoring a cheque — ambiguous phrasing has determined the outcome of multi-million-lira enforcement disputes.
