TTK Art. 923-930 close out the freight forwarding chapter, defining exactly how much liability a forwarder carries and how quickly claims against it expire.

## Security Rights in Chained Shipments

Under Art. 923, a forwarder holds a lien over goods in its possession for all claims arising from the forwarding relationship. Where a chain of carriers and forwarders handles a shipment, Art. 924-925 preserve that security down the chain: a later forwarder who pays off an earlier carrier's or forwarder's claim automatically inherits both the claim and its lien.

## Two Routes to Becoming a Carrier

Beyond the fixed-fee mechanism covered in the prior article in this series, Turkish law gives forwarders two further, deliberate routes into full carrier status. Under Art. 926 ("self-entry"), a forwarder may elect to carry the goods itself — gaining a carrier's full rights and duties for that shipment, plus the right to charge both its commission and ordinary freight. Courts require concrete proof of this election, typically a freight invoice issued in the forwarder's own name; absent that, the forwarder keeps its lighter-liability status. Under Art. 927, a forwarder consolidating multiple senders' goods into groupage shipments likewise takes on carrier status for that consolidated carriage.

## The Dual Liability Standard

Art. 928 is the operative liability rule, and it splits sharply in two. While goods are in the forwarder's own possession, it answers for loss or damage almost exactly as a carrier would — the full cargo liability regime (exemption grounds, damages calculation, SDR limits) applies by analogy. For losses unconnected to physical possession — a poor carrier selection, a late instruction — the forwarder is liable only for its own fault under Art. 918, judged by the prudent-merchant standard, and can escape liability by proving that standard was met. Art. 929 extends liability to the forwarder's own staff and to any third party engaged to perform the carriage, including the chosen carrier itself.

## A Short, but Not Absolute, Limitation Period

Art. 930 sets a flat one-year limitation period for claims under this chapter — markedly shorter than the tiered periods in general cargo carriage. But where the forwarder has actually become a carrier under Art. 921, 926, or 927, courts apply the general carrier limitation regime instead, including its extended period for intentional or reckless conduct.

## Practical Takeaway

For a claimant, the forwarder's classification at the time of loss — mere organizer or de facto carrier — decides both the liability standard and the clock that governs the claim, making early legal characterization essential.
