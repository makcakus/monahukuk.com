*Article 18/B – (Added: 28/3/2023-7445/37)*

*(1) In the following disputes, having applied to a mediator before bringing an action is a condition of action:*

*a) Disputes arising from a lease relationship, with the exception of the provisions on eviction of leased immovable property through enforcement without judgment under Law No. 2004.*

*b) Disputes concerning the division of movable and immovable property and the dissolution of joint ownership.*

*c) Disputes arising from the Condominium Ownership Law No. 634 of 23/6/1965.*

*d) Disputes arising from neighbour rights.*

*(3) Obtaining the annotation as to the enforceability of a settlement document drawn up within the scope of this article is compulsory; for settlement documents relating to immovable property, this annotation is obtained from the civil court of peace of the place where the property is located, and for other settlement documents from that of the place where the mediator performed the duty. (…)*

Added by Law No. 7445 and in force since **1 September 2023**, Article 18/B has extended the scope of mandatory mediation beyond labour and commercial law, into the most densely contested areas of everyday life. Lease, dissolution of joint ownership, condominium and neighbour cases, which make up a significant portion of the civil courts of peace's caseload, can now be brought only after the mediation stage has been completed.

Despite the short period of application, the provision has quickly produced settled case law on scope and timing.

## (a) Disputes Arising from a Lease Relationship

The scope is broad: determination of rent, collection of rent arrears, adaptation, eviction (need, undertaking to vacate, reconstruction, two justified notices), return of the deposit, and damage and defect compensation all fall within this sub-paragraph. No distinction is drawn between residential and roofed-workplace leases; commercial leases are also covered.

The only exception is expressly written: **the provisions on eviction of leased immovable property through enforcement without judgment under Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law No. 2004**. That is, no application to a mediator is required for eviction proceedings commenced before the enforcement office under Articles 269 et seq. of that Law on account of non-payment of rent. By contrast, debate continues as to actions for removal of the objection and eviction to be brought after an objection to such proceedings, and eviction actions brought before the general courts; in practice there is a tendency to require the mediation condition whenever a court is seised.

**The timing trap.** In its decision resolving a divergence between chambers of the regional courts of justice under Article 35 of Law No. 5235, the 3rd Civil Chamber of the Court of Cassation held that in **eviction actions based on need and reconstruction** under Article 350 of the Turkish Code of Obligations, the mediation application must be made after the right to bring the eviction action has arisen — that is, within the period for bringing the action. In fixed-term contracts, an application made before the lease term expires does not satisfy the condition of action, and the process must be started afresh. Because that criterion means early applications are "deemed not to have been made", it carries a serious risk of loss of rights.

## (b) Division and Dissolution of Joint Ownership

Disputes concerning the division of movable and immovable property and the dissolution of joint ownership (partition) are also subject to the condition of action. Division of an estate community, termination of joint or co-ownership and requests for dissolution by sale fall within this scope.

Because **all co-owners** must be parties to an action for dissolution of joint ownership, the mediation application must also be directed at all co-owners. Leaving one co-owner out of the application means that the condition of action is not satisfied as regards that person.

## (c) Disputes Arising from the Condominium Ownership Law

All disputes arising from Law No. 634 fall within scope: dues and common-expense claims, the manager's liability, annulment of decisions of the general meeting of owners, prevention of interference with common areas, non-compliance with the project, and disputes arising from the use of an independent section.

The 5th Civil Chamber of the Court of Cassation has interpreted the scope broadly, accepting that Article 18/B applies to a negative declaratory action brought against enforcement proceedings based on non-payment of dues and common expenses arising from Law No. 634. In a dispute arising from condominium ownership, the same Chamber assessed that the condition of action was regarded as fulfilled once the mandatory mediation process ended without settlement, and that an arbitration clause could not be operated in cases requiring judicial intervention under Article 33 of Law No. 634.

A point requiring attention in disputes to which site and apartment managements are parties is that the application must be directed at the correct addressee: the management is liable to the general meeting of owners like an agent, and its capacity to sue and to pursue enforcement is regulated in Article 35 of Law No. 634.

## (d) Disputes Arising from Neighbour Rights

Disputes arising from neighbour law regulated in Articles 737 et seq. of the Turkish Civil Code — prevention of excessive use, elimination of harmful effects such as noise, odour and smoke, damage caused to neighbouring property during excavation and construction, tree branches and roots, and disputes over water flow and rights of way — fall within this sub-paragraph.

Because in practice most neighbour disputes also arise from condominium ownership, sub-paragraphs (c) and (d) are frequently applied together; since the result is the same in either case, the classification debate is of little practical importance.

## Provisional Article 3: Not Applicable to Pending Actions

Under Provisional Article 3 added by Law No. 7445, the provisions of Article 18/B on mediation as a condition of action do not apply to **actions pending** before the first-instance courts, the regional courts of justice and the Court of Cassation as at the date the article entered into force (01.09.2023).

The 3rd Civil Chamber applies that provision by comparing the date the action was brought with 01.09.2023: the mediation condition is not required in actions brought before 01.09.2023, and is required in those brought after that date.

## The Settlement Document and the Compulsory Annotation of Enforceability

Under Article 18/B/2, where a settlement is reached, the settlement document is drawn up having regard to the restrictions, procedures and principles laid down in the laws concerning immovable property.

Contrary to the general rule in Article 18/4, Article 18/B/3 makes the annotation of enforceability **compulsory** and divides jurisdiction in two: for settlement documents relating to immovable property, the civil court of peace of **the place where the property is located**; for other settlement documents, that of **the place where the mediator performed the duty**. In documents relating to immovable property, the court reviews the content of the settlement not only for amenability to mediation and to enforcement but also for compliance with the statutory restrictions, procedures and principles concerning immovable property; it may request information or documents from institutions and organisations and hold a hearing where necessary.

The fifth paragraph added by Law No. 7531 of 07.11.2024 completes the process: after the annotation of enforceability has been granted, one of the parties to a settlement document concerning the transfer of immovable property or the creation of a limited right in rem may request registration from the land registry directorate, and the registration is effected **without a deed being drawn up**. This radically shortens the process, particularly for co-owners who agree on division in kind rather than sale in dissolution-of-joint-ownership disputes.

## Case Law of the Court of Cassation

The following decisions show the interpretation of Article 18/B in its first year of application.

**1. Court of Cassation, 3rd Civil Chamber, Case No. 2025/1495, Decision No. 2025/3048, dated 26.05.2025 (Resolution of Divergence)**

Resolving a divergence between chambers of the Bursa and Istanbul regional courts of justice, the Chamber held that in **eviction actions based on need** to be brought under Article 350 of the Turkish Code of Obligations — at the end of the term in fixed-term contracts, and from the date determined in accordance with the termination period and notice periods in indefinite-term contracts — the mandatory mediation application must be made **after the right to bring the eviction action has arisen, that is, within the period for bringing the action**. The reasoning emphasised that the period for bringing an action concerns public policy; that an application made before any dispute has arisen would take on the character of a formality; and that a process whose cost is met from the budget of the Ministry of Justice would be set in motion unnecessarily.

**2. Court of Cassation, 3rd Civil Chamber, Case No. 2025/2978, Decision No. 2025/5081, dated 22.10.2025**

The decision establishes that, under sub-paragraph (a) of the first paragraph of Article 18/B, in force since 01.09.2023, applying to a mediator before bringing an action is a condition of action in disputes arising from a lease relationship, with the exception of the provisions on eviction of leased immovable property through enforcement without judgment.

**3. Court of Cassation, 3rd Civil Chamber, Case No. 2025/1156, Decision No. 2025/4416, dated 29.09.2025**

The Chamber drew the temporal limit of application, stating that mandatory mediation in lease disputes entered into force on 01.09.2023 and that, under Provisional Article 3 added on the same date, these provisions do not apply to actions pending as at the date the article entered into force.

**4. Court of Cassation, 5th Civil Chamber, Case No. 2025/112, Decision No. 2025/1181, dated 03.02.2025**

The dispute concerns a claim for a negative declaration as to a debt subject to enforcement proceedings based on non-payment of dues and common expenses arising from Condominium Ownership Law No. 634. The decision establishes that Article 18/B of Law No. 6325 applies to such a dispute arising from Law No. 634.

**5. Court of Cassation, 5th Civil Chamber, Case No. 2025/12521, Decision No. 2025/15806, dated 01.12.2025**

In a dispute concerning judicial intervention and annulment of a decision of the general meeting of owners, the Chamber upheld the regional court of justice's assessment as being in accordance with procedure and law: the mandatory mediation condition had been fulfilled because the mediation sessions had ended in non-settlement before the date of the action; and **the arbitration clause in the management plan could not be applied in a dispute requiring judicial intervention under Article 33 of Law No. 634**, since a situation of contrariety to the mandatory provisions of the law is not among the matters left to the will of the two parties.
