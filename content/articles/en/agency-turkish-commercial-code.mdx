Articles 102 through 123 of the Turkish Commercial Code (TTK) regulate agency agreements — a structure common in insurance, automotive, pharmaceutical, fuel, and tourism sectors. An agent operates independently, unlike an employee, while owing continuous loyalty to the principal's interests. The most heavily litigated aspects of this chapter are the indemnity due to an agent on termination and the enforceability of post-contract non-compete clauses.

## What Makes Someone an Agent

Under Art. 102, an agent is someone who, without being bound to the principal the way an employee is, undertakes on a contractual basis to continuously broker or conclude contracts concerning a commercial enterprise within a defined territory. Turkish courts look past the label on the contract: where a person is subject to the principal's set working hours, instructions, and supervision, and works in a position of economic dependence, the relationship will be reclassified as an employment relationship regardless of what the contract calls it — with corresponding employee-protection consequences.

## The Agent's Duties and Commission Rights

An agent owes a duty of loyalty within its assigned territory or trade line, and must keep the principal informed of market and customer conditions (Art. 109-110). The agent earns commission on transactions concluded through its own efforts, and — where it has been granted an exclusive territory or client base — even on transactions concluded there without its involvement (Art. 113). Provisions reducing these commission protections to the agent's detriment are void (Art. 116).

## Termination and the Indemnity for Goodwill (Denkleştirme)

An indefinite agency agreement can be terminated on three months' notice, or terminated for cause at any time regardless of term (Art. 121). On termination, Art. 122 grants the agent a right to a reasonable indemnity — commonly compared to a "goodwill" or portfolio indemnity — where the principal continues to derive significant benefit from customers the agent brought in, the agent loses future commission on those customers as a result of the termination, and payment is equitable in the circumstances. This indemnity is capped at the average of the agent's annual commission over the last five years of activity, must be claimed within one year of termination, and is unavailable where the agent itself terminated without cause or was terminated for its own fault.

## Post-Contract Non-Compete Clauses

A clause restricting the agent's activity after termination is valid only if in writing, limited to two years and to the agent's own assigned territory, and only if the principal pays the agent reasonable compensation for the restriction (Art. 123).

Foreign principals appointing a Turkish agent should factor the potential goodwill indemnity into the economics of the relationship from the outset — it is not waivable in advance and frequently becomes a significant year-five liability.
