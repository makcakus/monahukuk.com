TTK Art. 1064-1067 introduce donatma iştiraki — a maritime-specific joint ownership structure with roots in traditional ship-finance practice, distinct from both partnerships and corporate entities.

## Definition and Scope

Art. 1064(1) requires three elements together: co-ownership of a ship in undivided shares, an agreement among the co-owners to operate it jointly for profit, and operation in all their names and for their collective account. It functions somewhat like a Turkish general partnership, but with a key difference — shares are proportionate to each co-owner's registered stake in the ship, not treated as equal by default. This structure has historical roots predating modern corporate law, reflecting how ship investments were traditionally pooled and risk-shared. Importantly, Art. 1064(2) excludes trading companies or other legal entities that own or operate a ship *solely*, since the structure inherently requires multiple co-owners in undivided shares — a company operating alone already has its own internal governance.

## Dual Registration

Within fifteen days of formation, the joint ownership must be registered in *both* the trade registry and the ship registry (Art. 1065) — capturing, respectively, its identity as a commercial organization and the ship's own ownership status. Required entries include co-owner identities, the entity's name and seat, its purpose, each co-owner's share size, and — critically for third parties — who has signing authority and whether individually or jointly.

## Contract First, Statute as Default

Art. 1066 establishes the governing hierarchy: relations among co-owners and the entity's representation are governed first by the co-owners' own agreement, with the statutory provisions (Art. 1067-1087) applying only to fill gaps. This makes nearly the entire remaining chapter default law that a well-drafted co-ownership agreement can override.

## Value-Weighted Majority Voting

Ordinary decisions require majority approval — but "majority" here means votes representing more than half of *all* shares in the ship, not merely a majority of those present at a meeting (Art. 1067(1)). Decisions amending the founding agreement, contradicting it, or falling outside the venture's purpose require unanimity instead (Art. 1067(2)), protecting minority co-owners from having the fundamental deal rewritten under them.

## Practical Takeaway

Turkish courts have confirmed that a company owning a ship solely — even if colloquially described as running it through a "partner" — falls outside donatma iştiraki entirely, meaning personal co-owner liability rules cannot be invoked against that company's shareholders.
