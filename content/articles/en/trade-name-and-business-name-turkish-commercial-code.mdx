Articles 39 through 53 of the Turkish Commercial Code (TTK) regulate the trade name — the legal identifier every merchant must use in commercial dealings — and the distinct concept of a "business name," which identifies the enterprise itself rather than its owner. Understanding how these two differ from each other, and from a trademark, is essential to knowing which protection applies against which kind of infringement.

## Mandatory Use and Registration

Every merchant must transact business under its trade name and sign related documents under that name (Art. 39). Registration with the commercial registry of the enterprise's location is required within fifteen days of opening (Art. 40). The required content of a trade name varies by legal form: an individual merchant's name is their own full name; partnerships must include at least one partner's name plus an indicator of company type; and joint stock, limited liability, and cooperative companies may choose freely but must include the mandatory type indicator, spelled out in full.

## Transfer and Protection

A trade name cannot be transferred separately from the underlying enterprise — but transferring the enterprise, absent an express agreement otherwise, transfers the trade name with it, and the transferee acquires the right to use it unchanged (Art. 49). Once duly registered and published, the right to use a trade name belongs exclusively to its owner (Art. 50). Turkish courts have held that using a validly registered trade name as registered does not by itself infringe someone else's trademark — but where a service-sector merchant uses its trade name on signage, catalogues, or advertising in a way likely to cause confusion with a registered trademark, this can constitute trademark infringement regardless of the trade-name registration.

## Remedies for Infringement

Where a trade name is used by another party in a manner contrary to commercial honesty, the rights holder may seek a declaratory judgment, an injunction, correction or deletion of the wrongful registration, destruction of infringing materials, and material and moral damages — including damages measured by the infringer's own gain (Art. 52). A frequently successful defense against such claims is "loss of rights through silence": a rights holder who knowingly tolerates the infringement for an extended period (Turkish courts have applied a benchmark of roughly five years) can be barred from later demanding cancellation under the good-faith principle.

Foreign companies establishing a Turkish subsidiary should confirm early whether their intended trade name conflicts with an existing registration anywhere in Turkey, since protection is nationwide, not limited to the local registry office.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### When must a trade name be registered?

Under TTK Article 40/1, <b>every merchant must, within fifteen days of the date the commercial enterprise opens, register and have published the commercial enterprise and the trade name it has chosen in the trade registry of the place where the enterprise's centre is located</b>. Furthermore, under Article 39/1, the merchant is required to carry out transactions relating to the enterprise <b>under the trade name</b> and to sign bills and other documents under that name.

### Can a trade name be sold separately from the enterprise?

No. Under TTK Article 49/1, <b>a trade name may not be transferred to another person separately from the enterprise</b>. Under Article 49/2, however, <b>the transfer of an enterprise results in the transfer of the name as well, unless otherwise expressly agreed</b>. That is, the name is regarded as an inseparable element of the enterprise; separating the name from the enterprise in a manner that would mislead its customer base is thereby prevented.

### What lawsuits can be brought in case of infringement of a trade name?

Under TTK Article 50/1, <b>the right to use a trade name that has been duly registered and published belongs solely to its owner</b>. Under Article 52, a person whose name is unlawfully used by another may request <b>a declaratory judgment establishing the infringement and its prohibition; if the wrongfully used name has been registered, its correction in accordance with the law or its deletion; the removal of the material consequences of the infringement; the destruction of the instruments where necessary; and, if damage has occurred, compensation proportionate to the degree of fault</b>. The court may also order publication of the judgment.
