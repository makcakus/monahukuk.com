Turkey charges VAT (Katma Değer Vergisi — KDV) on the sale of new residential and commercial properties. For most buyers, that tax is simply included in the asking price. For foreign nationals, however, Turkish law provides a meaningful exemption that can save a significant sum on a single purchase. If you are buying a new property from a developer in Antalya or another Turkish city and you are not a Turkish citizen, there is a good chance you can benefit from this relief — but only if you meet the conditions and follow the right process.

## What the VAT Exemption Means for Foreign Buyers

The exemption does not mean that you receive a cash refund from the tax authority. Instead, when you qualify, the developer issues you an invoice with zero VAT rather than the standard rate. The amount saved is passed on to you in the form of a lower sale price — or reflected in a separate line in your purchase agreement showing the discount. In practice, this means you pay less for the property at the time of purchase, with no need to file a separate refund claim.

The exemption only applies to the **first sale of a newly built property**. Second-hand properties bought from another individual do not qualify, no matter who the buyer is.

## Who Qualifies for the Exemption

To benefit, you must be a **foreign national** — a person who holds a foreign passport and is not a citizen of the Republic of Turkey. Turkish citizens who have lived abroad for many years or who hold dual nationality do not qualify for this specific relief.

Companies incorporated outside Turkey that purchase property in their own name can also qualify, provided the company is not registered or based in Turkey.

There is one additional condition: you must not be a tax resident in Turkey according to Turkish tax law. If you have lived in Turkey for more than six months in a calendar year, you are likely considered a tax resident there — and the exemption does not apply to you. This rule catches long-term expats who may be surprised to learn they no longer qualify.

## Which Properties Are Eligible

The exemption covers both residential and commercial units — apartments, villas, shops, offices — sold for the first time by the developer or the construction company. The key phrase is "first delivery": the property must be brand new and sold by the entity that built it or holds the first title.

Off-plan purchases also qualify, as long as the final title deed (*tapu*) is transferred only once and the payment conditions are met. If you put down a deposit on an unfinished project, the exemption is applied when the sale is formally completed at the title deed registry.

## How the Process Works

The process centres on one critical requirement: **the purchase price must be paid in foreign currency transferred from abroad** and then converted into Turkish lira at a Turkish bank. The bank must be an authorised institution, and the conversion record — a bank receipt called a *döviz alım belgesi* (foreign currency purchase document) — is essential.

In broad terms, the steps are:

- Transfer the purchase amount in foreign currency (USD, EUR, GBP, or another major currency) from your overseas bank account to a Turkish bank.
- Have the Turkish bank convert the funds to Turkish lira and issue the *döviz alım belgesi*.
- Provide this document to the developer as proof that the funds came from abroad.
- The developer applies the VAT exemption and issues a zero-VAT invoice for the sale.

The developer then declares the exemption in their own VAT filings. The buyer does not need to deal with the tax administration directly for this purpose.

It is strongly advisable to involve a lawyer early in this process. The documentation chain — from overseas bank transfer to the Turkish bank conversion certificate to the developer's invoice — must be complete and correct before the title deed is transferred. Errors at any stage can result in the exemption being denied or revoked. ## What Happens If You Sell Within the First Year

The VAT exemption is conditional on a one-year holding period. If you sell, transfer, or otherwise dispose of the property within one year of the purchase date shown on the title deed, the VAT that was exempted at the time of sale becomes payable. The tax authority can pursue this amount from the developer, who may in turn pursue it from you depending on how your purchase contract is drafted.

After one year, you are free to sell the property without triggering any VAT clawback.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q: Does the exemption also reduce title deed duty (*tapu harcı*)?
Title deed duty and VAT are separate charges. The VAT exemption applies only to the VAT component of your purchase. Title deed duty has its own rules and rates, which your lawyer can calculate based on the declared value.

### Q: Can I use a Turkish bank account I already hold to make the payment?
The requirement is that the funds must originate **from abroad**. If you transfer money from your overseas account to your existing Turkish bank account and then use it for the purchase, the trail from abroad must be traceable. If the funds mixed with other money in the account, the documentation chain may break. Confirm the approach with your bank and your lawyer before making any payments.

### Q: What if I am buying through a Turkish company I own?
If the purchasing entity is a Turkish company — even one wholly owned by a foreign national — the exemption does not apply. The exemption is reserved for foreign nationals and non-Turkish legal entities. A Turkish subsidiary would be treated as a domestic purchaser.

### Q: Is the exemption available on commercial properties too?
Yes. The exemption applies to both residential and commercial units, provided the property is new (first sale by the developer) and all other conditions are met.

### Q: Should I arrange a mortgage or use foreign currency savings?
If you are considering a mortgage, read our guide on [getting a mortgage as a foreign buyer in Turkey](/en/articles/mortgage-property-purchase-turkey-foreigners). Note that mortgage disbursements from a Turkish bank do not constitute "funds from abroad" for VAT exemption purposes — the exemption requires funds transferred from an overseas account.
