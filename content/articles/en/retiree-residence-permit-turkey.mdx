A significant proportion of the foreigners settling in Antalya and the surrounding area are retirees. This group — not intending to work, with income arriving from abroad, and planning a long stay — will find no dedicated "retirement residence permit" in the legislation. No such permit type exists in Turkish law. What a retired foreigner must do is choose, from among the grounds recognised by Law No. 6458, the one that fits their situation and satisfy its conditions. Choosing the right ground directly determines the duration of the permit and whether it can be renewed.

## Types of Residence Permit and Where the Retiree Fits

Article 30 of Law No. 6458 on Foreigners and International Protection lists the types of residence permit: short-term residence permit, family residence permit, student residence permit, long-term residence permit, humanitarian residence permit, and residence permit for victims of human trafficking.

For a retired foreigner the usual route is the **short-term residence permit**. The long-term permit requires an eight-year history and so cannot be a starting point, and the student permit is beside the point. The **family residence permit**, by contrast, should not be overlooked: a retiree married to a Turkish citizen, or to a foreigner holding a residence permit in Türkiye, may apply through the sponsor for a [family residence permit](/en/articles/family-residence-permit-spouse-turkish-citizen). Where that route is open it is more often than not the more favourable one. The assessment that follows is addressed to retirees without such a family tie.

## Which Ground for the Short-Term Permit?

The first paragraph of Article 31 of the Law lists, in eleven subparagraphs, the foreigners to whom a short-term residence permit may be granted. The grounds that may fit a retired foreigner's situation are selected from among them:

**(b) Those who own immovable property in Türkiye.** This is the ground most used by retirees. However, the sixth paragraph added to the article by Law No. 7533 of 21/11/2024 introduces an important qualification: **the nature and value of the immovable property that will serve as the basis for the grant of a short-term residence permit are determined by the Ministry.** Not every property therefore constitutes a ground for a permit automatically; the threshold of nature and value set by the administration applies. Anyone planning a [short-term permit based on property ownership](/en/articles/property-owner-short-term-residence-permit-turkey) should confirm that threshold before applying.

**(e) Those who will stay for tourism purposes.** This is the ground used by retirees who own no property and live in rented accommodation.

**(f) Those who will receive treatment, provided they do not carry one of the diseases classified as a threat to public health.** This may arise for retirees receiving ongoing treatment in Türkiye.

**(k) Citizens of the Turkish Republic of Northern Cyprus.** A separate subparagraph is provided for retirees who are citizens of the TRNC.

## Duration: The Two-Year Rule and Its Exceptions

The second paragraph of Article 31 — as amended by Law No. 6735 of 28/7/2016 — lays down the general rule: a short-term residence permit may be granted, save under subparagraphs (j) and (k) of the first paragraph, **for periods of not more than two years at a time**.

The exceptions appear in the fifth paragraph: permits granted to investors under subparagraph (j) and to TRNC citizens under subparagraph (k) may be granted **for periods of not more than five years at a time**.

It follows that a permit obtained by a retired foreigner on the ground of property or tourism will be for a maximum of two years and must be renewed at the end of that period. The words "not more than" call for care: the administration may grant a permit for less than two years; two years is an upper limit, not an entitlement.

## The Conditions Required

Article 32 of the Law lists the conditions required for the grant of short-term residence permits. Those most relevant to a retired applicant are as follows:

**Establishing the ground.** The applicant must put forward one or more of the grounds listed in the first paragraph of Article 31 and produce the information and documents relating to that request.

**Not falling within the scope of inadmissible passengers.** The applicant must not fall within the scope of Article 7 of the Law. That article governs the foreigners who are not to be permitted entry to Türkiye: those who have no passport, visa or permit, or who are found to have obtained those documents by fraudulent means; **those whose passport is not valid for at least sixty days beyond the expiry of the visa, visa exemption or residence permit period**; and the foreigners listed in the first paragraph of Article 15. The sixty-day passport rule means that retirees must plan the passport renewal calendar together with the permit period.

**Accommodation.** The article expressly requires the applicant **to have accommodation conditions meeting general health and safety standards**. For retirees this is evidenced by a title deed or a lease agreement.

**Criminal record.** Where requested, it may be necessary to produce a criminal record certificate obtained from the competent authorities of the country of nationality or of lawful residence.

**Address information.** Subparagraph (d) of the article requires the applicant to give the details of the address at which they will stay in Türkiye. This is a condition separate from, and preparatory to, the [address registration obligation](/en/articles/address-registration-obligation-foreigners-turkey) that arises after the permit has been issued.

In addition, two further elements are decisive for retirees in practice: **valid health insurance** and **sufficient income**. The [health insurance requirement](/en/articles/health-insurance-requirement-residence-permit-turkey) is the subject of separate regulation and, since obtaining a policy can become difficult for retirees on account of age, should be planned early.

## Refusal, Cancellation, and Non-Renewal

Article 33 governs the refusal, cancellation, and non-renewal of a short-term residence permit. The two grounds most often encountered by retirees are:

- **Failure to satisfy, or the disappearance of, one or more of the conditions required by Article 32.** The lapse of health insurance falls within this.
- **A finding that the residence permit is being used for a purpose other than that for which it was granted.** Actually working under a permit obtained on tourism grounds is the typical example.

An existing deportation order or ban on entry to Türkiye is also a ground for refusal.

The seventh paragraph added to Article 31 by Law No. 7533 also addresses judicial recourse: in applications to the court against decisions refusing a short-term residence permit application or cancelling a permit, the file is deemed complete upon the filing of the defence or the expiry of the period for filing it; whether a hearing is held is a matter for the court's discretion. It should be borne in mind that the [appeal against a refusal](/en/articles/residence-permit-refusal-appeal-turkey) proceeds under this accelerated procedure.

## The Long-Term Plan: Eight Years

For a retired foreigner the ultimate objective is usually a permanent status. The first paragraph of Article 42 of the Law provides that foreigners **who have resided in Türkiye continuously for at least eight years under a residence permit**, or who satisfy the conditions determined by the Ministry, are granted an indefinite residence permit by the governorates with the approval of the Ministry.

Because years spent under short-term permits count towards that eight-year period, renewing the two-year permits without interruption is the foundation of the long-term plan. Lengthy stays abroad that create an interruption are therefore risky; the [rules on time spent abroad](/en/articles/absence-rules-turkish-residence-permit) should be assessed separately.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is there such a thing as a "retirement residence permit" in Türkiye?

No. There is no category specific to retirement among the permit types listed in Article 30 of Law No. 6458. Retirees apply for a short-term residence permit by putting forward whichever of the grounds in Article 31 fits their situation — usually property ownership or tourism.

### I have bought a house — do I automatically get a residence permit?

No. Property ownership is listed as a ground in subparagraph (b) of Article 31, but under the sixth paragraph added by Law No. 7533 the nature and value of the property serving as the basis for the permit are determined by the Ministry. The other conditions in Article 32 must also be satisfied.

### For how many years is the permit granted?

A short-term residence permit is granted for periods of not more than two years at a time. This is an upper limit; the administration may grant a shorter permit. The five-year period is provided only for investors and TRNC citizens.

### My pension comes from abroad — is that a problem?

The income may originate abroad; what matters is being able to evidence sufficient and regular income. Pension payslips, bank statements, and any letters from the pension institution are used for this purpose.
