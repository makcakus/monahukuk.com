Having completed the entire Freight Contract chapter, the Code turns to two short but consequential provisions: a general prescription period for money claims, and the opening definition of a wholly new topic — carriage of passengers by sea.

## A One-Year Prescription Period — Not to Be Confused with the Cargo-Damage Time-Bar

Art. 1246(1) sets a one-year prescription period for all claims arising from bareboat charters, time charters, freight contracts, or bills of lading, running from the date the claim becomes due (Art. 1246(2)). Critically, this is expressly "subject to Art. 1188" — the separate one-year *preclusive* period for cargo loss, damage, or delay claims covered in an earlier article.

These two "one-year" periods are easy to confuse but function completely differently. Prescription under Art. 1246 is a defense (a plea) that a court will not raise on its own motion, and it can be interrupted or suspended by, for example, filing suit. The Art. 1188 preclusive period is not subject to those interruption rules and, in principle, a court must apply it even if neither party raises it. The Court of Cassation has reversed lower courts for conflating the two — misapplying Art. 1246's prescription rule to what was actually a cargo-loss claim governed by Art. 1188, with the wrong period potentially producing the wrong outcome.

## Passenger Carriage Contracts: The Opening Definition

Art. 1247(1) defines a sea passenger carriage contract as one made by, or on behalf of and for the account of, the carrier, for carrying a passenger or a passenger and baggage by sea — deliberately mirroring the structure used to define freight contracts, but shifting the subject from cargo to people. This shift matters: where cargo risk translates into a calculable monetary loss, passenger-carriage risk concerns physical safety and life, requiring an entirely different liability architecture that the Code builds out in the following articles.

The chapter also applies to commercial passenger carriage undertaken by the State or public entities (Art. 1247(2)) — public operators get no lower safety standard — while excluding carriage by hovercraft, which does not fit the ship-based model this chapter is built around (Art. 1247(3)).
