TTK Art. 1001-1005 cover the everyday mechanics of buying, selling, and losing ownership of a registered Turkish ship — the provisions that govern the overwhelming majority of real-world ship transactions.

## The Formal Requirements for Transfer

Art. 1001 requires two cumulative elements for a registered ship to change hands: an agreement between owner and acquirer on the transfer, and actual delivery of possession. The agreement itself must be in writing with notarized signatures (or executed at the ship registry office directly) — this formal requirement is a validity condition, not a mere evidentiary preference, and Turkish courts treat a transfer lacking it as void. Ownership passes at the moment agreement and delivery coincide — the registry update that follows is merely declaratory, though it does govern enforceability against third parties under the reliance principle.

## What Transfers Automatically

Under Art. 1002, ownership carries the ship's equipment and fittings (navigation gear, safety equipment) along with it unless the parties agree otherwise; if equipment turns out to belong to someone else, the general good-faith acquisition rules for movables apply, judged as of the moment possession was taken. Where a ship changes hands mid-voyage, the profit and loss of that voyage default to the new owner absent a contrary agreement — keeping ownership and operating risk aligned.

## Physical Loss and Voluntary Abandonment

Art. 1003 ends ownership automatically when a ship sinks beyond recovery, is destroyed, or is otherwise lost with no usable wreck remaining — but critically, the owner's wreck-removal and environmental obligations survive this loss of ownership; a ship cannot be declared "lost" as a way of escaping cleanup liability. Art. 1004 provides the voluntary counterpart: abandonment requires a formal notification to, and registration at, the registry office — not just an informal change of mind — precisely because an abandoned ship becomes ownerless property subject to the State's exclusive occupation right discussed in the prior article.

## Practical Takeaway

Turkish courts require solid documentary proof for ownership-transfer claims — even between family members, a disputed or forged signature on the transfer instrument has been fatal to an ownership claim, regardless of how long the relationship or informal arrangement had existed.
