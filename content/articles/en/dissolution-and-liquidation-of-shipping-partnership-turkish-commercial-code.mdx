TTK Art. 1082-1087 close the chapter on joint shipping ownership by setting out how it ends and how the ship is liquidated.

## Three Routes to Dissolution

The venture can be dissolved by the same value-weighted majority vote used for other decisions (Art. 1082) — and, tellingly, a decision to sell the ship itself is automatically treated as a dissolution decision, since without the ship there is nothing left for the entity to organize around. Second, any co-owner may seek court permission to exit for cause, and — if refused — ask the court to dissolve the entire venture instead (Art. 1083). "Cause" is deliberately narrow: it must stem from the other co-owners' own conduct constituting a breach, not merely the exiting co-owner's personal preference, to prevent this becoming an unrestricted unilateral exit right. Where the court finds cause established, it first gives the remaining co-owners a chance to buy out the exiting share at an expert-determined value before ordering full dissolution — preserving the venture where possible. This entire protection is mandatory and cannot be contracted away to the co-owners' detriment. Third, the venture's own bankruptcy ends it outright (Art. 1084).

## What Does *Not* End It

A co-owner's individual death or personal bankruptcy does not dissolve the venture (Art. 1085) — reinforcing, once more, that this is an asset-centered structure. The ship keeps operating; only the ownership of that co-owner's share changes hands.

## Liquidation by Auction

Once dissolved, the default is a public auction sale, chosen precisely to guarantee transparency and real market value rather than leaving disposal to one insider's discretion (Art. 1086). Sale timing is restricted while the ship is mid-voyage under an active carriage contract — protecting third-party cargo interests — unless a court has certified the ship as beyond repair. Sale terms can be modified only by unanimous co-owner agreement. Where co-owners cannot agree on sale terms or a liquidator, the court appoints one, applying the Code's general-partnership liquidator rules by analogy.

## An Additional Forum for Claims Against Co-Owners

As with the shipowner rules covered elsewhere in this series, claims against co-owners in that capacity may also be brought at the ship's home port (Art. 1087) — convenient given evidentiary and administrative concentration there.

## Practical Takeaway

Turkish courts have confirmed this entire liquidation regime applies only where genuine co-ownership of the ship was actually established — arrangements that merely resemble joint operation but lack registered co-ownership fall instead under ordinary Turkish Code of Obligations partnership dissolution rules.
