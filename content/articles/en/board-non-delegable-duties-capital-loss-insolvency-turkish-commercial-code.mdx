TTK Art. 374-378 define the outer edges of a Turkish joint-stock company board's authority: a broad catch-all power to decide any matter needed to run the business (Art. 374), a short list of duties that can never be delegated away (Art. 375), and a graduated set of obligations that kick in once the company's financial health seriously deteriorates (Art. 376-378).

## Non-Delegable Duties

Art. 375 lists seven core duties the board alone must exercise: top-level management and instructions, defining the management organization, setting up the accounting and financial-planning framework, appointing and dismissing general managers and equivalent officers (excluding branch managers), overseeing compliance by those managing the company, maintaining statutory records and preparing the annual activity report, and notifying the court of balance-sheet insolvency. Turkish courts have treated a shareholders' meeting appointing a general manager directly — bypassing the board — as void rather than merely voidable, since this duty belongs exclusively to the board; separately, the duty to notify the court of insolvency applies to every director regardless of whether they hold representation authority, exposing all board members to criminal liability for failing to file.

## Capital Loss: A Two-Stage Warning System

Where the last annual balance sheet shows losses have wiped out half of share capital plus statutory reserves, the board must immediately convene shareholders and propose remedial measures (Art. 376/1) — an early warning stage with no automatic sanction. If losses reach two-thirds of capital and reserves, shareholders must resolve either to continue with the remaining one-third or replenish capital; failing that, the company dissolves automatically without any court order (Art. 376/2) — one of the Code's harshest self-executing dissolution triggers.

## Balance-Sheet Insolvency and Concordat

Where signs suggest the company may be balance-sheet insolvent, the board must prepare an interim balance sheet on both a going-concern and liquidation basis; if assets fall short of covering creditor claims, the board must notify the commercial court and request bankruptcy (Art. 376/3) — unless creditors formally agree in writing to subordinate their claims by an amount sufficient to cure the shortfall, verified by court-appointed experts. Either the board or any creditor may request concordat (a court-supervised restructuring) alongside or during this bankruptcy filing instead of outright liquidation (Art. 377).

## Early Risk Detection

Listed companies must maintain a dedicated committee for early detection of risks threatening the company's survival, reporting to the board every two months; unlisted companies must do so only if the auditor formally requests it (Art. 378).
