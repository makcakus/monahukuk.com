*Article 18/A – (Added: 6/12/2018-7155/23)*

*(1) Where the relevant laws accept that application to a mediator is a condition of action, the following provisions apply to the mediation process.*

*(2) The claimant must attach to the statement of claim the original of the final minutes recording that no settlement was reached at the end of the mediation activity, or a copy certified by the mediator. (…) Where it emerges that the action was brought without applying to a mediator, the action is dismissed on procedural grounds for lack of a condition of action, without any further step being taken.*

*(9) The mediator concludes the application within three weeks of the date of appointment. In compulsory circumstances this period may be extended by the mediator by a maximum of one week.*

*(11) Where the mediation activity ends because one of the parties failed, without showing a valid excuse, to attend the first meeting, the non-attending party is identified in the final minutes and, even if wholly or partly successful in the action, is held liable for half of the litigation costs payable by the other side. (Second sentence amended: 7/11/2024-7531/25) In addition, half of the attorney's fee determined under the Minimum Attorney's Fee Tariff is awarded in favour of that other party. (…)*

*(15) In the period running from the application to the mediation bureau until the date on which the final minutes are drawn up, limitation is suspended and no forfeiture period runs.*

Article 18/A is the **umbrella provision** of mandatory mediation. It does not itself bring any dispute within the scope of mandatory mediation; which disputes fall within scope is determined by the relevant special laws (Art. 3 of Law No. 7036, Art. 5/A of the Turkish Commercial Code, Art. 73/A of Law No. 6502) and by Article 18/B of Law No. 6325. Article 18/A establishes the common procedure applicable to all of those disputes: where to apply, how the mediator is appointed, what the time limit is, the sanction for non-attendance, who bears the fee, and the effect of the process on limitation.

## The Nature of the Condition: It Cannot Be Completed Later

The harshest feature of mandatory mediation is what distinguishes it from the general conditions of action in the Code of Civil Procedure. The 11th Civil Chamber of the Court of Cassation holds that application to a mediator is "by its nature a **special condition of action that cannot be completed later**", so that applying to mediation after the action has been brought has no effect on the merits. The same Chamber has stated that the other (subsequently completable) conditions of action in the file cannot be examined before the mediation condition is met, and that the mediation condition must be examined first, with a procedural dismissal being given without even the need to complete the court fee.

Article 18/A/2 attaches different sanctions to two different defects:

- **Where no application to a mediator has been made at all**: the action is dismissed on procedural grounds without any step being taken, and without the statement of claim being served on the other side.
- **Where an application has been made but the final minutes are not attached to the statement of claim**: the court sends a summons containing a warning that the minutes must be submitted within a peremptory period of one week, failing which the action will be dismissed on procedural grounds. If the warning is not complied with, the action is dismissed.

In the second case, the document to be submitted must be the **original or a copy certified by the mediator**. In accordance with the 9th Civil Chamber's decision on the resolution of a conflict of case law, a photocopy is not sufficient, and the fact that the judge can access the document through the UYAP system does not affect the outcome.

One exception to this strictness has been developed by case law as regards jurisdiction ratione materiae. According to the practice of the 9th Civil Chamber, even where the mediation condition was not met before the action was brought before a court lacking jurisdiction, the condition is regarded as fulfilled if the mediator was applied to and the process ended with final minutes of non-settlement **before the file was registered on the docket of the competent court**.

## The Application and Appointment of the Mediator

The application is made, according to the subject matter of the dispute, to the mediation bureau **at the place of the competent court**, and in places where no bureau has been established, to the designated registry office (Art. 18/A/4).

The mediator is designated by the bureau from the list notified by the Department to the commission presidencies; but where the parties agree on any mediator appearing on the list, that mediator is appointed (Art. 18/A/5).

The applicant party gives the bureau every kind of contact information relating to itself and, if available, to the other side; the bureau is also empowered to research contact information in official records, and the relevant institutions and organisations are obliged to provide it (Art. 18/A/6). On the basis of that information, the mediator informs the parties and invites them to the first meeting; **the mediator also informs the party themselves even where they have a lawyer**, and documents these steps (Art. 18/A/7).

Making the invitation in due form is the mediator's responsibility. The 9th Civil Chamber has stated that even where it is accepted that the other side was not duly invited, no fault attributable to a party who made the application in accordance with the law can be spoken of.

## Objection to Jurisdiction: One Opportunity, the First Meeting

Article 18/A/8 regulates the only "judicial interlude" within the mediation process and imposes strict timing.

The mediator **cannot take into account of their own motion** whether the bureau making the appointment has jurisdiction. The other side may, **at the latest at the first meeting**, object to the bureau's jurisdiction by submitting the relevant documents. If this single opportunity is missed, lack of jurisdiction cannot be raised later.

Upon objection, the mediator immediately delivers the file to the bureau to be sent to the relevant civil court of peace. Following an examination on the file **without any fee being charged**, the court determines the competent bureau **finally** within one week at the latest and returns the file to the bureau. The decision is served on the parties by the bureau.

The consequences are these: if the objection is rejected, the same mediator is reappointed and the three-week period runs from the new appointment date. If the objection is upheld, the competent bureau may be applied to **within one week** of service of the decision; in that case the date of application to the incompetent bureau is deemed the date of application to the competent bureau — that is, the original application date is preserved for limitation purposes.

Unless the parties agree otherwise, the sessions are conducted within the jurisdictional area of the judicial commission to which the bureau appointing the mediator is attached (Art. 18/A/17).

## Time Limit: Three Weeks, Four in Compulsory Cases

The mediator concludes the application within **three weeks** of the date of appointment; in compulsory circumstances this may be extended by the mediator by a maximum of **one week** (Art. 18/A/9). The decision to extend belongs to the mediator and does not depend on a party's request.

The mediator terminates the activity, draws up the final minutes and immediately notifies the bureau where the parties cannot be reached, where no session could be held because the parties did not attend, or where the parties settle or fail to settle (Art. 18/A/10).

## The Non-Attendance Sanction and the 2024 Amendment

Article 18/A/11 is the deterrent core of mandatory mediation. A party who, without showing a valid excuse, **fails to attend the first meeting** is identified in the final minutes and, even if wholly or partly successful in the action to be brought, is held liable for **half of the litigation costs** payable by the other side.

The second sentence, amended by Law No. 7531 of 07.11.2024, has made the sanction more severe: in addition, **half of the attorney's fee** determined under the Minimum Attorney's Fee Tariff is awarded in favour of the attending party. Before the amendment a different method of calculation was provided for this item; today the criterion is directly the Tariff.

Where neither party attends the first meeting, the litigation costs incurred by the parties in the action to be brought are left on their own shoulders.

Three conditions are required for the sanction to apply: the non-attendance must relate to the first meeting, there must be no valid excuse, and the non-attending party must be **identified in the final minutes**. If that record is absent from the minutes, the court cannot apply the sanction. A party who attends but does not settle does not face this sanction.

## The Fee Regime and Necessary Expenses

Where a settlement is reached, the fee is met equally by the parties in accordance with the Second Part of the Tariff, unless otherwise agreed, and cannot be less than the two-hour fee in the First Part (Art. 18/A/12). Where the parties cannot be reached, where there is no attendance, or where no settlement is reached at the end of sessions lasting less than two hours, the two-hour fee is paid **from the budget of the Ministry of Justice**; the part exceeding two hours is met equally by the parties (Art. 18/A/13). In both cases the fee counts as litigation costs. Necessary expenses to be incurred by the bureau are met by the parties where a settlement is reached and, where none is reached, from the Ministry's budget, to be collected from the party who is ultimately unsuccessful (Art. 18/A/14).

## Cases Outside the Scope and the Reservation of Special Provisions

Article 18/A/18 sets an important limit: where special laws impose an obligation to resort to arbitration or another alternative dispute-resolution method, or where there is an **arbitration agreement**, the provisions on mediation as a condition of action do not apply. Arbitration under Article 30 of the Insurance Law and arbitration clauses inserted into contracts fall within this scope.

Under Article 18/A/19, special provisions accepted in the relevant laws concerning mediation as a condition of action are reserved; under Article 18/A/20, in cases not provided for in this chapter, the other provisions of the Law apply to the extent compatible with its nature.

## Case Law of the Court of Cassation

The following decisions show the practical limits of mandatory mediation.

**1. Court of Cassation, 11th Civil Chamber, Case No. 2024/5785, Decision No. 2025/3497, dated 20.05.2025**

Stating that application to a mediator is, by its nature, a special condition of action that cannot be completed later, and that applying to mediation after the action has been brought therefore has no effect on the merits, the Chamber found it correct that the action was dismissed on procedural grounds under Article 18/A of Law No. 6325 and Articles 114/2 and 115 of the Code of Civil Procedure.

**2. Court of Cassation, 11th Civil Chamber, Case No. 2021/9039, Decision No. 2023/3580, dated 07.06.2023**

The decision establishes that the subsequently completable conditions of action cannot be examined before the mediation condition is met, and that examining the mediation condition first and giving a procedural dismissal without the need to complete the court fee is in accordance with procedure and law.

**3. Court of Cassation, 9th Civil Chamber, Case No. 2024/1984, Decision No. 2024/4213, dated 06.03.2024**

The Chamber stated that even where the mediation condition was not met before the action was brought before a court lacking jurisdiction, the condition is regarded as fulfilled where the mediator was applied to and the process concluded with final minutes of non-settlement before the file was registered on the docket of the competent court.

**4. Court of Cassation, 11th Civil Chamber, Case No. 2021/8645, Decision No. 2023/2637, dated 03.05.2023**

In the dispute it was established that there was no record whatsoever of a mediation application and that the claimant had brought the action for annulment of the objection without applying to mandatory mediation as a condition of action. The Chamber found it correct that, under Article 18/A/2, the action was dismissed on procedural grounds for lack of a condition of action without any further step being taken.

**5. Court of Cassation, 9th Civil Chamber, Case No. 2026/72, Decision No. 2026/2339, dated 12.03.2026**

Against the employee's action for payment, the employer brought a counterclaim for pecuniary and non-pecuniary damages and a contractual penalty; the first-instance court dismissed the counterclaim on procedural grounds because the mediation condition had not been met. Although the employer argued that the same matters had been discussed in the mediation conducted for the principal action and that no separate mediation was needed for the counterclaim, the Chamber upheld the procedural dismissal as being in accordance with procedure and law. The decision shows that **the condition of action must be satisfied separately in respect of a counterclaim**.
