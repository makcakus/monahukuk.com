*Article 16 – (1) Where a mediator is applied to before an action is brought, the mediation process begins on the date on which the parties are invited to the first meeting and agreement is reached between the parties and the mediator on continuing the process and this is documented in minutes. Where a mediator is applied to after an action has been brought, the process begins on the date on which the court's invitation to mediation is accepted by the parties, or on which the parties declare in writing to the court outside a hearing that they have agreed to apply to a mediator, or on which such a declaration is recorded in the minutes of a hearing.*

*(2) The period elapsing from the commencement of the mediation process to its termination is not taken into account in the calculation of limitation periods and preclusive time limits.*

For a party applying to mediation, the most concrete legal benefit is the effect of the process on limitation periods and preclusive time limits. It would be unacceptable for a party attempting to negotiate a dispute to lose rights while making that effort; Article 16/2 guarantees against this. But **when** the protection begins differs between voluntary and mandatory mediation, and that difference is important enough to cause a loss of rights in practice.

## The Commencement of the Process: Three Separate Criteria

Article 16/1 fixes the commencement by distinguishing two situations.

**Where the application is made before an action is brought**, the process does not begin automatically at the moment of application. Two conditions must be satisfied together: the parties must be invited to the first meeting **and** agreement must be reached between the parties and the mediator on continuing the process, this being **documented in minutes**. In other words, an offer of mediation made to the other side and left unanswered does not start the process; under Article 13/2, if no positive answer is given within thirty days the offer is deemed rejected and the limitation period will never have been suspended.

**Where the application is made after an action has been brought**, there are three alternative starting moments: acceptance by the parties of the court's invitation to mediation, a written declaration by the parties to the court outside a hearing that they have so agreed, or the recording of such a declaration in the minutes of a hearing.

## A Different Starting Point in Mandatory Mediation: Article 18/A/15

In mandatory mediation the protection begins much earlier. Under Article 18/A/15, the limitation period is suspended and preclusive periods do not run for the time elapsing **from the application to the mediation office** until the date the final minutes are drawn up.

The difference is decisive: whereas Article 16/1 requires an invitation to the first meeting and documentation in minutes, Article 18/A/15 is satisfied by **the date of application to the office**. The practical consequence is that the protection operates even in files where the other party could never be reached or never attended. For a claim close to becoming time-barred, an application to mandatory mediation is therefore in itself a protective step.

The 6th Civil Chamber of the Turkish Court of Cassation notes that Article 18/A/15 is a provision similar to Article 16/2 and regards taking that provision into account as necessary in terms of **the right of access to a court**.

## "Suspension" or "Interruption"?

The technical concept chosen by the Law is suspension, not interruption. The difference matters: on interruption the period starts again from the beginning (Article 156 of the Turkish Code of Obligations), whereas on suspension the period resumes from where it stopped. Days elapsing in mediation are disregarded in calculating limitation; time that ran before the application is not erased.

An example makes this clearer. For a claim subject to a ten-year limitation period, if nine years and eleven months have passed and the party applied to the mediation office and the process lasted twenty days, the time remaining to bring an action is not one month but one month plus twenty days. The limitation period is not reset.

In one decision the 3rd Civil Chamber gave this calculation concrete form, expressly stating that the preclusive period did not run between 11 May 2020, when the mediation process began, and 4 June 2020, when it ended.

## The Scope of the Protection: More Than the Time Limit for Suing

The protection is not confined to general limitation and preclusive periods.

**The period for bringing an action after an interim measure or interim attachment.** Under Article 18/A/16, where an interim measure was granted before an action was brought, the period in Article 397/1 of the Turkish Code of Civil Procedure, and where an interim attachment was granted, the period in Article 264/1 of the Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law, does not run from the application to the mediation office until the date the final minutes are drawn up.

**The period for bringing a negative declaratory action.** Under a sentence added in 2023, if enforcement proceedings concerning the subject matter of the dispute are brought against the applicant after the application to the mediation office, and the applicant brings a negative declaratory action under Article 72 of the Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law **within two weeks** of the date the final minutes were drawn up and so requests, Article 72/2 of that Law applies.

**Arbitration and administrative law periods.** The 4th Civil Chamber has accepted that the date on which the final mediation minutes were drawn up must also be treated as the date of application to the Insurance Arbitration Commission. The Council of State, for its part, calculates the time limit in an action for annulment brought after a period suspended during mediation in accordance with the relevant provision of Law No. 7036.

**Additional time where the court lacks subject-matter or territorial jurisdiction.** The 6th Civil Chamber reads Article 18/A/15 together with Article 158 of the Turkish Code of Obligations. That article grants the creditor sixty additional days where an action or defence is dismissed because the court lacked territorial or subject-matter jurisdiction, because of a rectifiable error, or because it was brought prematurely, and the limitation or preclusive period has meanwhile expired.

## The Protection Ends When the Process Ends

The protection ends with the termination of the process. Under Article 17/2, whether the parties settled, failed to settle or how the activity concluded is documented in minutes; the periods start running again from the date those minutes were drawn up.

Miscalculation here is the source of one of the most frequent losses of rights in practice. Particularly for the one-month period in reinstatement claims, the short preclusive periods for certain claims arising from lease relationships, and limitation in traffic accident cases, the date on which the final minutes were **drawn up** must not be confused with the date on which they were **served** on the party or uploaded to the national judicial IT system; the Law expressly takes the date of drawing up as the reference.

## Judicial Decisions

The following decisions show how the effect of mediation on time limits is calculated in practice.

**1. Court of Cassation, 6th Civil Chamber, File No. 2023/4026, Decision No. 2024/1590, 16.05.2024**

The Chamber stated that Article 16/2 in the fourth part of Law No. 6325 and Article 18/A/15 on mandatory mediation are provisions of a similar character, and regarded taking Article 18/A/15 into account as necessary in terms of the right of access to a court.

**2. Court of Cassation, 6th Civil Chamber, File No. 2024/607, Decision No. 2024/1690, 23.05.2024**

The decision reads the suspension rule in Article 18/A/15 together with Article 158 of the Turkish Code of Obligations, which grants the creditor additional time where the action is dismissed because the court lacked territorial or subject-matter jurisdiction, because of a rectifiable error, or because it was brought prematurely.

**3. Court of Cassation, 3rd Civil Chamber, File No. 2022/4437, Decision No. 2022/7530, 10.10.2022**

The Chamber stated that, under Article 18/A/15 of Law No. 6325, the preclusive period does not run for the time elapsing from the application to the mediation office until the date the final minutes are drawn up, and set out the calculation expressly by reference to the dates on which the mediation process began and ended in the case at hand.

**4. Court of Cassation, 4th Civil Chamber, File No. 2024/10406, Decision No. 2025/15641, 24.11.2025**

In a dispute over a compensation claim arising from a traffic accident, the Chamber applied Article 18/A/15 and stated that the time elapsing during the mediation process is not taken into account in the calculation of limitation.

**5. Court of Cassation, 11th Civil Chamber, File No. 2024/3701, Decision No. 2025/2400, 14.04.2025**

Citing Articles 16/2 and 18/A/15 together, the Chamber established that the calculation of time must be based on the application and conclusion dates shown in the final mediation minutes.
