Turkey has seen a steady rise in international professionals settling in cities like Antalya, and more employers — both Turkish and foreign-owned businesses — are asking what steps they must follow when bringing foreign nationals onto their payroll. The rules under the International Workforce Law (*Uluslararası İşgücü Kanunu*) are clear, but they carry obligations that fall primarily on the employer, not the employee.

## Who Applies for the Work Permit?

This is the question that surprises many businesses: in Turkey, **the employer applies for the work permit**, not the foreign employee. Applications are submitted electronically through the Ministry of Labour and Social Security (ÇSGB) portal. Once the Ministry provisionally approves the application, the prospective employee must apply for the appropriate work visa at the nearest Turkish consulate in their home country.

This two-step structure means that planning ahead is essential. The employer needs to have the position, salary, and employment contract terms finalised before submitting, and the employee needs enough time to obtain the visa and travel to Turkey.

One practical upside: a valid work permit also serves as a residence permit for the duration of its validity. The foreign employee does not need to obtain a separate residence document while working legally under a Turkish work permit.

## Workforce Quota Requirements

The International Workforce Law places a limit on how many foreign employees a business can hire relative to its total insured Turkish workforce. This quota rule catches many growing companies off guard, particularly start-ups and small businesses that want to bring in international talent early.

Exceptions exist and can be significant. Companies operating in free trade zones, licensed R&D centres, and certain investment incentive zones benefit from more flexible arrangements. Highly skilled specialists, executives of internationally-headquartered companies, and professionals in shortage occupations are often assessed under separate criteria. Employers in Antalya's tourism, hospitality, and technology sectors regularly use these pathways to secure permits for the staff they need.

## Salary and Employment Conditions

The Ministry reviews the salary offered against minimum thresholds that vary depending on the role. Managers, senior engineers, and certain specialist positions face a higher minimum than standard roles. An offer below the applicable threshold is likely to result in the permit being refused.

Beyond salary, the employment terms must fully comply with Turkish labour law. A foreign employee's [annual leave entitlement](/en/articles/annual-leave-rights-foreign-employees-turkey), working hours, rest breaks, and overtime rights are identical to those of Turkish colleagues. Foreign nationals cannot be engaged on terms that fall below the statutory floor just because they are not Turkish citizens.

## SGK Registration: A Separate, Parallel Duty

Obtaining the work permit is not the end of the employer's obligations. Before the employee starts work, the employer must also register the foreign national with the Social Security Institution (SGK). Premiums are calculated and paid in the same way as for Turkish employees.

These are two distinct legal duties. A valid work permit does not automatically satisfy the SGK registration requirement, and failing to register carries its own set of administrative penalties separate from any work permit violation.

## Penalties for Employing Without a Permit

The consequences for hiring a foreign national without a valid work permit are serious on both sides. Employers face administrative fines, and they also become liable for the costs of the employee's deportation and subsistence until departure. Repeat violations attract higher penalties.

For the foreign national, the consequences include deportation and an entry ban that may prevent them from returning to Turkey for a considerable period. This means that an employer's administrative oversight directly harms the employee's future. For this reason, if a work permit application is [denied and the refusal seems unjustified](/en/articles/work-permit-denial-appeal-turkey), it is worth challenging the decision formally rather than simply starting work and hoping for the best.

## Renewal and End of Employment

Work permits expire and must be renewed before they lapse. The renewal application follows a similar process to the initial one, submitted again by the employer through the Ministry's online system. After several years of continuous legal employment, certain employees become eligible for longer-validity permits.

When the employment relationship ends, the work permit is tied to that employer and ceases to apply. A foreign national who wishes to continue working in Turkey must begin a new permit application with their next employer. This is also relevant for employers: [terminating a foreign employee](/en/articles/foreign-employee-termination-turkey) requires the same notice periods and statutory payments as terminating any Turkish employee, and the permit withdrawal process should be managed alongside the exit from employment.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q: Can my company start the hiring process before we have enough Turkish employees to meet the quota?

Quota rules depend on your current insured headcount. In practice, some exemptions and professional classifications allow smaller companies to bring in key foreign hires even before reaching the general quota threshold. A lawyer can assess your specific situation.

### Q: How long does it take to get a work permit approved?

The Ministry aims to process complete applications within a set statutory period. Well-documented applications for straightforward roles can come through in a few weeks. Incomplete files or unusual role descriptions take longer. Building in adequate lead time is essential for smooth onboarding.

### Q: Is a work permit required for short-term consultants or experts visiting Turkey?

Short-term specialist work may fall within a specific permit category or, in some cases, within visa exemptions for business visits. The lines between a legal business visit and work requiring a permit can be narrow, and the employer bears the risk if the boundary is crossed.

### Q: What if we are a foreign company with no registered presence in Turkey?

Foreign companies without a Turkish legal entity cannot sponsor a work permit directly. Establishing a branch, liaison office, or subsidiary is typically the first step. Each form of presence has different legal implications worth considering carefully.
