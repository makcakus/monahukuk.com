Facing a criminal accusation in Turkey is a heavy experience for any person; for a foreigner, it becomes more complicated due to language barrier, unfamiliarity with foreign legal system, and limited remote support. As a law firm representing foreign-national clients in criminal cases in Antalya for years, in this guide we cover the basic rights and procedural rules foreigners need to know about the criminal process.

## General Structure of Turkish Criminal Procedure

Turkish criminal procedure consists of two main phases:

### 1. Investigation Phase

When a criminal suspicion arises, the **Public Prosecutor** initiates investigation. In this phase:

- Suspect's statement is taken,
- Victims and witnesses are heard,
- Criminal evidence is gathered,
- Where needed, protective measures such as search, seizure, custody, detention are applied,
- At investigation's end, the prosecutor either issues an **indictment** or decides not to prosecute (KYOK).

### 2. Prosecution Phase

If the indictment is accepted, the case begins before the **court**:

- **Court of First Instance** — lighter offences,
- **Heavy Penal Court** — more serious offences,
- Special courts like **Juvenile Court / Family Court**.

The court evaluates evidence, hears witnesses, and renders judgment. **Appeal** and **cassation** routes are open against decisions.

## Foreign Suspect's Basic Rights

In Turkish criminal procedure, the foreign suspect has the same fundamental rights as Turkish citizens. Additionally, some rights specific to being a foreigner:

### Right to Translator

For foreigners without sufficient Turkish proficiency:

- **Free translator** can be requested during statements,
- Translator is provided in court as well,
- Indictment and documents given for defence are translated.

The translator must be sworn and competent; claims of translator inadequacy can be an important defence argument.

### Right to Consular Notification

When a foreigner is taken into custody or detained:

- Right to **notification to home state's consulate**,
- Right to communicate with consular officers,
- This right derives from Vienna Convention on Consular Relations.

Consular involvement provides critical support to the foreign suspect; it includes family communication, lawyer recommendations, and financial support.

### Right to Counsel

The suspect's **right to legal representation** functions from the moment of statement. For foreign suspect:

- Right to consult chosen lawyer,
- If financially insufficient, right to **bar-assigned free counsel**,
- Right to **private consultation** with lawyer (unmonitored)

are guaranteed.

### Right to Silence

The suspect is not required to give a statement. Right to silence:

- Cannot be used adversely,
- Is reminded at start and end of statement,
- Is communicated to foreign suspect via translator.

### Right to Fair Trial

Like all suspects, the foreigner has:

- Right to be tried before independent and impartial court,
- **Presumption of innocence** until proven guilty,
- Access to evidence and right to produce counter-evidence,
- Right to call and question witnesses.

## Common Offence Types Foreigners Encounter in Antalya

### 1. Criminal Liability from Traffic Accidents

Traffic accidents involving foreign drivers in Turkey are evaluated under offences such as **negligent injury** or **negligent causing of death**. Insurance presence may cover damages; criminal liability runs separately.

### 2. Narcotics Offences

Turkish law takes a **very strict approach** to narcotics-related offences. Even recreational use can be subject to investigation. **Treatment referral** of foreign suspect is possible under specific conditions.

### 3. Damage to Property

Disagreements with apartment neighbours, noise, property damage can become subject of criminal and civil proceedings.

### 4. Fraud and Personalised Attacks

Foreigners suffering fraud are in complainant position; in this case, our law firm follows the client's right before court as victim's counsel.

### 5. Sexual Offences

These offences carry extremely heavy penalties; meticulous legal representation is essential for both perpetrator and victim foreigner.

## Custody and Judicial Control

The most concerning dimension of criminal process is the possibility of **detention**. Detention:

- Only applies in cases where concrete criminal suspicion is high, with risk of flight or evidence destruction,
- By judicial decision,
- Within specific procedural safeguards.

For foreign suspect, **flight risk** is often presented as detention reason. In response, defence:

- Family ties in Turkey,
- Property ownership,
- Economic ties,
- Business relations,
- Long-term residence in country

elements are advanced to request **judicial control** instead of detention. Judicial control may include:

- Travel ban abroad,
- Periodic check-ins at police station,
- Surrender of passport,

allowing prosecution to continue without restricting physical freedom.

## Effect of Home Country on Foreign Suspect

There may be **extradition treaties** between Turkey and the suspect's home country. Under treaty:

- Suspect's extradition to Turkey can be requested,
- Sentence in Turkey serving in home country may apply (post-conviction),
- Information sharing can occur.

For some offence types, extradition is refused (political offences, cases with death penalty, etc.).

## Post-Criminal Trial: Administrative Consequences

For foreigner convicted in Turkey or under investigation:

- **Restriction code** may apply,
- **Deportation order** may issue,
- **Residence permit cancellation** may occur,
- **Disqualifying entry** in citizenship application.

Therefore, alongside trial outcome, defence strategy in criminal case must consider arising administrative consequences; good defence strategy considers both fronts together.
