Two short provisions close out the definitional portion of the passenger carriage chapter: a security right for the carrier's own claims, and a cross-reference covering the unfortunate case of a passenger who dies during the voyage.

## The Carrier's Lien Over Baggage

Art. 1254 gives the carrier a lien over a passenger's baggage, exercised under the general property-law retention rules (Turkish Civil Code Art. 950-953), securing all claims arising from the passenger carriage contract — unpaid fare, the highest-fare penalty for concealed baggage under Art. 1252(3), or the full-fare obligation for a no-show under Art. 1253. This is the direct counterpart of the carrier's lien over cargo in freight contracts, applying the same underlying security logic to a different kind of "thing" — a passenger's belongings rather than commercial cargo.

Worth noting, though the statute does not carve out an express exception: baggage is often deeply personal in a way commercial cargo is not, and cabin baggage — kept under the passenger's own custody rather than handed over to the carrier — is, as a practical matter, far less accessible for the carrier to actually retain than checked baggage held in the ship's hold.

## Deceased Passenger's Baggage: A Cross-Reference

Art. 1255 handles the case of a passenger who dies during the voyage by simply applying Art. 915 — a general, mode-independent passenger carriage provision located elsewhere in the Code, outside the maritime chapter. Rather than building a bespoke maritime-specific rule for this scenario, the legislature chose to apply the same general rule that governs land and other transport modes, avoiding unnecessary duplication and potential inconsistency between transport modes for what is fundamentally the same practical concern: who takes custody of a deceased traveler's belongings and how they reach the heirs.

## Why This Matters

For carriers, the lien is a real but limited tool — enforceable in practice mainly against baggage actually within their custody, and one that should be exercised with some sensitivity given the personal nature of the property involved.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### May the carrier retain a passenger's baggage?

Yes. Under Article 1254/1 TTK <b>the carrier has a lien over the passenger's baggage, under Articles 950 to 953 of the Turkish Civil Code, for all claims arising from the contract for the carriage of passengers by sea</b>. The right covers the unpaid fare, charges for additional services and other claims under the contract. Articles 950 et seq. of the Civil Code require the claim to be due and a natural connection to exist between the goods and the claim.

### What happens to the baggage if the passenger dies during the voyage?

Under Article 1255/1 TTK, <b>where the passenger dies during the voyage, Article 915 applies</b>. Under Article 915 the carrier must take the measures necessary to safeguard the baggage and deliver it to the heirs or other persons entitled. If those persons are not present, the carrier deposits the baggage in a suitable place and informs the interested parties.

### Does the lien extend to items the passenger is carrying on their person?

No. Article 1254 grants the lien expressly over <b>'the passenger's baggage'</b>. Cabin baggage and baggage carried in the hold are covered, but personal effects and clothing carried on the passenger's person cannot be the subject of a lien. In addition, under Article 950/3 of the Civil Code, no lien may be created over goods that by their nature cannot be realised.
