TTK Art. 954-958 open the Ship Registry chapter — over forty articles covering registration, deregistration, and the reliance principle that anchors Turkish maritime finance. This opening piece sets up the registry's structure and scope.

## Court-Supervised Registry Offices

Under Art. 954, registry offices operate within harbor master offices but function under the supervision of the local commercial court — a structural feature that matters because it means challenges to registry decisions go to that same court rather than an administrative body. Art. 954(3) also extends Turkish Civil Code state liability for registry errors, so a claimant harmed by a registrar's mistake can sue the State directly without first proving the registrar's personal fault.

## Which Registry Has Jurisdiction

Art. 955 ties registry jurisdiction to the ship's port of registry — the place from which its voyages are actually managed. Turkish courts read the exception for ships managed from a foreign location or directly from the ship narrowly: once a vessel is already tied to a specific Turkish port of registry, the owner cannot claim this exception to shop for a more convenient registry office.

## Registrable, Mandatory, and Barred Vessels

Art. 956 permits registration for any merchant ship with Turkish flag rights, plus recreational/training vessels and foreign-owned ships under construction in Turkey. Art. 957 sets the operative threshold: every merchant ship of 18 gross tons or more must register — smaller vessels may register voluntarily but are not required to. This distinction carries through to deregistration: voluntary registrations can be cancelled on a bare owner request, while mandatory registrations require proof of one of the specific grounds (sinking, unrepairable damage, loss of flag rights). Art. 958 bars registration outright for non-Turkish ships, Turkish ships already registered abroad, naval and auxiliary warships, and vessels dedicated exclusively to public service by the State or local government — the last category because the registry is designed for commercial dealings, not public-service assets.

## Practical Takeaway

For an owner planning to register or deregister a Turkish-flagged vessel, the 18-gross-ton line is the single most consequential threshold in this chapter — it decides both whether registration is optional and how difficult later deregistration will be.
