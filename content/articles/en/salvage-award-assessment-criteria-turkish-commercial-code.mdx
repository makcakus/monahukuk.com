The prior article on Art. 1304 established *that* a successful salvage earns an award. Art. 1305 answers the harder question: *how much*. It applies whenever the parties never agreed on a figure, or when an agreed award is challenged in court as unfair and needs judicial adjustment under Art. 1301.

## Ten Criteria, No Hierarchy

Under Art. 1305(1), the award must be set with a view to encouraging salvage activity, weighing ten factors together with no ranking among them. They fall into three natural clusters: (i) outcome and value — the salved property's post-salvage value and the degree of success achieved; (ii) effort, skill, and risk — care taken to prevent environmental damage, effort in saving the property or lives, time/expenses/losses incurred, and the liability risk and other risks borne by the salvor and its equipment; and (iii) timing and readiness — how promptly help was rendered, and whether equipment was kept ready and effectively used. A standalone factor — the nature and extent of the danger faced by the salved property and the danger the salvors themselves accepted — cuts across both sides of the operation. Courts apply these cumulatively rather than through a fixed tariff; a 2016/2021 Istanbul Commercial Court ruling expressly held that no fixed schedule can exist because danger, effort, and circumstances differ in every case.

## What the Award Does Not Cover

Art. 1305(2) excludes official authority fees and charges, customs duties on the salved property, and costs of safeguarding, appraising, and selling that property from the award calculation. These are administrative/logistical costs owed to third parties (state agencies, customs, storage providers), not compensation for the salvor's effort, and must be claimed — if at all — on a separate legal basis.

## Money, Not a Percentage

Art. 1305(3) requires the award to be fixed as a money sum and, unless otherwise agreed, bars setting it as a percentage of the salved value. Istanbul courts have struck down claims built around a flat percentage rate, insisting instead on an expert assessment running through the ten criteria individually. Notably, an award that happens to work out near a round percentage after that assessment is not the same as adopting a percentage formula from the outset — the distinction is the starting method, not the resulting number.

## Practical Takeaway

For shipowners, salvors, and insurers, this means salvage award disputes turn on a fact-heavy, multi-factor showing — usually via expert report — rather than a lookup table. Parties negotiating a salvage agreement up front should expect any later judicial recalculation to run through all ten factors, not shortcuts.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How is the salvage award calculated?

TTK Article 1305/1 lists ten criteria and lays down <b>no order of priority</b> among them. The main ones are: the value of the salvaged vessel and property; the salvor's <b>skill and effort in preventing or limiting environmental damage</b>; the measure of the result achieved; the degree of danger; the skill and effort shown by the salvor; the time spent and the expenses and losses incurred; the responsibility and risks undertaken; the promptness of the service rendered; and the readiness of equipment assigned to the salvage operation.

### Can the award be set as a percentage of the value of the salvaged property?

As a rule, no. Under TTK Article 1305/3 <b>the salvage award is fixed in money. Unless otherwise agreed, the award may not be determined as a percentage of the value of the property salvaged.</b> The reason is that salvage compensates not only for the value saved but also for the effort shown, the risk undertaken and the time spent. The parties may agree otherwise by contract.

### Which expenses are not included in the salvage award?

Under TTK Article 1305/2 <b>the expenses and fees of official bodies</b>, <b>customs duties and other duties payable on the salvaged items</b>, and <b>expenses incurred for the storage, preservation, valuation and sale of those items</b>, do not form part of the salvage award. These are claimed as separate items; folding them into the salvage award would reduce the compensation due for the salvor's own effort.
