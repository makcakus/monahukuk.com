For most families settling in Türkiye, the question that follows immediately after the residence permit concerns their children's schooling. Turkish law builds three separate layers here: the constitutional guarantee of the right to education, rules that vary according to the foreigner's status, and a distinct regulation governing the equivalence of school records obtained abroad. This article covers enrolment, the equivalence procedure, and the obstacles most often encountered.

## The Constitutional Framework

Article 42 of the Constitution provides that "no one shall be deprived of the right to education and instruction", framing the right without limiting it to citizenship. That wording is the basis for the constitutional protection of the right to education of foreign children present in Türkiye. The operating rule is that every child of compulsory school age is given access to schooling, provided their legal status in the country is documented.

Specific provisions also apply according to status. Article 89(1) of Law No. 6458 expressly provides that **applicants for or holders of international protection status, and their family members, benefit from primary and secondary education services**. Article 28 of the Temporary Protection Regulation provides that the educational activities of foreigners within its scope are conducted **under the supervision and responsibility of the Ministry of National Education**, both inside and outside temporary accommodation centres; the same article allows pre-school education for children aged 36–66 months (with 54–66 months given priority), and provides that education for those of primary and secondary school age is conducted under the Ministry's relevant legislation.

## What Enrolment Requires

Enrolling a foreign child in a state school rests on documenting the child's identity and status in Türkiye. The core elements sought in practice are:

- The child's foreigner identification number or the identity document appropriate to their status (a [valid residence permit](/en/articles/family-residence-permit-dependent-children-turkey), an international protection applicant identity document, or a temporary protection identity document),
- An address declaration — enrolment in state schools is as a rule made through the address-based placement system,
- Documents showing prior schooling, together with the equivalence certificate for them,
- Health and vaccination records and guardian details.

Enrolment is handled by the school administration and the student's data are entered into the Ministry's systems. The duration of the child's residence permit affects not the right to education itself but the administrative handling of enrolment and attendance; because a lapse can create difficulties for the school record, filing the [renewal application](/en/articles/renewing-turkish-residence-permit-guide) in time matters.

## Equivalence of School Records Obtained Abroad

Where a child has completed part of their schooling abroad, the year group they are placed in depends directly on the equivalence procedure. This area is governed by the **Regulation of the Ministry of National Education on Equivalence** of 9 July 2024, and is entirely separate from higher education equivalence (YÖK).

**Where is the application made?** Under the definitions article, equivalence centres are the units carrying out equivalence work within provincial directorates of national education and within the district directorates designated by the Board of Education. Proceedings are conducted electronically through the e-Equivalence System.

**Who applies, and with what document?** Under Article 4, within Türkiye, Turkish citizens apply with their identity card, and **foreign nationals apply with an application identity document**. Applications abroad are made to education counsellorships or attachés with a passport or identity document.

**Which documents are required?** Under Article 5, the application form and other documents are uploaded to the system and the originals are produced at the equivalence centre. One documentary requirement stands out: the applicant must produce, **together with the passport, the entry and exit pages for the country of study**, or, if those pages cannot be produced, evidence of presence in that country during the period of study. The purpose is to exclude records obtained without actual presence in the country concerned.

## Records That Will Not Be Given Equivalence

Article 12 of the Regulation addresses the point that most often causes hardship in practice. Under it:

- Equivalence is **not granted** to records obtained from private schools or institutions falling outside the scope of Law No. 5580,
- Equivalence is **not granted** to records and diplomas obtained by completing part or all of the studies **through distance learning**.

The distance-learning exclusion matters particularly: files of children who studied online because of the pandemic period or exceptional conditions in their country of origin can be refused on this basis. The route in such a case is to establish, by documents and by correspondence from the authorities of the country concerned, the nature of the schooling — whether it was face-to-face or remote. The same article also provides that a **level determination exam** may be applied to records from certain institutions; the exam is a method of establishing the year group, not a refusal of equivalence.

## Appeals: A Two-Stage Route

The appeal route against an equivalence decision is set out in Article 6 and has two stages. The appeal is made **to the equivalence centre that issued the certificate**, which assesses it and notifies the outcome. **Where a second appeal is made**, the centre refers it to the Board of Education, and **decisions given by the Board are final**.

"Final" here means final in administrative terms; it does not close off judicial review. An annulment action may be brought before the administrative court against the concluded adverse decision. At that point it should not be overlooked that the [time limit for bringing proceedings](/en/articles/administrative-act-appeal-deadlines-turkey) runs from the date of notification. Where the interference with the right to education is serious and irreparable, an individual application to the Constitutional Court may follow the exhaustion of ordinary remedies.

## Typical Obstacles in Practice

Recurring difficulties fall into a few categories. First, seeking enrolment before the child's residence permit process is complete; without an identification number, entry into the system becomes technically difficult. Second, submitting foreign documents without an apostille or a sworn translation. Third, failing to document guardianship and the family relationship where the guardian and child have different surnames; for divorced parents, a certified translation of the custody decision is required. Fourth, failing to obtain institutional correspondence establishing that the studies were face-to-face, in files caught by the distance-learning exclusion.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Our residence permit has not been issued yet — can my child start school?

The right to education is constitutionally protected irrespective of status; enrolment, however, requires the child's identity data to be entered into the system, so in practice completion of the residence permit application and the foreigner identification number is awaited. If the process is prolonged, it is prudent to make a written application to the provincial directorate of national education so that the situation is on record.

### My child attended an online school abroad — can we obtain equivalence?

Article 12 of the Equivalence Regulation expressly excludes equivalence for records and diplomas obtained by completing part or all of the studies through distance learning. The course is to establish through correspondence from the school and the authorities of the country concerned that the schooling was in fact face-to-face; failing that, to request placement through a level determination exam.

### Our equivalence application was refused — how many times may we appeal?

The Regulation provides a two-stage appeal: the first appeal goes to the equivalence centre that issued the certificate; on a second appeal the file is referred to the Board of Education, whose decision is administratively final. An annulment action before the administrative court remains open thereafter.

### Can children under temporary protection enrol in the same schools?

Article 28 of the Temporary Protection Regulation provides that the educational activities of these children are conducted under the supervision and responsibility of the Ministry of National Education, and that for those of primary and secondary school age the Ministry's relevant legislation applies.
