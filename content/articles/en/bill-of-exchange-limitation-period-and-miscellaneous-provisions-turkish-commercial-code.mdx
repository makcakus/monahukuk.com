TTK Art. 748-765 gather several distinct rules unified by their focus on how a bill's obligations evolve over time and what happens if the physical instrument is lost.

## Alteration of the Text

Art. 748 codifies the "independence of signatures" principle for alterations: signatories after a text change are bound by the altered text, those before it by the original text. Turkish courts require an alteration to be countersigned or initialed by the drawer to be effective; an unauthenticated alteration is disregarded and the bill is assessed as originally written.

## Limitation Periods

Art. 749 sets three tiered limitation periods depending on the obligor's position: three years from maturity against the acceptor; one year from the protest date (or from maturity if "no-expenses" applies) for the holder's claims against endorsers and the drawer; and six months from payment or from being sued for a given endorser's claims against earlier parties in the chain. These periods apply to promissory notes as well via Art. 778, with the note's drawer treated like the bill's acceptor for the three-year period. Turkish courts treat limitation as a defense the debtor must raise — it does not extinguish the instrument automatically, and a time-barred instrument may still serve as documentary evidence of the underlying debt. Limitation is interrupted by filing suit, initiating enforcement proceedings, notifying the claim in a related suit, or filing in bankruptcy proceedings (Art. 750), with the effect running only against the specific obligor targeted (Art. 751).

## Holidays, Computation, and No Grace Periods

Payment falling on a holiday shifts to the next business day, though intervening holidays still count toward interim deadlines (Art. 752); the day a period starts is never counted (Art. 753); and — critically — no statutory or judicial grace period is ever available on a bill of exchange (Art. 754), a strict rule reinforcing the instrument's need for predictable, immediate enforceability.

## Place of Action and Signature

Presentment, protest, and similar acts must occur at the relevant party's place of business or residence (Art. 755), and all declarations on the bill must bear a handwritten signature — no mechanical, stamped, or notarized substitute is permitted (Art. 756).

## Cancellation of a Lost Bill

Art. 757-765 set out a detailed judicial procedure for cancelling a lost or stolen bill: filing with the commercial court for a payment injunction against the drawee, a public summons process (three months to one year) inviting whoever holds the bill to come forward, and — failing that — a cancellation order. Even after cancellation, the original holder retains a direct claim against the acceptor (Art. 764/2), since cancellation only removes the instrument from circulation, not the underlying obligation.

## Practical Takeaway

Anyone facing an altered bill, computing a limitation deadline, or needing to replace a lost negotiable instrument should verify which of these tiered rules applies, since the consequences differ sharply by obligor type and by timing.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What are the limitation periods on a bill of exchange?

Article 749 TTK lays down three: <b>three years against the acceptor</b>, running from maturity; <b>one year for the holder against the endorsers and the drawer</b>, running from the date of a protest drawn up in time or, where the bill bears a 'no expenses' clause, from maturity; and <b>six months for an endorser against the other endorsers and the drawer</b>, running from the day the endorser paid the bill or was sued on it.

### If the text of a bill is altered, who is liable on which version?

Under Article 748/1 TTK, <b>where the text of a bill is altered, those who signed after the alteration are liable according to the altered text, and those who signed before it according to the original text</b>. This is an application of the principle of <b>the independence of signatures</b>: each signatory is bound by the text as it stood when they signed, and a later alteration cannot be imputed to them.

### What can be done about a lost bill of exchange?

The route has two stages. Under Article 757/1 TTK, <b>a person dispossessed of a bill against their will may ask the commercial court of first instance at the place of payment or of the holder's domicile to prohibit the drawee from paying it</b>. After that protective measure, an annulment action is brought under Articles 758 et seq.: the court summons the person holding the bill by public notice and, if no one comes forward within the period, orders annulment, after which the holder may exercise the right without the instrument.
