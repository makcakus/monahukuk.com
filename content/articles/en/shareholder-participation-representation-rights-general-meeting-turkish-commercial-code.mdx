TTK Art. 425-433 open the "personal rights" section of the general assembly rules, drawing a sharp line between who actually owns a share and who is entitled to exercise it against the company — a distinction that matters most in registered and bearer share structures.

## Attend in Person or Send Anyone

Under Art. 425, a shareholder may attend personally or appoint any representative, shareholder or not; an articles clause requiring the proxy to also be a shareholder is void. Art. 426 then determines who the company must actually recognize: for uncertificated or registered shares, only the person listed in the share ledger (or someone that person has authorized in writing) may exercise the rights; for bearer shares, whoever can prove possession and has notified the Central Registry Agency is authorized. This lets the company rely on a formal record rather than investigate true ownership before every meeting.

## Representation: Instructions, Custody, and Deposited Shares

Art. 427 makes clear that a proxy's failure to follow the represented shareholder's instructions does not invalidate the vote cast — it only creates liability between proxy and principal, protecting third-party reliance on the outcome. Someone merely holding a bearer share through pledge, lien, or custody cannot exercise shareholder rights without a separate written authorization from the actual shareholder. Under Art. 429, a custodian holding shares in deposit (typically a bank) must seek voting instructions before each meeting; absent specific instructions, it follows the depositor's general instructions, and failing those, votes with the board's recommendations.

## Joint Ownership and Usufruct

Where a share is co-owned, the co-owners must appoint a single representative to exercise rights (Art. 432(1)) — and Turkish courts require an actual, established co-ownership relationship before a court will appoint one; a disputed or unresolved ownership claim does not qualify. Where a usufruct exists over a share, the usufructuary — not the bare owner — exercises the voting right by default, though the usufructuary remains liable to the shareholder for failing to consider the shareholder's interests equitably (Art. 432(2)).

## Guarding Against Abuse

Art. 433 voids any share transfer made to circumvent voting-right limitations, and lets any shareholder object to unauthorized participation and have that objection recorded in the minutes — a record that later supports an annulment action if the improper participation affected the outcome.
