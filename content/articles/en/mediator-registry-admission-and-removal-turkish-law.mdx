*Article 19 – (1) The Department of Mediation keeps a registry of persons who have acquired the power to act as mediators in private law disputes. Information concerning the persons in this registry is also published electronically by the Department.*

*Article 20 – (2) In order to be entered in the mediator registry it is necessary: a) to be a Turkish citizen, b) to be a graduate of a faculty of law with at least five years' seniority in the profession, c) to have full legal capacity, ç) (Amended: 12/10/2017-7036/25) … not to have been convicted of an intentional offence carrying more than one year's imprisonment, or — even if pardoned — of offences against the security of the State, … forgery, breach of trust, … false testimony and perjury, d) (Added: 5/6/2017-Decree-Law 691/9; adopted unchanged: 31/1/2018-7069/9) not to have any connection or association with terrorist organisations, e) to have completed mediation training and — except for those with twenty years' seniority in the profession — to have passed the written (…) examination held by the Ministry.*

*(3) A mediator may begin practising from the date of registration.*

*Article 21 – (1) The Department of Mediation strikes out the registration of a mediator who was entered in the registry without meeting the conditions required for mediation, or who subsequently loses those conditions.*

Mediation is not an activity that may be practised freely under Turkish law. A person's ability to serve as a mediator depends on **entry in the registry** kept by the Department of Mediation of the Ministry of Justice, and that entry is an administrative act. Refusal or cancellation of registration is subject to review by the administrative courts; indeed, the entire case law on these articles consists of decisions of the 10th Chamber of the Council of State.

## The Function and Publicity of the Registry

Article 19/1 charges the Department with keeping a registry of persons who have acquired the power to mediate in private law disputes and requires that information about those persons also be published electronically. In this respect the registry is public: the parties can verify whether the person appointed to their case really is a registered mediator.

The registry's second function concerns appointment. Under Article 20/4 the Department lists registered mediators according to the judicial commissions of the first-instance courts in which they wish to serve and sends the lists to the presidencies of those commissions; a mediator may be entered on **no more than three commission lists**. In mandatory mediation, appointment is made from these lists under Article 18/A/5.

## Conditions of Registration

Article 20/2 lists six conditions, all of which must be satisfied together.

**Turkish citizenship.** Foreign nationals cannot be entered in the mediator registry. This follows from mediation being designed as a public service.

**A law degree with at least five years' seniority in the profession.** Two elements are required together: graduation from a faculty of law and professional seniority. The criterion of "seniority in the profession" means time spent in the legal professions — practice at the bar, service as a judge or prosecutor, an academic career in law — and it is the condition that generates most disputes in practice.

**Full legal capacity.**

**No conviction for certain offences.** Subparagraph (ç), which took its present form with the 2017 amendment, treats as a bar both a conviction of more than one year's imprisonment for an intentional offence — even where the periods under Article 53 of the Turkish Criminal Code have elapsed — and a conviction for the listed catalogue offences even if pardoned. The catalogue includes offences against the security of the State, offences against the constitutional order, embezzlement, extortion in office, bribery, theft, fraud, forgery, breach of trust, fraudulent bankruptcy, rigging of tenders, rigging of performance of an obligation, money laundering, smuggling, giving a false expert report, false testimony and perjury.

**No connection or association with terrorist organisations.** Introduced by decree-law in 2017 and enacted in 2018, this condition is in practice the most frequent ground for removal from the registry.

**Completion of mediation training and success in the examination.** Those with **twenty years' seniority** in their profession are exempt from the examination. In its original form the article provided for a "written and practical" examination; subsequently only a written examination has been required.

Under Article 20/3 a mediator may begin practising from the date of registration. A session conducted before registration does not count as mediation within the meaning of Article 2(b).

## Removal from the Registry: Three Separate Routes

Article 21 provides for three distinct routes to removal, and the deciding authority differs in each.

**Absence or loss of a condition (Article 21/1).** The Department of Mediation **itself strikes out** the registration of a mediator who was entered in the registry without meeting the required conditions or who subsequently loses them. The decision does not go to the Board.

**Breach of obligations (Article 21/2).** Where the Department finds that a mediator has failed to fulfil the obligations laid down by the statute, it first issues a **written warning**; if the warning is not heeded, it **takes the mediator's defence** and, where necessary, **asks the Board** to strike the mediator's name from the registry. On this route the deciding authority is the Mediation Board (Article 32/1-e). Breach of confidentiality, breach of the duty of impartiality, breach of the advertising ban and non-payment of dues are assessed within this framework.

**Removal at the mediator's own request (Article 21/3).** A mediator may at any time ask to be removed from the registry; no reasons need be given.

The 10th Chamber of the Council of State held that there was no unlawfulness in the act striking from the registry a mediator against whom a criminal case was pending before the assize court and who was found not to satisfy the condition of having no connection or association with terrorist organisations. The decision is notable in showing that this condition does not require a final conviction and that the administration may make its assessment on the basis of concrete findings.

## The Administration's Discretion in Admission

Satisfying the conditions does not by itself secure entry in the registry. The 10th Chamber of the Council of State consistently holds that whether a mediation examination is held at all and — where a required number is set — how many candidates are deemed successful lies within the Ministry's discretion, having regard to criteria such as the number of mediators currently serving, the volume of mediation applications and the widening scope of mandatory mediation.

The pass criterion changed on 17 November 2021: under Article 45 of the Regulation on the Law on Mediation in Civil Disputes the rule is that candidates scoring at least seventy out of one hundred are deemed successful; the Ministry may set the number of successful candidates only according to need, in which case the required number is announced in the examination notice.

This regime shows how mediation differs from the bar as regards entry into the profession: while admission to a bar association is the rule for anyone meeting the conditions, in the mediator registry both the timing of entry and — where a quota is announced — its size depend on administrative planning.

## Decisions of the Council of State

The following decisions illustrate the supervision of the admission and removal regime.

**1. Council of State, 10th Chamber, File No. 2022/1310, Decision No. 2025/5181, 11.11.2025**

The decision assesses the lawfulness of an act refusing an application for registration, taking as its basis the conditions in Article 20/2 of "being a graduate of a faculty of law with at least five years' seniority in the profession" and "having completed mediation training and passed the written examination held by the Ministry".

**2. Council of State, 10th Chamber, File No. 2021/6420, Decision No. 2023/6070, 30.10.2023**

In a case where proceedings brought before the assize court were still pending, the Chamber dismissed the action, holding that there was no unlawfulness in the act striking from the registry a claimant who was found not to satisfy the condition, required for entry in the mediator registry, of having no connection or association with terrorist organisations.

**3. Council of State, 10th Chamber, File No. 2021/7433, Decision No. 2024/2091, 22.05.2024**

The decision reviews an act refusing an application for registration by considering as a whole the purpose of Law No. 6325 and the admission conditions in Article 20.

**4. Council of State, 10th Chamber, File No. 2023/285, Decision No. 2024/5086, 19.11.2024**

The Chamber assessed an act refusing an application, noting that under Article 20/1 registration is effected upon written application by the person concerned to the Department of Mediation and that the conditions in Article 20/2 are required cumulatively.

**5. Council of State, 10th Chamber, File No. 2020/1974, Decision No. 2024/4075, 16.10.2024**

The decision considers Article 18/A on mandatory mediation together with Article 20 on the conditions of registration, setting out the connection between the admission regime and the operation of the mandatory mediation system.
