TTK Art. 1038-1043 address a ship mortgage's life after it is created: what happens when the secured claim is assigned, and how the parties can later modify its terms.

## Assignment Follows the Claim — With Registry as the Constitutive Step

Because a ship mortgage is accessory to its secured claim, Art. 1038 provides that assigning the claim automatically carries the mortgage with it, and neither may be assigned separately from the other. But assignment only takes legal effect once registered on the ship registry — a written agreement between old and new creditor is not enough on its own. Turkish courts have confirmed this is a constitutive, not merely evidentiary, requirement: an assignee who has not registered cannot bring foreclosure proceedings even with the owner's written consent to the transfer. A notable exception applies to maximum-amount (üst sınır) mortgages, where the claim can be assigned under general rules without the mortgage automatically following — reflecting the more fluid, not-yet-fixed nature of what such a mortgage secures.

## The Owner's Defenses Survive Assignment

Art. 1039 ensures a creditor cannot use assignment to strip the owner of defenses it had against the original creditor — those defenses remain available against the new creditor too, reinforced by the same registry-reliance principle covered in our ship registry article. A narrow carve-out limits this protection for interest and other secondary claims falling due well after the owner learned of the assignment, once a reasonable grace period has passed.

## Modifying Content, Rank, or the Secured Claim

Three related mechanisms — changing the mortgage's content (Art. 1041), swapping its priority rank with another mortgage (Art. 1042), and substituting an entirely different claim for the one originally secured (Art. 1043) — all follow the same notarized-agreement-plus-registration formula used for creating the mortgage in the first place, plus consent from any third party whose rights are affected. Rank swaps cannot prejudice mortgages sitting between the two being reordered.

## Practical Takeaway

For lenders buying into existing ship loans via loan-participation or syndication structures, the registration requirement is not a formality to skip — an unregistered transfer leaves the buyer without standing to sue or enforce, regardless of any private consent letter from the borrower.
