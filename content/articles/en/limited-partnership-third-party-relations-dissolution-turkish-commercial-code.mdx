Following the internal-relations rules discussed separately, TTK Art. 317-328 turn to how a Turkish limited partnership deals with the outside world — representation, the limited partner's liability exceptions, and dissolution.

## Representation and the Limited Partner's Liability Cap

A limited partnership is represented by its komandite (unlimited-liability) partners; komanditer partners cannot represent the company in their capacity as partners, though they may be appointed as commercial agents or attorneys-in-fact if the agreement permits (Art. 318). As a baseline, a limited partner's liability cannot exceed the capital they committed (Art. 319) — but the Code carves out three important exceptions that strip this protection.

## When a Limited Partner Loses the Liability Cap

First, if a limited partner's name appears in the company's trade name, they are treated as fully liable like a komandite partner toward third parties — the classic reliance-on-appearance principle (Art. 320). Second, a limited partner who transacts on the company's behalf without clearly disclosing their limited, agency capacity becomes liable like a general partner to good-faith third parties (Art. 321) — the burden of disclosure falls squarely on the limited partner. Third, for transactions before the company's registration, a limited partner is liable as a general partner unless they can prove the third party knew of the liability limit. Turkish courts have required creditors to first show that enforcement against the company itself was genuinely fruitless — a documented, substantive finding, not a mere formality — before pursuing a limited partner (Art. 322).

Importantly, ordinary participation — giving advice, voting on extraordinary matters, exercising inspection rights, or holding a minor internal role — does not itself trigger unlimited liability (Art. 321/5), and a limited partner cannot recover their contributed capital, directly or indirectly, while it remains impaired by losses (Art. 323).

## Bankruptcy Priority and Dissolution

In bankruptcy, partnership creditors are satisfied before personal creditors reach company assets, and a limited partner's committed capital counts among those company assets (Art. 324); if company assets fall short, creditors may then pursue the komandite partners' personal assets (Art. 325). Dissolution generally follows the same rules as general partnerships (Art. 328), with one key departure: unless the agreement says otherwise, a limited partner's death or incapacity does not dissolve the partnership — consistent with the automatic heir-succession rule for limited partners.

For creditors dealing with a Turkish limited partnership, verifying whether a limited partner's name appears in the trade name or whether they acted without disclosing their limited capacity can be decisive in determining who is actually on the hook.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the limit of a limited partner's liability?

Under TTK Article 319/1 <b>the liability of a limited partner may not exceed the amount of capital they have contributed or undertaken to contribute.</b> If they have paid the contribution in full, they cannot be held additionally liable to the creditors. Under Article 322/1, however, the limited partner is directly liable to the partnership's creditors <b>up to the unpaid amount of the capital they undertook to contribute</b>. This follows naturally from the capital's function as security for the creditors.

### What happens if a limited partner represents the partnership?

Their liability widens. Under TTK Article 318/1, limited partnerships are as a rule represented by the <b>general partners</b>. Under Articles 320 and 321, if the limited partner's name is included in the trade name or the limited partner acts in a way that represents the partnership, they become liable <b>without limit, like a general partner</b>, in respect of that transaction. Cases where the third party knew of the situation are assessed separately.

### How does a limited partnership come to an end?

Under TTK Article 328/1 <b>Articles 243 to 303, on the dissolution and liquidation of general partnerships and on the withdrawal and expulsion of partners, apply to limited partnerships as well.</b> The grounds of dissolution, dissolution on just grounds, the right of a partner's personal creditor to seek dissolution, the appointment of liquidators and the liquidation procedure are therefore subject to the same regime. The death or incapacitation of a limited partner does not, as a rule, bring the partnership to an end.
