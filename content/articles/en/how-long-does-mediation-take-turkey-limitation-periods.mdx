Mediation is very short compared with court proceedings — in mandatory mediation most files close within a month. But the real significance of the time limits lies not in how long you wait, but in **what happens to the limitation period** while you do. A miscalculated period can let an otherwise recoverable claim become time-barred.

## Statutory Periods: They Vary by Type of Dispute

In mandatory mediation the mediator must conclude the application within a fixed period running **from the date of appointment**. That period differs according to the type of dispute:

- **General rule** (employment, consumer, lease, dissolution of joint ownership, condominium and neighbour-law disputes): **three weeks**, extendable by a maximum of one week in cases of necessity.
- **Commercial disputes** (Article 5/A of the Turkish Commercial Code): **six weeks**, extendable by a maximum of two weeks in cases of necessity.
- **Disputes arising from agricultural production contracts** (Article 13 of Law No. 5488): **two weeks**, extendable by a maximum of one week in cases of necessity.

The decision to extend belongs to the **mediator** and depends on the existence of "cases of necessity"; it is not dependent on a party's request.

In voluntary mediation there is no statutory maximum; the parties determine the process. Only where mediation is started after an action has been brought does the court adjourn the proceedings **for no more than three months**; that period may be extended by up to three months on the joint application of the parties.

## Exceeding the Period Does Not Invalidate the Process

A frequent question: what happens if the mediator exceeds the three weeks? The period is directory; exceeding it does not render the process or the final minutes invalid, and the condition precedent is still deemed satisfied. At most, the overrun may be assessed as a breach of the mediator's obligations for registry purposes.

The only situation in which the period starts afresh is an objection to jurisdiction: if the objection is rejected, the same mediator is reappointed and the periods run **from the date of the new appointment**.

## The Real Point: Limitation Is Suspended

This is mediation's most valuable legal effect. In mandatory mediation, the limitation period is suspended and preclusive periods do not run for the time elapsing **from the application to the mediation office until the date the final minutes are drawn up**.

In voluntary mediation the protection starts later: the process begins on the date on which the parties are invited to the first meeting and agree to continue the process, and this is recorded in minutes; the protection applies from that moment.

The practical consequence of this difference is critical: **an unanswered offer of voluntary mediation does not suspend limitation.** Unless otherwise agreed, if an offer to apply to a mediator is not answered positively within thirty days it is deemed rejected, and since no process has begun the limitation period keeps running.

By contrast, in mandatory mediation the date of application to the office starts the protection even if the other party can never be reached and never attends. For a claim close to becoming time-barred, an application to mandatory mediation is in itself a protective step.

## The Difference Between "Suspension" and "Interruption"

The technical concept used by the Law is **suspension**, not interruption. On interruption the period starts again from zero; on suspension the period resumes from where it stopped.

Example: for a claim subject to a ten-year limitation period, if nine years and eleven months have passed and the mediation process lasted twenty days, the time remaining to bring an action is not one month but **one month plus twenty days**. The limitation period is not reset.

The 3rd Civil Chamber of the Turkish Court of Cassation has given this calculation concrete form, expressly stating that a preclusive period does not run between the dates on which the mediation process began and ended.

## The Protection Covers More Than the Time Limit for Suing

The protection extends to other procedural periods as well:

**The period for bringing an action after an interim measure or interim attachment.** Where an interim measure was granted before an action was brought, the period in Article 397/1 of the Turkish Code of Civil Procedure, and where an interim attachment was granted, the period in Article 264/1 of the Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law, does not run from the application to the mediation office until the date the final minutes are drawn up.

**The period for bringing a negative declaratory action.** If enforcement proceedings concerning the subject matter of the dispute are brought against you after you have applied to the mediation office, and you bring a negative declaratory action under Article 72 of the Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law **within two weeks** of the date the final minutes were drawn up and so request, Article 72/2 of that Law applies.

**Insurance arbitration and administrative law periods.** The 4th Civil Chamber accepts that the date on which the final mediation minutes were drawn up should also be treated as the date of application to the Insurance Arbitration Commission.

**Additional time where the court lacks subject-matter or territorial jurisdiction.** The 6th Civil Chamber reads the suspension rule in mediation together with Article 158 of the Turkish Code of Obligations, which grants the creditor sixty additional days where the action is dismissed for lack of subject-matter or territorial jurisdiction or for a rectifiable error.

## Which Date Counts?

This is where the most common calculation error occurs. The protection ends on the date the final minutes were **drawn up**. What counts is the date of drawing up shown on the document — not the date it was served on the party, uploaded to the national judicial IT system or learned of by the lawyer.

For that reason the accuracy of the date in the final minutes should be separately checked. In a case where the date the minutes were drawn up conflicted with the date of the signature on them, the 9th Civil Chamber found the minutes not to be in due form and stressed that where there is doubt about a preclusive period it must be construed **in favour of the person against whom the period runs**.

## The Special Position of Reinstatement Claims

Reinstatement claims are where the calculation of time is tightest. An employee whose contract has been terminated must apply to a mediator seeking reinstatement **within one month** of service of the termination notice. If no settlement is reached at the end of the mediation, an action must be brought before the labour court **within two weeks** of the date the final minutes were drawn up.

Because both periods are preclusive, the smallest error in counting days produces an irremediable result.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q: How many sessions does mediation take?

The Law does not fix a number of sessions. In practice a significant proportion of mandatory mediation files conclude in a single session. The mediator determines the number of sessions having regard to the nature of the dispute and the parties' wishes; the parties may also freely agree the procedure.

### Q: Can I extend the mediation process?

Extending the statutory period is at the mediator's discretion and depends on the existence of "cases of necessity". The extension may be at most one week (two weeks in commercial disputes). The parties cannot prolong the process among themselves; in mandatory mediation the period is mandatory.

### Q: Can I bring an action before the process ends?

No. To bring an action the process must have ended and the final minutes must have been drawn up. An action brought while the mediation is continuing is dismissed on procedural grounds for want of the condition precedent, and that defect cannot be cured later.

### Q: Is limitation interrupted or suspended by mediation?

Suspended. Days elapsing during the mediation are not counted for limitation purposes, but time that ran before the application is not erased. The limitation period resumes from where it stopped as from the date the final minutes were drawn up.

### Q: What happens to the period if jurisdiction is challenged?

The mediator hands the file to the office and the court determines the office having jurisdiction finally within one week. If the objection is rejected, the same mediator is reappointed and the periods run from the date of the new appointment. If it is upheld, an application may be made to the office having jurisdiction within one week of service of the decision, and **the date of application to the office lacking jurisdiction** counts as the date of application to the office having jurisdiction.
