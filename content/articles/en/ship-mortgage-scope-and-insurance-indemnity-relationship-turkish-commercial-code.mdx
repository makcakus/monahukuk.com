TTK Art. 1020-1029 answer a question every ship lender cares about: what happens to my mortgage if the ship is lost, damaged, or expropriated?

## What the Mortgage Covers

Under Art. 1020, the mortgage covers the ship, its integral components, and — while still attached and not removed in the ordinary course of business — its accessories. More importantly, Art. 1020(4) extends the mortgage to expropriation proceeds and to the owner's damages claims against third parties for loss or damage to the ship. This "value substitution" principle culminates in Art. 1022: the mortgage automatically extends to any insurance indemnity covering the mortgaged interest, without a separate pledge agreement. Where a single claim is secured across multiple ships (a common structure in fleet financing), Art. 1021 makes each vessel liable for the whole debt unless the creditor formally apportions the claim with registry approval.

## The Insurer-Creditor Relationship

Art. 1022-1029 build a detailed protective framework once insurance is in the picture. An insurer cannot claim ignorance of a registered mortgage (Art. 1022(3)) — but gets a two-week window after notifying the creditor of a loss before it may pay the owner directly and be discharged. Art. 1024 obliges the insurer, once notified of the mortgage, to keep the creditor informed of premium defaults or policy termination, with a further two-week buffer before termination becomes effective against the creditor. Most importantly, Art. 1027 shields the creditor from the owner's own misconduct: even if the insurer is discharged toward the owner because of the owner's breach, its obligation to the mortgage creditor survives — with narrow exceptions for unpaid premiums, unseaworthiness, and route deviation, the classic core risks of marine insurance.

## Practical Takeaway

For lenders, this means the standard practice of being named "loss payee" (dain-i mürtehin) on a hull and machinery policy has real legal teeth under Art. 1022 — but that right survives an assignment of the underlying secured claim only if the assignment itself is registered on the ship registry, as Turkish courts have confirmed. Lenders acquiring participations in existing ship loans should always verify registry-level assignment, not just contractual assignment.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What does a ship mortgage cover?

Under TTK Article 1020/1, <b>Articles 862 and 863 of the Turkish Civil Code</b> apply to the scope of the mortgage. Accordingly, the mortgage covers the vessel, its <b>integral parts</b> and its <b>appurtenances</b>. A mortgage may also be established over a share in a vessel. Furthermore, under Article 1022, if the owner's interest has been insured, the mortgage <b>also covers the insurance indemnity</b>; this prevents the creditor from being left without security if the vessel is lost.

### If the ship is insured, to whom is the indemnity paid?

As a rule, the mortgagee's rights are protected. Under TTK Article 1022, the mortgage also covers the insurance indemnity. Under Article 1023, if the insurer has made a payment to the owner, to be credited towards the indemnity amount, <b>for the purpose of restoring the vessel to its former condition or of giving it to the ship's creditors</b>, and the achievement of these purposes has been secured, the payment is also valid against the mortgagee. Otherwise, the creditor's consent is required.

### Is the mortgagee protected if the premium is not paid?

Yes, if notification has been made. Under TTK Article 1024/1, if the mortgagee <b>has notified the insurer of the mortgage</b>, and the insurance premium is not paid on time, requiring a period for payment to be set for the insured, the insurer must <b>notify the creditor of this without delay</b>. Furthermore, under Article 1027, even if the insurer is released from the obligation to pay indemnity due to an act of the policyholder or the insured, <b>its obligation towards the mortgagee continues to exist</b>.
