TTK Art. 390-396 govern how a Turkish joint-stock company's board actually functions day to day — meeting and voting quorum, when resolutions are void, directors' compensation, and the conflict-of-interest rules that prevent directors from putting personal gain ahead of the company.

## Meetings and Void Resolutions

Absent stricter bylaw provisions, the board convenes with a majority of the full membership and resolves by majority of those present, including in electronic meetings; directors cannot vote by proxy (Art. 390). A written-consent procedure lets the board act without a meeting if no director requests one and a majority approves a written proposal in writing. Separately, Art. 391 allows a court to declare a board resolution void — not merely voidable — where it breaches equal treatment, undermines the company's basic structure or capital-maintenance principle, violates a shareholder's inalienable rights, or encroaches on another organ's non-delegable authority.

## Information Rights and Conflict of Interest

Every director may demand information, ask questions, and inspect company records, and this request cannot be refused; disputes escalate first to the board and then, if needed, to the commercial court, whose decision is final (Art. 392). A director must abstain from deliberations where their own interest — or that of a spouse or close relative — conflicts with the company's, even if the conflict is not known to the board; directors who violate this, or who knowingly allow a conflicted colleague to participate, are jointly liable for resulting damage (Art. 393). Turkish courts have applied this recusal duty strictly, for instance barring a manager related to an opposing party from deciding whether to withdraw litigation on the company's behalf.

## Compensation and Self-Dealing

Directors may receive attendance fees, salary, bonuses, or a share of profit, but only amounts fixed by the bylaws or a shareholders' resolution (Art. 394). A director cannot transact with the company, for themselves or another, without shareholder approval — and if they do, only the company (not the other party) may claim the transaction is void (Art. 395/1). Non-shareholder directors and their close relatives are flatly barred from borrowing cash from the company or having the company guarantee their obligations (Art. 395/2). Similarly, a director cannot compete with the company's business without shareholder consent; violation lets the company choose between claiming damages, treating the deal as its own, or claiming the resulting benefit (Art. 396). Turkish courts have held that a resolution lifting these restrictions is a personal matter requiring the affected director (and their relatives) to abstain from that specific vote, though they may still vote on similar waivers for other directors.
