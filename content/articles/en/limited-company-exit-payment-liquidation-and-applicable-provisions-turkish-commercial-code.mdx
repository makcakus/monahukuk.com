TTK Art. 641-644 close out the entire Limited Company chapter (Art. 573-644) of the Turkish Commercial Code, addressing what a departing or expelled partner is owed, how liquidation proceeds, and which joint-stock company liability rules apply.

## Exit Payment at Fair Value

Whether a partner leaves by court order, contractual or just-cause withdrawal, or expulsion, Art. 641 entitles them to an exit payment matching the "fair value" (gerçek değer) of their capital share — a concept Turkish courts consistently distinguish from book value. Fair value must reflect the company's actual market worth as close as possible to the date the exit becomes final, typically requiring expert appraisal; using an outdated balance sheet or the tax-return equity figure is grounds for reversal on appeal. The articles may set a different valuation formula for contractual withdrawal rights, but this flexibility is narrower for court-ordered exits.

## When the Exit Payment Becomes Due

Under Art. 642, the exit payment only becomes due once one of three conditions is met: the company has freely available equity to cover it, the departing partner's shares are freely transferable, or capital has been formally reduced. Any unpaid balance ranks behind all other company creditors — a subordination that protects creditors from a payment that would otherwise erode the company's capital base.

## Liquidation and Imported Liability Rules

Liquidation procedure and the powers of liquidators follow the joint-stock company rules by reference (Art. 643) — a reference Turkish courts have interpreted broadly enough to extend even to the "supplementary liquidation" mechanism for assets discovered after a company has already been deregistered. Art. 644 then imports a specific, itemized list of joint-stock company provisions covering founder, manager, and auditor liability (Art. 549-561), the ban on borrowing from the company, and void board resolutions. Turkish courts apply a key distinction here: a manager's mismanagement typically damages the company first, and any resulting loss to a partner or creditor is treated as "reflective" (indirect) damage — meaning compensation must be claimed for the company, not paid directly to the individual partner or creditor.

## Practical Takeaway

A partner exiting a Turkish limited company should expect the exit payment to be calculated at current fair value near the date of exit, not at book value from an old balance sheet — and should be aware that payment may be delayed if the company lacks freely available equity to fund it without endangering its capital base.
