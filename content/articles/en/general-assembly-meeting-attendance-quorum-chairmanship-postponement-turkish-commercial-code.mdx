Once a general assembly has been properly called, TTK Art. 417-420 govern how the meeting itself is actually run — and these mechanics matter more than they might appear, because a defect here can render resulting resolutions non-existent rather than merely voidable.

## The Attendance List Is Load-Bearing

Under Art. 417, the board prepares a list of shareholders entitled to attend — sourced from the Central Registry Agency for dematerialized and bearer shares, and from the share ledger for others — and those who actually show up and sign it become the "list of persons present," the evidentiary basis for both quorum and the meeting minutes. Turkish courts treat a properly prepared and signed list as a precondition for the meeting to count as a valid "first meeting" at all — get this step wrong, and a later meeting cannot rely on the relaxed quorum meant for a second meeting.

## Quorum: The General Rule and the No-Quorum Second Meeting

Art. 418 sets the default bar at 25% of capital represented, maintained throughout the meeting, with resolutions passed by a majority of votes cast. If the first meeting fails to reach 25%, a validly held second meeting requires no quorum at all — a practical rule that keeps companies from being paralyzed by low turnout, provided the first meeting was genuinely conducted according to procedure.

## Chairmanship and the Internal Bylaw

Art. 419 lets the assembly elect a chairperson (no shareholder status required) who runs the meeting with a minute-taker and, if needed, a vote-counting officer. Turkish case law treats the chair's own stated opinions as compatible with impartiality, but denying the floor to a dissenting shareholder group crosses the line.

## Postponing the Financial Statements

Art. 420 gives shareholders holding 10% of capital (5% in listed companies) the right to force a one-month postponement of the financial statements' review — without even needing a formal assembly vote, just the chairperson's decision. Because board member discharge and re-election are treated as tied to the financial statements agenda item (Art. 413(3)), Turkish courts hold that postponing the statements automatically postpones those linked items too; auditor election, by contrast, is not considered part of "the financial statements and related matters" and can proceed regardless.

Turkish courts have repeatedly voided resolutions where the attendance list was never properly signed, where quorum was miscalculated after forged signatures were discovered, or where postponement rules were sidestepped — underscoring that these procedural articles are frequently the actual battleground in shareholder disputes, not a formality to skim past.
