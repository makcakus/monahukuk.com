Every decision a general assembly makes on dividends, capital increases, or discharging management rests on financial data. TTK Art. 514-518 govern how that data must be prepared and what it must contain.

## The Preparation Duty and the "True and Fair View" Standard

Art. 514 requires the board to prepare financial statements, their annexes, and the annual report within the first three months of the following fiscal year and present them to the general assembly. Art. 515 goes further than formal compliance with Turkish Accounting Standards: statements must present a complete, understandable, comparable, transparent, and genuinely faithful picture of the company's assets, liabilities, equity, and results. Courts have invalidated discharge (release) resolutions and profit-retention decisions based on financial statements or annual reports that failed this standard — including a case where 95% of nine years' profit was funneled into reserves without any concrete financial justification.

## What the Annual Report Must Contain

Art. 516 requires the board's annual report to reflect the year's business and financial position accurately and completely, evaluated against the financial statements, and to address the company's outlook and foreseeable risks. It must additionally disclose material post-balance-sheet events, R&D activity, and — importantly for transparency — all remuneration, bonuses, and benefits paid to directors and senior management. Recent Court of Cassation decisions treat financial statements that legally require independent audit, but were never audited, as not having been "prepared" at all; general assembly resolutions on discharge, dividends, or capital increases based on such statements are void as violating mandatory law.

## Group Companies

Art. 517-518 extend the same true-and-fair-view standard to consolidated financial statements, and assign responsibility for the group annual report exclusively to the parent company's board.

## Why This Matters

Foreign investors evaluating a Turkish target, or shareholders reviewing a general assembly agenda, should verify that statements requiring audit were in fact audited before relying on discharge or dividend resolutions built on them — a defect here can unwind an entire chain of subsequent corporate decisions.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### By when must the annual financial statements be prepared?

Under TTK Article 514 the board of directors prepares the financial statements for the past accounting period, their annexes and the annual activity report <b>within the first three months of the accounting period following the balance sheet date</b> and submits them to the general assembly. For companies whose accounting period is the calendar year, this means preparation by 31 March. The ordinary general assembly must in turn convene <b>within three months</b> of the end of each activity period under TTK Article 409/1.

### What does the true and fair view principle mean?

The true and fair view principle laid down in TTK Article 515 requires the financial statements to reflect the company's assets, debts and obligations, equity and results of operations in a <b>complete, understandable and comparable</b> manner appropriate to the nature of the business. The principle is not merely a formal accounting rule: where the statements fail to reflect reality, board members may incur civil liability under TTK Article 553 and criminal liability under Article 562.

### What must the annual activity report contain?

Under TTK Article 516 and the Regulation on Determining the Minimum Content of the Board of Directors' Annual Activity Report, the report covers events of particular importance occurring after the close of the activity year, the company's research and development work, the attendance fees, remuneration and similar financial benefits paid to board members, the measures taken to improve the company's financial structure and the anticipated risks. The report is the principal means of informing shareholders at the general assembly.
