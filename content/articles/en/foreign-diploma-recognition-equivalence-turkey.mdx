A higher education diploma obtained abroad is not automatically valid in Türkiye. To use it in Türkiye, to apply for public office, or to practise a regulated profession, equivalence must be obtained from the Council of Higher Education (YÖK). The process rests on Article 7(p) of Higher Education Law No. 2547 and on the Regulation on the Recognition and Equivalence of Foreign Higher Education Diplomas of 5 December 2017. This article explains how the application is made, when it is refused, and what routes remain open against a refusal.

## Legal Basis and Scope of the Regulation

The equivalence power is conferred on YÖK by Article 7(p) of Law No. 2547. The Regulation, however, rests not only on that provision but on two international instruments: the **Convention on the Recognition of Qualifications concerning Higher Education in the European Region of 11 April 1997** (the Lisbon Recognition Convention, approved by Law No. 5463) and the **International Agreement of 16 January 2009 on the Mutual Recognition of Universities Established under the Laws of the Republic of Türkiye and the Turkish Republic of Northern Cyprus** (Law No. 6165). These foundations show that the equivalence assessment is not left to purely domestic discretion but framed by international recognition standards.

The Regulation covers recognition and equivalence proceedings for associate, bachelor's, and master's degrees.

## A Recognition Certificate Is Not an Equivalence Certificate

This is the point most often confused in practice. A **graduation recognition certificate** establishes that the diploma was genuinely issued by the higher education institution concerned and that the institution is a recognised one. A **diploma equivalence certificate** goes a step further, determining which programme in the Turkish higher education system the qualification is deemed equivalent to.

Article 8 also ties the type of certificate to the documents submitted: for diplomas granted equivalence, where the application was made with a "graduation document", a **graduation equivalence certificate** is issued; where the original diploma is produced, a **diploma equivalence certificate** is issued.

## The Application Procedure

Under Article 4, the equivalence application is made **in person or by a person holding a power of attorney issued by the competent authorities**, following the method and procedures determined by YÖK. Foreign nationals must produce an identity or passport document issued by their state of nationality. Notifications are made **by post or electronically** under Article 14 — failure to monitor the electronic notification address is among the most common causes of missed deadlines.

The administration is not passive during the examination. Under Article 6, confirmation of documents and additional information are sought **from the applicant, from the higher education institution concerned, or from the competent authorities of the country in question** as to whether the diploma and other documents are genuine and as to the type, nature, and level of the programme. Where necessary, opinions are taken from the Field Advisory Commission and from universities, a preliminary examination report is prepared, and it is submitted to the Commission.

## Grounds for Refusal and the Ranking Requirement

Article 7(6)(c) sets out the core ground for refusal: the application is refused where the higher education programme underlying the recognition certificate or the diploma for which equivalence is sought is **not considered equivalent, in terms of educational programme/field and degree, to the relevant programmes in the Turkish higher education system**.

Certain fields face an additional filter. In assessing diplomas in **medicine, dentistry, pharmacy, and law** — the programmes subject to the ranking requirement — applications from graduates of universities placed **within the top thousand in at least two** of the world university rankings compiled by the ranking organisations designated by YÖK for the year higher education commenced are accepted and examined individually.

Where the practice of a profession is regulated by statute, Article 9 preserves the provisions of that profession's legislation and the principles determined by YÖK. The same article contains a further restriction: a separate regime applies to programmes in **foreign languages and literatures, the teaching of those languages, philology, and translation and interpreting** at higher education institutions abroad, other than those in the Turkish Republic of Northern Cyprus.

Where forgery is established, the consequence is not merely refusal: Article 11 provides that applications submitted with forged documents are refused and **a criminal complaint is filed with the public prosecutor's office**.

## Level Determination Exams and Completion Requirements

Equivalence does not always end in a simple yes or no. Under Article 11, the Commission may decide — directly or after taking the opinion of the Field Advisory Commission and universities — on one or more of a **level determination exam, an academic identity assessment, course/internship completion, practical training, clinical practice, or project work**. The definitions article describes the academic identity assessment as an examination conducted through commissions formed by universities, comprising oral and, where necessary, written and practical stages, and equivalent to the written level determination exam.

Council of State case-law recognises this power while marking its limits. The 8th Chamber has held that YÖK has broad authority in equivalence proceedings but **must exercise it in accordance with objective principles**, and found unlawful a refusal that made the equivalence certificate conditional on passing a level determination exam even though the university consulted by the administration had confirmed that the applicant's undergraduate education was equivalent ([Council of State, 8th Chamber, E. 2006/3937, K. 2006/4549](https://mevzuat.adalet.gov.tr/ictihat/17274800)). By contrast, calling an applicant to a level determination exam on the ground that the level of education was not equivalent to a comparable Turkish institution was found lawful ([Council of State, 8th Chamber, E. 2004/1626, K. 2004/5190](https://mevzuat.adalet.gov.tr/ictihat/17929700)). The distinction is clear: the exam requirement must rest on a concrete doubt and cannot be applied mechanically against favourable expert and university opinions on the file.

In a recent decision the Council of State placed the matter on rights-based ground as well: the right to education secures not only access to education provided by the State but also **the recognition of the diploma obtained and acceptance of its consequences** ([Council of State, 8th Chamber, E. 2024/6984, K. 2025/5097](https://mevzuat.adalet.gov.tr/ictihat/1182143600)).

## Challenging a Refusal

Refusal of an equivalence application is a final and enforceable administrative act and is therefore subject to an annulment action. The route is:

- Identify precisely the reasoning of the refusal and the regulatory provision relied on — the phrase "not deemed equivalent" must be traced to a concrete comparison,
- Establish the date of notification by post or electronic means; the [time limit for bringing proceedings](/en/articles/administrative-act-appeal-deadlines-turkey) runs from that date,
- Optionally apply to the administration under Article 11 of Law No. 2577 and calculate the running of time accordingly,
- Place on the file the transcript, course contents, ECTS data, internship and clinical practice records, and a comparative programme analysis,
- Invoke comparable equivalence decisions and any favourable YÖK decisions concerning the same university or programme, on equal treatment grounds,
- Bring the annulment action before the Ankara Administrative Court, with a request for a stay of execution where appropriate.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can my lawyer file the equivalence application under a power of attorney?

Yes. Article 4 expressly permits the application to be made in person or by a person holding a power of attorney issued by the competent authorities. For those abroad, this allows the process to be conducted from Türkiye on a power of attorney executed at a consulate.

### I have a graduation recognition certificate — may I practise my profession?

A recognition certificate alone is not sufficient. Recognition establishes the authenticity of the diploma and the standing of the institution; practising a profession depends on the equivalence certificate identifying the Turkish counterpart programme and on the separate conditions in the professional legislation.

### Does being called to a level determination exam mean my application is refused?

No. The exam is a stage in the equivalence assessment, and equivalence may be granted on its result. But the exam requirement must rest on a concrete doubt; where the university opinion on file confirms equivalence, a refusal conditioned on the exam is open to challenge.

### My diploma falls within a field subject to the ranking requirement — is there no prospect?

The ranking requirement imposes the top-thousand criterion on medicine, dentistry, pharmacy, and law. That criterion is assessed against the rankings valid for the year higher education commenced. A concrete examination of the start year, the ranking organisation, and the lists in question can change the outcome.
