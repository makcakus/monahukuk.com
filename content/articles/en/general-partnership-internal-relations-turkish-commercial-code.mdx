Once a Turkish general partnership is formed, TTK Art. 217-231 governs how the partners deal with one another internally — management, oversight, voting, profit distribution, and competition. Because a general partnership is a "person company" built on mutual trust, most of these rules are default provisions that apply only where the partnership agreement is silent, leaving partners wide contractual freedom to shape their own relationship.

## Management and Its Limits

Under Art. 218, every partner is entitled to manage the partnership individually unless the agreement or a majority vote assigns management to one or more specific partners. Where management is granted by the founding agreement itself, that partner's authority cannot be curtailed by the others and can only be limited or withdrawn by court order for just cause — such as negligence or incapacity — under Art. 219. Management assigned later, by a post-formation resolution, can be revoked by majority vote instead. Critically, management authority is capped at ordinary business: extraordinary acts such as donations, suretyships, or disposing of core production assets require unanimous partner consent under Art. 223.

## Voting, Oversight, and Profit Sharing

Each partner holds one vote regardless of capital contribution, and any contrary agreement is void (Art. 226) — reflecting the personal, non-capital-weighted character of a person company. Amendments to the partnership agreement require unanimity; other decisions pass by simple majority. Partners excluded from management retain a mandatory, non-waivable right to inspect the partnership's books and demand a personal account of its financial position (Art. 225). Annual profits are determined and allocated at year-end by the managing partners and approved by majority (Art. 227); a partner may challenge an unfair allocation in court within three months.

## Non-Compete Duty

Art. 230 bars a partner from competing with the partnership's line of business, whether personally or through another company, without the other partners' consent — a direct expression of the fiduciary duty inherent in unlimited personal liability. A breach gives the partnership a choice under Art. 231: claim damages, treat the partner's competing transactions as done on the partnership's behalf, or claim the benefits the partner obtained from third parties. Turkish courts have treated an unauthorized competing directorship as sufficient grounds for expelling the offending partner, and have required a formal partner-majority resolution before any of these remedies can be pursued in litigation.

For foreign investors, the key practical takeaway is that a Turkish general partnership's internal governance is highly customizable by contract, but core protections — equal voting, oversight rights, and the unanimity requirement for extraordinary transactions — cannot be contracted away.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Who manages a general partnership?

Under TTK Article 218/1 <b>each of the partners has, individually, the right and the duty to manage the partnership.</b> Management may be entrusted to one or more partners by the partnership agreement or by a resolution of the partners' board. Moreover, under Article 217/1, <b>freedom of contract</b> governs the regulation of relations among the partners; the statutory provisions step in where the agreement leaves a gap.

### May a partner who is not a manager inspect the partnership's books?

Yes. Under TTK Article 225/1, <b>even if a partner does not have the right and duty of management</b>, they have the right <b>to inform themselves personally about the conduct of the partnership's affairs, to examine its documents and books and to draw up on that basis, for their own use, a statement showing the partnership's financial position</b>. This right cannot be removed or rendered unexercisable by the agreement.

### May a partner carry on the same business on their own account?

No. Under TTK Article 230/1, a partner <b>may not, without the consent of the other partners, carry on for their own account or for that of another a business of the kind carried on by the partnership of which they are a member, nor join as a partner with unlimited liability a company engaged in the same kind of commercial business.</b> Under Article 231, in case of breach the partnership may claim damages or treat the transaction as having been concluded on its own behalf; this right is time-barred <b>three months</b> after becoming aware of it and in any event <b>one year</b> after the date of the transaction.
