TTK Art. 658-669 govern bearer instruments (hamile yazılı senet) — the most freely negotiable category under Turkish negotiable instrument law, where simple possession establishes entitlement.

## Definition and the Debtor's Limited Defenses

Under Art. 658, any instrument whose text or form indicates that whoever holds it is entitled counts as a bearer instrument — no specific wording is required for the "bearer" designation. This simplicity is what makes bearer instruments the fastest and safest to circulate, but it sharpens the question of what defenses a debtor may raise. Art. 659 permits only three categories: defenses going to the instrument's invalidity, defenses apparent from its text, and defenses the debtor personally holds against whoever is currently claiming payment. A defense arising from a direct relationship with a prior holder can only be raised against a later holder if that holder knowingly acquired the instrument to the debtor's detriment — and Turkish courts place the burden of proving both the underlying defense and the holder's bad faith on the debtor. Notably, a debtor cannot defend on the basis that the instrument entered circulation without their consent (e.g., through theft) — the risk of an instrument going astray falls on the debtor who signed it, not on a good-faith holder.

## Interest Coupons

A debtor cannot resist a coupon claim merely by showing the principal was already paid, since coupons circulate as their own instruments (Art. 660/1); but a debtor paying principal may withhold amounts for outstanding coupons not yet due or surrendered, until the relevant limitation period runs, unless those coupons are separately cancelled or secured.

## Cancellation Procedure

Art. 661-666 set out a detailed cancellation process for lost bearer instruments (except individual coupons, covered separately): a court application, requiring only a convincing — not conclusive — showing of possession and loss; a payment ban served on the debtor under threat of double payment; a public notice period of at least six months inviting the unknown holder to come forward; and, if no one appears, a final cancellation order after which the applicant may request a new instrument or immediate payment. Individual coupons follow a faster deposit-based procedure (Art. 667). Notably, Art. 668 bars cancellation entirely for banknotes and similar mass-issued payment instruments — protecting the reliability of the monetary system from individual claims of loss.

## Practical Takeaway

A party dealing with lost or stolen bearer instruments in Turkey should expect a formal court-supervised cancellation process with a minimum six-month public notice period before a replacement or payment can be secured — there is no shortcut for instruments other than individual coupons.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is a bearer instrument and how is it transferred?

Under Article 658/1 TTK, any negotiable instrument <b>whose text or form shows that whoever holds it is to be treated as the person entitled</b> is a bearer instrument. It is the simplest type to transfer: the right passes <b>by transfer of possession of the instrument alone</b>, without endorsement or written assignment. That ease is balanced by restricting the defences available, so as to protect the security of circulation.

### Which defences may the debtor raise against the holder?

Under Article 659/1 TTK the debtor may raise, against a claim arising from a bearer instrument, <b>only defences concerning the invalidity of the instrument or appearing from its text, together with defences personally available against whoever the creditor happens to be</b>. Defences arising from the underlying relationship with earlier holders cannot be raised against a new holder. Moreover, under Article 660 the debtor <b>may not plead payment of the principal against bearer interest coupons</b>.

### How is a lost bearer instrument annulled?

Under Article 661/1 TTK, annulment of bearer instruments — <b>coupon sheets and talons, excluding share certificates, bonds, participation certificates and individual coupons</b> — is ordered <b>by the court on the application of the person entitled</b>. The court first prohibits payment by the debtor, then publishes a notice calling on whoever holds the instrument to come forward; if no one does so within the period, it orders the instrument annulled. For individual coupons, the special procedure in Article 667 applies.
