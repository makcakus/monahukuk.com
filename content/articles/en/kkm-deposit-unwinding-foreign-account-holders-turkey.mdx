Turkey's KKM scheme — formally known as *kur korumalı mevduat*, or currency-protected deposit — is winding down. Introduced in late 2021 to shield savers from lira depreciation, the program attracted wide participation, including from foreign nationals and companies [established in Turkey](/en/articles/foreign-investor-company-formation-turkey) who held Turkish bank accounts. As the scheme phases out through natural account maturities, understanding your rights as a foreign account holder is essential before your deposit reaches its end date.

## What Is KKM and How Did It Work?

The legal foundation of the KKM program is the Central Bank of the Turkish Republic's Communiqué No. 2021/14 on the Support of Conversion of Turkish Lira Deposits and Participation Accounts. The scheme offered depositors a government-backed guarantee: if the Turkish lira fell more than the contracted interest rate over the deposit period, the state topped up the difference. This made KKM accounts attractive to foreign investors holding funds in Turkey, since the exchange-rate risk was effectively absorbed by the government.

Two main product types existed. The first involved converting existing foreign-currency holdings — US dollars, euros, or similar — into a TL time deposit with the exchange-rate guarantee. The second covered existing TL deposits switching into the same protected format. Both types were structured as fixed-term accounts, typically rolling over every 92 days or six months.

## Why KKM Is Being Phased Out

From mid-2023, Turkey pivoted to orthodox monetary policy and raised its policy interest rate sharply. Traditional high-yield TL deposits became competitive again, removing the economic rationale for the government guarantee. Banking regulator BDDK progressively reduced incentives for banks to renew KKM contracts, and from 2024 the program has been unwinding through natural account maturities — without an abrupt cut-off date.

For foreign account holders in Antalya or anywhere in Turkey, this raises a straightforward question: what happens when your account matures and cannot be renewed under the old terms?

## What Happens When Your KKM Account Matures?

At the end of each term, the bank is obliged to return the principal, the contracted interest, and any applicable exchange-rate top-up. Turkish commercial courts have confirmed this basic obligation: if the lira depreciated more than the contracted rate, the top-up is due. If the lira held its value or strengthened, only the standard interest applies.

Once the scheme is no longer renewable, the bank will typically convert the maturing balance into a standard TL time deposit, a demand account, or — for accounts originally funded with foreign currency — offer reconversion into that currency. The contractual terms govern which options the bank must present. If the bank's offer seems vague or unfavourable, you have the right to request a full written calculation and to dispute it formally.

## Legal Risks: Bank Set-Off Rights and Blocked Funds

A significant issue that has come before Turkish commercial courts involves banks exercising their right of set-off (*takas ve mahsup hakkı*). When a customer holds both a KKM deposit and an outstanding loan at the same bank, the bank may seek to apply the maturing KKM proceeds against the loan balance rather than returning them. This right is not unlimited: it requires an express contractual basis or the satisfaction of the general set-off conditions under Turkish law. Courts have scrutinised these cases closely in recent commercial litigation and have not recognised a blanket right of offset.

If you believe a bank has improperly withheld or set off your KKM funds, the dispute pathway begins with a formal written objection to the bank. Commercial banking disputes in Turkey require mandatory mediation (*arabuluculuk*) before any lawsuit can be filed. If mediation fails, the case is heard by the specialised commercial courts (*ihtisas asliye ticaret mahkemeleri*) that handle banking and finance matters. For parties who prefer an alternative route, [Turkey's arbitration options](/en/articles/arbitration-turkey-icc-istac-guide) may also be available depending on the contract terms. A complaint to BDDK is a parallel option if the bank's conduct appears to violate banking regulations.

## Tax Considerations for Foreign Account Holders

The tax treatment of KKM income differs depending on whether the depositor is an individual or a company. For companies, Turkey's Corporate Tax Law (5520 sayılı Kurumlar Vergisi Kanunu, geçici madde 14) provided specific exemptions for exchange-rate gains realised under the KKM scheme during particular periods — confirmed in decisions of the Council of State (*Danıştay*). These exemptions were time-limited and are unlikely to apply to accounts maturing in 2025 or later.

For individual foreign depositors, the general Turkish income tax rules for interest and exchange-rate income apply. The applicable withholding rate and any exemptions depend on residency status and the relevant tax year. Given the rules changed during the scheme's lifespan, consulting a Turkish tax adviser before making any conversion or withdrawal decision is the prudent course. Foreign investors in Turkey should also monitor regulatory updates through the [Capital Markets Board framework](/en/articles/capital-markets-board-spk-foreign-investors-turkey), which governs broader financial market activity for non-residents.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q: Can a foreign national hold a KKM account in Turkey?

Yes. Foreign individuals and foreign-registered companies with Turkish bank accounts could open KKM accounts, provided the bank's standard know-your-customer requirements and BDDK regulations were satisfied.

### Q: The bank has not paid the exchange-rate top-up at maturity. What should I do?

Request a written calculation from the bank first. If the top-up appears underestimated or withheld, send a formal written objection to the bank's customer complaints unit. If unresolved, proceed to mandatory mediation and then, if necessary, to the specialised commercial court.

### Q: Can the bank use my KKM proceeds to repay my loan with that same bank?

Only if the deposit or loan agreement expressly permits it, or if the legal conditions for set-off are met under Turkish law. Courts have not recognised a general right for banks to apply KKM funds without sufficient contractual or legal grounds.

### Q: Are KKM exchange-rate gains taxable income for individual foreign depositors?

Generally yes. Interest and similar income from Turkish bank accounts is subject to withholding tax at source. The rate depends on your tax residency status and the year in which the income arises. Verify the current rules with a Turkish tax adviser.

### Q: What happens if I give the bank no instructions at maturity?

The account contract typically provides that the balance converts automatically to a standard account if no instruction is given. To avoid an unwanted conversion, provide clear written instructions to the bank before or on the maturity date.
