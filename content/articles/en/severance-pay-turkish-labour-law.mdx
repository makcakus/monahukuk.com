Foreign employers who set up operations in Turkey are often surprised to learn that severance pay — one of the most litigated issues in Turkish labour law — is not governed by the current Labour Act, Law No. 4857, but by a single surviving article of an older, otherwise repealed statute. Article 14 of the former Labour Act, Law No. 1475, remains the sole legal basis for severance claims two decades after it was supposed to be replaced. Understanding why this provision survives, and precisely which termination scenarios trigger a severance obligation, is essential for any business budgeting for workforce costs or planning a termination in Turkey.

## Why a Repealed Law Still Governs Severance Pay

When Law No. 4857 was enacted in 2003 and overhauled almost every area of Turkish labour law, lawmakers could not reach agreement on a modern severance-pay mechanism — in particular, a proposal to convert severance into a fund-based insurance scheme was debated but never adopted. Rather than leave a gap, Article 120 of Law No. 4857 expressly kept Article 14 of the old Law No. 1475 in force while repealing everything else in that statute. More than twenty years later, no replacement law has been passed, so this single article continues to apply directly to every severance dispute in Turkey. Its cross-references to "Article 17" and "Article 16" of the old law are now read as referring to the corresponding provisions of the current Labour Act — Article 25 (employer's termination for just cause) and Article 24 (employee's termination for just cause), respectively.

## The Two Basic Conditions for Severance Pay

Severance pay arises only when two conditions are met together: the employee must have at least one full year of service with the same employer, and the employment relationship must have ended for one of the specific reasons the law lists. For the one-year threshold, continuous employment is not required — periods worked for the same employer at one or more of its workplaces, even with breaks in between, are added together. As for qualifying termination reasons, the law uses a closed, exhaustive list rather than a general standard: dismissal by the employer for a reason other than the employee's misconduct, termination by the employee for just cause, discharge due to compulsory military service, leaving to draw an old-age or disability pension or a lump-sum payment, and a female employee's resignation within one year of her marriage. The employee's death is treated as a separate qualifying event in its own right. Any other way the relationship ends — most importantly, a voluntary resignation without just cause, or a dismissal by the employer for just cause — does not, as a rule, create an entitlement to severance pay.

## Termination Grounds That Trigger the Obligation

The first category covers dismissals initiated by the employer for any reason other than the just-cause grounds set out in Article 25/II of the current Labour Act (which covers breaches of the rules of morality and good faith). This means that even an economic, operational or performance-based dismissal by the employer still triggers severance pay — the employer's right to terminate for business reasons does not eliminate its severance obligation. Where the employee terminates for just cause under Article 24, the same result follows, and this is one of the few situations in Turkish practice where an employee can walk away with severance even though no notice pay is owed to the employer. Departure for military service and departure to draw an old-age or disability pension fall into the same protected category. So does a female employee's resignation on account of marriage, but this ground is narrower than it looks: only a female employee may rely on it, and the right must be exercised within one year of the marriage date — once that year passes, a resignation citing marriage no longer generates severance. Where the termination is pension-related, the employee is expected to show that they have actually qualified for an old-age pension and have applied to the relevant social security institution, since this documentation is what distinguishes a genuine retirement from an ordinary resignation dressed up as one.

## Calculating Length of Service, Business Transfers and the Statutory Cap

A transfer of the workplace to another employer, or its relocation, does not interrupt the employee's length of service for severance purposes — service is calculated by adding together the periods worked at the transferred or relocated workplace(s), a rule closely tied to how employee claims are treated more broadly on a change of employer. The wage used as the basis for the severance calculation is the employee's last gross wage plus any recurring cash and cash-equivalent benefits. That calculation, however, is not open-ended: Turkish law imposes an annual severance cap tied to the retirement bonus paid to the highest-ranking state civil servant, and this ceiling is republished each year. Crucially, the cap cannot be exceeded even by agreement — neither a collective bargaining agreement nor an individual employment contract may lawfully provide for a higher severance amount that would actually be paid above the statutory ceiling; if such a contractual promise exists, payment is still limited to the cap.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does resigning without cause ever entitle an employee to severance pay?**
No. A plain resignation that does not fall within one of the law's listed grounds — just cause, military service, pension eligibility, or (for a female employee) marriage within the preceding year — does not create a severance entitlement.

**If an employer dismisses an employee for business or performance reasons, is severance still owed?**
Yes. Severance is owed for any employer-initiated dismissal except one based on the employee's breach of the rules of morality and good faith under Article 25/II of the Labour Act. Economic, restructuring or performance-based dismissals still trigger the obligation.

**Can an employer or a collective agreement promise severance above the statutory cap?**
No. The annual cap, tied to the top state civil servant's retirement bonus, cannot be exceeded regardless of what a contract or collective bargaining agreement provides.

**Does transferring the business to a new owner reset the employee's length of service?**
No. Periods of service at the workplace before and after a transfer or relocation are added together for severance purposes; the transfer itself does not interrupt continuity of service.
