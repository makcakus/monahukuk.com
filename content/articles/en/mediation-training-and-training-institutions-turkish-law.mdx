*Article 22 – (1) Mediation training means the training taken after completion of a law degree, comprising the basic knowledge relating to the conduct of mediation, communication techniques, negotiation and dispute resolution methods and behavioural psychology, together with the other theoretical and practical knowledge to be set out in the regulation.*

*Article 23 – (1) Mediation training is provided by the law faculties of universities that have a faculty of law, by the Union of Turkish Bar Associations and by the Turkish Justice Academy. These institutions may provide training upon obtaining permission from the Ministry. (…)*

*(3) If, on the basis of the documents submitted with the application, it is established that the training will achieve its purpose and that the continuity of training activity at the institution will be ensured, permission valid for a maximum of three years is granted to the training institution concerned.*

*Article 25 – (1) Training institutions issue to those who successfully complete their training a certificate confirming that they have completed mediation training.*

Mediation training is one of the statutory conditions for registration (Article 20/2-e), and the training regime is therefore a direct part of the regime governing entry into the profession. The Law has designed training not as a free market activity but as a public service that only a limited number of institutions may deliver with the Ministry's permission — a permission that is time-limited and revocable.

## The Content of the Training: More Than Legal Knowledge

Article 22 states that mediation training is taken **after completion of a law degree**. The training is therefore a specialist course added to legal education; those who are not law graduates cannot become mediators by taking it.

The subject headings listed in the Law are striking: basic knowledge relating to the conduct of mediation, **communication techniques**, **negotiation and dispute resolution methods** and **behavioural psychology**. This list shows that mediation is positioned not as an adjudicative technique but as a structured discipline of managing negotiation. Other theoretical and practical knowledge set out in the regulation is also included.

The duration, content and standards of the training and the principles and rules of the examination are determined, under Article 36/1, by a regulation issued by the Ministry. The 10th Chamber of the Council of State locates the statutory basis of those regulatory provisions in Article 36 of Law No. 6325 and draws attention to the Mediation Board's task of establishing the principles and standards relating to training, to the qualities of training institutions and to the examination.

## Who May Provide Training: A Closed List

Article 23/1 identifies a **limited number** of institutions that may provide training: the law faculties of universities that have a faculty of law, the Union of Turkish Bar Associations and the Turkish Justice Academy. This list is closed; private training companies, associations and foundations may not provide mediation training.

Even the institutions on the list have no automatic right to deliver training; they must **obtain permission from the Ministry**. The list of authorised training institutions is published electronically.

The application for permission is made in writing and must give reasoned information about the **training programme**, the **number and specialisations of the trainers** and the **funding sources of the institution or of the training programme** (Article 23/2). The obligation to disclose funding sources reflects a concern to prevent training from turning into a commercial venture.

Permission is granted if, on the basis of the documents submitted, it is established that the training will achieve its purpose and that the continuity of the training activity will be ensured, and it is valid for a maximum of **three years** (Article 23/3).

## Extension of Permission and Supervision

Under Article 24, a registered training institution may request in writing an extension of the validity of its registration **at the earliest one year and at the latest three months** before the registration expires. Extension is subject to two conditions: it must appear from the reports submitted under Article 26 that the mediation training is continuing successfully, and none of the grounds for revocation in Article 27 must be present. Where those conditions are met, permission may be extended **for three years at a time**. An institution that applies in time remains on the list until a decision is taken on its request.

The continuous instrument of supervision is Article 26: training institutions submit to the Department **every January** a report on the scope, content and success of the training activity carried out in the previous year.

## The Certificate

Under Article 25, training institutions issue to those who successfully complete their training a certificate confirming completion of mediation training. This certificate serves as proof of the condition in Article 20/2-e when applying for registration; but it does not by itself make a person a mediator — success in the written examination held by the Ministry is also required (except for those with twenty years' seniority in their profession), and registration must additionally be effected.

## Revocation of Permission: Five Grounds

Article 27 lists the cases in which the permission granted to a training institution is revoked **by the Board upon the request of the Ministry**:

- Where one of the conditions required for the permission has ceased to exist or is found not to exist,
- Where the training cannot be provided adequately,
- Where **forgery or significant errors** are committed in issuing mediation certificates,
- Where the reporting obligation under Article 26 is not fulfilled despite a warning,
- Where it is established that the continuity of the training activity has not been ensured.

The architecture of powers is again twofold here: the request comes from the Ministry and the decision is taken by the Board (Article 32/1-d).

## Examination and Quota: The Obstacle Beyond Training

Completing the training is only one stage of becoming a mediator. According to the settled case law of the 10th Chamber of the Council of State, deciding whether a mediation examination will be held lies within the Ministry's discretion; indeed the decisions record that the examination was scheduled because new mediators had to be brought into the system once employment disputes were included in mandatory mediation in 2018 and commercial actions in 2019.

As to the pass criterion, the Regulation changed in 2021. Before the amendment of 17 November 2021, the examination was held directly "according to the number of mediators required as determined by the Ministry", and only that number of candidates was deemed successful. In its present form, Article 45 of the Regulation provides that, as a rule, **candidates scoring at least seventy out of one hundred are deemed successful**; but the Ministry may set the number of candidates to be deemed successful according to need, in which case that number is announced in the examination notice.

The practical consequence is that a lawyer who has completed the training cannot be entered in the registry as long as no examination is held; the training certificate creates no time-bound right.

## Decisions of the Council of State

The following decisions illustrate the judicial review of the training and examination regime.

**1. Council of State, 10th Chamber, File No. 2019/12682, Decision No. 2021/1716, 08.04.2021**

The decision recalls the registration conditions in Article 20 and the rule in Article 36/1 that the supervision of training institutions and the duration, content and standards of the training are determined by regulation; it records that the examination was scheduled because new mediators were to be brought into the system after employment disputes were included in mandatory mediation in 2018 and commercial actions in 2019, and that determining the number of mediators lies within the Ministry's discretion.

**2. Council of State, 10th Chamber, File No. 2020/3766, Decision No. 2021/6503, 20.12.2021**

Listing the registration conditions in Article 20/2 — including having no connection or association with terrorist organisations and completing mediation training and passing the written examination held by the Ministry — the Chamber sets out that, under Article 36/1, the supervision of training institutions, the duration, content and standards of the training and the principles and rules of the written examination are governed by regulation.

**3. Council of State, 10th Chamber, File No. 2020/190, Decision No. 2021/6046, 07.12.2021**

The decision sets out that the Mediation Board's duties include determining the basic principles of mediation services and the professional rules, establishing the principles and standards relating to **mediation training and to the qualities and working procedures of training institutions** as well as the basic principles and standards of the examination held at the end of the training, and laying down the rules on the supervision of mediators.

**4. Council of State, 10th Chamber, File No. 2020/5637, Decision No. 2021/6507, 20.12.2021**

Within the same framework, the Chamber addressed the Board's task of establishing training and examination standards and assessed the lawfulness of the regulatory acts adopted for the performance of those tasks.

**5. Council of State, 10th Chamber, File No. 2021/7190, Decision No. 2025/2687, 22.05.2025**

A bar association sought annulment of the act treating the pass mark in the mediation examination of 24 November 2019 as 91 — so that candidates who exceeded the 70 threshold but fell below 91 were deemed unsuccessful — and of the Regulation provisions on which it was based. Since those provisions had been repealed and amended on 17 November 2021, the Chamber held that there was no need to adjudicate on the regulatory act; as to the implementing act, it found no unlawfulness in the administration's using the qualification criterion (a minimum of 70 points) and the quota criterion together, or in determining the requirement of 5,000 mediators on the basis of data such as the existing number of mediators and the volume of applications, and dismissed the action.
