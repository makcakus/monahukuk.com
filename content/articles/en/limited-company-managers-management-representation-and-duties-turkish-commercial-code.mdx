TTK Art. 623-632 govern managers (müdür), the second mandatory organ of a Turkish limited company, covering their appointment, powers, duties, representation authority, and removal.

## Appointment and Core Duties

Management and representation may be assigned by the articles to one or more partners, all partners, or third parties — but at least one partner must always hold management and representation authority (Art. 623). Where there are multiple managers, the general assembly appoints one as chair, and decisions pass by majority with the chair holding the tie-breaking vote (Art. 624). Certain functions can never be delegated away from managers: top-level direction, setting up the management organization, establishing accounting and financial controls, supervising delegates, forming a risk-management committee (except in small companies), preparing financial statements, and — critically — notifying the court if the company becomes over-indebted (Art. 625). Turkish courts treat a manager's failure to keep proper books or allowing key decisions to bypass co-partners as a breach of this organizational duty, sufficient grounds for removal.

## Loyalty, Non-Competition, and Equal Treatment

Managers owe a duty of care and must pursue the company's interests in good faith (Art. 626/1). Unlike ordinary partners, managers face an automatic, statutory non-competition duty unless the articles or unanimous written partner consent excuses it. Turkish courts have found that a manager simultaneously managing a competing company in the same sector breaches this duty, justifying removal and a claim for the benefit obtained. Managers must also treat all partners equally under equivalent conditions (Art. 627).

## Representation Authority and Removal

The scope, limitation, and registration of representation authority follow the joint-stock company rules by analogy (Art. 629); a manager's authority generally cannot be restricted against good-faith third parties except through registered and published limitations. The general assembly may remove a manager at any time without cause (Art. 630/1), while any individual partner — even a minority holder — may apply directly to a court for removal on just cause, such as serious breach of duty or loss of competence to manage (Art. 630/2-3). The company is liable for a manager's tortious acts committed while performing their duties (Art. 632).

## Practical Takeaway

A foreign investor appointing a manager to run a Turkish limited company subsidiary should build explicit non-competition and conflict-of-interest terms into the appointment, since the statutory duty automatically restricts the manager from running or holding an interest in a competing business.
