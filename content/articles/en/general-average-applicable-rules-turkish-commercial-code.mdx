Having defined what qualifies as general average in Art. 1272, TTK Art. 1273 answers the follow-up question: by what concrete technical rules is the contribution actually calculated?

## A Default Rule Pointing to the York-Antwerp Rules

Art. 1273(1) opens with "absent contrary agreement between the parties" — signaling this is a default rule the parties are free to displace by contract. Where they have not, general average adjustment is governed by the *latest* York-Antwerp Rules (YAR), as prepared by the Comité Maritime International (CMI) and translated into Turkish and published under the mechanism this article sets out. The reference to "latest" rather than a fixed edition means Turkish law automatically tracks CMI's most current published version without requiring the Code itself to be amended each time the YAR are updated.

## What the York-Antwerp Rules Actually Are

The YAR are not an international treaty — no state signs or ratifies them. They are a purely private-law instrument, prepared by CMI (a private professional body of maritime law associations and experts), and applied worldwide because bills of lading, charterparties, and freight contracts routinely and voluntarily incorporate them by reference. Art. 1273's technique of pointing Turkish statutory law at this private international standard — while providing for its periodic translation — reflects a deliberate choice to keep pace with an inherently international, fast-evolving field without constantly amending domestic legislation.

## Translation and Publication Mechanism

Art. 1273(2) assigns translation to a specialist committee jointly formed by the General Directorate of Insurance and the Ministry of Transport, Maritime Affairs and Communications — pairing insurance/finance expertise with maritime technical expertise. The translation, published alongside the original text in the Official Gazette, must also be updated through the same process whenever CMI revises the YAR, either on the relevant authorities' own initiative or upon application by interested parties.

## Default, Not Mandatory Law — By Design

Unlike the mandatory-law protections seen elsewhere in this Code for cargo interests and passengers, Art. 1273 imposes no such protection here — a deliberate choice reflecting who actually participates in general average: shipowners, cargo interests, and freight creditors are typically sophisticated commercial actors capable of evaluating and insuring their own risk, and carriage contracts routinely specify a particular YAR edition (e.g., the 1994 or 2016 version) by name.

## Case Law

A 2025 Sakarya Regional Court of Appeal decision (7th Civil Chamber, 2025/126-2025/371) applied Art. 1273(1) directly, confirming that the latest York-Antwerp Rules (in that case, the 2016 edition) governed a general average declared after an engine breakdown, and evaluated in detail which repair, towage, and adjuster costs qualified under specific YAR rules. Because general average adjustment confirmation proceedings are typically classified as non-contentious matters under Turkish procedural law, Regional Court of Appeal decisions in this area are often final, which explains the comparative scarcity of Court of Cassation precedent on Art. 1273.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Under which rules is the general average account drawn up?

Under TTK Article 1273/1, <b>unless the parties have agreed otherwise</b>, general average contribution is governed by the <b>most recent York-Antwerp Rules</b> prepared by the Comité Maritime International and translated into Turkish and published. Rather than regulating the technical framework of the calculation itself, the statute thus refers to an internationally accepted body of rules.

### Are the York-Antwerp Rules an international treaty?

No. The York-Antwerp Rules are not a treaty prepared by the Comité Maritime International (CMI) and submitted to States for ratification, but a body of <b>model rules</b>. They have no binding force of their own; in practice the parties adopt them by inserting a reference into their contract. TTK Article 1273 gives these rules the character of <b>default (supplementary) law</b> in Turkish law, so that they apply even where the contract contains no such reference.

### May the parties agree on rules other than the York-Antwerp Rules?

Yes. The phrase in Article 1273/1, <b>'unless the parties have agreed otherwise'</b>, shows plainly that the provision is of a default character. The parties may agree on the York-Antwerp Rules of a different year (for example the 1994, 2004 or 2016 texts) or on an entirely different set of rules. This freedom is recognised in a field whose participants are professionals with equal bargaining power.
