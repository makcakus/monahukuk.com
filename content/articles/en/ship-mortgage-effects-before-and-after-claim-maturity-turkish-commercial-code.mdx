TTK Art. 1030-1037 govern the practical rights of a ship mortgage creditor and owner across the life of the security — both while the secured claim is still not yet due, and once it matures.

## Protecting the Security Before Maturity

If the ship or its equipment deteriorates and jeopardizes the mortgage's value, Art. 1030 lets the creditor give the owner a deadline to fix the problem — after which the creditor may immediately foreclose, even before the claim is otherwise due. Where the danger stems from the owner's own manner of operating the ship, courts can escalate through provisional attachment, appointing a custodian other than the captain, and ultimately authorizing enforcement — a graduated, proportionate sequence designed to intervene as lightly and as late as possible. Where a third party (not the owner) threatens the ship's value, the creditor's remedy is narrower: only an injunction against that third party (Art. 1031), since attachment-style remedies against someone with no legal tie to the mortgage would be disproportionate.

## The Third-Party Owner's Defenses

A recurring theme is protection for an owner who is not personally the debtor — for example, where a company's loan is secured by a third party's ship. Under Art. 1032, that owner may raise any defense the actual debtor could raise (limitation, invalidity, set-off), and — importantly — the debtor's waiver of a defense does not automatically strip the owner of the same defense (Art. 1032(2)). Turkish enforcement courts treat the debtor and the third-party owner as mandatory joint parties in foreclosure proceedings, reflecting this independent protection.

## After Maturity: Payment, Subrogation, Deed Release

Once the claim matures, the owner may pay, deposit, or set off the debt (Art. 1035). If the owner is not personally the debtor, payment triggers subrogation — the mortgage passes to the paying owner up to the amount paid, without prejudicing the original creditor's remaining claim (Art. 1036). Once satisfied, the owner can demand the documents needed to update or cancel the registry entry (Art. 1037), preventing a fully repaid mortgage from lingering indefinitely as a "phantom" registry encumbrance.

## Practical Takeaway

Turkish courts have confirmed that provisional measures like seizing a ship must be tied to a concrete, evidenced risk — not an abstract fear that the vessel might disappear — reinforcing that Art. 1030's protections, while powerful, are not automatic.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What can a mortgagee do if the vessel loses value due to neglect?

Under TTK Article 1030/1, if the security provided by the mortgage is endangered as a result of deterioration of the vessel or its equipment, the creditor <b>may grant the owner a reasonable period to remove the danger</b>. If the period passes without result, the creditor may demand <b>immediate satisfaction from the vessel</b> even if the claim has not yet fallen due. This is an early-enforcement power granted to the creditor against the erosion of the security.

### Can the mortgagee sue if a third party damages the vessel?

Only in a limited way. Under TTK Article 1031/1, if there is concern that the vessel will deteriorate, due to an act of a third party, to a degree that endangers the security provided by the mortgage, the creditor may bring against the third party <b>only an action to prevent that act</b>. In other words, the creditor may seek a preventive remedy; the right to claim compensation belongs to the owner, since it is the owner's property that is damaged.

### Can the shipowner pay the debt if they are not the debtor?

Yes. Under TTK Article 1035/1, once the claim becomes due against the owner, or the debtor becomes entitled to pay the debt, <b>the owner may pay the debt</b>. The owner has this power even if they are a person other than the debtor; upon payment, the owner is subrogated to the creditor's rights and the ship mortgage passes to them. Furthermore, under Article 1032, the owner may raise against the mortgagee the defences that the debtor has against the creditor.
