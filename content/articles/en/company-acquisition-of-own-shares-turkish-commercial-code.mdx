If a Turkish joint-stock company could freely buy back or take as pledge its own shares, it could quietly return capital to shareholders behind creditors' backs. TTK Art. 379-389 respond with a tightly conditioned regime — a strict cap, mandatory shareholder authorization, targeted exceptions, and mandatory disposal timelines.

## The General Cap and Shareholder Authorization

A company cannot acquire for value or accept as pledge its own shares beyond one-tenth of its capital — including acquisitions made indirectly through a third party acting for the company's account (Art. 379/1). Even within that 10% limit, the general shareholders' meeting must authorize the board, for a maximum of five years, specifying the number and value range of shares and the price band (Art. 379/2). An additional "net assets" test requires that, after deducting the acquisition price, the company's remaining net assets still cover its capital plus non-distributable reserves (Art. 379/3) — and only fully paid-up shares may be acquired.

## Anti-Circumvention and Exceptions

Financial assistance to help a third party buy the company's shares — advances, loans, or security — is generally void, subject to narrow exceptions for financial institutions and employee share schemes (Art. 380). Three situations bypass the authorization requirement entirely: an urgent need to avoid imminent serious loss (with mandatory disclosure to the next shareholders' meeting, Art. 381); capital reduction, universal succession, statutory buy-out obligations, or debt collection through enforcement proceedings (Art. 382); and gratuitous acquisitions of fully paid shares, such as by gift (Art. 383).

## Mandatory Disposal

Shares acquired under the exceptional routes cannot simply sit on the company's books indefinitely. Shares acquired via universal succession, statutory obligation, or debt collection must be disposed of within three years, unless the company's total holding stays under 10% of capital (Art. 384). Shares acquired unlawfully — in breach of Art. 379-381 — must be disposed of, or any pledge released, within a much shorter six months (Art. 385). Shares that remain undisposed after these deadlines must be cancelled through a capital reduction (Art. 386).

## Subscription Ban and Frozen Rights

Separately, a company can never subscribe for its own shares itself, whether directly or through a third party or subsidiary acting on its behalf; violations shift subscriber liability to the founders or, on a capital increase, to the board members involved (Art. 388). Shares the company holds in itself carry no shareholder rights (except for gratuitously acquired shares) and are excluded from quorum calculations, and a subsidiary's holding of its parent's shares has its voting and related rights frozen (Art. 389) — preventing the company from effectively voting on itself.
