Most foreigners applying for a residence permit in Türkiye only discover how many separate payments the process involves once they are already in it. The card fee, the residence fee, a single-entry visa fee where applicable, and mandatory health insurance are distinct items governed by different laws, and the total cost can vary considerably depending on the applicant's nationality. This article summarises the figures in force as of 2026; because fees are reset every year, you should confirm the current amounts with the Presidency of Migration Management (goc.gov.tr) at the time of your application.

## What Fees Does a Residence Permit Application Involve?

A residence permit application is never a single payment. The first item is the fee for the residence permit document (card) itself, which falls under Law No. 210 on Valuable Papers; as of 2026 this is 964 TL, fixed regardless of nationality or permit type. The second item is the residence fee under Law No. 492 on Fees: for the first month it is calculated at 348.10 TL per day, subject to a floor of 653.70 TL and a ceiling of 3,359.90 TL per document, and 2,232.30 TL for each subsequent month of validity. Nationals of certain visa-exempt countries applying for a first residence permit from within Türkiye are also charged a single-entry visa fee, which varies by nationality but sits roughly between 6,500 TL and 10,000 TL in 2026. For children under 18, the residence fee is charged at half the adult rate.

## The Reciprocity Principle: Why Fees Differ by Nationality

Residence fees are not the same for every foreigner. Under Law No. 492, the Ministry of Treasury and Finance sets fees according to the principle of reciprocity: the treatment Türkiye's citizens receive in a given country directly shapes the fee charged to that country's citizens here. As of 2026, nationals of the Czech Republic, Denmark, Ireland, Kosovo, Nepal, Sri Lanka, Syria, Turkmenistan, the Turkish Republic of Northern Cyprus, and Palestine are exempt from the residence fee on reciprocity grounds, while citizens of some other countries face notably higher amounts. There is accordingly no single answer to "how much does a residence permit cost" — the exact figure depends on the applicant's nationality and is fixed by the amount Migration Management assesses.

## Health Insurance: A Mandatory, Separate Cost

Private health insurance is a distinct but compulsory item in every application. Applicants aged 18 to 65 must submit a valid [health insurance policy](/en/articles/health-insurance-requirement-residence-permit-turkey) covering the intended stay; applicants over 65 are not required to hold one, though a policy can still be arranged if requested. Policies run for a minimum of one and a maximum of two years, and by law the coverage and waiting periods are identical across every insurer — only the premium varies by company and by the applicant's age. As of 2026, annual premiums generally range from roughly 2,000 TL to 8,000 TL, rising with age. This cost is not included in the card fee or the residence fee and must be arranged separately, in advance.

## Notary, Translation, and Other Ancillary Costs

Beyond the official fees and insurance, assembling the application file carries its own cost. Sworn translation and notarisation of foreign-language documents — passports, birth certificates, marriage certificates — are required in most applications, as is notarising a lease or obtaining certified copies of title deeds or contracts. Totals vary with the number of documents involved, but in practice applicants should budget around 5,000 TL for this category. Family applications, which require more documents per household, see this figure rise proportionally.

## How and Where to Pay — and What Happens if You Don't

Once an appointment has been scheduled through the e-residence system, the resulting accruals can be paid through the Interactive Tax Office (ivd.gib.gov.tr) — code 9233 for the residence fee and code 9207 for the document sales fee — with e-Devlet providing a direct link to the same system. Payment can also be made in cash or by card at any tax office cashier or PTT branch. The residence fee and card fee must be paid by the time the application file is submitted, or within the period set for payment; unpaid amounts prevent the application from being completed, and the file is held pending payment. Prolonged non-payment can result in the application being rejected for incompleteness, forcing the applicant to restart the [short-term residence permit process](/en/articles/short-term-residence-permit-turkey-guide) from scratch.

## Refund Conditions and Calculating Total Cost for Families

The general principle under Law No. 492 is that any portion of a collected amount not corresponding to a service actually rendered is refundable to the payer. In practice, this is generally understood to mean that the card (valuable paper) fee can be reclaimed if the application is refused before the card has actually been printed and delivered, whereas the residence fee for an application that has already been assessed is, as a rule, not refunded. Because outcomes depend on the specific file, refund requests should be confirmed directly with the relevant Provincial Directorate of Migration Management or tax office. When calculating the total cost for a family application, each item — card fee, residence fee, and health insurance — must be totalled per family member: for example, for two adults and one child under 18, the adults pay the full residence fee while the child's fee is halved, health insurance is purchased separately for all three, and notary/translation costs scale with the number of documents required.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Are the card fee and the residence fee the same thing?

No. The card fee falls under Law No. 210 on Valuable Papers, relates to the physical printing of the document, and is fixed at 964 TL in 2026. The residence fee falls under Law No. 492 on Fees, varies with the permit's duration and the applicant's nationality, and is generally assessed separately from the card fee.

### Why isn't the fee the same for everyone?

Türkiye sets residence fees according to the reciprocity principle, meaning the fee regime a given country applies to Turkish citizens shapes the amount charged to that country's citizens here. As a result, some nationalities are fully exempt while others face a higher rate.

### Can my application proceed if I don't pay the fee?

No. Failing to pay the residence fee and card fee within the prescribed period prevents the application from being processed. The file is treated as incomplete until payment is made, and the longer payment is delayed, the greater the risk the application is rejected.

### Can I get a refund if my application is refused?

If the card has not yet been printed and delivered, the valuable paper fee can generally be reclaimed. The residence fee for an application that has already been assessed is, as a rule, not refunded. Since the outcome depends on the specific file, you should confirm directly with the relevant migration authority or tax office.
