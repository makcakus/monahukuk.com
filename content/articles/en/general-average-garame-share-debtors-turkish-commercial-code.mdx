Having defined general average and set the applicable adjustment rules, TTK Art. 1274 and Art. 1275 answer two practical questions: who personally owes a contribution share, and how do creditors actually collect it?

## Three Debtors, Three Different Reference Dates

Art. 1274(1) assigns a personal debtor to each of the three interests named in Art. 1272(3) — ship, freight, and other property — but notably uses a *different* point in time for each. The ship's contribution falls on whoever was shipowner at the moment the general average act occurred, since that party bore the ship's risk and benefited from the sacrifice at that precise moment; a later sale of the ship does not shift this liability. Freight and cargo contributions, by contrast, are tied to the *discharge date* — because freight claims and cargo ownership can change hands mid-voyage (through assignment, sale, or transfer of the bill of lading), and pinning liability to the discharge date offers a more stable, easily determinable reference point than the moment of the general average act itself.

## Consignee Liability for Knowing Acceptance

Art. 1274(2) adds a targeted rule: if a consignee accepts delivery of cargo *knowing* a general average contribution is owed on it, that consignee becomes personally liable for the share — up to the value the goods had at the time of delivery, calculated at the same proportional rate that would have applied had the goods instead been sold to satisfy the contribution. This preserves creditors' practical ability to collect once cargo has physically left their reach, while capping the knowing consignee's exposure to the actual value received.

## Three Parallel Security Devices

Art. 1275(1) secures general average creditors with three distinct instruments, each matched to the nature of the underlying interest: a maritime lien on the ship, a right of retention under Turkish Civil Code Art. 950–953 on the contributing cargo (the same provisions underlying a carrier's freight-related lien covered elsewhere in this series), and a pledge of claim under Civil Code Art. 954–961 on the freight — since freight is itself a claim rather than tangible property, only a claims-pledge, not a possessory lien, is available. Debtor identification (Art. 1274) and security (Art. 1275) are thus symmetrically linked across the same three categories of interest.

## Case Law

The 2025 Sakarya Regional Court of Appeal decision (7th Civil Chamber, 2025/126-2025/371) applied Art. 1274 directly, confirming that contribution shares were correctly calculated from the ship's sound value, the cargo's invoice value, and the applicable freight amounts, and rejecting a cargo interest's objection that its machinery had been overvalued.

## Practical Takeaway

Cargo owners and consignees should be aware that knowingly accepting delivery despite a pending general average declaration creates personal exposure up to the cargo's delivered value — making early notice from the carrier or adjuster commercially significant.
