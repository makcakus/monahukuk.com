Where a capital increase brings resources into the company, a capital reduction sends them out — and TTK Art. 473-475 build a three-stage safeguard around that outflow: disclosure and a board report at the resolution stage, a formal call to creditors before execution, and a registration bar that blocks the whole process until both are satisfied. Unlike the shareholder-protective focus of the capital increase rules, this chapter is built primarily around protecting creditors.

## The Resolution: Disclosure and the Asset-Sufficiency Test

Under Art. 473(1), the general assembly resolves a reduction as an articles amendment, and the call notice, letters, and website announcement must explain the reasons, purpose, and method of the reduction "in accordance with accountability principles" — with the board's supporting report submitted to, and approved by, the assembly and then registered and published. The substantive gatekeeper is Art. 473(2): a reduction cannot be resolved at all unless it is established that the company's remaining assets will fully cover creditor claims. The resolution requires the same 75% supermajority as other structural articles amendments (referencing Art. 421(3)), and any accounting profit generated by the reduction may be used only to cancel shares, not distributed to shareholders — otherwise the reduction could function as a disguised profit distribution. Capital can never fall below the statutory minimum (Art. 473(5)).

## Calling In the Creditors

Art. 474 requires the board to publish the reduction resolution on the company website and, three times at seven-day intervals, in the Turkish Trade Registry Gazette, inviting creditors to request payment or security for their claims within two months of the third announcement; known creditors also get individual letters. Where the reduction is purely nominal — offsetting an accumulated loss, with no actual outflow of company assets — the board may skip the creditor call and payment/security process entirely (Art. 474(2)), since there is nothing for creditors to be protected against.

## Execution Is Gated by Registration

Under Art. 475, the reduction can only be executed once the two-month period has expired and any claims made have been paid or secured; failing that, creditors have a two-year window from the registration's publication to sue for annulment of the reduction. Turkish courts have confirmed that this annulment action is the creditors' exclusive remedy — courts have no independent authority to intervene in or certify a capital reduction outside it. The final safeguard is Art. 475(4): neither the reduction resolution nor the fact that capital has actually been reduced can be registered without documentary proof that the Art. 473-474 requirements were met — a substantive, not merely formal, check by the registry.
