Articles 180 through 194 of the Turkish Commercial Code (TTK) regulate the third and final type of structural change — conversion (tür değiştirme) — and then set out rules common to all three: merger, demerger, and conversion. Conversion differs fundamentally from a merger or demerger: no assets move between companies; the same legal person simply changes its legal form. As Art. 180 puts it, "the converted company is the continuation of the former one."

## Permitted Conversions

A capital company may convert into another capital company or a cooperative; a general or limited partnership may convert into a capital company, a cooperative, or into each other; a cooperative may convert only into a capital company (Art. 181). Notably, conversion between general and limited partnership follows a lighter, simpler procedure outside the standard conversion rules, reflecting how closely related the two person-company forms already are (Art. 182).

## Shareholder Protection and Approval

As with mergers, shareholders' rights and shares must be preserved through the conversion, with equivalent compensation for holders of privileged or non-voting shares (Art. 183). Because the company already exists with an established estate and shareholder base, the new form's incorporation rules apply but without requirements like minimum founder count or in-kind capital contribution (Art. 184). A conversion report must specifically disclose any change in shareholders' personal liability regime — a conversion from a limited liability company into a general partnership, for instance, can shift shareholders from limited to unlimited personal liability, making this disclosure critical (Art. 186). Conversion decisions require a qualified majority — generally two-thirds — and take legal effect only upon registration (Art. 189).

## Continuing Liability and Common Remedies

Partners of a converted person company remain liable for pre-conversion debts under the same three-year rule that applies to mergers; employee transfer protections likewise carry over (Art. 190). Two remedies apply uniformly across mergers, demergers, and conversions: first, any shareholder whose shares or rights were inadequately protected may apply to the commercial court within two months of publication for a court-determined equalization payment, without affecting the validity of the underlying transaction (Art. 191); second, a shareholder who voted against the resolution and had that opposition recorded in the minutes may bring a special annulment action within the same two-month window if the statutory procedure was violated (Art. 192). Turkish courts apply this two-month deadline strictly, and have confirmed it is available to shareholders of any company involved in the transaction, not only the absorbed or converting party.

Foreign shareholders considering a challenge to a Turkish merger, demerger, or conversion should be aware that the two-month window under Art. 191-192 is a hard deadline — Turkish courts have consistently dismissed claims filed even shortly after it expires.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can a limited liability company convert into a joint-stock company?

Yes. Under Article 181/1(a) TTK <b>a capital company may convert into a capital company of another type or into a cooperative</b>; conversion of a limited company into a joint-stock company is the commonest example. Under Article 180/1 <b>the company converted into the new type is the continuation of the old one</b>: no liquidation is needed, the tax number and the contracts are preserved and the assets pass by universal succession. The formation rules of the new type must nevertheless be complied with, in particular the minimum capital requirement.

### How are members' rights protected on conversion?

Under Article 183/1 TTK <b>on conversion the members' shares and rights are preserved</b>; for non-voting shares their holders receive shares of equal value or shares carrying voting rights. In exchange for privileged shares, shares of equal value or appropriate compensation are given; for participation certificates, equivalent rights or the real value are paid. Where that protection is thought insufficient, an action for an equalisation payment may be brought under Article 191.

### Within what period must an action for an equalisation payment be brought?

Under Article 191/1 TTK, where on a merger, division or conversion the members' shares and rights have not been properly preserved or the exit consideration has not been fairly determined, each member may ask the <b>commercial court of first instance</b> at the seat of one of the participating companies to fix an appropriate equalisation payment, <b>within two months of publication of the resolution in the Turkish Trade Registry Gazette</b>. Under Article 191/4 this action <b>does not affect the validity</b> of the resolution.
