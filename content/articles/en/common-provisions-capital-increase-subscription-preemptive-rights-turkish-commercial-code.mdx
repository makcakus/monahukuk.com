Capital increases are among the most frequently litigated areas of Turkish company law. TTK Art. 456-461 lay out the rules common to all increase types, the mechanics of increasing through new subscriptions, and the preemptive right that protects existing shareholders from dilution.

## The Baseline Rule and the Three-Month Registration Trap

Under Art. 456, capital generally cannot be increased (except through internal resources) until prior cash contributions are fully paid, minor unpaid amounts aside. Critically, Art. 456(3) requires the increase to be registered within three months of the resolution — miss that window and the resolution itself becomes invalid, a sharper consequence than the general "no effect against third parties until registration" rule for articles amendments. Turkish courts have distinguished this hard deadline from softer procedural defects in the board's declaration (discussed below), treating missed registration as fatal but defective signatures on the declaration as, at most, grounds for annulment rather than automatic invalidity.

## The Board's Declaration

Art. 457 requires the board to sign a declaration, tailored to the increase type, confirming (among other things) that cash has been fully subscribed and paid, that in-kind contributions were properly valued, or that any preemptive-right limitation is adequately justified. This declaration is the shareholders' — and the market's — window into whether the increase is genuine.

## Two Systems for Deciding: General Assembly or Board

Under the standard (esas sermaye) system, the general assembly decides and shares are subscribed either in the amended articles or via separate subscription undertakings (Art. 459). Under the registered capital (kayıtlı sermaye) system — available to non-listed companies — the articles can delegate increase authority to the board up to a pre-set ceiling, for a maximum of five years (Art. 460); this trades a slower assembly process for faster board action, but resolutions can still be challenged within a shorter one-month window.

## Preemptive Rights: Strong by Default, Narrowly Limitable

Art. 461 gives every shareholder the right to subscribe new shares in proportion to their existing holding. Limiting or excluding that right requires both a justified reason and at least 60% shareholder approval — Turkish courts require the reason to genuinely serve equal treatment and the company's overall economic interest, not a controlling shareholder's private advantage, though converting company debt into capital has been accepted as a valid justification. Shareholders get at least fifteen days to exercise the right, and it is transferable regardless of any articles-based restriction on transferring registered shares.
