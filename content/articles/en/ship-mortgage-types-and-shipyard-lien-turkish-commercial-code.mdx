TTK Art. 1012-1013 open the ship security interests division of the Code, covering two narrow but practically important scenarios: pledging a share in an unregistered ship, and a shipyard's statutory lien for construction or repair costs.

## Pledging Shares in Unregistered Ships

Where an unregistered ship is operated through a joint shipping ownership arrangement (donatma iştiraki — Turkey's ship-specific co-ownership structure), a co-owner's share in that arrangement can be pledged under Art. 1012, but only under the Turkish Civil Code's general rules on pledging claims and rights, not under the ship-mortgage regime described below. This matters because the registered ship mortgage discussed in our companion articles is available only for ships entered in a registry — an unregistered vessel's co-ownership share is treated as a personal right rather than a registrable asset, so financing against it follows ordinary movable-claim pledge formalities (written agreement, notice to obligors).

## The Shipyard's Statutory Lien

Art. 1013 gives shipyards a powerful, non-waivable right: any shipyard that builds or repairs a ship may demand registration of a mortgage over that vessel to secure its unpaid construction or repair claims. Crucially, a shipyard cannot validly waive this right in advance — even if a shipowner's contract tries to make it do so. This mirrors the mechanic's lien concept familiar in many jurisdictions: whoever invests labor and materials in an asset gets automatic priority protection against the owner's non-payment. Formation follows the Civil Code's contractor-lien provisions (Art. 895-897), and — importantly for foreign lenders — a shipyard can register a preliminary notice on the ship or newbuilding registry to lock in this future lien right even before construction is finished, including a security mortgage covering only the completed portion of the work.

## Practical Takeaway

For banks financing a newbuilding or repair, this creates a real priority risk: under the Civil Code contractor-lien rules incorporated by reference, a shipyard's statutory lien can in some circumstances outrank an earlier-registered bank mortgage if the bank knew or should have known the shipyard's claim would be prejudiced. Turkish courts have confirmed this priority is available but strictly conditional — the shipyard must show both that its claim could not otherwise be recovered and that the prior mortgagee had actual or constructive knowledge of the harm.
