A "letter of indemnity" (LOI) — a shipper's private promise to compensate the carrier for issuing a clean bill despite doubts about the cargo — is common commercial practice and a well-known threat to the integrity of the reservation system. TTK Art. 1241 draws Turkey's line on it, consistent with the international consensus.

## Void Against Good-Faith Third Parties

Art. 1241(1) folds the general duty of accurate disclosure (Art. 1145) into bill of lading entries. Art. 1241(2) then targets the core problem directly: any promise by a shipper to indemnify the carrier for issuing a bill *without* the reservation that Art. 1239 would otherwise require — because of doubts about the cargo's description or apparent condition — is void against every good-faith third party who acquires the bill, consignee included. Without this rule, a carrier and shipper could privately neutralize the entire reservation system that protects downstream holders; the LOI would let the carrier collect a side payment while a bank or buyer relies on a "clean" bill that both original parties knew was misleading.

## Valid Between the Parties — Unless There Is Intent to Deceive

Art. 1241(3) draws a sharp distinction. Between carrier and shipper themselves, the indemnity remains valid — ordinary risk allocation between commercial parties who negotiated it is respected. But if the carrier omitted the reservation with the *intent to deceive* third parties relying on the bill's description, the carrier loses even its right to seek reimbursement from the shipper under Art. 1145 for that specific deception. A carrier who knowingly participates in misleading third parties cannot then be compensated by its co-conspirator for the consequences.

## Loss of Limitation Rights

Where that deceptive intent is present, Art. 1241(4) goes further: the carrier loses the benefit of the SDR-based liability limits under Art. 1186 entirely, and is exposed to unlimited liability toward good-faith holders — the same consequence as reckless conduct under Art. 1187.

## Practical Note

Turkish courts, even under the predecessor 1956 Code, have confirmed that such indemnity letters are void against third parties regardless of whether a clean bill was actually issued in reliance on them — a carrier cannot use the LOI as a shield even hypothetically.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is a letter of indemnity for a clean bill of lading valid?

Between the parties, yes; against third parties, no. Under TTK Article 1241/2, an undertaking or agreement whereby the shipper agrees to indemnify the carrier for damage the carrier suffers as a result of issuing a bill of lading <b>without adding a reservation</b> regarding the particulars declared by the shipper or the apparent condition of the goods, is <b>invalid against third parties</b>. Under Article 1241/3, however, it is valid as between the parties.

### Can this undertaking also be invalid between the parties?

Yes, if there is intent to deceive. Under the exception in TTK Article 1241/3, the undertaking is valid between the parties <b>unless the carrier, or a representative of the carrier, by omitting the reservation, intends to deceive third parties — including the consignee — who act in reliance on the description of the goods in the bill of lading</b>. In that case, the undertaking provides the carrier with no protection whatsoever.

### What is the carrier's liability in the case of intent to deceive?

It is unlimited. Under TTK Article 1241/4, <b>where intent to deceive exists, the carrier is liable to third parties — including the consignee — who act in reliance on the particulars in the bill of lading, without being able to invoke the limits of liability provided in Article 1186</b>. That is, the caps of 666.67 SDR per package or 2 SDR per kilogram do not apply; the carrier is liable for the entirety of the damage. This is the strongest deterrent placed against the practice of issuing clean bills of lading.
