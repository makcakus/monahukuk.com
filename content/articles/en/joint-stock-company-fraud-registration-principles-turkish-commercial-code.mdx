TTK Art. 353-358 close out the general provisions on Turkish joint-stock company formation with three related topics: what happens when the formation process itself was unlawful, when the company legally comes into being, and two foundational principles — equal treatment of shareholders and a ban on shareholder borrowing — that run throughout the rest of the Code.

## No Voiding a Company — Only Prospective Dissolution

Art. 353 sets a striking rule: a joint-stock company can never be declared void or non-existent, however serious the formation defect. Instead, where formation defects seriously endangered or harmed creditors, shareholders, or the public interest, the company may only be dissolved prospectively by court order, on application by the board, the Ministry, a creditor, or a shareholder — and the court may first grant time to cure the defect. This suit must be filed within three months of registration and publication, a strict deadline Turkish courts have also applied by analogy to capital increase registrations.

## Registration as the Trigger for Legal Personality

The company acquires legal personality only upon registration with the trade registry — a constitutive, not merely declaratory, act (Art. 355). Anyone who transacts on the company's behalf before registration is personally and jointly liable for those commitments, unless the deal was explicitly made "for a company to be formed" and the company later ratifies it within three months of registration. Turkish courts have held a person who signs a lease "on behalf of" an as-yet-unregistered company personally liable for the full rent, since an entity without legal personality cannot be represented at all.

## Anti-Circumvention, Equal Treatment, and No Borrowing

Art. 356 prevents founders from dodging the in-kind capital valuation rules by having the newly registered company "buy" or "lease" a founder's asset shortly after formation — any such deal above 10% of capital within two years of registration needs shareholder approval, court-appointed expert valuation, and registration to be valid. Art. 357 states the equal-treatment principle in a single sentence — shareholders in equal circumstances must be treated equally — which Turkish courts have applied broadly, including to capital calls and rights offerings, and have in one case treated a board decision stripping one shareholder group's board-representation privilege as void outright for breaching this principle. Finally, Art. 358 bars a shareholder from borrowing from the company unless their own capital debt is fully paid and the company's free reserves plus profit cover past losses — closing off a backdoor route to withdrawing capital.
