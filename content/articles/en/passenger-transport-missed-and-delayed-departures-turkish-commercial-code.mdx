TTK Art. 906-911 open Book Four's passenger-transport section, and depart noticeably from the goods-carriage rules covered elsewhere in this series — because what is being carried here is a person, not cargo, so the Code centers lost time and disrupted plans rather than pure economic loss.

## Departure Cancelled Before It Happens

Art. 907 sorts a pre-departure cancellation into four scenarios: force majeure (death, illness) voids the contract with no compensation to anyone; a vehicle-related or mutual-fault obstacle does the same; a cancellation caused by the carrier's own fault entitles the passenger to damages; and if the passenger simply missed that specific departure for any reason, the passenger gets a right to travel on a later comparable service — and a carrier that cannot offer one owes triple the ticket price as compensation, without needing to prove any actual loss.

## The Delay Rule That Skips Proof of Damage

Art. 908 is the standout provision in this part: if departure is delayed beyond what a passenger can reasonably be expected to tolerate, the passenger may cancel and reclaim the fare (plus any provable damages) or travel anyway and sue only for the delay itself — and regardless of which path is chosen, the court must award triple the ticket price as compensation even if no actual damage can be proven at all. This is a rare departure from Turkey's ordinary damages-require-proof rule, designed specifically to force punctuality and spare passengers an often-impossible evidentiary burden.

## Mid-Journey Deviations and Stoppages

Art. 909 lets a passenger cancel and claim damages if the carrier deviates from the route or stops somewhere off-schedule through its own conduct — though this rule can be contracted around. Art. 910 handles government orders, mandatory repairs, or sudden dangers: the passenger can either leave (paying only a distance-proportional fare) or wait (paying the full fare, including any lost meal costs). Art. 911 completes the picture for en-route stoppages generally: a passenger-initiated stop means full fare is owed; a carrier-fault stop means no fare and preserved damages; and a stoppage attributable to neither side means a distance-proportional fare with no compensation either way.

## Practical Takeaway

A foreign traveler facing an unreasonably delayed departure in Turkey should know that Art. 908's triple-fare compensation is available without proving any concrete loss — a right Turkish courts apply even where the passenger cannot show what the delay actually cost them.
