Art. 1306 fixes the owners at completion of salvage as the primary debtors of a salvage award. Art. 1307 adds a second, narrower debtor: the consignee who actually receives the goods, even though a consignee is not necessarily the "owner" that Art. 1306 addresses.

## Personal Liability Triggered by Knowledge, Not Ownership

Under Art. 1307(1), a consignee who knew — at the time of taking delivery — that a salvage award was owed on the goods becomes personally liable for that award, in the same proportion that would have applied had the goods never been delivered and instead been sold to satisfy the claim. The structure mirrors the consignee-liability rule for general average contribution under Art. 1274(2), covered earlier in this series: in both regimes, the creditor's normal security is a lien on the property itself, and that security disappears the moment the goods pass into the consignee's hands. The law responds by shifting personal liability onto a consignee who took delivery knowing the burden attached — while an innocent consignee, unaware of the charge, owes nothing extra.

## When Does "At the Time of Taking Delivery" Attach

Turkish courts have addressed a practical question: delivery is often a process, not an instant, so when exactly must the knowledge exist? In a case involving an LNG carrier disabled in the Dardanelles, the consignee learned by fax that a salvage award would be charged just four minutes after cargo discharge began. The consignee argued it had no knowledge at the *start* of delivery and so could not be liable. Turkish courts rejected this narrow reading: discharge was found to be a roughly 23-hour process, and knowledge acquired four minutes into that process — well before delivery was complete — was enough to satisfy Art. 1307(1). The consignee was held personally liable for the salvage award apportioned to that cargo. The practical rule: knowledge acquired at any point before delivery is complete counts, not only knowledge held at the very first moment.

## Liability Capped at the Consignee's Own Goods (Art. 1307/2)

Where other property was salvaged together with the goods actually delivered to the consignee, Art. 1307(2) caps the consignee's liability at the amount that would fall to those specific goods if total salvage costs were apportioned across everything salvaged. A consignee answers only for its own cargo's share — never for the share attributable to another owner's property salvaged in the same operation. This tracks the no-joint-and-several-liability principle already seen for the primary debtors under Art. 1306(2): the extra personal liability Art. 1307 creates for informed consignees is exceptional, but it does not become solidary.

## Practical Takeaway

For salvors, an informed consignee is a useful additional target for recovery — but the claim against that consignee is capped at its own cargo's share, and proving "knowledge before delivery was complete" is a factual, not merely a start-of-process, inquiry. For cargo interests taking delivery mid-salvage dispute, timing of notice matters far less than whether notice arrived before physical delivery was actually finished.
