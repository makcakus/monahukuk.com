Whether a foreigner wants to buy property in Türkiye, set up utility subscriptions, incorporate a company, or simply open a bank account, the first technical requirement is a number. Commonly referred to as the "tax number", it is obtained by different routes depending on the foreigner's status, and the type of number obtained shapes everything that follows. This article covers the types of number, the process of opening a bank account, and whether holding a number creates a tax liability in itself.

## The Foreigner Identification Number (YKN)

Article 8 of Civil Registration Services Law No. 5490, as rewritten in 2018, confers three distinct powers on the Ministry: to **issue identification numbers** to foreigners falling within Law No. 6458, to **enter them in the foreigners register**, and to **issue a foreigner identification number (YKN), upon request, to foreigners who apply for any transaction to a public institution in Türkiye or to our missions abroad**. Members of diplomatic missions fall outside this provision. The same article provides that the validity period of the YKN and the documents to be requested at and after application will be determined.

The third power matters a great deal in practice: the YKN is not confined to residence permit holders. A foreigner applying to a public institution in Türkiye for a transaction may obtain a number on request. That keeps the door open for foreigners who have not yet obtained a residence permit but need to carry out transactions in Türkiye.

Foreigners granted a [residence permit](/en/articles/short-term-residence-permit-turkey-guide) receive the number directly; the eleven-digit number, beginning with 99, appears on the identity card.

## The Tax Identification Number and the "Potential" Number

Not every foreigner needs a separate tax number for tax purposes. In practice, the **foreigner identification number also serves as the tax identification number**, so a foreigner holding a YKN need not obtain a further number from the tax office.

For foreigners without a YKN, the **potential tax identification number** comes into play. Foreigners not resident in Türkiye and without a residence permit, but who will purchase property, handle an inheritance matter, open a bank account, or become a company shareholder, may obtain this number from the tax office or through the Revenue Administration's online system. The identity page of the passport and — where the application is made from abroad — its notarised or apostilled translation, together with address details, are typically sufficient. Application by power of attorney is possible, which is a critical convenience for investors preparing a [title deed transaction](/en/articles/title-deed-transfer-checklist-foreign-buyers) without travelling to Türkiye.

The essential point is this: obtaining a number is not the same as becoming a taxpayer. The number is a technical identifier that allows transactions to be recorded; liability depends on the separate criteria discussed below.

## Opening a Bank Account

Opening an account at a bank in Türkiye is subject to the **customer identification** obligation imposed by banking legislation and by Law No. 5549 on the Prevention of Laundering Proceeds of Crime. Banks must establish the customer's identity, verify address details, and obtain information about the purpose of the transaction and the source of funds. This is not bank "bureaucracy" but a statutory requirement, which is why applications with incomplete documents are refused.

The documents banks typically request from foreign customers are: passport (with a notarised translation where required), foreigner identification number or potential tax identification number, a document showing the address in Türkiye (residence certificate, lease, or utility bill), a telephone number, and a specimen signature. Depending on its risk assessment, the bank may request further documents on the source of funds.

Three points deserve emphasis in practice. First, banks' internal policies differ; an application refused by one bank may be accepted by another, and that is not unlawfulness. Second, for foreigners without a residence permit, account opening usually comes with a narrower product range and more detailed documentary requests. Third, in processes such as citizenship by investment, currency conversion and fund transfers may need to run through particular banking channels, so account opening should be planned in line with that process.

## Does Holding a Number Create Tax Liability?

No — but the length of stay in Türkiye may. Article 4 of Income Tax Law No. 193 sets out two heads under which a person is deemed resident in Türkiye: **those whose domicile is in Türkiye** and **those who reside continuously in Türkiye for more than six months within a calendar year**. The same provision states expressly that temporary absences do not interrupt the period of residence in Türkiye — the idea that short trips abroad reset the six-month clock is therefore mistaken.

A person deemed resident becomes a **full taxpayer** and is, as a rule, taxed on all income earned inside and outside Türkiye. Those not deemed resident are **limited taxpayers**, taxed only on income earned in Türkiye.

The Law makes an important exception to that test. Under Article 5, the following foreigners are **not deemed resident in Türkiye even if they stay for more than six months**:

- Businesspeople, scientists, experts, officials, press and broadcast correspondents coming to Türkiye for a specific and temporary assignment or task, and others in comparable positions,
- Those coming for education, treatment, rest, or travel,
- Those detained or kept in Türkiye for reasons beyond their control, such as custody, sentence, or illness.

This exception is decisive for experts on project-based assignments, students, and those staying for lengthy medical treatment. In addition, the **double taxation treaties** to which Türkiye is a party contain tie-breaker rules determining which state has taxing rights where a person could be treated as resident in both; the domestic six-month test does not settle the matter on its own.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I obtain a tax number without coming to Türkiye?

Yes. The potential tax identification number can be obtained through the Revenue Administration's online system or via a representative acting under a power of attorney. A power of attorney executed abroad must meet apostille and sworn translation requirements.

### I have no residence permit — can I open a bank account?

In principle yes; the law imposes no residence permit condition, but banks must discharge the customer identification obligation under Law No. 5549. More documents are therefore usually requested from those without a permit, and the outcome may vary with bank policy.

### I have obtained a tax number — will I pay tax in Türkiye?

Obtaining a number does not by itself create liability. What matters are the criteria in Article 4 of the Income Tax Law: domicile in Türkiye, or continuous residence for more than six months within a calendar year. The exceptions in Article 5 and the applicable double taxation treaty must also be assessed.

### Do short trips abroad reset the six-month period?

No. Article 4(2) of the Income Tax Law expressly provides that temporary absences do not interrupt the period of residence in Türkiye. Proceeding on the assumption that the clock resets risks a later finding of full tax residency.
