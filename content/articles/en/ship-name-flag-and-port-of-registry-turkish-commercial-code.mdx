TTK Art. 938-946 govern a ship's legal identity: its name, its flag, and its port of registry — with the Turkish flag test being the most consequential of the three.

## Naming and Marking

Under Art. 938, a ship's first Turkish owner may choose any name, provided it will not be confused with another vessel; changing it later requires ministry approval. Art. 939 requires the name to be painted, durably and legibly, on both sides of the hull, with the port of registry on the stern — a safety and identification requirement, not mere decoration.

## Who Qualifies for the Turkish Flag

Art. 940 is the doctrinal core of this chapter: every Turkish ship must fly the Turkish flag, and "Turkish ship" turns on ownership by Turkish nationals. For co-owned vessels, a majority of shares or owners must be Turkish; for companies, the test focuses on actual control — a majority of those authorized to manage the company, a Turkish majority of voting rights, and (for joint-stock companies) registered shares with board approval required for any transfer to a foreigner. This multi-layered test is designed to prevent flag-of-convenience-style workarounds through nominal Turkish ownership.

## Bareboat Charter Exceptions

Art. 941 accommodates international chartering practice: a Turkish ship chartered out for at least a year to parties who would not otherwise qualify may be permitted to temporarily fly a foreign flag, and conversely, a foreign ship chartered to Turkish-qualifying parties may temporarily fly the Turkish flag, subject to biennial re-verification. Art. 942 provides that flag rights are lost automatically the moment the underlying ownership/control conditions fail, though a six-month grace period may be granted for correctable situations.

## Proof and Small-Vessel Exemption

Under Art. 943-945, the "ship's certificate" is the primary proof of flag rights (a consular "flag certificate" substitutes abroad), and vessels under 18 gross tons, along with recreational and training vessels, are exempt from needing either document.

## Practical Takeaway

For anyone structuring ship ownership through a Turkish company, the flag test looks past the cap table to who actually controls the entity — a foreign majority stake dressed up with nominal Turkish management will not satisfy Art. 940, and Turkish courts treat the flag-loss conditions as an exhaustive, non-extendable list.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can a vessel be given any name desired?

As a rule, yes. Under TTK Article 938/1, <b>the first Turkish owner of the vessel is free to give the vessel any name they wish</b>. The only limitation is this: <b>the chosen name must be sufficiently different from the names of other vessels so as not to cause confusion</b>. Under Article 939, the vessel's name is painted on both sides of the bow and, together with the port of registry, on the stern of a registered vessel; a master who fails to comply with this obligation is subject to penalty under Article 950.

### Which vessel is a Turkish vessel?

Under TTK Article 940/2, <b>a vessel owned solely by a Turkish citizen is a Turkish vessel</b>, and under Article 940/1, <b>every Turkish vessel flies the Turkish Flag</b>. Vessels belonging to legal entities established under Turkish law and having their administrative centre in Turkey are likewise deemed Turkish vessels, subject to the conditions listed in the same Article. Under Article 942, if one of these conditions ceases to exist, the vessel loses the right to fly the Turkish Flag.

### How is the port of registry determined?

Under TTK Article 946/1, <b>a vessel's port of registry is the place from which the voyages relating to that vessel are managed</b>. The criterion is not the place where the vessel actually anchors, but the place where operational decisions are taken. The port of registry has practical importance because, under Article 955, it determines the registry office at which the vessel is to be registered, and, under Articles 1063 and 1087, it establishes an additional court of competent jurisdiction for actions brought against the shipowner and co-owning shipowners.
