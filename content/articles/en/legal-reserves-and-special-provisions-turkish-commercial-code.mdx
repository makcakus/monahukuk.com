Before profit reaches shareholders, Turkish law requires part of it to stay inside the company as a capital buffer. TTK Art. 519-528 set out this reserve system.

## The Mandatory 20% Legal Reserve

Art. 519 requires 5% of annual profit to be set aside as a general legal reserve until it reaches 20% of paid-in capital — a mandatory minimum that the articles of association cannot reduce, only increase (Art. 521). Once the 20% threshold is reached, additional sources (share premium, forfeited-share proceeds, and 10% of amounts remaining after a 5% first dividend) continue feeding the reserve. Below the 50%-of-capital threshold, this reserve may only be used to cover losses or sustain operations during downturns — the Court of Cassation has struck down articles-of-association provisions that purported to eliminate rather than merely raise the 20% floor, treating it as a violation of mandatory law that harms shareholders' dividend rights.

## Discretionary Reserves Need a Real Justification

Beyond the mandatory reserve, the general assembly may set aside additional (extraordinary) reserves under Art. 523, but only for two limited reasons: the need to restore assets, or a justified interest in the company's continued growth and stable dividend policy. Recent case law demands concrete, forward-looking investment projections — vague statements like "protecting the company's viability" are insufficient, and a decision not to distribute profit made without such justification violates good faith. Notably, no dividend can be determined until statutory and discretionary reserves are set aside first — reserves come before dividends in the calculation order.

## Employee Welfare Reserves and Repurchased-Share Reserves

Art. 520 requires a matching reserve when the company buys back its own shares. Art. 522 allows reserves for employee welfare foundations, but the earmarked assets must be spun off into a separate foundation or cooperative — insulating them from the company's general creditors — and employees who paid contributions but never benefited must at least get their contributions back with statutory interest.

## Confidentiality and Regulated Sectors

Art. 527 prohibits disclosure of trade secrets learned while inspecting company books, backed by liability for resulting damages. Art. 528 gives priority to sector-specific rules (banking, insurance, capital markets, factoring) over the general TTK reserve regime.
