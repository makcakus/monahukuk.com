The one moment in mediation that cannot be recovered once missed is the first meeting — and the reason is not settlement but the **objection to jurisdiction**. A process conducted before the wrong office remains valid if no objection is raised; and if an objection is to be raised, the first meeting is the only opportunity.

## Which Office Do You Apply To?

The statutory rule is a single sentence: the application is made to the mediation office **at the place of the court having jurisdiction over the subject matter of the dispute**. Where no mediation office has been established, the application is made to the registry of the designated civil court of peace.

In other words, the jurisdiction of the mediation office follows the jurisdiction of the court in which the action would be brought. The situations most often encountered in practice:

- **Employee claims:** the court of the employer's domicile or of the place where the work is performed has jurisdiction; the employee may choose between them.
- **Commercial receivables:** the defendant's domicile, the place of performance of the contract, or the court agreed in the contract.
- **Lease disputes and immovables:** the place where the immovable is situated.
- **Condominium and neighbour law:** the place where the immovable is situated.
- **Consumer disputes:** the consumer's domicile also has jurisdiction.

Unless the parties agree otherwise, the sessions are conducted **within the territorial area of the judicial commission** to which the office that appointed the mediator belongs.

## The Mediator Cannot Raise Lack of Jurisdiction of Their Own Motion

The express rule of the Law is this: the mediator **cannot of their own motion take into account** whether the office that made the appointment has jurisdiction.

This has consequences in two directions. On the one hand, an application made to the wrong office is not rejected by the mediator; the process proceeds normally. On the other hand, if the other party does not object, the lack of jurisdiction produces no effect at all and the process conducted satisfies the condition precedent.

Lack of jurisdiction, in short, is a problem only if it is raised.

## The Only Opportunity to Object: The First Meeting

The other party may object to the jurisdiction of the mediation office **at the latest at the first meeting**, by submitting the documents relating to jurisdiction.

Two elements are required together: the **timing** of the objection (at the latest at the first meeting) and its **documentation** (submission of the documents relating to jurisdiction). An oral objection without documents may not suffice; supporting material such as a residence certificate, a trade registry record or the jurisdiction clause in the contract must be produced.

If no objection is raised at the first meeting, lack of jurisdiction cannot be raised later. The defence "the office lacked jurisdiction" will not be heard at a second session or in the action subsequently brought.

## How Does the Process Work After an Objection?

Step by step:

**1.** Upon the objection, the mediator immediately hands the file to the office so that it may be sent to the relevant **civil court of peace**.

**2.** The court, after an examination conducted on the file **without any court fee**, determines the office having jurisdiction **finally** and **within one week at the latest**, and returns the file to the office. There is no appeal against the decision.

**3.** The court's decision is served on the parties by the office in accordance with the provisions of the Notification Law.

## Rejection and Acceptance of the Objection: Two Different Outcomes

**If the objection is rejected:** the same mediator is reappointed, and the three-week period (six weeks in commercial disputes) runs **from the date of the new appointment**. The process restarts from the beginning, not from where it stopped.

**If the objection is upheld:** an application may be made to the office having jurisdiction **within one week** of service of the decision. And here a critical rule protecting the applicant comes into play: **the date of application to the office lacking jurisdiction is deemed to be the date of application to the office having jurisdiction.**

The importance of this rule lies in limitation. From the application to the mediation office until the date the final minutes are drawn up, the limitation period is suspended and preclusive periods do not run; because the date of the application to the office lacking jurisdiction is preserved, applying to the wrong office does not cause a loss of rights in terms of limitation.

But this protection depends on the **one-week period**. If no application is made to the office having jurisdiction within one week of service of the decision, the possibility of preserving the original application date is lost.

Upon the application made to it, the office having jurisdiction appoints a new mediator from its list.

## What Use Is Lack of Jurisdiction at the Litigation Stage?

A frequent question in practice: "the mediation was conducted before the wrong office — can I raise that in the action?"

As a rule, no. The Law prescribes a single procedure and a single moment for objecting to jurisdiction; if that route was not taken, an allegation of lack of jurisdiction is not heard at the litigation stage and the condition precedent is deemed satisfied.

This is in fact a protective provision: otherwise, the jurisdictional review of the mediation process would be repeated in every case, and the acceleration expected from the mandatory mediation system would never materialise.

## When Does an Objection to Jurisdiction Make Strategic Sense?

An objection to jurisdiction extends the process by at least one or two weeks and restarts the appointment of the mediator. In practice it therefore makes sense in two situations:

**Where the geographical burden is real.** A process conducted at an office at the other end of Türkiye significantly increases the cost of attendance. Since non-attendance carries a sanction, that cost is real.

**Where the contract contains a jurisdiction clause.** Since jurisdiction agreements between merchants and public legal persons are valid, an objection to an application made to an office other than the place agreed in the contract will succeed.

By contrast, a groundless objection raised merely to gain time may, while prolonging the process, also work against the party raising it: when the objection is rejected the process starts afresh, and throughout that period the debt remains due.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q: I applied to the wrong office — is my application invalid?

No. The mediator cannot raise lack of jurisdiction of their own motion; if the other party does not object at the first meeting, the process runs validly and satisfies the condition precedent. If an objection is raised and upheld, your original application date is preserved provided you apply to the office having jurisdiction within one week.

### Q: Can I appeal against the decision on the objection?

No. The civil court of peace determines the office having jurisdiction **finally**; there is no appeal or cassation against that decision.

### Q: Is a court fee payable for the objection?

No. The court conducts the examination on the file and without any fee.

### Q: Is the same mediator appointed after an objection?

If the objection is rejected, the same mediator is reappointed. If it is upheld, the office having jurisdiction appoints a new mediator from its own list.

### Q: If more than one place has jurisdiction, which should I choose?

Where the statute grants an option (for example, in employee claims, the employer's domicile and the place where the work is performed), the applicant may choose. Since this choice in practice also determines the jurisdiction of the court in the subsequent action, it should be made with the whole case in mind.
