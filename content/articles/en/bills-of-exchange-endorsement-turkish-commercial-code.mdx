TTK Art. 681-690 govern endorsement (ciro) — the characteristic method of transferring a bill of exchange under Turkish law, and one of the richest sources of case law in the entire negotiable instruments chapter.

## Negotiability by Default, and Blank Endorsement

Under Art. 681, a bill of exchange is transferable by endorsement even without an express "to order" clause; a drawer can reverse this by writing "not to order," forcing transfer through an ordinary assignment of claim instead — losing the protections endorsement provides. Endorsement must be unconditional (any condition is deemed unwritten) and can never be partial — a partial endorsement is void outright (Art. 682). It must be written on the bill itself or an attached slip and signed by the endorser; a "blank endorsement" naming no endorsee, consisting only of the endorser's signature, is expressly permitted (Art. 683). A holder under blank endorsement may fill in their own name, re-endorse blank or to a named party, or simply hand the bill to someone else without any further endorsement at all (Art. 684).

## Two Functions of Endorsement: Transfer and Guarantee

Endorsement transfers all rights under the bill along with delivery (Art. 684/1). It also functions as a guarantee: unless stated otherwise, every endorser is liable for non-acceptance and non-payment, just like the drawer — though an endorser can limit this by prohibiting further endorsement, remaining liable only to their immediate transferee (Art. 685).

## The Holder's Presumption of Title and Defenses

Art. 686 creates a critical procedural shortcut: whoever holds the bill is presumed the rightful holder if their claim traces through an unbroken, connected chain of endorsements — a debtor need not verify the authenticity of each endorser's signature. Turkish courts confirm that being the "formally entitled holder" is enough to pursue collection; being the "materially true owner" is not required. Art. 687 then bars a bill debtor from raising personal defenses arising from their direct relationship with the drawer or a prior holder against the current holder — unless the current holder knowingly acquired the bill to the debtor's detriment. This defense-cutoff is central to why bills function as reliable credit instruments.

## Collection and Pledge Endorsements

A "for collection" endorsement authorizes the holder to exercise rights under the bill without transferring ownership — such a holder cannot invoke the defense-cutoff of Art. 687 (Art. 688). A "pledge" endorsement creates a security interest; the holder can exercise all rights but may only further endorse "for collection" (Art. 689). Notably, Turkish courts have confirmed that pledge endorsement — unlike for bills and promissory notes — is not available for checks, since the check rules do not reference Art. 689.

## Practical Takeaway

A party taking a Turkish bill of exchange by endorsement should verify the chain of endorsements is unbroken and consecutive, since this formal chain — not proof of the true underlying owner — is what Turkish courts use to determine who may enforce the instrument.
