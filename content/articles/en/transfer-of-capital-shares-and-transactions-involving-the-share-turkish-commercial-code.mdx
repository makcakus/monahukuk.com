Unlike shares in a joint-stock company, a capital share in a Turkish limited liability company (limited şirket) can only change hands through the closed set of procedures in TTK Art. 593-600 — a stricter, more personal regime reflecting the intuitu personae character of the limited company.

## Form and Approval Requirements

Under Art. 595, a share transfer requires written form with notarized signatures, and — unless the articles of association provide otherwise — approval by the general assembly, which may refuse without giving reasons or even ban transfers altogether in the articles. If approval is refused or transfer is banned, the partner retains a right to withdraw for just cause. A request is deemed approved if the assembly does not reject it within three months.

## Inheritance, Marital Property, and Enforcement Transfers

Art. 596 treats involuntary transfers differently: shares passing by inheritance, marital property liquidation, or compulsory execution transfer automatically without assembly approval. The company may still refuse to recognize the new holder within three months of learning of the transfer, but only if it offers to buy the share at fair value for itself, a partner, or a third party it designates.

## Fair Value Disputes and Registration

Where the law or the articles reference "fair value" and the parties disagree, Art. 597 lets either side apply to the commercial court in the company's registered location for a binding (non-appealable) valuation. Art. 598 requires managers to register any share transfer with the trade registry within thirty days; a departing partner may otherwise apply directly to have their name removed, and good-faith reliance on the registry is protected.

## Practical Takeaway

Foreign investors acquiring shares in a Turkish limited company should expect a notarized transfer document plus a general assembly approval step as standard closing conditions — and should not assume that a signed transfer agreement alone completes the transaction.

Turkish courts have held that in two-partner companies, a notarized transfer agreement signed by both partners can itself substitute for a formal general assembly resolution, and that under the current Code, registration in the share ledger is only declaratory, not a condition of validity (unlike the old 1956 Code).

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How is a share transferred in a limited liability company?

In three stages. First, under TTK Article 595/1, the transfer agreement must be made <b>in writing</b>, with the parties' signatures <b>notarised</b>. Next, under Article 595/2, unless otherwise provided in the articles of association, <b>the approval of the general assembly of shareholders</b> must be obtained; the transfer becomes valid upon this approval. Finally, under Article 598, the managers apply to the trade registry for registration of the transfer, and the transfer is entered in the share ledger.

### Can the general assembly refuse a share transfer?

Yes. Under TTK Article 595/3, unless otherwise provided in the articles of association, the general assembly of shareholders may refuse approval <b>without stating a reason</b>. Under Article 595/4, the articles of association may even prohibit the transfer of the share entirely. However, under Article 595/5, if the articles have prohibited the transfer or the general assembly has refused approval, the shareholder's <b>right to withdraw from the company for good cause</b> remains reserved; the shareholder is not left locked in.

### What happens if the general assembly does not respond to the transfer request?

Approval is deemed to have been given. Under TTK Article 595/7, <b>if the general assembly does not refuse within three months of the application, it is deemed to have granted its approval</b>. This implied-approval rule prevents the transfer request from being left indefinitely pending. The period begins on the date of the application by which the transfer was submitted for approval; for this reason, it is important that the application be made in a demonstrable manner (notarial notice, registered mail with return receipt).
