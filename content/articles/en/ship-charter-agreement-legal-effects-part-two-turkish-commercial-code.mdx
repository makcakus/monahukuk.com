TTK Art. 1127-1130 close out the bareboat charter chapter with crew liability, payment security, and redelivery rules.

## Crew Employment Liability

Under Art. 1127, all obligations from employing crew fall on the charterer as a general rule. But if the ship was chartered *with* crew already aboard (a "manned bareboat" scenario), the lessor remains jointly and severally liable alongside the charterer for crew-related obligations — protecting seafarers, typically the weakest party, with a double avenue of recourse. Notably, this is a matter of personal liability allocation only: Turkish courts have clarified that crew wage claims also carry an independent maritime lien on the ship itself under separate provisions, so even a lessor not personally liable under Art. 1127 can still see the ship itself pursued for such claims.

## Getting Paid and Redelivery

Art. 1128 requires the charter fee to be paid monthly in advance and gives the lessor a three-layer security package: a lien over the charterer's movable property, a pledge over freight receivables owed to the charterer, and a further lien under Art. 1201 — though third-party debtors are only bound once notified of the pledge. Art. 1129 requires returning the ship as delivered, minus normal wear, and imposes an escalating late-redelivery penalty: ordinary charter rate for the first fifteen days of delay, double that rate afterward, unless the lessor proves higher actual damages.

## Falling Back on General Lease Law

Art. 1130 confirms that where this chapter is silent, Turkish Code of Obligations lease provisions apply by analogy, to the extent their nature allows — bareboat charter is not a freestanding contract type but a maritime-specific branch of general lease law.

## Practical Takeaway

For lessors, the crew-liability split under Art. 1127 is worth checking carefully before assuming a bareboat structure fully insulates the shipowner from crew claims — a maritime lien on the ship can still reach the vessel regardless of the personal liability allocation between lessor and charterer.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Who employs the crew under a ship charter?

The charterer. Under TTK Article 1127/1, <b>all debts and obligations arising from the employment of the crew belong to the charterer</b>. The obligations relating to wages, social security and occupational health rest with the charterer; the charterer is also liable to third parties for the fault of the crew under Article 1062. This is the fundamental distinction between a ship charter (bareboat charter) and a time charter.

### When is the hire payable under a ship charter?

Under TTK Article 1128/1, hire is payable <b>at the time agreed in the contract</b>; if there is no agreement on this, it is payable <b>monthly and in advance, starting from the day the possession of the vessel is transferred to the charterer under the terms of the agreement</b>. The rule of advance payment is intended to reduce the risk of non-collection in a contract under which the lessor transfers full actual control of the vessel.

### Is the charterer liable for wear and tear on the vessel?

Not for ordinary wear and tear. Under TTK Article 1129/1, at the end of the contract the charterer returns the vessel <b>in the condition in which it was received</b>; however, <b>the charterer is not liable for a deficiency, alteration or wear arising from a normal manner of use of the vessel and its equipment</b>. Damage exceeding ordinary use, or deficiencies arising from negligence or use contrary to the contract, however, fall within the charterer's liability.
