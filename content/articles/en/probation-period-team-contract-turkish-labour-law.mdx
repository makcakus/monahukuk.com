Turkish labour law gives employers and employees two distinct tools for structuring the beginning of an employment relationship: the probationary clause and the team contract. Both are addressed together in the Labour Code because they deal with how an employment relationship comes into being and how it may be tested before it becomes fully binding. For foreign employers hiring staff in Turkey — whether onboarding a single manager on a trial basis or bringing on a whole crew for a seasonal hospitality, construction or agricultural project through one intermediary — understanding exactly what these mechanisms do, and where their limits lie, is essential to avoiding unenforceable clauses and unexpected liability.

## What a Probationary Clause Actually Does

A probationary period is not automatic. Under Article 15 of the Labour Code, it only exists if the parties expressly agree to include it in the employment contract; without such an agreement, no trial period applies at all. Where the parties do agree to one, the clause does not change the underlying nature of the contract — a probationary employment agreement is still either a fixed-term or an indefinite-term contract, as described in [our overview of employment contract types](/articles/definition-and-types-of-employment-contract-turkish-labour-law). What the clause adds is a temporary, mutual right of easy exit: during the trial period, both the employer and the employee are testing whether the role, the workplace and the working relationship actually work for them.

The statutory ceiling on this period is strict. A probationary clause in an individual employment contract cannot exceed two months. A collective bargaining agreement may extend this to up to four months, but an individual contract on its own can never lawfully exceed the two-month limit — this cap is mandatory and cannot be stretched to the employee's disadvantage by private agreement. The clock only starts once the employee has actually begun working; periods during which the contract is suspended, such as sick leave or annual leave taken very early in the relationship, do not count toward the probationary period and effectively pause it.

## Termination Without Notice or Severance During Probation

The defining feature of a probationary contract is what happens if either side wants out during the trial period. Ordinarily, termination is governed by the notice-period regime set out in Article 17, discussed in more detail in [our article on term terminations and notice periods](/articles/notice-period-termination-turkish-labour-law), under which the length of required notice scales with the employee's seniority. During probation, that regime is set aside: either party may end the contract immediately, without observing any notice period and without paying severance or notice compensation.

This flexibility is deliberately narrow. It applies only within the probationary window itself — once the trial period expires, any subsequent termination reverts fully to the ordinary rules, meaning notice periods and, where applicable, severance pay obligations come back into play as if there had never been a trial period. It is also important not to overstate what the exemption covers: it relieves the parties only from the procedural and compensatory burden of termination. It does nothing to strip the employee of wages or other entitlements already earned for days actually worked during the probationary period — those remain fully due regardless of how or when the contract ends.

## Guarding Against Abuse of the Probationary Clause

Because a probationary clause exists to let the parties genuinely get to know one another, Turkish courts will not enforce one that is used for a different purpose — most commonly, to sidestep job-security protections or avoid notice and severance obligations that would otherwise apply to an existing employee. The Turkish Court of Cassation has addressed this directly: where an employee had, in practice, already been working at the same workplace for roughly five months before a new contract containing a probationary clause was signed, the court found that the employer had no legitimate legal interest in subjecting an employee who was already performing the job to a fresh trial period, and held the probationary clause invalid.

The practical takeaway is that a probationary clause only has genuine legal force at the start of a new working relationship, or where the employee's suitability for the role genuinely still needs to be assessed. Retrofitting a probationary clause onto what is really a continuation of an existing employment relationship will not achieve the intended effect, since courts look past the label in the contract to the substance of the underlying relationship.

## Team Contracts: Formation, Formalities and Parties

Article 16 of the Labour Code addresses a distinct hiring structure: the team contract. Here, a person designated as the team leader contracts with the employer on behalf of a group of workers who together make up the team. This mechanism appears most often in sectors where a large number of workers are engaged collectively through a single intermediary — seasonal agricultural work, construction and haulage being typical examples in the Turkish market.

Formation happens in two stages. First, a contract of employment arises directly between the team leader and the employer once the team contract is signed. Second, for every other worker named in the team contract, an individual employment contract with the employer only comes into existence once that particular worker actually starts work — not merely by virtue of being listed. The law also imposes a mandatory form requirement: a team contract must be made in writing regardless of the duration envisaged for the resulting employment contracts, and it must separately state the identity of each worker and the wage each is to receive. This formality exists precisely so that individual workers' rights do not become blurred or hidden behind the team leader.

## Wage Payment and the Team Leader's Limited Role

The Labour Code takes particular care to prevent the team leader from turning into an intermediary who controls, or skims from, the other workers' pay. Once an employment contract comes into existence for a worker through actually starting work, that worker's wages must be paid directly and separately by the employer (or the employer's representative) — not funneled through the team leader. The team leader is expressly prohibited from making deductions from the other workers' wages for arranging the work or on any similar pretext.

In practice, disputes often arise where only the team leader has signed anything in writing with the employer, leaving the other workers with no direct paper trail. Once such a worker has actually started work, the terms set out in the team contract are treated as constituting a direct employment contract between that worker and the employer, regardless of how informal the arrangement looked on the ground. Termination of employment contracts formed through a team contract is not subject to any special regime: the same general rules on job security, notice and severance that apply to ordinary employment relationships, covered in [our article on job security and reinstatement claims](/articles/job-security-reinstatement-turkish-labour-law), apply equally here. The team contract only affects how the relationship is formed, not how it ends.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can we agree to a probationary period longer than two months in an individual employment contract?**
No. Two months is the absolute ceiling for an individual contract. A longer probationary period is only possible where it is provided for in a collective bargaining agreement, which can extend it to up to four months.

**If we dismiss an employee during probation, do we owe severance or notice pay?**
No. Termination during the probationary period may be carried out by either party without notice and without severance or notice compensation. Wages and other entitlements for days already worked remain due regardless.

**Can we put a new employee who has already been working for us informally on a probationary contract?**
This is risky. Turkish courts have invalidated probationary clauses added to contracts with workers who were already performing the same role beforehand, on the basis that the employer has no genuine interest in "testing" someone already doing the job.

**As a team leader, can I deduct a fee from my team members' wages for arranging their employment?**
No. The Labour Code expressly prohibits the team leader from deducting anything from other team members' wages for intermediation or similar reasons. Wages must be paid directly by the employer to each worker.
