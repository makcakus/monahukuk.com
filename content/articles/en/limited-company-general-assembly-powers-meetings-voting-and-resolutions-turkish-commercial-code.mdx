TTK Art. 616-622 govern the general assembly, one of the two mandatory organs of a Turkish limited company, opening the chapter on company organs.

## Non-Delegable Powers

Art. 616 lists powers that can never be delegated to managers: amending the articles, appointing and removing managers and auditors, approving financial statements, deciding on profit distribution, setting manager compensation, approving share transfers, applying to court to expel a partner, authorizing the company to acquire its own shares, and dissolving the company. A second, narrower list becomes non-delegable only if the articles so provide. Turkish courts consistently treat these as procedural prerequisites — a lawsuit seeking, say, expulsion of a partner or payment of profit shares fails if the underlying general assembly resolution was never adopted. A sole partner exercises all assembly powers alone, but resolutions must still be in writing. **Important exception for two-partner companies:** by decision of 25 December 2025 (E: 2025/128, K: 2025/273) the Constitutional Court annulled Art. 616(1)(h) and the words "applying to court for the expulsion of a partner for just cause…" in Art. 621(1)(h) **as they apply to two-partner limited companies**, on the ground that the vote of the partner whose expulsion is sought makes such a resolution practically impossible and thereby disproportionately restricts access to justice. In a two-partner limited company a prior general assembly resolution is therefore **no longer a prerequisite** for an expulsion claim; the requirement continues to apply to companies with more than two partners.

## Meetings and Voting

Managers call the assembly; the annual meeting occurs within three months of the fiscal year-end, with at least fifteen days' notice (adjustable by the articles between ten days and longer). Joint-stock company rules on calling procedures, minority rights, and unannounced ("universal") meetings apply by analogy (Art. 617) — meaning a meeting can be valid without formal notice if all partners attend without objection. Voting power is generally tied to the nominal value of shares, with each 25 Turkish Lira giving one vote unless the articles set a different threshold (Art. 618); certain matters — auditor elections, special audits, and liability lawsuits — always use value-based voting regardless of what the articles say. Partners are excluded from voting on conflicted matters: discharging their own management conduct, the company's acquisition of their own shares, or approving their own breach of loyalty or non-competition duties (Art. 619).

## Resolution Majorities and Challenges

Ordinary resolutions pass by simple majority of votes represented (Art. 620). Nine categories of major decisions — changing the business purpose, creating privileged voting shares, restricting share transfers, increasing capital, relocating the registered office, expelling a partner, and dissolution among them — require both a two-thirds majority of votes represented and an absolute majority of voting capital (Art. 621). Nullity and annulment of resolutions follow the joint-stock company regime by reference (Art. 622), including the three-month deadline for annulment actions; resolutions passed in violation of a mandatory voting exclusion are treated as void rather than merely voidable, and courts must examine this on their own motion regardless of any filing deadline.

## Practical Takeaway

Before initiating litigation over manager removal, profit claims, or partner expulsion in a Turkish limited company, confirm that the required general assembly resolution was properly adopted first — Turkish courts routinely dismiss such claims for lack of this procedural prerequisite. The exception is expulsion in a **two-partner** company, where the Constitutional Court's decision of 25 December 2025 (E: 2025/128, K: 2025/273) removed that requirement.
