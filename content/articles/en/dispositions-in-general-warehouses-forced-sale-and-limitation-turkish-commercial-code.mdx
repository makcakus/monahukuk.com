TTK Art. 841-849 complete the general warehouse regime introduced by Art. 832-840 by governing what happens once the paired warehouse receipt and warrant have already changed hands — how the underlying goods are protected, how an unpaid warrant holder collects, and what happens if a document is lost.

## Goods Are Protected From Outside Seizure

Art. 841 bars third-party creditors from directly seizing, attaching, or pledging goods held under this system (except in cases of loss of the instruments, inheritance, or bankruptcy) — creditors must instead go after the instruments a debtor holds, not the goods themselves. A receipt-holder separated from the warrant can redeem the goods early by prepaying the secured debt plus interest through maturity (Art. 842), and can redeem in partial lots for fungible goods by paying a proportional amount under the warehouse's supervision (Art. 843).

## Forced Sale and Its Proceeds

If the secured debt goes unpaid at maturity, the warrant holder — after protesting the instrument — can force a sale of the goods ten days later under pledge-enforcement rules, and Art. 841's seizure ban does not block this internal remedy (Art. 844). Sale proceeds are distributed in a fixed order: customs duties, taxes, and the warehouse's own storage costs and fees come first; the secured debt comes second; any surplus goes to the receipt holder as the residual owner (Art. 845). A shortfall gives the warrant holder recourse against the debtor or endorsers generally, though failing to protest or to attempt a timely sale forfeits rights against endorsers (though not against the primary debtor) (Art. 846). Insured goods that are lost or damaged let the warrant holder collect directly from the insurance proceeds instead (Art. 847).

## Limitation and Lost Instruments

Claims under this regime follow the same limitation periods as bills of exchange, except that the clock for claims against endorsers runs from the date of sale rather than protest or maturity (Art. 848). A holder who loses a warehouse receipt or warrant can obtain a court-supervised duplicate — proving ownership, posting security, and publishing notice — rather than relying on the bill-cancellation procedure used elsewhere in the Code (Art. 849).

## Practical Takeaway

For a lender relying on a Turkish warrant as collateral, the forced-sale mechanism under Art. 844-845 gives a genuinely self-executing remedy — but only if the protest and sale timeline is followed precisely, since procedural lapses cost recourse against endorsers even where the primary claim survives.
