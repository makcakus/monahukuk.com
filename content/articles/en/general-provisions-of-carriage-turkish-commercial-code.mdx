TTK Art. 850-855 open Book Four of the Commercial Code, "Carriage of Goods and Persons," and function as the general framework that governs every carriage relationship not carved out by a more specific regime — in practice, this makes Book Four the default rulebook for domestic road transport in Turkey.

## Who Is a Carrier, and Who Is Covered

Under Art. 850, a carrier is anyone who undertakes to move goods or passengers to a destination in exchange for freight, and carriage is automatically classified as commercial business activity. Art. 851 extends the Book's protections even to someone who carries only casually, as a one-off — a sender does not need to verify that the other side runs a licensed transport business to rely on these rules.

## What Falls Outside This Book

Art. 852 preserves the special regimes governing sea, rail, air, and postal carriage, meaning Book Four functions mainly as the road-transport rulebook domestically; internationally, the CMR Convention on road carriage displaces it entirely for cross-border shipments Turkey is party to. Art. 853 closes an obvious loophole: a carrier cannot subcontract part of a shipment to an operator under one of those special regimes and then invoke that regime's more limited liability against its own customer.

## Liability Cannot Be Waived

The clearest consumer- and shipper-protective rule in this part is Art. 854: any contract clause, general terms, ticket, tariff, or similar document that reduces or eliminates the liability the Code assigns to a carrier, forwarding agent, or state-licensed transport operator is void — regardless of how it is dressed up. Turkish courts have consistently treated this as mandatory law, striking down even routine liability caps printed on the back of an invoice.

## Limitation Periods

Art. 855 sets the limitation clock for the whole of Book Four: ten years for claims arising from a passenger's death or bodily injury, one year for other losses (extended to three years for willful or reckless conduct), running from delivery or the date delivery should have occurred.

## Practical Takeaway

A foreign shipper or carrier operating in Turkey should assume that any liability-reduction clause in a standard transport contract is unenforceable, and should confirm early whether a shipment falls under Book Four, a sector-specific regime, or an international convention like CMR — the answer changes both the applicable law and the limitation period.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the limitation period in carriage?

Under TTK Article 855/1, <b>where a passenger dies as a result of an accident or suffers injury to their bodily integrity, claims are time-barred after ten years; in the case of other losses, after one year</b>. Under Article 855/5, where the loss was caused by an act of the carrier committed <b>with intent, or recklessly and with knowledge that the loss would probably result</b>, liability is time-barred after <b>three years</b>.

### When does the limitation period start to run?

Under TTK Article 855/2 the period runs from <b>delivery of the goods to the consignee in the carriage of goods</b> and from <b>the passenger's arrival at the destination in the carriage of passengers</b>. If the goods have been totally lost or the passenger has not reached the destination, the period runs from <b>the date on which the goods should have been delivered and the passenger should have arrived</b>. In addition, under Article 855/4, a consignor or consignee who has duly asserted their rights within one year may raise them <b>by way of defence at any time</b>.

### Can the carrier's liability be limited by contract?

No. Under TTK Article 854/1, <b>all contractual provisions that have the effect of mitigating or removing in advance the liabilities imposed by the Code on the carrier, the freight forwarder and transport undertakings whose activities are subject to State authorisation are invalid.</b> Moreover, under Article 853, a carrier who has the work performed by an organisation subject to special rules, such as sea, rail or air transport, may not thereby claim a mitigation of its own liability.
