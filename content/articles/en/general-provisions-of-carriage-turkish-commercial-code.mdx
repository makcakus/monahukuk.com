TTK Art. 850-855 open Book Four of the Commercial Code, "Carriage of Goods and Persons," and function as the general framework that governs every carriage relationship not carved out by a more specific regime — in practice, this makes Book Four the default rulebook for domestic road transport in Turkey.

## Who Is a Carrier, and Who Is Covered

Under Art. 850, a carrier is anyone who undertakes to move goods or passengers to a destination in exchange for freight, and carriage is automatically classified as commercial business activity. Art. 851 extends the Book's protections even to someone who carries only casually, as a one-off — a gönderer does not need to verify that the other side runs a licensed transport business to rely on these rules.

## What Falls Outside This Book

Art. 852 preserves the special regimes governing sea, rail, air, and postal carriage, meaning Book Four functions mainly as the road-transport rulebook domestically; internationally, the CMR Convention on road carriage displaces it entirely for cross-border shipments Turkey is party to. Art. 853 closes an obvious loophole: a carrier cannot subcontract part of a shipment to an operator under one of those special regimes and then invoke that regime's more limited liability against its own customer.

## Liability Cannot Be Waived

The clearest consumer- and shipper-protective rule in this part is Art. 854: any contract clause, general terms, ticket, tariff, or similar document that reduces or eliminates the liability the Code assigns to a carrier, forwarding agent, or state-licensed transport operator is void — regardless of how it is dressed up. Turkish courts have consistently treated this as mandatory law, striking down even routine liability caps printed on the back of an invoice.

## Limitation Periods

Art. 855 sets the limitation clock for the whole of Book Four: ten years for claims arising from a passenger's death or bodily injury, one year for other losses (extended to three years for willful or reckless conduct), running from delivery or the date delivery should have occurred.

## Practical Takeaway

A foreign shipper or carrier operating in Turkey should assume that any liability-reduction clause in a standard transport contract is unenforceable, and should confirm early whether a shipment falls under Book Four, a sector-specific regime, or an international convention like CMR — the answer changes both the applicable law and the limitation period.
