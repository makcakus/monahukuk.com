A general partnership acquires legal personality upon registration with the trade registry, and any contrary contractual arrangement has no effect against third parties (TTK Art. 232). TTK Art. 232-242 then define how the partnership and its partners stand toward outsiders — chiefly through representation authority and the partners' unlimited joint liability.

## Representation Authority

Under Art. 233, whoever holds representation authority may bind the partnership in any matter within its business purpose, and internal contractual limits on that authority cannot be raised against a good-faith third party — a direct application of the registry's reliance principle. The one exception that does bind outsiders is a joint-signature requirement, but only if it has been duly registered and published. Acts performed by an authorized representative, including torts committed while carrying out partnership duties, bind the partnership directly under Art. 234. For just cause, a court may withdraw a partner's representation authority on another partner's application, appointing a custodian if urgent (Art. 235).

## Unlimited Liability and Its Sequencing

The partnership's defining trait is unlimited, joint personal liability: every partner — including one who joins after a debt arose — answers for partnership debts with their entire personal estate, and contrary agreements have no effect against outsiders (Art. 236). This liability is subsidiary rather than immediate: creditors must first pursue the partnership itself, and may turn to a partner's personal assets only once enforcement against the partnership has proven fruitless or the partnership has dissolved (Art. 237) — though creditors may still seek a precautionary attachment on personal assets before that point. The same sequencing applies to enforcing a court judgment obtained solely against the partnership (Art. 238), and Turkish courts have confirmed this rule is specific to general partnerships and cannot be applied by analogy to joint-stock company shareholders.

## Bankruptcy and Set-Off

If the partnership becomes bankrupt, partnership creditors are satisfied before the partners' personal creditors may reach partnership assets (Art. 239), and a partner's bankruptcy does not automatically trigger the partnership's own bankruptcy unless a capital call goes unpaid (Art. 240). Set-off between a third party's debt to the partnership and a claim against an individual partner is generally barred (Art. 242), preserving the separation between partnership and personal estates until a partner becomes personally pursuable under Art. 237.

For anyone extending credit to or dealing with a Turkish general partnership, the practical point is that recovery from individual partners is not automatic — it depends on first exhausting recourse against the partnership itself.
