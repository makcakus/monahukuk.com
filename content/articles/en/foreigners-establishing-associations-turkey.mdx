In cities with a large foreign population such as Antalya, it is natural for people from the same country to wish to come together and organise for cultural, social, or mutual support purposes. The legal vehicle for that wish is the association. Law No. 5253 on Associations recognises the right to found an association broadly; but two different regimes apply to structures containing a foreign element, and confusing them produces serious consequences.

## The General Framework of the Right to Found an Association

Article 3 of Law No. 5253 on Associations lays down the basic rule: **natural or legal persons having legal capacity have the right to found associations without obtaining prior permission.**

The wording of the article is striking. The law does not say "Turkish citizens"; it says **"natural or legal persons having legal capacity"**. Nationality does not appear in the text of the article as a criterion of distinction. Nor is any permission system provided for; founding an association is subject to notification, not to permission.

The second paragraph of the article reserves an exception: restrictions imposed by their own special laws on members of the Turkish Armed Forces and law enforcement forces, and on officials of public institutions and organisations holding civil servant status, are reserved.

The paragraph added by Law No. 7262 of 27/12/2020 imposes a prohibition on holding office in the organs of an association: even if the periods specified in Article 53 of the Turkish Penal Code No. 5237 have elapsed or the sentence has been subject to an amnesty, **persons convicted of offences within the scope of Law No. 6415 on the Prevention of the Financing of Terrorism, or of the offences of manufacturing and trafficking in narcotic or stimulant substances or of laundering assets derived from crime under the Turkish Penal Code**, may not hold office in the organs of associations other than the general assembly. The office of persons convicted of such offences after being elected to an association's organs comes to an end. This provision does not apply where a decision restoring prohibited rights has been given.

## The Critical Distinction: An Association Founded in Türkiye, or a Foreign Association?

This is the most important distinction in practice, and the two situations are subject to entirely different regimes.

**The first situation:** an association is founded in Türkiye under Turkish law and its founders include foreigners. This is subject to the general regime in Article 3 — no prior permission is required.

**The second situation:** an association founded abroad wishes to carry on activity or open a branch in Türkiye. This is an entirely different regime.

Article 5 of the Law governs the second situation: **foreign associations may, with the permission of the Ministry of Interior obtained after taking the opinion of the Ministry of Foreign Affairs**, carry on activity or cooperate in Türkiye, open a representative office or branch, found an association or umbrella organisation, or join an existing association or umbrella organisation.

Three elements are required together here: **the opinion of the Ministry of Foreign Affairs**, **the permission of the Ministry of Interior**, and the completion of both steps *before* any activity. The permission that is not required for an association founded in Türkiye is mandatory for a foreign association.

The same article also governs associations in Türkiye reaching outwards: associations may, in order to achieve the objectives set out in their statutes, engage in international activity or cooperation, open a representative office or branch abroad, found an association or umbrella organisation abroad, or join associations or organisations founded abroad.

## The Sanction for Activity Without Permission

Subparagraph (g) of Article 32 of Law No. 5253 attaches a sanction to breaches of the permission regime: **those who, without the permission of the competent authorities, open representative offices or branches in Türkiye of foreign associations and of non-profit organisations headquartered abroad, carry on their activities, cooperate with them, or admit them as members, are given an administrative fine of one thousand Turkish lira, and the closure of the branch or representative office opened without permission is also ordered.**

The scope of the subparagraph is broad and calls for care. The penalty applies not only to the person who opens the branch but also to **those who carry on its activities, those who cooperate with it, and those who admit it as a member**. An association in Türkiye cooperating with a foreign association operating without permission may therefore face a sanction as well. Moreover the sanction is not limited to a fine; a **closure** order is also given.

The amount in the article is the nominal figure in the text of the Law; because administrative fines are updated at the beginning of each calendar year by the revaluation rate, the current amount should be confirmed against the tariff in force at the date of application.

## Children's Associations: Three Tiers by Age

Article 3 of the Law establishes a detailed scheme for minors:

**Minors who have completed the age of fifteen and are capable of discernment** may, **with the written permission of their legal representatives**, found children's associations or become members of existing children's associations, for the purpose of protecting and developing their social, mental, moral, physical, and intellectual capacities, their rights to sport, education and training, their social and cultural existence, their family structure, and their private lives.

**Minors who have completed the age of twelve** may become members of children's associations with the permission of their legal representatives, **but may not hold office on management or supervisory boards.**

**Persons over the age of eighteen** may not be founders or members of children's associations.

This scheme applies directly to sports or cultural associations in which the children of foreign families will take part. For foreign parents, the legal representative's written permission will most often require a notarised and translated document.

## What the Legal Capacity Condition Means for Foreigners

The requirement in Article 3 of "having legal capacity" is, for foreigners, assessed under Article 9 of Law No. 5718 on Private International Law and Procedural Law. The first paragraph of that article is clear: **legal capacity and capacity to act are governed by the person's national law.** The practical consequence is this: because the age of majority varies from country to country, a foreigner who is to be a founder must have capacity to act under their own national law.

The second paragraph of the same article introduces a measure of flexibility: a person who lacks capacity under their national law is nonetheless bound by the legal transaction they have concluded **if they have capacity under the law of the country in which the transaction was made.** Transactions concerning family law and the law of succession, and those concerning rights in rem over immovable property situated in another country, fall outside that provision. For founders that are legal persons, the fourth paragraph applies: the legal capacity and capacity to act of legal persons are governed by the law of the administrative centre stated in their statute; however, Turkish law may be applied where the actual administrative centre is in Türkiye.

This takes on further importance where the founding process is to be conducted through [representation under a power of attorney](/en/articles/power-of-attorney-turkey-foreigners); the validity of the power of attorney as regards capacity is assessed by the same criterion.

## Does Founding an Association Confer a Residence Permit?

No. Founding an association or becoming a member of one does not give rise to a right of stay under Law No. 6458. Activity in an association is not among the grounds enumerated in the law for a [short-term residence permit](/en/articles/short-term-residence-permit-turkey-guide).

In addition, if paid work is to be carried out within the association, the [work permit](/en/articles/work-permit-application-process-turkey) regime comes into play; membership or office in an association does not on its own confer a right to work. Overlooking this distinction gives rise to sanctions for both the foreigner and the association.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I found an association in Türkiye as a foreigner?

Article 3 of Law No. 5253 recognises the right to found an association for "natural or legal persons having legal capacity" and draws no distinction on the basis of nationality. No prior permission is required for an association to be founded in Türkiye under Turkish law.

### Can I open a Turkish branch of the association in my own country?

That is a separate regime and is subject to permission. Under Article 5 of the Law, foreign associations may open a branch or representative office in Türkiye with the permission of the Ministry of Interior obtained after taking the opinion of the Ministry of Foreign Affairs. A branch opened without permission is closed and an administrative fine is imposed.

### What happens if I cooperate with a foreign association operating without permission?

Subparagraph (g) of Article 32 provides that an administrative fine applies not only to those carrying on activity without permission but also to **those who cooperate with them and those who admit them as members**. Liability is not confined to the person who opens the branch.

### If I found an association, can I obtain a residence permit?

No. Founding an association or becoming a member does not constitute a ground for a residence permit under Law No. 6458. A residence permit must be obtained separately and on its own conditions.
