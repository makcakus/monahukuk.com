TTK Art. 1054-1058 extend Turkey's ship mortgage regime to newbuildings — vessels still under construction at a shipyard, not yet launched.

## When the Mortgage Can Attach

A mortgage over a ship under construction can only attach once the keel has been laid and the hull carries a visible, permanent name and number distinguishing it from other projects at the yard (Art. 1054(2)). This threshold matters: the law wants the mortgaged object to be a concrete, identifiable thing that third parties checking the registry can actually locate at the yard, not an abstract design. Vessels that will end up under 18 gross tons once complete can never be mortgaged at this stage either — consistent with the general tonnage floor for ship registration.

## Formation and Scope

Formation mirrors the ordinary ship mortgage: a notarized written agreement (or one executed at the registry office) plus registration — here, on the special registry for ships under construction (Art. 1055). The scope, however, is deliberately broader than for a completed vessel: the mortgage covers not just parts already installed, but also materials sitting at the yard that are earmarked for the build (Art. 1056). This matters because an unfinished hull's standalone value is often minimal — the real economic value sits in the steel, engines, and equipment waiting to be fitted, so limiting the mortgage to installed parts would badly undervalue the lender's actual security. Insurance proceeds, by contrast, are *not* automatically covered here (unlike for completed ships under Art. 1022) — they're included only if the owner has separately arranged insurance, reflecting the more variable, project-specific nature of construction-phase risk.

## Rank Preservation on Completion

The capstone rule, Art. 1057, is simple but critical: when construction finishes and the vessel transfers to the ordinary ship registry, the mortgage keeps its original rank. Without this, a lender financing the riskiest phase of a project could lose priority to later creditors during the gap before re-registration — which would gut the commercial value of financing construction in the first place.

## Practical Takeaway

Turkish courts treat mortgages over multiple newbuildings securing a single loan as a "collective mortgage," meaning the lender generally must enforce against all mortgaged vessels together rather than picking and choosing — a structuring point worth flagging in multi-hull newbuilding finance.
