*TCC ARTICLE 5/A – (Added: 6/12/2018-7155/20)*

*(1) Among the commercial actions specified in Article 4 of this Code and in other laws, in actions for payment, damages, removal of objection, negative declaration and restitution whose subject matter is a sum of money, having applied to a mediator before bringing the action is a condition of action.*

*(2) The mediator concludes the application within six weeks of the date of appointment. In compulsory circumstances this period may be extended by the mediator by a maximum of two weeks.*

Mandatory mediation in commercial disputes was introduced by Article 5/A added to the Turkish Commercial Code by Law No. 7155 of 6/12/2018 and entered into force on **1 January 2019**. Coming a year after the practice in labour disputes, this arrangement made a significant portion of commercial claim actions subject to a pre-litigation negotiation stage.

The current form of the provision is the product of the 2023 amendment made by Law No. 7445, and its scope has expanded markedly. That expansion means the legislature deliberately went beyond limits previously drawn by the Court of Cassation's case law.

## Scope: Which Actions?

Article 5/A requires two criteria to be met **together**.

**First criterion — being a commercial action.** The dispute must be among the commercial actions specified in Article 4 TCC or in other laws. Article 4 TCC envisages three groups: **absolute commercial actions** arising from matters regulated in the TCC regardless of the parties' capacity; **relative commercial actions** arising from matters relating to the commercial enterprises of both parties where both are merchants; and actions deemed commercial by special laws. Determining whether a dispute is commercial in nature is at the same time determining whether the mediation condition applies.

**Second criterion — type of action.** Before the 2023 amendment, the provision covered only "claims for payment and damages whose subject matter is the payment of a sum of money". The present text lists five types of action: **payment, damages, removal of objection, negative declaration and restitution**.

Commercial actions not on that list fall outside mandatory mediation. Actions for annulment of a general meeting resolution in company law, actions for dissolution and liquidation, actions for the determination and prevention of unfair competition, and prevention of trade mark infringement — claims whose relief is not the payment of money — are outside the scope. Where a claim contains both a monetary and another form of relief, the condition is regarded as required in respect of the monetary claim.

## The Negative-Declaration Debate and the Legislature's Intervention

Under the original version of the provision, the most debated question was whether negative declaratory actions fell within scope. In a 2021 decision, the 11th Civil Chamber of the Court of Cassation stated that including negative declaratory actions within Article 5/A TCC merely because they are commercial actions, and compelling a person wishing to bring such an action to apply to a mediator first, was contrary to law, and that a purposive reading of the provision also led to the conclusion that the legislature had deliberately left negative declaratory actions outside the scope.

In 2023 the legislature made the opposite choice to that case law and expressly added actions for removal of objection, negative declaration and restitution to the text. The transitional regime is, however, specially regulated: under Provisional Article 1 of Law No. 7445, the provision added to the first paragraph of Article 5/A TCC concerning negative declaratory and restitution actions applies to **actions brought on or after 1/9/2023**. In negative declaratory actions brought before that date, the mediation condition is not required.

The same transitional rule applies to the negative declaratory and restitution actions added to Article 3 of Law No. 7036; the same date is taken as the basis in labour disputes.

## Time Limit: Six Weeks in Commercial Disputes

Article 5/A/2 provides a period specific to commercial disputes: the mediator concludes the application within **six weeks** of the date of appointment; in compulsory circumstances this may be extended by a maximum of **two weeks**.

This is a special provision departing from the general rule of three weeks plus one week in Article 18/A/9 of Law No. 6325, and it is an application of the rule in Article 18/A/19 that "special provisions accepted in the relevant laws concerning mediation as a condition of action are reserved". The longer period is justified by the fact that commercial disputes often require examination of current accounts, comparison of ledger entries and institutional approval mechanisms.

## The Rest of the Procedure: Article 18/A of Law No. 6325

Article 5/A regulates only scope and time; the rest of the procedure is subject to Article 18/A of Law No. 6325. Accordingly, the application is made to the mediation bureau at the place of the competent court; the mediator is designated by the bureau from the list; an objection to jurisdiction must be raised at the latest at the first meeting; a party who fails without excuse to attend the first meeting is liable for half of the litigation costs and half of the attorney's fee determined under the Minimum Attorney's Fee Tariff; and from the application to the bureau until the date the final minutes are drawn up, limitation is suspended and no forfeiture period runs.

As regards the settlement document, there is a facility specific to commercial disputes: under Article 18/4 of Law No. 6325, in commercial disputes a settlement document signed jointly by **the lawyers and the mediator** is deemed a document having the character of a judgment without the parties' personal signature and without an annotation of enforceability.

## The Strictness of the Condition of Action

In commercial disputes too, mandatory mediation is a special condition of action that cannot be completed later. The 11th Civil Chamber consistently holds that applying to mediation after the action has been brought has no effect on the merits and that the action must be dismissed on procedural grounds. The same Chamber has stated that the subsequently completable conditions of action cannot be examined before the mediation condition is met, and that a procedural dismissal must be given without even the need to complete the court fee.

The requirement to attach **the original of the final minutes or a copy certified by the mediator** to the statement of claim applies equally in commercial actions; a photocopy is not sufficient.

Where a commercial contract contains an arbitration clause, Article 18/A/18 of Law No. 6325 comes into play: where there is an arbitration agreement, the provisions on mediation as a condition of action do not apply.

## Case Law of the Court of Cassation

The following decisions show the scope and application of Article 5/A TCC.

**1. Court of Cassation, 11th Civil Chamber, Case No. 2020/3235, Decision No. 2021/5629, dated 21.09.2021**

Taking the text as it then stood, the Chamber stated that including negative declaratory actions within Article 5/A TCC merely because they are commercial actions, and compelling a person wishing to bring an action to apply to a mediator, was contrary to law, and that a purposive reading of the provision led to the conclusion that the legislature had deliberately left negative declaratory actions outside the scope. That case law lost its validity with the amendment made by Law No. 7445, as regards actions brought on or after 1/9/2023.

**2. Court of Cassation, 11th Civil Chamber, Case No. 2020/5495, Decision No. 2022/1020, dated 11.02.2022**

The decision establishes that the dispute was an absolute commercial action under Article 4/1(a) TCC, that under Article 5/A TCC having applied to a mediator before bringing the action is to be taken into account as a condition of action, and that under Article 18/A/2 of Law No. 6325 the claimant must attach to the statement of claim the original of the final minutes recording that no settlement was reached, or a certified copy.

**3. Court of Cassation, 11th Civil Chamber, Case No. 2025/2809, Decision No. 2025/7367, dated 08.12.2025**

On the ground that mediation, made compulsory in commercial actions by Law No. 7155, is **not a completable condition of action**, the Chamber found it correct that, where the mediation condition had not been fulfilled at the date the action was brought, the action was dismissed on procedural grounds for lack of a condition of action.

**4. Court of Cassation, 23rd Civil Chamber, Case No. 2020/1943, Decision No. 2020/4052, dated 04.12.2020**

The decision sets out the temporal starting point of the provision, stating that under Article 5/A, added to the TCC by Law No. 7155 and in force since 01.01.2019, applying to a mediator before bringing an action is a condition of action for claims in the nature of a commercial action.

**5. Court of Cassation, 11th Civil Chamber, Case No. 2020/3244, Decision No. 2021/5698, dated 23.09.2021 (by majority)**

In a negative declaratory action arising from a bill of exchange, the Chamber held by majority that applying to mediation is not compulsory in commercial negative declaratory actions and reversed the procedural dismissal. The **dissenting opinion** stated that Article 5/A concerns not the type of action but the **subject matter of the action**, and that negative declaratory actions too relate, by their subject matter, to the collection of a claim. With the 2023 amendment the legislature adopted the approach of the dissent and expressly added negative declaratory actions to the text.
