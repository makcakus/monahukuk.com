Registered shares transfer freely by default, but TTK Art. 491-501 set out the two sources of restriction the Code allows — one statutory, one articles-based — along with the refusal grounds, timelines, and the share ledger that ultimately determines who counts as a shareholder toward the company.

## The One Statutory Restriction

Art. 491 requires company approval to transfer registered shares that are not yet fully paid — except where the transfer happens through inheritance, estate division, marital property rules, or compulsory execution. The company may refuse approval only where the transferee's solvency is doubtful and requested security has not been provided. Turkish courts have treated this as the sole statutory exception to free transferability and have required approval whenever the transferee is solvent and acting in good faith — the mere fact that a share is unpaid does not itself justify refusal.

## Articles-Based Restriction: The General Framework

Art. 492 lets the articles condition registered share transfers (and usufruct grants over them) on company approval, but Art. 492(3) lifts all such restrictions once the company enters liquidation, since protecting the shareholder circle no longer serves any purpose. Turkish courts have struck down articles clauses that use this mechanism to make transfer effectively impossible — for instance, requiring majority shareholder consent — as exceeding what Art. 493 permits.

## Refusal Grounds: Unlisted Shares Versus Listed Shares

For unlisted companies, Art. 493 allows refusal on two grounds: an articles-specified "important reason" concerning the shareholder base composition, business purpose, or the company's economic independence, or the "escape clause" — offering to buy the shares from the transferor at true value. Art. 493(7) bars the articles from making transfer conditions any harder than these two channels, and Turkish courts have struck down blanket board discretion to refuse without any stated reason. Failing to reject within three months, or rejecting without justification, results in deemed approval (Art. 494).

Listed shares follow a different, faster-moving regime built for market speed rather than case-by-case scrutiny: the sole refusal ground is a percentage acquisition ceiling specified in the articles (Art. 495), the Central Registry Agency reports the transaction to the company (Art. 496), rights transfer automatically with a stock-exchange acquisition (Art. 497), and the company has only twenty days — not three months — to reject before the transferee is deemed recognized (Art. 498).

## The Share Ledger's Role

Art. 499(4) makes the ledger determinative: only the person recorded there counts as shareholder toward the company, even though delivery and endorsement complete the transfer between the parties themselves — Turkish courts describe ledger entry as having declarative, not constitutive, effect. Art. 501 ties this back to unpaid shares: registration obligates the acquirer to pay the balance, while the transferor is released from that debt upon registration, except in a narrow two-year post-formation insolvency scenario.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can registered shares be transferred freely?

As a rule, yes. Under TTK Article 490, unless otherwise provided by law or the articles of association, registered shares <b>may be transferred without being subject to any restriction</b>. There are two exceptions: <b>statutory restriction</b> (Article 491) — shares not yet fully paid up may be transferred only with the company's consent — and <b>restriction by the articles of association</b> (Article 492), under which the articles may provide that shares may be transferred only with the company's consent.

### On what grounds may the company refuse a share transfer?

It depends on whether the shares are listed on an exchange. For shares <b>not listed</b>, under TTK Article 493/1 the company may refuse either by <b>invoking an important cause provided for in the articles of association</b>, or by offering to acquire the shares from the transferor, for its own account or that of others, <b>at their true value at the time of the request</b>. For <b>listed</b> shares, under Article 495 registration can be refused only on the ground that the <b>percentage acquisition limit</b> set out in the articles has been exceeded.

### Does the transfer take effect if the company does not respond?

Yes. Under TTK Articles 494 and 498, if the company does not refuse within <b>three months</b> of receiving the request for consent, or if its refusal is unjustified, <b>consent is deemed to have been given</b>. This rule of implied consent prevents a transfer request from being left pending indefinitely. Until consent is given, ownership of the shares and the rights arising from them remain with the transferor.
