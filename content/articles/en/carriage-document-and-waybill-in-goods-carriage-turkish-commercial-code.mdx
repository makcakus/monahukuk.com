TTK Art. 856-861 govern how a Turkish goods-carriage contract gets documented — and clarify a point that surprises many foreign shippers: the consignment note is not required to form a valid contract, but it becomes powerful evidence once both sides sign it.

## The Note Is Optional, Not Constitutive

Under Art. 856, a carriage contract forms through the parties' matching intent alone; handing goods to a carrier already raises a presumption that a carriage contract exists, with or without paperwork. If either party wants a note, it is issued in three originals — one for the sender, one traveling with the goods, one retained by the carrier — and must cover the content items listed in Art. 857: party identities, pickup and delivery details, description and marking of the goods, freight and charges, and any customs instructions.

## Evidentiary Weight and the Carrier's Reservation

Art. 858 is the practical center of this part: a note signed by both parties proves the contract was made, its content, and that the goods were received — and creates a presumption that the goods' outward condition and package count matched what the note records. A carrier can rebut this by entering a justified reservation on the note, including simply lacking the means to verify what is inside sealed packaging. Where no note exists, Art. 859 gives the sender a fallback right to demand a simpler, carrier-signed waybill — but a waybill, signed by only one party, carries none of Art. 858's strong presumptions.

## Accompanying Documents and Dangerous Goods

Art. 860 obliges the sender to hand over customs and other official documents before delivery, and makes the carrier liable (subject to a fault-based defense) for their loss, damage, or misuse — capped at the value payable for total loss of the goods. Art. 861 requires timely, clear, written notice of any dangerous cargo; without it, the carrier can unload, store, return, or destroy the goods without any liability to the sender, and can bill the sender for the cost of doing so.

## Practical Takeaway

For a foreign sender working with a Turkish carrier, insisting on a two-party-signed consignment note is the single most effective way to lock in favorable evidence before a dispute arises — an unsigned or single-signed document leaves the shipper with a much weaker starting position if goods arrive damaged.
